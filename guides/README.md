# Guides

Written introductions drawn from the patents and machines in this library. Every patent, machine and feature named
links to its page. The summaries behind them were made by machine from OCR, so check a patent's page and drawings
before relying on a detail.

## All kinds together

- [The master timeline](master-timeline.md): index, type-element and typebar machines in one chronology, era by era,
  with the features each era brought. The interactive version is the [timeline browser](../explore/timeline.md).
- [The American typewriter, landmark by landmark](../views/american-timeline.md): from Sholes & Glidden to the IBM
  Executive, which machine or patent brought each familiar feature, with sources and the disputed firsts.
- [Typewriter features through time](../views/typewriter-features.md): shift, ribbon, bell, margins, tabulator,
  backspace and more: when each appeared, when it became standard, and what survives today.

## Typebar machines

- [Typebar typewriters](typebar-typewriters.md): a type bar and a key per character, the form that won: understrike to
  visible writing, shift and keyboards, the makers.

## Type-element machines

- [Type-element typewriters](type-element-typewriters.md): keyboard machines printing from one wheel, shuttle,
  cylinder or sleeve: Crandall, Hammond, Blickensderfer and their kin.

## Index machines

- [What is an index typewriter?](index-typewriters.md): what makes a machine an index machine, why they were made,
  the ways of choosing a character, type carriers, printing, inking and spacing, and a glossary of English and German
  terms.
- [How index machines work](how-index-machines-work.md): the mechanisms subsystem by subsystem, with the variants and
  the best historical examples.
- [A timeline of index-machine features](timeline-of-features.md): the innovations that mattered to index machines,
  1829 to 1950, era by era.
- [German index typewriters, 1880–1950](german-index-typewriters.md): the hand-index machines of the 1880s–90s, the
  Mignon and its pointer-field cylinder, the small and toy machines of the 1920s–30s.
