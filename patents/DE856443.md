**How it works (reading):** Mount the intermediate-lever bearing on a movable carrier driven by the shift mechanism so the pull rod needs no slot.

## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** With segment (type-basket) shift, the key stroke differs between normal and shifted position; slotted intermediate levers give idle travel, bounce and wear, while a lever placed in front of the pivot wastes space.

**Solution:** Mount the intermediate-lever bearing on a movable carrier driven by the shift mechanism so the pull rod needs no slot.

**How it works:** The type-lever segment hangs on a parallelogram of two arms and is lifted by a shift lever through a pull rod, cam lever and spring. Each key lever pulls an intermediate lever by a rod, and the intermediate lever pulls the type lever by another rod, both pinned without slots. The intermediate lever bearing sits on a plate with adjustable rollers running on guide pins and is pulled by tension springs against adjustable stops. When shifted the cam moves this bearing so the rod angle is kept symmetrical about the vertical and the key works the same in both positions.
