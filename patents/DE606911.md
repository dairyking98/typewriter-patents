## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** An electric typewriter with a type wheel needs separate drives for rotating the wheel and for printing; the inventor wants one electromagnet to do both.

**Solution:** A single drive electromagnet pushes a plate carrying the type wheel against the platen, and a cord-and-spring drive rotates the wheel until a lever hits a stop set by the key's small control electromagnet.

**How it works:** Each key closes a contact that energises its own small control electromagnet in a circle on a plate, pushing out a non-magnetic stop, and also the drive electromagnet. The drive magnet moves the plate with the type wheel toward the paper roller. While it moves, a cord wound on a roller on the wheel shaft turns the wheel until a lever on the shaft hits the extended stop. A spring in the cord lets the plate keep moving, so the selected letter is struck against the paper. The same plate movement pushes a pawl on the rack and advances the carriage one tooth, which is pulled by a spring drum and held by a second pawl. Release returns the plate and wheel by springs. A lever lifts the wheel shaft by one row spacing for capitals, and a felt ink roller touches the wheel.
