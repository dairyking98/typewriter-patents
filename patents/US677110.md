## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** A visible-writing typebar machine with three characters per bar needs a platen that locks firmly in three positions and paper-handling parts (scale, feed roll, pointer) that open together for loading paper.

**Solution:** A three-position shifting platen carriage with positive locking in its normal middle position, plus a scale, pointer and feed roll linked so that lifting the scale also frees the feed roll.

**How it works:** Bell-crank typebars, each with three types, are worked by links from the key levers and strike a ribbon and platen at one point in view of the operator. Two shift key levers rock a shaft and levers that swing the platen carriage to either side of its normal middle position, selecting one of the three characters on each bar. A cam and spring-pressed locking lever, or a hooked arm, locks the carriage positively in the middle position and returns it there when the shift key is released. Spacing uses a fast and loose dog on a rack with a spring-drawn carriage. A double-space key adds a second tooth of spacing by releasing the loose dog, and a release lever frees the dogs for carriage return. A spring-held scale presses the paper to the platen and, at the end of its lift, pushes the feed roll away from the platen, and a spring pointer at the print point also acts as a friction paper holder. The typebar hanger takes up pivot wear with a socketed set screw.
