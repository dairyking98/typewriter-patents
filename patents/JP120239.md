## Summary

**Kind:** type-element machine · **Subject (patent classes):** Carriers stationary for impression, e.g. with the types or dies not moving relative to the carriers with the types or dies moving relative to the carriers or mounted on flexible carriers; Manual press type

**In the English translation of the patent’s own words:** The object: by arranging alphabet, Japanese kana, numeral or other types round a circle and printing the wanted one by turning it, to make the whole very small and simple, with no risk of the types falling out, so that the typewriter is easy to carry, convenient and cheap.

**How it works (reading):** Loose types stand in a ring between two nested cylinders, held by a ridge in cross-grooves; turning the outer cylinder brings the chosen type under a spring plunger that carries it down to print and, through a slanted plate and pawl, steps the whole double cylinder one space sideways.

## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Ordinary typewriters are large and heavy, and loose types in a compact machine can fall out; the inventor wants a very small, simple, cheap, carryable machine that can also print on things that cannot wrap round a roller.

**Solution:** Loose types stand in a ring between two nested cylinders, held by a ridge in cross-grooves; turning the outer cylinder brings the chosen type under a spring plunger that carries it down to print and, through a slanted plate and pawl, steps the whole double cylinder one space sideways.

**How it works:** Many types stand round the circle between an inner and an outer cylinder, one type-thickness apart; a ridge round the inner cylinder engages a cross-groove (nick) in every type so none can fall out. The outer cylinder is turned by hand until the wanted character, marked on its outside, lines up with an arrow at the single vertical gap in the inner cylinder. A lug on the printing plunger sits in that gap and engages the nick of the type there; pressing the plunger against its coil spring carries that type down onto the work. As the plunger descends, a slanted plate drives a slider whose pawl is held in a rack, so instead of the slider moving the double cylinder moves one character space; on release the spring lifts the plunger, the plate returns, and the pawl lifts and steps on one tooth. The cylinder rides on a rail across a base plate, two spring-loaded ink rollers in pockets ink the type faces as the outer cylinder turns, and an arrow-and-scale and a window show position and the printed text.

## Description (English translation of the Japanese text, opening)

> Specification. Typewriter. Nature and object of the invention. The outer of two nested cylinders, an inner and an outer, can be turned. Many types are arranged round the circle between the two cylinders. A raised ridge runs round the outside of the inner cylinder and fits into a cross-groove in each type (called a "nick"), which holds every type in place. The inner cylinder has one vertical gap, and into this gap fits a pusher projecting from a printing plunger that moves up and down. Turning the outer cylinder brings the wanted type to the gap; pressing the plunger down against its spring carries the type down with it and prints; and linked to the plunger's up-and-down stroke, the whole double cylinder moves sideways by one character space. The object: by arranging alphabet, Japanese kana, numeral or other types round a circle and printing the wanted one by turning it, to make the whole very small and simple, with no risk of the types falling out, so that the typewriter is easy to carry, convenient and cheap.
