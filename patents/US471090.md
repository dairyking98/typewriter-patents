## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** On Hammond-type machines the paper shield that keeps the type from soiling the paper hid the freshly printed line, was hard to remove, and could smear the print.

**Solution:** A thin sheet-steel paper shield with a recessed hammer opening and wide sight openings, mounted by end sleeves on posts carried by hinged arms so it can be flipped away or swapped quickly.

**How it works:** The shield is a thin steel strip with sleeves at the ends that slip onto posts. Its centre has a hammer opening formed in a vertical groove so the side bars stand away from the paper when the hammer retracts, and the rest of the shield is slightly convex toward the paper so a moving printed line never rubs it. Sight openings left and right of the hammer opening show the last impression and the approaching paper. The posts hang from arms hinged on the type-wheel guard, joined by an arched bridge, so the whole shield swings up and back over the type segments; a hook holds it down in use.
