## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** The connections between key and typebar in front-strike machines were complicated, loose and exposed to dirt.

**Solution:** Fold the typebar from sheet metal so it shields its bearing, join it to a solid hanger with an adjustable-fit hollow rivet, and connect keys to bars through fan-shaped arms, sublevers and links with no lost motion.

**How it works:** Each typebar is sheet metal folded along its centre line, its sides spread at the pivot end to straddle a solid hanger and cover the bearing. A hollow eyelet through bar and hanger is spun over, its pressure setting a close working fit. Hangers have staggered enlarged slotted portions screwed to a curved segment, allowing radial adjustment. A key lever's pin-and-slot drives a fan-shaped arm pivoted at the rear, whose link rocks a bell-crank sublever, which through a link throws the typebar up to the platen. A plate-spring tongue repels the sublever to prevent double impressions. The three working points of each arm lie in a straight line so it does not twist.
