## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typewriters are too bulky to carry; the inventor wants a complete machine small enough for a coat pocket, about 5 x 5 x 1 in. and one pound.

**Solution:** A flat wheel carrying spring-returned radial typebars is turned by pegging an indicator arm through a hole in the top plate, and the same peg then presses a single main lever that drives one hammer to print and space.

**How it works:** Seventy-five spring-loaded type pins slide radially in a 4 in. wheel, with the type on their outer ends, grouped in three cases (full stop and capitals, comma and lower case, figures and punctuation). Three indicator arms of different length on the wheel arbor each correspond to one case. The operator drops a peg through an indicator eye and a hole in the top plate, moves it to the character's hole, which turns the wheel, then presses the peg down on the main lever. The lever feet rock a spindle and hinged hammer, which strikes the inner end of the selected pin and drives it out against a flat typing board. The same hammer stroke moves a ratchet plate that releases a detent on the notched bar, and a spring drum pulls the board one step; a side lever spaces words and another frees the carriage. A spring-mounted ink pad touches the type heads as the wheel turns.

**Parts:** type wheel; arbor; type pin; return spring; indicator arm; operating peg; top plate; main lever; hammer; ink pad; typing board; notched bar; spring drum; detent; casing

**Numbers:** 5 in. square by 1 in. deep; weight about 1 lb; type wheel 4 in. diameter, 1/4 in. deep; 75 characters; 28 holes in top plate; plates 3/4 in. apart; wheel arms 1/32 in. thick; groove 3/16 in. wide, 1/8 in. deep; 3 indicators

**Improves on:**

- conventional roller-platen typewriters: much smaller; flat typing board takes postcards and envelopes without bending
