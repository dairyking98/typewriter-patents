## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typebar key-lever mechanisms are complicated, and the type system cannot easily be swung away or replaced for a different language.

**Solution:** Each typebar gets a floating bell-crank key lever guided on a fixed rail and joined by a single spring, in a self-contained upper frame that shifts relative to the platen and can be detached.

**How it works:** Normally vertical type-bars with three types each pivot in an arc-shaped upper frame. A floating key lever has a vertical slot over a fixed rail and an arm engaging a recess in the typebar near its pivot. A tension spring joins key lever and typebar, so depressing the key swings the typebar while the spring extends, and on release the spring contracts and restores both. Each typebar's cam-shaped end operates a universal escapement bar. A shift key moves the whole upper frame on pivoted arms relative to the platen, and the frame can be swung clear or removed to swap type sets and keyboards for other languages.

**Features:** [ELEM:typebar](../features/elem-typebar.md), [SEL:keyboard-full](../features/sel-keyboard-full.md), [ESC:pawl-rack](../features/esc-pawl-rack.md), [SHIFT:triple](../features/shift-triple.md), [ELEM:interchangeable](../features/elem-interchangeable.md), [FRM:few-parts](../features/frm-few-parts.md), [FRM:cleaning-access](../features/frm-cleaning-access.md)

**Parts:** type-bar; floating key lever; fixed rail; tension spring; upper frame; arc-shaped block; universal escapement bar; shift key; pivoted arm; platen

**Numbers:** three types per type-bar
