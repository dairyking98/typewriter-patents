## Summary

**Category:** related office machine, not a typewriter (calculating machine blocking device)

**How it works (reading):** The locking lug has an extension below its pivot that a second pin in the tens tooth pushes against, so the tens tooth alternately moves the lug back and replaces the spring.

## Reading (short summary from the opening, OCR fair)

**Problem:** A two-part locking lug in a calculator's rotating-part lock relies on a spring that can break, so the lug may not reset after a carry.

**Solution:** The locking lug has an extension below its pivot that a second pin in the tens tooth pushes against, so the tens tooth alternately moves the lug back and replaces the spring.

> Description Translated from German Device for locking the rotating parts on calculating machines By patent 475 3 = o, a locking of the rotating parts on calculating machines is protected, which makes it possible to achieve a completely mandatory function in those places where no decimal operation occurs. At the point in which a ten circuit takes place, the blocking part SP-K, since the ten circuit can also take place when the block is closed, must be triggered. As a result, the locking part, as shown in the drawing of the patent, Figs. 6 to 17, must consist of two parts, and the part K is held in the correct position by a spring F. In the event of a break in the spring, the locking lug K would possibly fail, insofar as the locking lug K would not be reinserted into the ratchet wheel Er by the spring F after completing the ten circuit. For this reason, in the present improved arrangement, the locking lug K is provided with an elongated approach down below the pivot point, against which a second pin Ase located in the tens tooth Z abuts, which then causes the locking tooth K to move in alternately with the first and thereby takes over the function of the spring F and replaces it.
