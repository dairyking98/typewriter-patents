## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** A carriage that moves between two typebar systems needs simple, cheap letter feeding, with longer spacing for one system than for the other.

**Solution:** Connect both keyboards' universal bars to a single escapement and give the carriage a double-length rack of two racks end to end, one meshing a small pinion and the other a large pinion on the same escapement wheel.

**How it works:** Two Underwood machine frames sit on a common base, each with its own type system; a roller platen in a shiftable frame on one carriage travels between them. Each universal bar is pivoted to arms on one transverse rock shaft, so any key in either system rocks the shaft, which has an arm engaging the dog rocker of a single escapement of letter-feeding dogs and an escapement wheel. Each keyboard's space key also works this common escapement. The rack device on the carriage, hinged for release, has a small-pinion rack and a large-pinion rack; the pinions are rigidly joined and drive the escapement wheel through a pawl and ratchet, so the smaller pinion gives short feeds and the larger gives long feeds.
