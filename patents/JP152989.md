## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A Japanese-language typewriter needs a striking device that prints the selected type reliably, is sturdy and has few parts to break or go out of adjustment.

**Solution:** A striking key lever drives, through a curved lever, an auxiliary lever and a spring-returned vertical crank shaft, a link that swings a bent lever carrying a printing hammer.

**How it works:** A main lever (5) pivoted mid-length rises at one end when the key (2) is struck, and its roller (6) pushes up one end of a curved lever (8). The other end of the curved lever draws an auxiliary lever (11) back, which turns the crank (13) of a vertical crank shaft (12) against a return spring (14). The upper crank (15) pulls a link (16) joined to the bend of a bent lever (17) pivoted at its short arm. Its long arm swings a T-shaped hammer (23) in a holder, and the hammer presses on the back of the type that the type cylinder has brought to the print position, driving it onto the paper on the platen. The spring returns everything. Type selection and paper positioning are done by separate mechanisms not shown.
