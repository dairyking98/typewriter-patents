## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** On an electrically actuated typewriter, the carriage feed needs to be positive and reliable with few parts, and must not overrun by more than one space.

**Solution:** A sliding plate carrying a pivoted feed pawl is moved by a lever tied to the solenoid armature, with a cam that engages and releases the pawl and a checking pawl that is locked to the rack at the end of each space.

**How it works:** The key-controlled solenoid armature swings an operating lever. The lever's roller runs in an inclined slot in a plate sliding on the frame. Moving the plate backward lifts the feed pawl into the carriage rack through a slotted cam on the plate. The return stroke slides the plate forward, and the pawl pushes the carriage one space. Near the end of the stroke an eccentric edge of the cam hits a fixed stud and drops the pawl out of the rack. A spring-pressed checking pawl rides on the rack, and a rock-shaft arm linked to the plate locks it hard against the rack just before the end of the stroke, so the carriage cannot move more than one space.
