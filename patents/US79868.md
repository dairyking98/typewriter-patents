## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** A simple, compact, reliable machine is wanted for writing correspondence with printing types instead of a pen.

**Solution:** A slotted annular disk guides radial typebars to a single central point beneath a fixed platen, with keys and rods working the bars and a weight-driven escape-wheel carriage that advances one tooth per key.

**How it works:** A brass disk with a central hole carries radial grooves and edge slots, one per character; steel typebars hang on a single circumferential pivot wire and each is linked by a rod to a key lever below. Striking a key throws its type up through the hole against the paper and platen hung above, then the bar falls back. A rod resting across all key levers (plus an extra spacing key with no type) trips a two-pawl escape lever so a weight-driven drum and escape wheel advance the paper carriage by one letter width. A pawl on the carriage hitting a row of pins on the return stroke moves the paper one line at right angles, and a hinged clamp holds paper and carbon sheet or ribbon.

**Parts:** annular disk; typebar; pivot wire; key lever; connecting rod; platen block; paper carriage; weight and drum; escape wheel; escape lever with two pawls; universal rod across keys; space key; line-space pin row; line-space pawl; paper clamp

**Numbers:** disk 4 to 5 in. diameter; disk rim 0.5 to 0.75 in. thick; central hole 1 to 1.5 in. diameter; disk web 0.25 to 0.375 in. thick

**Improves on:**

- [US79265](../patents/US79265.md): typebars now driven by rods and levers from keys instead of fingers on the key ends; escape-wheel and drum drive replaces the forked-lever ratchet bar
