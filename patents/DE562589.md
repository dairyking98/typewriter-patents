## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** An electric type-cylinder typewriter needs to rotate the cylinder either way from a single electromagnet with a fixed armature stroke.

**Solution:** A magnet armature moves an actuator that acts through wedge surfaces on two oppositely movable toothed racks, one of which is stopped by the struck key, so the cylinder is turned by the difference of travel.

**How it works:** Type characters sit in six rows on a type cylinder. Struck keys push stops into pairs of gaps in two toothed racks. The rotary magnet pulls an actuator that first moves both racks evenly until one is caught by a stop, then carries the free one along, which moves an adjusting body sideways and rotates the cylinder one way or the other through a rotary lever and gear sector meshing with a gear on the cylinder shaft. The armature always travels the same stroke whatever the rotation. Two shift keys lift the cylinder shaft by rows through levers and tie rods; a second magnet gives an extra lift by one row, and a further magnet presses the cylinder against the paper roller to print. Two keys of vertically adjacent types share one stop.
