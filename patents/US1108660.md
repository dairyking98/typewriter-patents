## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** A shorthand typewriter that prints on a continuous paper strip needed a better paper feed (the earlier push feed could crimp soft paper), a way to rewind the notes, and shift keys that let few keys reach many characters without the type frame springing back.

**Solution:** Three rotatable type frames on one shaft are turned by shift keys against stops so that each of sixteen simultaneously strikable key bars prints a chosen row, while a pivoted feed frame pulls the paper strip up by a gripping action and a rewinder takes it up.

**How it works:** Sixteen key bars lie side by side under three type frames (sector-like frames carrying rows of characters) that turn on a common shaft and are held against a rear stop by springs. With no shift the first row of each frame is over its key bar; pressing a shift key swings a frame until a stop pin meets a stop on the shift key, bringing another row over the keys. The shift keys are arranged so each one's stop is removed from the path when it is pressed, so two shift keys select among three rows and the frame is held rigid by two stops. Striking one or more key bars presses the paper against the types and lifts a pivoted feed frame; a pressure frame on it grips the strip and pulls it upward through a one-way clutch, which prevents backward motion. A ribbon runs between reels driven by an elastic belt from the paper roll, and a ratchet-driven spring-coupled rewind plate collects the notes.

**Parts:** key bar; type frame; shift key; stop pin; feed frame; pressure frame; one-way clutch; ribbon reel; elastic belt; ratchet wheel; rewind plate; guide block

**Numbers:** 16 printing keys; 3 type frames; 28 characters per central-style frame in 4 rows of 7; recess about 1/4 in. long and 1/16 in. deep in key bars; 8 sheets of drawings

**Improves on:**

- US581570 (Anderson shorthand machine): paper is pulled up by a positive grip instead of pushed, with the two grips at the same level
