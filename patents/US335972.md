## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typebar machines needed better guiding of the types to the printing point, better spacing, and a better paper carriage and carriage drive.

**Solution:** Typebars hinged to the ends of sheet-metal key levers are steered by inclined guides into a central throat at the print point.

**How it works:** Each key lever is a light bent sheet-metal bar with a typebar hinged to its rear end by a vertical pivot. Pressing the key first lifts the bar, then an inclined guide swings it toward the centre until the type enters a throat piece that centres it, and further depression strikes the paper. Wire springs return the bars. A U-shaped spacing frame under the key levers on a rock shaft, operated by a space bar, drives the spacing; the carriage construction is described in the truncated part.

**Features:** [Typebars](../features/elem-typebar.md), [Full keyboard, a key per character](../features/sel-keyboard-full.md), [Element struck to the platen](../features/imp-element-to-platen.md), [Space key or bar](../features/esc-space-key.md), [Frame construction](../features/frm-frame-construction.md), [Adjustment and wear take-up](../features/frm-adjustment-wear.md)

**Parts:** key lever; typebar; type; inclined guide; throat piece; return spring; tension screw; spacing frame; space bar; paper carriage
