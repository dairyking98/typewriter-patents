## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** A skipping (tabulating) device from the inventor's earlier patent had to be adapted to machines such as the Caligraph with fixed rack and movable detent, and the Remington with a liftable rack.

**Solution:** A skipping key rocks the trip rod so the detent drops out of the feed racks and an adjustable block stops the carriage at a preset lug, giving column skips.

**How it works:** On a Caligraph-type carriage the detent is slotted so it can slide down off its pivot, held up by a spring. A skipping key links through a rod to an arm on the trip rod, which both pushes the detent down out of the racks and tips a sliding set-screwed block into the path of a lug on the carriage. The carriage runs freely forward until the lug meets the block; releasing the key re-engages the detent. Any number of blocks can be set along the rod for columns, and modifications adapt it to Remington-style raised racks.
