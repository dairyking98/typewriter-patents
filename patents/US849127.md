## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** A single-key typebar with a head of several characters needs a way to bring each character to the same print point without shifting a platen.

**Solution:** Typebar heads carry three faces across them; pivot shafts are tilted by shifting inner or outer journal-plate ends so a chosen face reaches the print point, held by an alignment guide with teeth and notches.

**How it works:** Typebars are mounted in two groups, either side of a central alignment guide with parallel cheeks. Each bar head carries several type faces transverse to the bar, on planes inclined to each other so one is parallel to a flat platen. Two shift keys move one end of a triangular journal plate outward or inward and rock the plate, so lower, middle or upper face strikes the printing point. Teeth on the guide and notches on the head set each character exactly. A shifting ribbon guide keeps the print point visible.

**Features:** [ELEM:typebar](../features/elem-typebar.md), [SHIFT:triple](../features/shift-triple.md), [ELEM:alignment-lock](../features/elem-alignment-lock.md), [SEL:keyboard-full](../features/sel-keyboard-full.md), [FRM:writing-visible](../features/frm-writing-visible.md), [PAP:flat-bed](../features/pap-flat-bed.md), [INK:ribbon](../features/ink-ribbon.md)

**Parts:** typebar; type head; journal plate; pivot shaft; alignment guide; shift key; ribbon guide; flat platen; base frame

**Numbers:** three characters per key (3x as many characters as keys)

**Improves on:**

- platen-shifting typebar machines: shifts the typebar bearings instead of the carriage or platen
