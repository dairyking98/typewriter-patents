## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** On the Hammond machine the carriage motor spring drives the hammer stroke, so impression strength could not be adjusted for manifolding or for a weak or strong ribbon without separate adjustments.

**Solution:** A single adjustment that changes the carriage-motor spring tension and the hammer spring tension together.

**How it works:** The carriage motor spring sits in a toothed spring barrel meshing with the carriage rack bar. The spring's inner end engages a pin on a sleeve that rides in a longitudinal channel of the spindle, so turning the spindle winds or relaxes the spring while the spindle can also move lengthwise. A screw thread in one spindle bearing makes that lengthwise motion, which also adjusts the hammer spring. Friction plates with leather or rubber washers hold the setting, and a ratchet and dog let the spindle be wound without fighting the friction. It can be fitted to any Hammond by tapping the rear spindle bearing in the bed plate.
