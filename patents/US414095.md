## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** The text gives no explicit problem; the design aims to keep type-carrier movement short by using a second interchangeable carrier with fewer types, and to have one key both position and print.

**Solution:** A flat carrier of thin typebars slides in a straight line in guides to bring a chosen bar in front of a fixed hammer, with the guides raised or lowered to choose between the two types on each bar.

**How it works:** A flat carrier holds a row of resilient steel typebars, each with two types one above the other, and slides in right-line guides in front of the platen. Each key carries a cam lever whose pivot distance sets how far a lever system moves a slide and thus the carrier sideways; the keys come in pairs, one of which tilts the guides up and the other down through a common key rod, a slotted slide and a link frame to choose the upper or lower type. The same key-rod motion works a cam lever that drives a hammer against the typebar and platen. Each cam lever has a cavity that engages a pin on the slide to locate the type accurately at the hammer. A second carrier with other types can be swapped for the first by sliding it into place through rack and toothed segments, and idle carriers are locked by a projection and recess. The platen carriage is advanced one tooth per stroke by a tapered dog on a slide driven by the hammer lever, and the ribbon spools are fed by pawls in either direction and can be raised to use the full ribbon width.
