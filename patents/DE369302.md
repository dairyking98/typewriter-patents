## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Automatic typewriters needed a separate hole in the control strip for every letter, making a wide strip.

**Solution:** A type-wheel (Hammond-type) machine whose wheel is positioned by combinations of 5-7 holes or 4-5 keys acting through coded setting discs and a pawl, driven pneumatically.

**How it works:** The type wheel sits on a vertical shaft with a gear driven by a rack on a pneumatic piston, and the shaft is lifted in steps by two levers of different length to bring one of three type rows to the hammer. Several rotatable setting discs with notches at different spacings on the wheel shaft are each turned by a key or vacuum-operated lever. A spring-pressed pawl in the wheel catches only when the notches of all discs line up, locking the wheel at the position that matches the combination. A vacuum pneumatic valve and piston then drive the hammer, with paper and ribbon between hammer and wheel. Operation is manual by keys or automatic from a narrow perforated paper strip read by a suction mouthpiece.
