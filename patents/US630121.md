## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** The carriage escapement needed a lighter touch and quicker action, feeding one letter-space per key stroke.

**Solution:** A fixed escapement wheel and a loose spring-coupled wheel on one shaft, alternately held by a key-operated dog, so the carriage steps half on key down and half on key release.

**How it works:** An intermittently rotating spacer shaft is driven by the ordinary carriage spring through a pinion on the rack bar. Fixed to it is an escapement wheel with two slots; a loose wheel below is joined to it by a coiled spring and carries two headed screws passing through the slots. A rock shaft with a dog, worked by the letter-space lever, engages one wheel then the other. On key depression the loose wheel moves a fraction less than a space until its screws hit the slot ends, while the dog holds the fixed wheel during the impression. On release the dog withdraws and the fixed wheel and shaft advance the full letter-space, the loose wheel following at the slot ends.

**Features:**

- **Spacing and escapement:** [Escape-wheel escapement](../features/esc-escape-wheel.md), [Pawl-and-rack escapement](../features/esc-pawl-rack.md), [Space key or bar](../features/esc-space-key.md)

**Parts:** spacer shaft; fixed escapement wheel; loose escapement wheel; coiled spring; slotted wheel; headed screw; rock shaft; dog; letter-space lever; rack bar; loose cog wheel with ratchet

**Numbers:** two opposite slots in the fixed wheel
