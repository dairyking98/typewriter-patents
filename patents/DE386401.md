## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Carriage switching drums with pawls or bolts as locking bodies are costly to make and imprecise in spacing.

**Solution:** Use spring-loaded steel balls radially in a switching drum as the locking bodies.

**How it works:** A drum built of a middle disc and two side discs with covers holds 12 balls, each pushed outward by a coil spring. Two vertically sliding pawls act as stops against the balls; on each keystroke the switching bridge lifts one pawl so one ball moves inward, the drum is turned by the carriage spring until the next ball meets the other pawl. The drum shaft carries a gear on the carriage rack, so one ball pitch moves the carriage one letter space. A wedge on the pawl lets the drum finish its step even if the key is released slowly, and a lever with a cam disc keeps the second pawl from dropping.

## Description (Google Patents OCR, opening)

> Description Translated from German Paper carriage switching device. Switching devices for the paper carriages of typewriters are known in which the switching is carried out by a switching drum with several spring-action locking bodies, through one in the path of movement the locking body protruding stop and through a the locking body over the stop lifting ratchet that comes into effect with every keystroke. The known Switching devices of this type, however, are extremely complex to manufacture and costly and of secondary importance in their usefulness. The locking bodies consist of locking pawls rotatably mounted in the shift drum or axially displaceable locking bolt mounted in the shift drum, their storage and guidance inaccuracies in the distance between the individual locking bodies from one another. The shortcomings of the known devices are alleviated by the invention eliminated. according to which balls are used as the locking body, which in the Switching drum are arranged radially displaceable under spring action.
