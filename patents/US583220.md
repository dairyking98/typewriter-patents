## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Ribbons fed lengthwise wear a single narrow stripe, need frequent hand reversing and hand lateral shifting of the spools.

**Solution:** The carriage's own travel pushes the ribbon sideways across its width, and the ribbon is wound lengthwise only one small step per full carriage cycle.

**How it works:** The two ribbon spools sit on shafts joined by a bent wire rod running obliquely. A bifurcated arm on the paper carriage straddles that rod, so as the carriage steps along the line the rod, shafts and spools slide in and out and carry the ribbon bodily across its width, slightly less than one ribbon width per full line. The spools cannot slide on their shafts. During the quick carriage return the ribbon is moved back to the front, and a weighted pawl on a lever, whose inner end rides up an inclined bar fixed to the frame, turns a ratchet on the spool head by about one letter space. A second pawl stops the spool backing up. The whole ribbon width is used and the ribbon needs no attention for hundreds of thousands of impressions.

**Features:**

- **Inking:** [Ink ribbon](../features/ink-ribbon.md), [Ribbon feed](../features/ink-ribbon-feed.md)
- **Spacing and escapement:** [Pawl-and-rack escapement](../features/esc-pawl-rack.md)

**Parts:** ribbon spool; spool shaft; connecting rod; bifurcated carriage arm; ratchet teeth; feed pawl; holding dog; weighted lever; inclined bar; perforated bearing

**Numbers:** ribbon moves slightly less than its own width per carriage pass; ribbon winds about one letter space per carriage cycle; hundreds of thousands of impressions per ribbon

**Improves on:**

- straight lengthwise ribbon feed with hand reversal: no reversing and no manual lateral shifting
