## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A simple, cheap, fast wheel typewriter in which the operator should not have to place the pointer exactly on each character of the letter plate.

**Solution:** An oscillating pointer arm turns the type wheel via a gear segment over a letter plate, and a V-toothed wheel with a V-shaped tooth on the platen's primary carriage snaps the wheel to the nearest character at every print stroke while the platen's fore-and-aft vibration also spaces the letters.

**How it works:** A type wheel with two rows of type is fixed on a shaft that slides axially (a key lever raises it to change row) and is turned through a loose pinion and V-notched wheel linked to it by a pin, driven by a gear segment on a hand-held oscillating arm that sweeps over the letter plate. A separate print key tilts a non-traveling primary platen carriage, which throws the longitudinally traveling secondary platen carriage forward against the type; at the same stroke a V-shaped tooth on the primary carriage enters the nearest V notch, correcting a rough pointer position and locking the wheel. An inking roller is pushed away from the type by a link from the platen carriage as it advances. A pawl fixed to the frame drops into the next tooth of a ratchet bar on the secondary carriage on the forward stroke, so on the return the platen moves one tooth for letter spacing; a second word-space key tilts the carriage only enough to space without printing, or to release the pawl for carriage return. A hook in a slit stops the platen at the end of a line to prevent overprinting.
