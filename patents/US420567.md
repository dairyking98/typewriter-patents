## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** In typebar machines with the key-levers below the bed, the closely spaced levers touch each other and the stem-keys and connecting rods bind in the perforations they pass through.

**Solution:** Each key-lever is pivoted in a socket piece that slips onto its own upright post fixed to a base support, so all levers swing in parallel vertical planes without touching.

**How it works:** A base plate sits under the bed plate and carries upright posts with collars. A socket piece with a forked top slips over each post and holds the key-lever at its middle pivot. A stem-key socket pivoted at one end of the lever passes up through the perforated bed plate, and a connecting-rod socket at the other end passes up to the typebar. Pressing the key lowers its end and raises the rod, swinging the typebar to the platen; releasing returns everything. The lever is assembled by inserting it between the plates, turning the stem socket up through the bed hole, then dropping the middle socket over its post, and removal is the reverse.

- **Selection:** [Full keyboard, a key per character](../features/sel-keyboard-full.md)
- **Typebars:** [Typebars](../features/elem-typebar.md), [Key touch and even impression](../features/bar-touch.md)
- **Impression:** [Element struck to the platen](../features/imp-element-to-platen.md)
- **Frame and body:** [Few parts, low cost](../features/frm-few-parts.md)

**Parts:** bed plate; base support plate; post; socket piece; key-lever; stem-key; connecting rod; typebar; type circle; platen
