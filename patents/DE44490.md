## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Known gear or pawl escapements advance the paper by whole teeth regardless of letter width, so spacing cannot follow the characters; also space limits the number of types.

**Solution:** Types sit in two rows on the upper and lower faces of a rotatable type ruler that slides in a frame, and notches of different depth in the ruler drive a friction-disk feed that advances the paper by a letter-dependent amount.

**How it works:** A frame b, moved by a handle along a rail, carries a pivoted inner frame holding the type ruler (flippable between small letters and capitals) and a felt inking ruler that rocks as the frame is pressed. The operator slides the ruler until the wanted type is at a fixed pointer, reading an index scale on the frame, then presses the frame down to print through a lug that exposes one type. The paper carriage runs in a rail with a toothed rack driven by a gear fixed to a finely knurled disk. A knife-edged lever drops into the notched bar under the ruler and presses a knurled curved lever on the disk, turning it by an amount set by notch depth, so the paper moves by the type width. A weight returns the lever without reversing the disk. A bell sounds at line end.
