## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** A conventional typewriter needs about 40 keys plus a shift key for roughly 80 characters, so the fingers wander and the key mechanism is complicated and costly.

**Solution:** Group the characters on a few type sectors, each carrying 6 to 9 characters, and print any one by pressing two keys together: a selection key from one small keyboard (which tilts the sector to the wanted character) and an operating key from the other (which swings that sector to the platen).

**How it works:** Nine type carriers each have a sector carrying nine characters, and each is tied to one operating key. A selection key (eight of them, in two groups of four, or five keys in a later version) moves a common parallel-guided cross bar up or down by one of four amounts through levers of different lengths, which tilts all sector bars to a given angle. When an operating key then swings its sector to the platen, the chosen character on that sector meets the paper, and the middle character prints with no selection. In the second form each sector lever carries a pulley on a friction sleeve joined by an endless cord to a pulley on the sector; selection keys raise levers that the lever's own sector faces catch, releasing a stop so the sector turns relative to its lever by one of eight steps (the ninth is the default). A hook and spring latch keeps the selection lever up if the selection key is let go early. In the third form there are 13 operating keys with sectors of six characters (78 total) and a five-key selection pad whose key moves a cross bar that tilts the sector bar by a key-specific angle during its forward stroke through a groove-and-wedge coupling.
