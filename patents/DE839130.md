## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** On punch-card controlled business machines the control holes O, X and R only give three letter groups, which limits the printable characters and prevents mixed upper and lower case.

**Solution:** A second hole sensed in the digit zone shifts the clutch release one tooth later, so the same selection mechanism picks the neighbouring character and doubles the character set.

**How it works:** Each print position has a type wheel carrying digits, capitals, lower case and special characters, mounted on a swinging arm. A toothed sleeve turns continuously, and a clutch pawl on a gear coupled to the wheel is released by a lever at a time set by the card hole sensed (1-9). The wheel then turns by a number of teeth and is stopped when the pawl is disengaged. A zone selector disk set by the O, X or R control hole picks the letter group, and a second digit hole moves a sliding plate that delays the release one tooth to select the adjacent type. When the wheel is at the right tooth, a locking pawl releases the arm and a rocker swings the wheel against a pressure roller with the ribbon between. The rocker then returns the wheel to its base position.
