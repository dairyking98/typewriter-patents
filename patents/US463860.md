## Summary

**Kind:** type-element machine · **Subject (patent classes):** Typewriters or selective printing mechanisms characterised by the mounting, arrangement or disposition of the types or dies with types or dies mounted on carriers rotatable for selection the plane of the type or die face being parallel to the axis of rotation, e.g. with type on the periphery of cylindrical carriers

**In the patent’s own words:** This invention relates principally to keytype-writing machines in which the keys or the stems of the keys are employed to operate the machine directly without the use or interposition of key-levers.

**How it works (reading):** A type sleeve is driven through a small pinion by a quadrant and stopped by a geared stop-finger that meets the depressed key stem, with a spring-released ram and a spring-loaded floating paper roller.

## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Gardner's earlier machine (US463859) used a type segment, which has momentum and vibrates. It also needed a better paper carriage and a safer field-shifting lever.

**Solution:** A type sleeve is driven through a small pinion by a quadrant and stopped by a geared stop-finger that meets the depressed key stem, with a spring-released ram and a spring-loaded floating paper roller.

**How it works:** Key stems, bent twice at right angles, press an inclined arc lever below the lower plate. This turns a crank and quadrant that drive a small pinion on the type sleeve. A stop-finger geared to the sleeve pinion sweeps above the plate and is stopped by the upturned part of the depressed key stem. This locates the sleeve at the right character. The same key motion works a quadrant lever with fast and loose dogs on an escape wheel, which spaces the carriage one tooth, and trips a weighted detent to release a spring-loaded ram that prints. The shift key can also space alone, without printing, by working a separate rocking-shaft arm. A spring inside the sleeve returns it after an axial shift between fields. A lengthened field-lever handle under a slot in the top plate gives three or four fields without mistakes. The paper carriage has pivoted loose ends and a spring rod that press the feed roller evenly on a rigid bed, plus a detachable rod to hold the paper.
