## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Japanese-script typewriters with a large type plate are heavy to turn, noisy and break down; the inventors want a light, quiet, sure-printing machine.

**Solution:** Mount the type frames in holders riveted to an endless steel or leather belt whose rivet heads engage recesses in a rotating plate, so the whole belt of type frames circulates, and print by pushing one type up from a frame at the feed-out point.

**How it works:** Frame holders, each carrying a frame of a row of types, are fixed to an endless belt; the hemispherical rivet heads fit recesses around a main rotating plate (and a groove in a following plate), and end rollers run in grooves of fixed support plates. The operator turns a shaft to bring a frame to the feed-out point, finding the character on an index face eleven places ahead with a search piece that moves with the carriage. Pressing the knob lever feeds the frame onto a support table, a push rod lifts the chosen type up through a guide hole, and a drawbar-driven linkage closes a holding blade on the type and swings the type holder to strike the platen. Releasing the knob returns everything.
