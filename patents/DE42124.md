## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** In machines where the printing unit moves over the paper, the unit tends to slip too far right, and the setting for the next letter must be fixed firmly; a double alphabet is also wanted.

**Solution:** A hand lever carries a pointer over an index plate with two alphabets while swinging a rubber type segment to the same letter, then a stamp presses that type through a window onto the paper and the pressing also advances the unit.

**How it works:** The paper is clamped between rollers over a base. Pushing the hand lever d forward or back shows one of two concentric alphabets in the arcuate cutout of the top plate and, through a pivot pin, tilts the segment-shaped rubber type plate to match. The pointer on the lever is moved over the letter on the index plate, shifting the type plate while it slides over an ink pad. Pressing the lever down depresses a stamp lever whose stamp presses the selected rubber type through a cutout in the lower plate onto the paper. The same motion pushes an arm that moves a spring pawl along a ratchet rack, advancing the whole writing unit one step, with a second rack and pawl holding it against sliding back. Pressing the pawl lever directly gives word spaces.
