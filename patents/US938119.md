## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Changing between the two colour fields of a two-colour ribbon (and to a stencil position) needed a switch lever or two separate keys, so the operator had to reach to different places and the control did not always return to the same spot.

**Solution:** A single ribbon key that always returns to the same position toggles a pivoted shifting member alternately in opposite directions on successive presses, changing the ribbon vibrator's throw to select the black field, the red field, or (via a finger piece) no ribbon for stencil work.

**How it works:** On each type stroke, a universal bar rocks a shaft with a curved arm, and a sliding block on that arm sets the leverage of a link that lifts the ribbon vibrator; the block's distance from the pivot sets the lift. A pivoted notched shifting member sets the block position through a link, and a spring-loaded roller detent holds it in one of three notches. The ribbon key works a bell crank carrying a floating T-shaped puller whose head has notches. The puller engages a stud on the shifting member on one side of its pivot, then on the other side at the next press, so the member swings back and forth. The notches also limit the swing. A pointer on the member shows black, red or white (no ribbon) against marks on the corner post. Pushing the pointer further moves the block past the pivot so the vibrator drops away from the platen for stencil cutting.
