## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typebar front-strike machines either printed out of sight or crowded all the bars into half the type ring; three-case machines also needed two shift keys.

**Solution:** A visible front-strike typewriter with bars pivoted all round a full type ring, and a platen carriage that rocks about its guide rail so one shift key (pressed down for capitals, lifted for special characters) selects three cases.

**How it works:** Typebars carrying two or three characters are pivoted around a full ring tilted slightly back, with long and short sets double-bent for clearance, and strike through the ring centre so the writing is seen at once. The paper carriage rides on a grooved rail on three bearing balls, and a front rail on a shifting frame; the whole carriage rocks about the rear rail axis. Depressing the shift key lowers the platen to upper case, lifting it raises it to a third case, with centring springs and a beveled spring check cam that stops it quickly at middle. The carriage can also be lifted by hand to read the line. Spring-tensioned feed bands press paper to the platen, a rack disengages from the escapement dog by a release key, and double-spacing keys are on a separate row.
