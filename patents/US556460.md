## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Basket-style typebar machines are long, and worn typebar pivots upset alignment, while key leverage falls off during the stroke.

**Solution:** Hang the typebars in a short vertical-circle segment with long, widely spaced pivots, and connect keys to the bars through bell-crank levers that keep a constant leverage, so the keyboard sits close to the carriage.

**How it works:** Typebars pivot in rows stepped at 45 degrees above and outside each other, each bar shorter than the one below so the outer ends line up while all print at one point. Each bar has a long pivot shaft passing freely through enlarged holes in the adjacent hanger plates and carried only in distant plates, giving a wide bearing. Finger-keys drive bell-crank levers whose upper ends, joined by wire links with U-bends, lift the short ends of the typebars so the leverage stays the same through the stroke. A universal frame with adjustable cross-bars is pushed by the levers and rocks an arched frame carrying feed dogs, which advance the carriage on its rack; the frame also lifts the ribbon. Shift bars raise the whole typebar frame for upper case.

**Numbers:** pivot rows on a line at 45 degrees to the vertical through the print point
