## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** A ribbon vibrator that is only lifted by a lever and returned by a spring can overshoot on hard key blows, rattle, and does not return to a definite position; it also must present different colour fields or stay idle for stencils.

**Solution:** The vibrator arm runs in two crossed slots, one in a fixed guide plate and one in an adjustable lever on the key-driven rocking bar, so the ribbon is positively lifted and positively returned, and the angle of the lever's slot sets the throw.

**How it works:** A bell-crank ribbon vibrator pivoted on a front bracket holds the ribbon in a loop below the printing point; its lower vertical arm (optionally with a roller) passes through a transverse slot in a fixed guide plate and also through a slot in a finger lever pivoted on the key-driven rocking bar. Each key stroke rocks the bar, and the lever's slot walls cam the arm sideways along the fixed slot, swinging the vibrator to lift the ribbon; on the return stroke the slot walls drive it back positively. When the lever slot is at right angles to the fixed slot, the arm just slides and the ribbon stays inactive for stencils. Tilting the lever to a second or third position changes the slot angle, giving a small or large throw to present a black or red field; intermediate positions use the whole width of a single-colour ribbon. A spring-plate detent holds the lever in place with an indicating window (S, B, R), and a light spring holds the vibrator at rest when the carriage is folded.
