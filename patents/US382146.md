## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** The Becker pointer-and-arc index typewriter with rubber types needed a larger character capacity without a second index plate and centering arc.

**Solution:** A type disk carrying two sets of flexible rubber types is turned a part-revolution by a finger plate so the same index and centering arc serve capitals or small letters.

**How it works:** The pointer arm and centering arc swing on a pivot over a type disk whose edge carries two sets of rubber types. A stop on the disk bears against the arm so one set sits under the centering arc; the printing lever enters a hole in the arc to centre the chosen type. Pressing a hinged finger plate pulls a connecting rod that turns a gear wheel and a pinion under the disk, rotating the disk until the stop meets the other end of the arc and the second set is in position. A spring lifts the finger plate and returns the disk to the normal set when released.
