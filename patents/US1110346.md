## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typewriters make a tapping noise as type strikes the paper and as the mechanism works, which is disturbing; quiet designs usually cost light touch or visibility of the writing.

**Solution:** Enclose the key and driving mechanism in a closed housing with only the type cylinder and paper carrier outside, joined by a rod through the housing wall that a fast rotating striking member pushes only over a short final travel.

**How it works:** Keys pull a rod that sets the type cylinder (a laminated cylinder of plates, one per type) by pinion and shaft inside the housing; the shaft projects through the housing. A rotating striking member (disc or lever) driven by the key rides a roller up the lower arm of an unequal-armed pressure lever, entering tangentially to avoid shock. The lever pushes a cranked rod passing through the housing, which carries the selected type plate out of the cylinder periphery against the platen, so the type travels only a short distance and the rotating mass gives a strong push at the end of the stroke. When no key is pressed the upper edge of the cylinder lies below the writing line, and key depression raises it into position, keeping the writing visible. Variants use a swinging lever or a constantly rotating striking lever on an axially shifted shaft with a stop.
