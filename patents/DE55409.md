## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** A key-operated type-cylinder machine needs a simple way to set the cylinder to each letter, change case or row, print, and space the carriage, with few parts.

**Solution:** Each key presses a stepped lever that gives the type cylinder (on a vertical shaft) a different rotation, while a slide key lifts the cylinder to a second row or a hand lever lifts it to another field, and a spring hammer plus ratchet wheel prints and advances the carriage.

**How it works:** Key stems push on a beveled U-shaped lever so each key gives a different deflection; a pin on the lever's spindle drives a toothed segment that turns a pinion on the type-cylinder shaft to the angle for that key. The cylinder carries letters in fields of two rows, and the shaft slides vertically. A finger on the cylinder axis sweeps an arc of key-stem extensions and stops against the depressed key's extension, fixing the angle. A slide key (pressed with the letter key) tilts an angle lever that lifts the cylinder to its second row; a hand lever with notches and locking cone selects the field (small letters, capitals, figures). The same key stroke depresses a lever that releases a spring-loaded print hammer behind the paper, and a two-thumb escapement steps a ratchet wheel one tooth, driving the paper carriage by a pinion on a rack; the ratchet also resets the hammer. A trigger releases the thumbs so the carriage can be pulled back by hand. A cord and spring return the cylinder to rest.
