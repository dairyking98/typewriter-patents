**In the English translation of the patent’s own words:** This invention relates to a sideways-feed stop device for a Japanese-language typewriter in which: a swinging pawl is pivoted, so that it can rise and fall under a spring, on a feed pawl that slides back and forth and engages a rack fixed to the platen frame;

## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** While the platen feeds sideways at each stroke, the operator sometimes needs to stop the feed and print again at the same position (overstrike).

**Solution:** A spring-raised swinging pawl pivoted on the sliding feed pawl is brought into line with it by a knob on the operating lever, so both engage the same rack tooth and no feed occurs.

**How it works:** A feed pawl slides back and forth at each print stroke along a rack on the platen frame; a swinging pawl pivoted on it, spring-loaded, alternately takes the rack so the platen advances one step. An L-lever's horizontal end normally holds the swinging pawl at the feed distance. A sliding sleeve with a knob near the handle of the operating lever, when squeezed to the handle, moves a pin, swinging lever, links and bent lever to shift the L-levers. The swinging pawl is then raised upright into line with the feed pawl, so both slide within the same tooth and the platen does not advance.
