## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Typewriting for transfer-ink sheets later transferred to stone for lithographic printing needs very exact impressions and letter spacing, which ordinary machines with lost motion and springy parts do not give.

**Solution:** A large rigid type wheel slides bodily on straight ways to print, is set by a lever dropping onto adjustable pins that both locate the letter and set the paper feed for that letter's width, and has types of graduated length to even out the impression.

**How it works:** The operator turns a hand lever on the type wheel's axis until it is over the pin for the wanted character in a fixed ring, then lowers it so its V-groove centres on the pin and so locates the wheel exactly. A foot treadle drives a bell-crank that slides the ring-shaped carriage, with the wheel on it, straight along rigid ways to strike the paper held on a vertical cylinder. How far the lever can sink depends on pin height; this sets the stroke of a feed lever and pawl that turns a fine ratchet and so rotates the paper cylinder, giving wide letters a large feed and narrow ones a small one. Pins can be bent to correct any error in position or spacing. Types sit in radial grooves clamped by a ring and are ground to graduated lengths so large faces strike harder. Spring-held ink rollers roll on the wheel, which has a smooth ink table to spread the ink. A tubular paper-cylinder shaft with internal bar, rack and catches steps the cylinder up for each new line, and up to three wheels (Roman, italic, heading) may be used.
