## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Integral-tooth carriage racks must be replaced whole when one tooth wears, are noisy on return, drop slightly on the key stroke, and allow only fixed spacing.

**Solution:** Make the feed rack from loose, independently lifting teeth that a vertically and laterally moving feeding dog lifts one at a time past a fixed holding dog.

**How it works:** Loose teeth sit in paired slots in a rack bar on the carriage, each with a body, shoulders, lower lugs and a notch. A fixed holding dog stops the carriage by blocking one tooth body against the spring-drum pull. Pressing a character or space key pulls the feeding dog down clear of the lugs, and a spring moves it sideways against a stop; the carriage does not move yet. On key release the dog rises and lifts the next tooth so its notch clears the holding dog, and the carriage advances exactly one tooth before the next tooth strikes the holding dog. A switch changes the lateral stop so two teeth are lifted for double spacing. A release bar under the teeth lifts all of them for free running, and removing teeth gives built-in tab jumps. Spring fingers force down any sticking tooth, and a rod holds the teeth in.
