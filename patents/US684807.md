## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A single shifting type bar needs accurate sideways positioning from the keys, quick return to centre, and a way to use several faces of the bar for more characters.

**Solution:** One multi-faced type bar on a sliding cross-head is shifted sideways to the chosen position by the bevelled foot of the pressed key post, then the same key stroke swings the bar down onto the platen and spaces the carriage.

**How it works:** A single type bar, with flat faces carrying lower case, capitals and numerals, is pivoted on a standard riding in a cross-head that slides on two rods. Key posts stand on either side; the first part of a key's downward travel brings its bevelled foot onto a cross-bar of a centrally pivoted shifting frame, whose forked end pushes the standard along the rods, further when the key is nearer the pivot. Continued travel presses a hinged table with a bail onto a hook on the type bar's shaft, drawing the bar down onto the platen to print. The same bail pushes the space-lever cross-bar and so works the feed dogs, so printing and spacing are one action. Springs restore the frame and a detent seat holds it at centre. A turn-button, pinion and gear rotate the bar to bring another face over the platen, held by a flat disc against a spring. An inking roller sits under the bar while it is raised.
