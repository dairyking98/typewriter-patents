## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** The carriage feed of a type-ring machine should move exactly one tooth per stroke without shock.

**Solution:** A rocker-arm carries two pawls working on opposite toothed edges of a double rack bar, one pawl checking the carriage while the other advances.

**How it works:** The carriage rides on two rods and a front track and roller and hinges up on its rear rail. A double rack bar with teeth on its front and rear edges is attached to the carriage yokes and pulled by the driving mechanism. A rocker-arm, moved by a cross-rod under the key-levers, carries a spring pawl on the front rack and a pivoted pawl on the rear rack through a yielding spring. Normally the spring pawl holds the carriage between forks; on a stroke the rocker withdraws it and brings the rear pawl forward a tooth, then releases it to engage the next tooth, so the carriage steps one space. A spring returns the rocker.
