## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** In the earlier machine (DE50242) the whole frame swung down once the type wheel was set, so the key had to be pushed too far and needed a sudden extra force to release the frame and raise the platen; the inventor wants faster, more regular action.

**Solution:** Keep the frame fixed and let the key lever alone set the type wheel, lock it, step the paper roller and then raise the roller against the wheel in successive parts of the one downstroke.

**How it works:** The type wheel (a cylinder or row of discs with the characters in parallel circles) turns on a horizontal axis over the paper roller and slides along that axis by a hand lever with three snap positions, or by an end knob, to bring a circle of characters into the print position. A key lever hits a stepped plate of different depths and swings a sub-frame whose rack and gear turn the wheel to the angle for that key. A transverse lever then swings the locking lever so a cone enters a tooth of one of five small, staggered ratchet wheels on the wheel axle, locking the wheel in the printing position; the rack on the sub-frame is spring-held so it stays still as the key goes further down. The remainder of the stroke presses a cross frame that raises the paper roller carrier against the wheel. A separate switching frame moved by the key advances a toothed rack and the paper roller carrier one letter; a second rack and pawl lock the roller during printing, a word key gives wider spacing, and a variant raises the roller by sliding a tooth over a rack back. A porous ink roller on sprung arms inks the types, and the key levers have open bearings so any one can be pulled off its axis.
