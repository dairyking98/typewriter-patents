## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typewriter with a type-wheel needed keys that turn the wheel to the right letter, print and space in one stroke.

**Solution:** Banks of keys carry cam slots that shift a sliding frame by a key-specific amount; the frame rotates the type-wheel by rack and pinion, and continued key travel prints and feeds the paper.

**How it works:** Each key plate has an inclined cam slot whose lower end sits over a cross-wire of a laterally sliding frame; depressing the key moves that frame left or right by an amount set by the slot's slope. A second frame carrying a rack meshes with a pinion on the type-wheel arbor, so the wheel rotates partly to bring the character to the print point. Further depression makes an incline on the key feed the paper carriage and impress the type. A latch on the rack teeth of the paper-roll frame lifts to let a spring advance it one space; the space key has no slot but only the incline, so it feeds without printing. The wheel is restored after each stroke to a blank position.
