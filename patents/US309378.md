## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A simple, cheap hand-operated typewriter is wanted in which a single type-wheel is set by hand to the letter, printed by pressing it down, and the paper is spaced automatically.

**Solution:** A tilted type-wheel with two rows of type (capitals and small) on a beveled rim is turned by a knob to a scale, pressed down on a hinged plate against an impression bar, and the same press steps the paper carriage one notch with a pawl.

**How it works:** The type-wheel sits on an inclined pivot stud on a plate hinged to a case top, so one side of its beveled rim lies flat over a horizontal impression bar. The operator turns the wheel by a centre knob until the wanted letter agrees with one of two pointers (capitals or small letters) on a curved scale above it. Pressing the wheel down tilts the hinged plate and drives the type onto the paper over the impression bar; a tapered registering point enters a hole in the wheel to correct slight misalignment. Just before the impression a pawl on the plate drops into a notched guide bar and shifts the paper carriage one notch; a slide pressed by a finger lets the pawl move further for word spaces. At line end the carriage is pushed back by hand and a second pawl advances a paper clip for the next line. Fixed ink pads on each side of the print point ink the type as the wheel swings over them.
