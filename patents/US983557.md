## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** On Oliver typewriters with extra-long carriages for wide paper, the long guide-bars of the shift-frame cannot be made stiff enough to stay parallel and aligned throughout their length.

**Solution:** Brace the guide-bars together near their ends and tie the front bar by links to a rocking frame on the base-board that follows the shift movement.

**How it works:** The shift-frame carries front and rear transverse guide-bars on which the paper carriage rides, and the bars project well past the machine frame. Longitudinal braces join the ends of the two bars to hold them parallel. A rock-shaft runs parallel to the bars near the rear of the base-board and carries three upright rock-arms. Two arms are linked to the ends of the front guide-bar and the middle arm is linked to the shift-frame through a yoke. As the frame shifts forward and back, the rocking frame swings with it and holds the bar ends in line. The links are set low enough not to foul the carriage.
