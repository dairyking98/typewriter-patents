## Summary

**Kind:** type-element machine · **Subject (patent classes):** Typewriters or selective printing mechanisms characterised by the mounting, arrangement or disposition of the types or dies with types or dies mounted on carriers rotatable for selection the plane of the type or die face being parallel to the axis of rotation, e.g. with type on the periphery of cylindrical carriers

**In the patent’s own words:** My invention relates to that form of typewriting machine known as the Hammond, in which the printing is done by a hammer and type-carrying shuttle, the paper and ribbon being interposed between these parts to receive the impression.

**How it works (reading):** Make the Hammond universal by giving it a reversible carriage drive, a turret hammer with several faces, an exchangeable shuttle and rotating multi-face keys, built on a U-shaped frame that backs the anvil.

## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** The Hammond machine needed to write any language (left-to-right, right-to-left) and Braille without factory rework, and its hammer, anvil and shuttle lift needed a stronger, lighter-keyed construction.

**Solution:** Make the Hammond universal by giving it a reversible carriage drive, a turret hammer with several faces, an exchangeable shuttle and rotating multi-face keys, built on a U-shaped frame that backs the anvil.

**How it works:** Keys drive a curved type shuttle (three or more rows) around a ring anvil, as in the ordinary Hammond. For capitals or figures the key also lifts the slotted anvil post by a bell-crank, whose hooked end locks over a roller to stop the shuttle at the right height; a hand stop can halt it at an intermediate row. A sliding hammer, driven through a power-multiplying bell-crank by a spring released by the escapement, presses paper and ribbon against the type; the hammer is a rotatable turret with rubber-covered faces and bare faces for manifold, mimeograph and Braille pits. The carriage spring pulls the carriage by a cord that can be re-clipped to the other end, and a shifting change-gear beside the escapement gear reverses the stepping direction. Hammer-spring tension is geared to the carriage-spring winding, and a rod blocks the hammer when the space key is struck.
