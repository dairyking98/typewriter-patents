## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** Ribbon printing on lever typewriters gives smudged, dull impressions, needs many support and feed parts, smears paper, soils hands and wears out quickly.

**Solution:** Replace the ribbon with a central ink well topped by cone-shaped fabric pads that the rising and falling typebars brush to ink their faces.

**How it works:** A standard at the machine's base holds an ink well whose hub sits in a socket. Pads of silk or jaconet, clamped under a tapering holder and cover, project as an inverted truncated cone with their axis at the printing point. Ink similar to ribbon ink fills the reservoir and wicks to the pads. As each typebar swings up toward the paper it passes across the pads and picks up ink, and as it descends the pads touch it again.

**Features:** [INK:pad](../features/ink-pad.md), [ELEM:typebar](../features/elem-typebar.md), [FRM:few-parts](../features/frm-few-parts.md), [FRM:cleaning-access](../features/frm-cleaning-access.md)

**Parts:** ink well; ink pad; holder and cover; standard; hub; typebar; type ring; platen

**Improves on:**

- ribbon inking on lever machines: inks the type directly so impressions are sharp and no ribbon mechanism is needed
