## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** Typewriter escapements are either slow (carriage moves after the stroke), or the carriage is moving at impact so rebounds print ghosts, or feed half-spaces on down and up strokes.

**Solution:** An escapement that lets the carriage off near the end of the key downstroke, just before or at impact, and feeds it forward during key return and the early next stroke, with a cam or retraction to control feed.

**How it works:** The rack is normally engaged with the spacing dog; on key depression a rocker frame disengages the spacing dog and engages the detaining dog. The rack hangs on friction pivots and an adjustable point on the rocker strikes it to control feeding. The let-off timing is adjusted so the carriage is hardly moving at the instant of printing but has time to space accurately. Several variants apply to Remington and Caligraph escapements and one uses the dog spring to feed the carriage without a mainspring.

**Features:**

- **Selection:** [Full keyboard, a key per character](../features/sel-keyboard-full.md)
- **Type element:** [Typebars](../features/elem-typebar.md)
- **Spacing and escapement:** [Pawl-and-rack escapement](../features/esc-pawl-rack.md), [Mainspring-drawn carriage](../features/esc-spring-drum.md)

**Parts:** rack; spacing dog; detaining dog; rocker frame; adjustable point; friction pivot; key lever; typebar; carriage mainspring

**Improves on:**

- Remington escapement: let-off timed near impact to avoid ghost impressions
- Caligraph escapement: invention adapted to it
