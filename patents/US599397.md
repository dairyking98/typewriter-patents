## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Cheap, simple index typewriters were too slow because one hand with one index had to travel over the whole keyboard; small compact index letters were also hard to read.

**Solution:** Two independently pivoted rubber-type segments, each with a rigid finger-ring handle swung over a keyboard of imitation finger keys, so both hands select and one common impression device prints.

**How it works:** Two arc-shaped rubber-type carriers are stacked on a vertical post and each swings independently by a long handle ending in a finger ring that slides in and out of a tubular arm. The operator puts a finger in the ring, moves it over a keyboard of round keys in concentric arcs, which turns the carrier so the matching type sits at the print point. Pressing down on the key first depresses the upper carrier through a toggle (the shift) and then lowers the whole keyboard or universal bar, which swings a printing lever. That lever carries a locking pin that drops into one of a row of holes in the selected carrier (the other carrier is passed by a slot) and an impression device that presses the type back against the paper on a flat-bar platen. The same keyboard motion works a bell-crank and push pawl that feeds the paper carriage one space, and a space key sits on the same bell-crank. Lower or capital case is chosen by which half of the keyboard the finger is on, since the two carriers carry complementary lower and capital sets, so no shift key is needed.
