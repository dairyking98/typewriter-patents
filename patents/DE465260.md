## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** On low typewriters with carriage shifting, the parallel links carrying the carriage have close, rearward pivots giving uncertain guidance; stroke screws are hard to reach; shift forces act at different levels and warp the machine.

**Solution:** Both link pairs carrying the carriage swing the same direction on widely spaced pivots, the front link is driven directly by the shift key, and the stroke-limit screws sit on that driven link where they are accessible.

**How it works:** A shift key moves a lower link which turns a multi-armed bell crank mounted far forward; its pivot is level with the pivot of the rear carriage link. The two links, joined by cheeks carrying the carriage and platen, swing equally about the type-strike direction, so the carriage path is perpendicular to the strike. Two accessible screws, one in the normal and one in the shifted position, limit the stroke against the frame and a bracket on the bell crank. A second shift key (downshift) drives a two-armed lever through guide links arranged so its rod lies along the carriage link's direction, so downshifting does not disturb the carriage shift.
