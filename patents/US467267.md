## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Keyless typewriters were costly to build and slow to learn; the inventor wants a cheaper, simpler machine that also helps the operator gain speed.

**Solution:** A carriage carries a free-floating type segment tied to an index plate and index so that placing the index on a recessed sign and pressing down aligns the type, prints, and advances the carriage one tooth.

**How it works:** A carriage runs on toothed guide-bars over a paper roller. On it, an arm pivoted on a transverse spindle carries an index-plate of recessed signs and a rigid spindle on which a type segment, with type on its lower half circumference, can swing radially and slide laterally. A cross-bar with an index is joined to the segment, so setting the index on a sign puts the matching type over an inked tape, the recess giving a slight locking of the index. Pressing the index down brings the type onto the paper, and a pawl on the arm advances the carriage by one tooth of the guide-bar, so printing also spaces. A spring lifts the arm on release; a pawl on the arm advances one of two ribbon rollers on each stroke, and a bell gong sounds near the end of the line. A roller can be pushed down to release the paper and has ratchets of different pitch for line spacing.
