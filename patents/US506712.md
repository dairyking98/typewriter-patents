## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Inventor wants a cheap portable typewriter within everyone's reach; his earlier 1892 machine (US473945) lacked ribbon feed driven by the type-bar and a positive paper feed in both directions.

**Solution:** A hand-held straight type-bar with a letter on each edge is positioned against a pointer on a counterbalanced tilting arm over a stationary paper support, and pressing it prints, spaces the carriage and advances the ribbon in one stroke.

**How it works:** The paper lies on a fixed wooden support with a rubber strip, between two longitudinal rollers that a thumb knob turns by pinions to feed it either way. The operator holds the loose straight type-bar, with type on both edges, and slides it along the guide of a counterbalanced tilting arm until the desired letter is opposite the pointer. Pressing the bar prints the character and at the same time tips the arm and a weighted ribbon lever. The ribbon lever's pawl turns a ratchet on the ribbon shaft one character width, with a detent and a spring brake controlling the other spool. When the bar is lifted, the weight returns the arm, which through a rock shaft and pawl advances the carriage one tooth along a notched guide bar.
