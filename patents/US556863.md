## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** The inventor wants a typewriter simple enough to sell at a low price, with a way to see the last printed line.

**Solution:** A flat printing plate of three rows of type is slid sideways by bell-crank levers driven from the key levers until the depressed key lever hits a central stop on the plate, then a spring-released hammer prints and the carriage steps by one ratchet pitch, all from one key stroke.

**How it works:** Key levers each carry a rigid depending arm that rocks one of two facing bell-cranks, which push pins on a sliding bed holding the printing plate left or right according to which side of centre the key is on. The inner end of the depressed key lever meets a stop pin on the plate and arrests it with the chosen character at the print point. Two extra keys lift the plate vertically to bring the capitals or figures row to the print line. The same key stroke trips a latch: a hammer spring is released and the hammer drives the paper and ribbon against the type, while a two-dog ratchet lets the spring-driven carriage advance one tooth. A ribbon is wound between two spools, each driven by friction disks from the carriage; a rocking bar swaps which spool is driven. The whole bed and carriage lift so the last line is visible.

**Parts:** key lever; printing plate; sliding bed; bell-crank; stop pin; elbow lever; hammer; hammer spring; latch; two-dog ratchet; ratchet bar; carriage spring; cable; paper roll; milled head; spring pawl; paper guard; ribbon; ribbon spool; rocking bar; friction disk; guard plate; space key; bed plate

**Numbers:** 9 sheets of drawings; three rows of type on the plate: lower case, capitals, figures; two concentric aperture rings give full or half line space

**Improves on:**

- Prior shiftable type-bar or printing plate machines: one pivoted lever between key lever and plate, and a central stop engaged directly by the key lever
- Segmental curved-plate rocking designs: flat plate slid in a straight way with a stop for each key
