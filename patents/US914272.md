## Reading (full text, OCR poor)

**Problem:** Type-roller machines without a keyboard needed large hand movements to position the type and print; the inventor wants adjustment and printing from only a very slight movement of the hand.

**Solution:** A knob on an oscillating, pantograph-magnified linkage moves the type roller by a small amount matched to the type spacing while a pointer moves magnified over a large index plate, and pressing the knob down prints.

**How it works:** A type roller whose types are laid out like a draughts board sits on a spindle carried in a rectangular frame that can slide horizontally and rock on pivots. A handle or knob on a toothed rod turns the roller through a gear wheel (axial toothed rod to roller rotation) and slides the roller shaft, so the knob moves the type in two directions. A pointer on the toothed rod, magnified by a pantograph-like device, moves over an index plate of 84 letters so that the small hand movement is shown as a larger, easy-to-read movement. Knife edges on an oscillating arm drop into a comb and a star wheel on the roller spindle to finish and exactly correct the position. Pressing the knob down swings the roller to the paper, inking it and printing, and a spring lifts the knob again. The OCR is badly broken in the last part of the description.
