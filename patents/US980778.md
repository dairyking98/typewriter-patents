## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Braille is normally punched by stylus through a folding ruler from right to left and read from the reverse side, which is awkward.

**Solution:** A six-key embossing typewriter writes Braille characters directly in the usual left-to-right reading direction.

**How it works:** Paper passes between two friction rollers (with high-friction bands) turned by a knob, held by a leaf spring. Six levers in pairs pivot on supports under a case; each lever drives a pin whose tip embosses one point of the six-dot cell. Levers extend to front flanges forming keys in two transverse rows of three. A sliding bar acts as the carriage.
