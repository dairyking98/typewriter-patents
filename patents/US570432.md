## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Typebars of a Rapid-type machine strike slightly differently each time, giving poor alignment, and the line-space detent and pawl interfered with free platen turning.

**Solution:** Add a funnel-shaped type guide that the typebar carries forward to the platen so every type strikes the same spot, plus a push pawl that also disengages its detent.

**How it works:** Typebars converge on the platen and slide through a perforated rear plate and a slotted front plate that allow lateral play but no vertical play. A funnel guide with inclined walls and a small apex opening sits on swivelling arms, weighted to rest away from the platen. When a bar is thrown forward its shoulder catches the opening edge and carries the guide to the platen, so the type is centred on a fixed point. An ink roller sits over the opening and is pushed aside by the type, or a ribbon is held across the opening by clips. Eccentric stops adjust the guide's travel. Separately, the line-space push pawl, when tipped up, also lifts the detent lever by a pin so the platen turns freely.
