## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Typebar escapements feed the carriage after printing; the inventor wants an escapement that can feed before the print (for fast operators), where the key momentum alone finishes the impression, and that can have silent keys for accents.

**Solution:** A two-dog escapement worked by a feed-bar with two spaced operating members, driven from the universal bar through a coupling that can be set to feed on key depression or on key return, with a movable link letting a silent key deflect the feed-bar so it does not escape.

**How it works:** Depressing a key moves the typebar and the universal bar; the bar is stopped by a stop so that the typebar reaches the paper by momentum alone. The universal bar, through an arm, link and a shiftable coupling member, drives a vertical rod and feed-bar, either on the rearward stroke (feed before print) or the return stroke (feed after, via a spring). The feed-bar's two operating members, slightly closer than the two spring-pressed feed dogs, trip the dogs alternately: one dog is lifted from the carriage rack and slides a letter-space under its spring while the other dog is held as a stop. Each stroke of the feed-bar thus moves the carriage one space under the mainspring, with a brake cushion retarding it. A silent key connects through a rock-shaft and hooked lever to swing the feed-bar sideways so it misses the dogs, and its crank arm can be shifted along the shaft to fit any key. The universal bar also lifts the ribbon guide in a forward-and-up path.
