## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Make a small lap-sized typewriter with few parts in which a motor, not the operator's key depression, selects and brings the character to the print point.

**Solution:** A continuously rotating pinned shaft driven by extraneous power, with a fan governor, and keys that stop the rotation at the chosen character on a type wheel with two sets of characters.

**How it works:** A type wheel carries two character sets (upper and lower case) on its periphery. A pivoted frame holds a shaft with pins equal to the number of characters in one set, and the shaft is continuously rotated by a motor, speed-regulated by a fan governor whose blades work against air. A key on a bell-crank shifts the shaft lengthwise to bring either character set over the printing point. A latch or stop-key reserves the motive power at the operator's will. Depressing the frame operates the escapement dogs on the rack bar, as does the space bar; an inking roller is on a pivoted arm.
