## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A key-driven rotating body such as a type wheel must be stopped positively and accurately at a set point for each key, without relying on the operator's stroke.

**Solution:** Two opposed bails with inclined edges, linked together to move in opposite directions, are pushed by the key levers at different heights; one bail turns the shaft while the other's incline meets the same lever and locks it at the exact end point.

**How it works:** A row of key levers sits between two bails, each with an inclined upper edge sloping in opposite directions. Each bail is on a shaft joined by a rack and link to a pinion on the driven shaft (the type wheel shaft), so the two bails always move together but in opposite directions. Depressing a lever pushes one bail down by an amount that depends on where it meets the incline, rotating the shaft. As the shaft reaches its limit the other bail's incline rises to meet the same lever and blocks further travel, stopping the shaft exactly. Springs return the levers; cord and drum linkages are given as alternatives.

- **Type element:** [Type wheel (types on the rim)](../features/elem-wheel-rim.md)

**Parts:** key lever; bail; inclined edge; shaft; rack; pinion; return spring; stop rod; type wheel
