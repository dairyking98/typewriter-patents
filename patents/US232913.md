## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Earlier typewriters needed separate motions to choose a type and to print, and had awkward ribbon, paper-clamp and carrier-feed arrangements; the writing also had to stay visible.

**Solution:** Each key is a lever that, in one continuous movement, first engages the type segment to set it to the right character and then swings the segment with its support against the paper carrier, and the same stroke also releases the carrier feed.

**How it works:** An oscillating type segment with types on its periphery hangs on a swinging support, and one lever per type shares the support's axis. Each lever has a notch that takes up one of two horizontal bars on the segment, so pressing a key first rocks the segment to the right position and then swings segment and support onto the vertical paper carrier. Springs return everything on release. Toes on the support tilt a rack frame, which releases a movable dog and lets a fixed dog check the carrier, and on release the rack lets the spring-driven carrier advance one tooth, giving the letter space. The same tilting of the rack frame trips pawls that wind the ribbon one step onto one of two reels, and a slide reverses which reel winds. The ribbon rests away from the paper and is moved to the paper by a spring wire frame tied to the swinging support, keeping the writing visible, and a griper and spring clamp handle paper line adjustment.
