## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** The inventor wants a simple, cheap typewriter any person can use, able to take several type sizes and fonts and to space words out at line ends.

**Solution:** A rocking and sliding frame of spring-seated type pins is moved by hand to put the chosen type under a fixed impression key, and the upward swing of the frame steps the paper carriage by a pawl on a rack while the downward swing prints.

**How it works:** A type-carrying frame holds one or more interchangeable type holders (pins in slotted bars or perforated plates, each pin sprung up), pivoted at the rear on a guide rod so the front can be raised and lowered and slid sideways by knobs. An index plate with lettered holes on the frame shows which letter is aligned: a centring pin on the fixed impression key enters the hole, and adjustable pins on the key seat in cavities in the type pin heads. Raising the front of the frame presses a bar on the frame against a stud on a pivoted lever that carries an adjustable pawl, which pushes the paper carriage along a rack bar by one or more teeth. Lowering the frame presses the type pin against the pivoted impression key to print. Rack bars with coarse, medium and fine teeth, or the pawl lever radius, set the step for large or small type. Inking is by rollers on the paper carriage fed from end inking tables and evened by distributing rolls, or by ribbon reels instead. Reversible holders on trunnions swap upper and lower case or sizes.
