## Summary

**Kind:** typebar machine · **Subject (patent classes):** Typewriters or selective printing mechanisms characterised by the mounting, arrangement or disposition of the types or dies with types or dies carried on sliding bars or rods on side surfaces thereof, e.g. fixed thereto the types or dies being movable relative to the bars or rods

**In the patent’s own words:** My invention relates to that class of writing-machines in which the platen is movable at right angles to its axis at will, so that the printing-point thereon will be caused to occupy two or more positions to receive the impact of two or more characters on the type-bar.

**How it works (reading):** Typebars carry three characters in line along the bar and the platen is moved obliquely at will, by two shift keys of different travel, to bring the first, second or third character to the printing point.

## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A visible-writing typebar machine with three characters per bar needs a case-shift that keeps writing in view and keeps the ribbon out of the way until the moment of impact.

**Solution:** Typebars carry three characters in line along the bar and the platen is moved obliquely at will, by two shift keys of different travel, to bring the first, second or third character to the printing point.

**How it works:** Typebars pivot in a curved lower bar and rest on a backstop, each linked to a key lever; the platen frame hangs on parallel links in an obliquely tilted carriage. Two shift keys, depressing different distances, rock a shaft whose cross-bar pushes the platen frame back on its links to one of two further positions so the middle or outer character meets the platen. A ribbon is normally retracted; a lever worked by the universal bar lifts a bifurcated carrier over the printing point just before the strike, with a variable leverage so the lift adapts to the platen position. Ribbon bobbins are wound by pawls on the universal bar, with a lever and cams selecting which bobbin is driven. Carriage feed is by a pawl and rack, the rack being swung out of engagement by a thumb lever for free travel, and line spacing is by a pull-pawl on a ratchet limited to one or two teeth.
