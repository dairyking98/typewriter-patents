## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typewriters needed simpler, more perfect operation, with a positive carriage drive and a view of the printed matter without lifting the carriage.

**Solution:** A vertically journaled type-wheel is turned and raised by keys through a rocking frame of two oppositely inclined bars and is struck by an impression plunger.

**How it works:** Key levers rise against one of two crossed inclined bars on a rocking positioning frame, which rotates until both bars lie horizontal against the lever. A plate on the frame pulls flexible connections that turn a sleeve and the splined vertical shaft carrying the type-wheel, so a given key rotates it by a fixed amount and locks it. The same key action raises the wheel to a row of type so different type forms line up with the impression plunger. The carriage is moved positively and held still during impression, and the paper is fed by rollers to and from the print point so the print can be inspected.
