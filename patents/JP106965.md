## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** On a Japanese-script typewriter with a type magazine, the paper cylinder must be fed in the vertical (column) direction, stepped sideways after each column and returned after the last, which is awkward to operate and demanding on the operator when printing into forms.

**Solution:** Use the unwinding force of one wound spring to push cams that move the cylinder, with the pawls of the feed device holding the cams back and a dead-centre linkage letting them snap through for column change and cylinder return.

**How it works:** An operating lever winds a spiral spring in a barrel on a spindle through a toothed segment, gear, operating pawl and ratchet. The spring tries to turn an elliptical cam linked eccentrically to an arm on the cylinder shaft, but the shaft is held until the key-lever or vertical-feed lever frees the feed pawl; when freed, the cam turns and feeds the cylinder one vertical step. After a set number of steps the link pivot passes dead centre, the cam snaps back, and its high part pulls a connecting rod that moves the sideways-feed bar to make a sideways feed. A second cam on the spring's inner shaft is turned a little at each sideways feed; when the last sideways feed is made a four-link linkage reaches dead centre, the spring turns the cam quickly and the cylinder runs back to its start.
