## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** The inventor's earlier twirl-and-press type-wheel machines had one wheel with one style of letter, usually capitals only; two wheels (upper and lower case) need a quick way to switch, a way to stop the idle wheel printing, and a feed step matched to each wheel's letter widths.

**Solution:** Two type-wheels are fixed to one twirling handle on a depressible lever and slid in or out to choose case, with a slotted guard plate stopping the idle wheel and a feed pawl whose tail enters rim recesses of varying depth to set the carriage step.

**How it works:** The operator twirls a handle on the end of a pivoted lever to turn a pair of coaxial type-wheels (lower case outer, capitals inner) driven through a toothed locking-wheel and a crank pin, and watches an index finger on a dial geared to the wheel. Pressing the handle swings the lever down so the wheel at the print plane strikes the paper on a narrow stationary anvil (horn), and the locking-wheel teeth engage a fixed stop tooth to hold the wheel from turning at the instant of printing. Sliding the handle in or out on a grooved sleeve, held by a spring catch in two notches, puts one wheel or the other at the print plane; a thin slotted guard plate with feet over the paper lets only the active wheel through the slot while the idle wheel lands harmlessly on the plate. A reciprocating feed pawl on an elbow lever is cocked on the downstroke and, on the return, steps the rack-carriage; its tail drops into a recess between types whose depth equals the letter width, lifting the pawl out of the rack for longer or shorter parts of the stroke, so the step is proportional to the letter. A pendulous arm swings an inked disc against the rim and away from the print point during the stroke, and a fully depressed knob lifts the pawl clear to free the carriage.
