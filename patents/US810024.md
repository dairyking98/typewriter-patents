## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A three-row type wheel (lower case, upper case, figures) that is lifted and rotated by a typebar action must be brought into exact vertical and horizontal alignment at the printing point.

**Solution:** Cut a row of recesses in the type wheel beside each row of type and let fixed studs on the paper-guide frame enter the recess just before impact, centering the type face.

**How it works:** The vulcanite type wheel rides on a post, moving vertically for case shift and rotating to the chosen character, under control of the typebar action. Three rows of recesses in the wheel correspond exactly in circumferential position to the three rows of type faces. Stationary studs on the paper-guide frame, slightly smaller than the recesses, sit above and below the printing position at spacings matching the row lift. As the wheel approaches the platen the relevant stud enters the recess in front of it and forces the type face to its exact position both vertically and around the wheel. A lower extra stud shares wear for the much-used lower-case row.
