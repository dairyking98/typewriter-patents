## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Typing columns of figures needs the operator to note the decimal-point position and count spaces to each line start, which is slow and error-prone.

**Solution:** A slip-on auxiliary scale clipped over the machine's carriage scale, graduated by denomination (cents, units, tens, thousands) so the pointer shows where each line's first figure belongs.

**How it works:** A thin elastic metal plate is clipped over the fixed scale bar, with a cutout so the ordinary graduations stay visible. Its marks have a blank space for the decimal point, two marks for tenths and hundredths on one side, and units, tens, hundreds, then thousands and beyond on the other. The plate is slid and snapped by a catch into the ordinary scale's notches until the decimal space sits at the chosen column. Each line is started by moving the carriage until the pointer meets the mark for its first figure. The plate has duplicate graduations at both ends so it can be reversed for scales that read in either direction.
