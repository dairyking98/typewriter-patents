## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** On an electric Japanese typewriter the typist needs a light, simple way to trigger printing without letting go of the type tray handle.

**Solution:** An electromagnet releases the clutch control piece, switched by a push button on the type tray handle.

**How it works:** A motor shaft carries a fixed clutch; a sprung sliding clutch is held off by a pivoted control piece and re-latched after each turn by a pocket cam. A magnet on the bed has an armature that pulls a rod joined to the control piece, freeing the clutch so its cam swings a starting lever and prints. The magnet circuit includes a push-button switch on the handle that slides the type tray. The typist selects the character with the handle and presses the button with a finger of the same hand.

## Description (English translation of the Japanese text, opening)

> Nature and object of the invention. An electric typewriter characterised in that the movement of the control piece that governs engagement and disengagement between the driving shaft and the printing mechanism is ruled by a magnet, and the magnet is controlled by a push-button electric switch on the handle that moves the type tray. The object is an electric typewriter, light and simple to work, in which a magnet at a fixed position is energised exactly, and printing done reliably, by pressing the button with the fingers already holding the handle that moves the type tray. Brief description of the drawings. The drawings show one embodiment. Fig. 1: side view, partly in section. Fig. 2: view of the main parts, to show how they work. Detailed description. For light, simple working and quick, accurate printing, this electric typewriter has the control piece that engages and disengages a motor-driven shaft and the starting part of the printing mechanism worked by a magnet, and the magnet controlled by a push-button switch on the handle of the type tray, which the typist holds all the time while printing.
