## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** On a tray-type Japanese typewriter whose moving body carries the printing and paper-cylinder mechanisms, the sideways character feed of the cylinder recoils the moving body, so an anti-recoil rack was needed and the line could go crooked.

**Solution:** Make the cylinder's sideways feed happen during the printing key's down-stroke while the type lifter is dug into the tray, timed by printing-bar acceleration and cylinder inertia so the feed follows the strike, and keep a fixed pawl on the feed gear during character search.

**How it works:** Pressing the printing key pushes the type lifter tip into the tray, then drives the printing bar to grip and strike the type. The same motion raises a relay lever, change-over lever and bearing lever, which rock a plate carrying a fixed pawl and a spring-loaded movable pawl on the feed gear. The movable pawl engages and the fixed pawl releases, so a spring pulling the cylinder turns the feed gear through a worm spindle and worm wheel, and the cylinder steps sideways. Because the lifter is in the tray, the recoil is taken by the tray, and the cylinder's inertia delays its start until after the strike. On key return the fixed pawl re-engages the gear and holds the cylinder steady while the body moves to search for the next character.

This invention relates to a typewriter in which individual types are laid out lengthwise and crosswise on a flat type tray, and a moving body carrying the printing and paper-cylinder mechanisms is brought to any part of the tray to grip and print the wanted type.
