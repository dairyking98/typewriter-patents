## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A Japanese-script typewriter that writes vertical columns needs the platen to turn the line-feed way either from top to bottom or from bottom to top (for example to type account figures from below), and to be turned back freely at the end of a sheet.

**Solution:** Both ends of one coil spring are tied to two independent movers; a pin on a platen-driven gear is set beforehand to one side or the other, so the spring's return force turns the platen a fixed step per impression in either direction.

**How it works:** A coil spring sits in two nested turnable boxes, its outer end fixed to one box and its inner end to the other; each box carries a mover (8, 9), and a pin (10') on gear (12) lies between the movers. Before printing the operator turns the platen by hand with shaft (18) pressed in so pinion (24) meshes both gears (11, 12), winding the spring through the pin. When the print lever or frame is pressed, a wedge pushes shaft (18) forward so the pinion locks gear (12) and the platen stays still while the printing cylinder strikes. On return the pinion drops back to gear (11) only, and the spring turns gear (12) and the platen by a fixed amount through a linking tension spring (34), a slot (28) and a locating bar (26) with teeth that fix the step. Winding the pin to the other mover reverses the direction; a second shaft (19) with a fork lever frees both gears at once so the platen can be spun to load or remove paper, and a pawl (16) on a ratchet (14) marks the line start. The same spring and mover arrangement can instead move the platen sideways.

> Specification. Typewriter platen-turning device. Nature and object of the invention.
