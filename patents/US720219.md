**In the patent’s own words:** This invention relates to improvements in type-writer carriages, and has especial referra ence to the carriages of machines with individual type-bars, each carrying a plurality of types, although certain features of the invention are applicable to other forms of typewriting machines.

## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A typebar machine carrying three types per bar needs a platen carriage that can be moved to three heights without upsetting the rack escapement or the line alignment.

**Solution:** The platen carriage rides on a rod that two shift keys raise or lower through matched eccentric cam slots from a central rest position, while wide knife-edge escapement detents keep engaging the rack at every height.

**How it works:** Each typebar carries a block of three types stacked vertically, and the platen is held at one of three heights to bring the chosen type to the print point. A rock-shaft with an arm at each end carries cam slots, each with a central rest, that raise or lower the carriage guide rod; both ends move alike so the platen stays parallel. A spring helps the rod rise and gravity returns it. The rack bar at the back of the carriage bears on a tall roller so it can slide up and down, and two tall knife-edge detent plates on a pivot post, worked by the universal bar, engage the rack in turn to release one tooth per stroke under the mainspring. A key-operated lever pushes the second plate away to free the carriage, and a ribbon frame vibrates with the universal bar while fixed notched guide plates align the type blocks at the print point.
