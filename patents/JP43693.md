## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Character spacing needs to be changeable (for example halved when Western text is mixed into Japanese), but the feed stroke of the pawl lever is fixed.

**Solution:** Pivot the feed pawl loosely on the pawl lever and add a knob-turned springy stop piece that either locks the pawl rigid or leaves it free to tilt, so it engages later on the return stroke and feeds fewer teeth.

**How it works:** Print or space levers swing a feed frame whose pawl lever draws back and turns a ratchet wheel on the paper cylinder, giving one character space. The pawl is a separate piece pivoted at the pawl lever tip with a rearward projection. A springy stop piece on a pin has one end recessed to fit a ridge on that projection and the other end flat. Turned one way it clamps the pawl rigid, so it meshes at once and feeds two teeth; turned 180 degrees it leaves the pawl free so it tilts over a tooth, catches only the next tooth, and feeds one tooth, halving the spacing. The flat end under the projection also tilts the pawl tip so it releases earlier, letting the cylinder's inertia finish the small step accurately.
