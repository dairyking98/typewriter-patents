## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Earlier type-wheel machines moved the type wheel to the paper; the inventor wants a stationary wheel with the paper carried to the type, and a carriage that can be run back while its drive belt keeps moving one way.

**Solution:** A pointer on a vertical shaft is pressed into a countersink beside the wanted character on a fixed disc, which also turns a type wheel below so the matching type faces the paper, and the same press brings a presser bar carrying the paper against the type.

**How it works:** A fixed disc has the characters in a circle, each with a conical countersink. A shaft through the disc carries a crank with a spring-armed conical point and, below the disc, a type wheel that turns with it, so the type for the chosen character is on the side facing the paper. Pressing the shaft down seats the point in the countersink, tilts a lever and a rock-shaft, and a presser bar swings the paper against the inked type. An endless belt driven by a ratchet on the lever motion carries the paper carriage forward by friction when a jam piece nips the belt against a cross bar; lifting pins out of rack wheel teeth releases the carriage to run back, and turning the paper roll advances the pins to set the line space. Ink comes from a roller that the type pass over.
