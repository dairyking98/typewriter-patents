## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** Typewriter spacing is uniform, so narrow letters like i look spaced out against w and m; the inventor wants spacing to follow letter width and lines dead straight.

**Solution:** Each letter lever's head has a notch flattened to a different depth, so it depresses a spacing pawl by a letter-specific amount before printing.

**How it works:** A frame D carrying all letter levers side by side on one bolt slides sideways on rollers; the operator moves it by the chosen lever's button until its head is over a vertical slot in a rail. Pressing the lever down, its head first pushes a spring-loaded slide with a pawl, which advances the rack on the paper frame M by an amount set by the notch depth (flat 1 wide, 2 mean, 3 narrow letters). Only then does the type, a font-metal letter clamped in a slot in the lever, reach the paper. An inked copying strip runs between type and paper. The frame can be tipped open to read the writing, and a screw shifts the paper frame for line spacing.
