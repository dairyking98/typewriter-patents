## Summary

**Kind:** type-element machine · **Subject (patent classes):** Typewriters or selective printing mechanisms characterised by the mounting, arrangement or disposition of the types or dies with types or dies mounted on carriers rotatable for selection the plane of the type or die face being parallel to the axis of rotation, e.g. with type on the periphery of cylindrical carriers

**In the patent’s own words:** This invention relates to improvements in that kind of type-Writer in which a sector of a cylinder carrying type is turned by the striking of a key to the position in which the type corresponding with the key is in the path of the hammer.

**How it works (reading):** A cylinder sector with four symmetric type rows and a blank middle is rotated to the right angle by the key and slid sideways to the right row, while a springy lost-motion link makes the hammer fall only after the sector is in place.

## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** In keyboard machines where a cylinder type sector is turned to the struck key's character, the mechanism that brings the sector to position and locks it needed to be simpler, symmetrical and easier to control.

**Solution:** A cylinder sector with four symmetric type rows and a blank middle is rotated to the right angle by the key and slid sideways to the right row, while a springy lost-motion link makes the hammer fall only after the sector is in place.

**How it works:** The type sector is keyed to a ball-bearing shaft and carries four parallel rows of type, with no type in its middle, so at rest it sits clear of the hammer. Each key lever has two projections: the first presses a rod on the shaft's arms and turns the sector until the character lines up with the hammer, then the second meets a second rod and stops it there, with the two projection lengths differing per key. Keys on the left half shift the sector sideways to the left rows and right-half keys to the right rows via a four-armed lever on a vertical axis, with the outer rows reached by also holding a shift key that removes a stop. Further key travel, once the sector is arrested, compresses springs and releases an escapement anchor on a crown wheel that is driven by the carriage spring through a rack and pinion; the roller of a hammer lever riding over a crown-wheel tooth raises and drops the hammer against the paper and ribbon while the carriage advances half a space, and the second half space comes when the key is released. A space key makes the same escapement action without moving the sector, and the hammer is held up. On release a spring returns the sector.
