## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** Typewriters give every character equal space, so narrow and wide letters look awkward and special condensed faces are needed.

**Solution:** Space-stops of different lengths on each key lever set how far a graduated pawl-and-ratchet drive advances the work after each character.

**How it works:** Each key lever carries a space-stop of length proportioned to its character's width. Pressing the key brings the stop down onto a pressure bar until the lever hits a stop bar, swinging a frame and pawl levers carrying several pawls of different lengths. On release, one pawl engages the ratchet wheel and turns it by an amount set by the stop length. Gears and a pinion turn a screw shaft, whose nut moves the bed carrying the material. Separate sets of stops serve upper and lower case, with the pressure bar moved under either set.

**Features:**

- **Selection:** [Full keyboard, a key per character](../features/sel-keyboard-full.md)
- **Shift and cases:** [Shift by moving the platen](../features/shift-platen.md)
- **Spacing and escapement:** [Proportional spacing](../features/esc-variable-spacing.md), [Pawl-and-rack escapement](../features/esc-pawl-rack.md)

**Parts:** key lever; space-stop; stop bar; pressure bar; swinging frame; pawl lever; pawl; ratchet wheel; gear wheel; pinion; screw shaft; nut; bed
