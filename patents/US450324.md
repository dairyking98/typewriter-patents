## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Key-levers of different banks need different travel and force to raise the typebars.

**Solution:** A compound key-lever of two equal-length levers linked at their short arms gives every key the same depression and force.

**How it works:** Two levers are fulcrumed on a common standard fixed to a cross-piece near the machine bottom. Their short arms are joined by a pin in an elongated hole so lever 2 vibrates lever 1. A V-shaped wire spring seated in the standard hooks under both long arms and returns them. The stem key attaches at the end of lever 2 by a stirrup and riveted pin through a bushing, and a connecting rod joins the lever to the typebar.

**Features:**

- **Selection:** [Full keyboard, a key per character](../features/sel-keyboard-full.md)
- **Typebars:** [Typebars](../features/elem-typebar.md)
- **Frame and body:** [Few parts, low cost](../features/frm-few-parts.md)

**Parts:** compound lever; standard; cross-piece; wire spring; stem key; stirrup; connecting rod; typebar

**Improves on:**

- second and third order straight-line key-levers: all keys now share the same stroke and force
