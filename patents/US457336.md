## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** The inventors want a typewriter that occupies small space, is easily operated and is cheap.

**Solution:** A small index machine in which a circular four-section type-plate sits on an index plate that creeps along the paper one step at a time, and the same finger-piece stroke that steps the plate also presses the type down on the paper.

**How it works:** A sheet-metal index plate with an arc of character markings carries a circular type-plate pivoted on its upper side, with peripheral teeth centred by a tooth on the printing yoke; a hand lever and a spring button in a four-notch cam plate let the operator turn the type-plate by quarter turns to present one of four type sections. The index plate slides along a guide rod over the paper, and can be rocked up on that rod to see the writing. An oscillating rod carries a curved feed plate with one toothed edge and one smooth edge, which works a double crank on the index plate, so each finger-piece stroke steps the plate one space forward and the smooth edge returns the crank positively. The same stroke depresses a yoke that presses a spring strip with a central tooth onto the flexible edge of the type-plate, pressing the chosen type onto the paper over a flat rod. A second, shorter-stroke spacing finger limited by an eccentric disk advances one step without printing, a spring on the rock-shaft returns it, and a feed roll with ratchet line-spaces the paper; two ink pads sit beneath the type-plate.
