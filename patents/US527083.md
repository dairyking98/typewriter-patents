## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** On shifted-platen machines the platen can only rest at base line or upper case, so numerators over denominators, abbreviation marks or chemical symbols cannot be printed in one letter space.

**Solution:** An adjustable stop key stops the platen midway on its transverse shift and holds the carriage from spacing while more characters are struck.

**How it works:** The shift lever and space lever arms lie side by side at the keyboard. A stop key with a pad and a threaded stem, carrying lugs on opposite sides and a limiting nut, passes through a bracket on the keyboard frame between the two arms. Pressing it holds the platen at an intermediate station on its transverse travel and prevents longitudinal movement, so an elevated character is printed and then others in the same letter space below it.

**Features:** [SHIFT:platen](../features/shift-platen.md), [ESC:space-key](../features/esc-space-key.md), [USE:accessory](../features/use-accessory.md), [USE:special-script](../features/use-special-script.md)

**Parts:** stop key; pad; threaded stem; limiting nut; bracket; shift lever; space lever

**Improves on:**

- earlier intermediate platen stops: also holds the carriage from spacing so a second character can be struck in the same space
