## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** Pneumatic typewriters needed many mechanical parts for letter spacing.

**Solution:** The blow of the type against a yielding rubber printing disc releases the spacing rack tooth, so spacing needs no separate mechanism.

**How it works:** Type rods slide in a pyramidal block of type tubes and are driven pneumatically by air pushes through connecting tubes, all printing at one common point. A hard rubber disc platen on a pivoted arm yields upward when a type strikes paper there; an escapement piece on the arm releases one tooth of a spacing rack on the light paper carriage. The disc turns loosely so a fresh surface faces each blow, and can be slid in a slot to shift the worn ring. The carriage has no platen and runs on friction rollers. A device on the keyboard changes the writing from small letters to capitals or italics.
