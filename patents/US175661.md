## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A cheap toy that resembles a printing telegraph, can really print on a paper strip, and helps children with spelling.

**Solution:** A hand-cranked type wheel with a ratchet (rag) wheel and spring detent is turned to the letter shown on a dial, and a finger lever presses an impression pad against the inked type while its pawl feeds a paper strip.

**How it works:** A type wheel and a notched rag wheel sit on a shaft at the centre of a dial ring, and are turned by a crank to the letter named by a pointer. A spring with a double-inclined end drops into the rag-wheel notches, which equal the number of letters, so the chosen letter is positioned accurately and held while printing. An ink roller on links rests against the type wheel. A printing lever pivoted nearby carries a finger rest, an impression pad that presses the paper against the type at the print point, and a guide roller. Pressing the lever prints; a pawl on the lever works a feed roller to move the paper strip from its reel, and a second pawl stops it going back. A spring returns the lever, and the guide roller then draws more paper off the reel for the next letter.
