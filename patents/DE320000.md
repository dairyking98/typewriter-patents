## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** In music-note typewriters, shifting the paper by key levers with angled cutouts gives friction and play, so the carriage position is inaccurate on heavy keystrokes.

**Solution:** Pitch keys in two rows strike a swinging angled-ceiling bow lever whose motion is transmitted by a connecting rod to the flat paper table, which is held by a toothed segment and spring roller.

**How it works:** The paper is stretched over a flat table that runs on balls in two guides, instead of a cylindrical platen. Each pitch key moves a lever of different length that strikes with a small roller against a inverted-V ceiling formed of two sloped plates on swinging arms. Longer levers give larger swings, so the displacement of the table matches the staff position of the note. A connecting rod turns the swing into straight table travel. A toothed segment with a spring-loaded roller locks the position so both hands are free for the typing keys; the table may also move vertically, with springs balancing its weight.

**Features:** [Flat bed or book typewriter](../features/pap-flat-bed.md), [Special scripts and characters](../features/use-special-script.md), [Small keyboard with shift rows](../features/sel-keyboard-few-keys.md), [Selection locked for printing](../features/sel-selector-locking.md), [Platen pressed to the element](../features/imp-platen-to-element.md)

**Parts:** flat paper table; guide; ball; pitch key; lever; angled ceiling plate; swing arm; connecting rod; toothed segment; spring roller

**Improves on:**

- earlier note typewriters with shift keys with angled cutouts: strike point lies between the bearings with low friction and central action
