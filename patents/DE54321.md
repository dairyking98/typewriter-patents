## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** A radial-typebar typewriter needs an inking ribbon that is advanced on every impression, uses its full width, can be reversed, and a platen with adjustable paper guide and single or double line spacing.

**Solution:** A disengageable ribbon unit winds the ribbon from one spool to the other by a ratchet driven from the key frame on every stroke, with the ribbon holder shifting sideways so the whole width is used and a pawl switch reversing direction.

**How it works:** Type levers mounted radially in a circular-segment frame swing down under key action to hit a ribbon lying above the paper on a platen roller. The key frame operates a lever that, on each print, limits the platen carriage's travel and pushes a pawl turning a ratchet on the ribbon spool by one or more notches, winding ribbon from one spool onto the other. A fixed pawl prevents backward turning. A carrier on a dovetail guide moves the ribbon sideways so each new impression falls on fresh ribbon width. When one spool is run out, pulling or pushing a rod shifts a block under spring tension so the pawls engage the other ratchet, reversing winding. The platen carries an adjustable paper guide and a pre-turning device for single or double line spacing.
