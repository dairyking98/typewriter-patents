## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** In earlier escapements, a carriage moved by hand and released would creep one or more letter-spaces left before the loose dog straightened and caught, so operators had to overshoot to the right of where they wanted to stop.

**Solution:** A loose spring-propelled feeding dog is locked against endwise travel whenever it is pushed down out of the rack, so it always re-enters the rack at the limit of its travel and stops the carriage at once.

**How it works:** A rocking trunnion below the vertical feed-rack carries an arm with a fixed holding dog and a loose feeding dog, which slides on slots and pins under a weak spring. When a key or the space key rocks the trunnion, the loose dog is withdrawn and the holding dog engages the rack to hold the carriage, while the spring shoots the loose dog one tooth to the right. On release the holding dog withdraws, the loose dog re-enters one tooth behind, and the carriage spring or drum pulls it left to the stop, one letter-space. When the carriage is pulled right by hand the rack teeth depress the loose dog, and a projection on it then catches a stop on the trunnion so the spring cannot slide it. On release it re-engages at the end of its travel and halts the carriage at that exact point.
