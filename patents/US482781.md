## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** On Remington and Caligraph machines that print on the underside of the platen, the whole platen frame has to be hinged up to see the writing.

**Solution:** A handle-operated pawl and ratchet rotates the platen about its own axis to expose its underside without lifting the frame.

**How it works:** A handle throws a pawl into a toothed wheel on the platen end and turns the platen a half revolution to show the printed line. A loose second toothed wheel connected by a pin-and-slot is held by a spring-loaded anchor spur so it stays still during the forward turn. When the pawl is released a spring returns the platen to its normal position, where a notch seats on the front rail of the carriage. A friction roller stays on the paper during the motion.

**Features:** [FRM:writing-visible](../features/frm-writing-visible.md), [PAP:platen-roller](../features/pap-platen-roller.md)

**Parts:** platen; toothed wheel; pawl; handle; pin-and-slot wheel; anchor spur; return spring; friction roller

**Improves on:**

- Remington and Caligraph platen frames: turns the platen on its axis instead of lifting the whole frame
