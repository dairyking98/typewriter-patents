## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** On a pad-inked typebar machine the types must lie face-down on the ink pad at rest yet strike the platen squarely, and a ring-shaped pad is hard to replace without disturbing the typebars.

**Solution:** A pinion on each typebar rolls on a fixed rack to half-turn the bar between pad and platen, a fixed guide aligns the types at the print point, and an annular pad holder takes the pad endwise from outside.

**How it works:** Each typebar is mounted rotatably in a swinging yoke with a bevel pinion on its shank. As the bar returns from the platen, the pinion passes along a stamped rack on the hanger and half-revolves the bar so the types press on the annular ink pad. In the upswing the rack turns the bar back, then a guide arm on the hanger stops further rotation. At the printing point a cross bar with two oblong holes and a bevelled centre aligns the two types. The pad is stiffened by a toothed metal strip and cranked into the outside holder by a spur wheel.
