## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Geared rapid line-spacers throw work out of the machine on one stroke but the return was free, so a following slip or record sheet could not be fed back and spaced correctly.

**Solution:** A two-way pawl-and-ratchet clutch between the lever-driven pinion and the platen, controlled by a shield so it is disengaged at rest and engaged both ways once the lever moves, with adjustable unequal travel in each direction.

**How it works:** A hand lever with a toothed sector drives a pinion and disk loose on the platen spindle, giving the platen more angle than the lever. The disk carries two oppositely acting pawls working on two ratchet wheels fast on the spindle. A spring-loaded shield held by a pin on the sector keeps the pawl tails lifted at rest, so the platen is free for normal hand line-spacing. As the lever moves, the pawls move out from under the stepped shield one after the other and drop in, locking the platen to the lever both ways. On the return stroke the shield catches the tails and releases the pawls in turn, so travel is greater one way than the other; shifting the shield sideways reverses or equalises this, and a bell-crank stop can make it work one way only. An adjustable finger on the sector sets the stroke length.
