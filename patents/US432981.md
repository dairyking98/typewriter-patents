## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Not stated at length; the patent offers a simple hand-operated index typewriter in which one lever selects a character and prints it.

**Solution:** A single handle swings a pointer over a character segment, which winds the type wheel by cord and spring to the same letter, and pressing the handle down prints and advances the paper carriage one tooth.

**How it works:** The type wheel sits on a spring-loaded shaft in a frame that pivots on arms and is held up by a spring. A cord runs from a sheave on the wheel shaft to an angle-lever whose pointer moves over an index segment engraved with the keyboard characters; a pin on the lever drops into notches in the segment. Lifting the handle frees the pin, moving it to a letter winds the wheel to the matching angular position against the shaft spring, and pressing down lowers the whole wheel frame so the inked wheel meets the paper over a cushioned anvil. The same downward motion, through a link and levers, kicks a pawl that advances a ratchet and a cog wheel meshing with a rack on the paper carriage by one tooth. Blank wheel positions give spaces, a stop shoulder prevents more than one wheel turn if the cord breaks, a pawl release frees the carriage for return, and a weight takes up lost motion and trips a bell near line end.
