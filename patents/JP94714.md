## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** In a Japanese-text typewriter whose cylinder carriage is fed sideways by a fixed rack, a loose rack and a pawl driven by a wound spring, backing the carriage earlier required changing over the intermediate gear between the drive gear and rack bar.

**Solution:** A hand key on a swinging shaft with a cam, a delayed pawl change-over lever and a loose-rack return arm backs the carriage exactly one rack pitch per press without disturbing the forward drive.

**How it works:** The carriage is pulled sideways by a mainspring through a pinion on a rack bar, and a pawl hanging from the carriage alternately engages a fixed rack and a sliding loose rack to release one pitch per stroke. Pressing the back-space key turns a shaft carrying a cam. The cam's raised top first pushes a moving piece on the loose rack back by one pitch while the pawl is still in the loose rack, so the carriage goes back with it. After some free play a lever on the shaft's rear end presses down the frame that lifts the pawl, shifting it onto the fixed rack. A spring-loaded arm on the cam, stronger than the loose-rack feed spring, then pushes a pin on the loose rack to return it to its start position. Releasing the key lets a return spring restore everything and the pawl re-engages the loose rack.

## Description (English translation of the Japanese text, opening)

> Specification. Back-spacing device for the paper-cylinder lateral feed of a Japanese-text typewriter. Nature and object of the invention. This invention relates to a back-spacing device for the lateral feed of the paper cylinder in a Japanese-text typewriter of the following kind: a loose rack is laid alongside a fixed rack, and a pawl hanging from the cylinder carriage frame is made to engage the fixed and loose racks alternately, spanning the two; at the moment the pawl passes from the fixed rack onto the loose rack, the force of a wound spring at the back of the carriage moves the whole carriage, with the loose rack, sideways by a set distance, so feeding the cylinder sideways with the carriage. In it: a swinging shaft that governs the backward feed is hung from the fixed rack, with a return spring coiled on it and a hand key for starting the backward feed fixed on its front end. A cam of special shape is fitted on the same shaft, and a moving piece that touches the cam's side is mounted on the loose rack.
