## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Index-type typewriters with a type disc and index circle were complicated and slow; the inventor wants a simpler arrangement that works faster.

**Solution:** A rubber type band on a wheel that slides along a rotating cylinder is selected by a pivoted indicator arm over an index ring, and each print stroke of a rocking frame ratchets a stepped spiral to space the wheel along the line.

**How it works:** A rocking frame carries a shaft with a fixed inner cylinder bearing a spiral band with steps, and a loose hollow slotted outer cylinder carrying the type wheel, which slides on it and turns with it. The operator swings an indicator arm over a toothed index ring; bevel gears turn the outer cylinder so the chosen type sits over the impression bar, the arm tooth locking in a groove between index teeth. Pressing the frame down prints on the sheet laid on the impression bar. When released, a spring lifts the frame and a pawl advances a ratchet on the shaft one tooth, turning the inner spiral one step so a tooth on the wheel hub slips down one step and rubber springs shift the wheel one letter space along the line. Ink comes from a roller that rides the type via a second roller, and a pin on the indicator enters recesses to choose upper or lower case type alternating on the rim. Paper is advanced a line by a swinging feeder frame with a clamp strip.
