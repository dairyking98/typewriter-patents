## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** A portable typewriter needs a very low build height, but the ribbon drive and carriage feed gear normally sits above the key levers.

**Solution:** Place the carriage feed and ribbon movement drive parts between or just above the key levers, driven by a divided sector lever on a shaft.

**How it works:** Typebars are circular-section levers whose upper arms strike a sector lever on a continuous shaft. That shaft's rod works a spring-loaded toggle lever that releases the escapement pawl on the carriage rack, and the shift key can also advance the carriage. Another rod moves the ribbon across to the print point; its pivot sits in a slot so that going to capitals shifts the pivot and adds extra ribbon stroke. The ribbon spools turn through gears and a ratchet pawled by a lever on every key stroke. Accent keys bypass the sector and move only the ribbon cross-stroke lever without spacing.
