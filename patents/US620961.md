## Summary

**Kind:** type-element machine · **Subject (patent classes):** Typewriters or selective printing mechanisms characterised by the mounting, arrangement or disposition of the types or dies with types or dies arranged in stationary or sliding cases or frames or upon flexible strips, plates, bars or rods

**In the patent’s own words:** My invention relates to improvements in type-writing machines of the class in which all the types are in one type-carrier.

**How it works (reading):** A celluloid type-plate with molded type slid on a guide rod by a rocking shaft whose arms are locked by interlocking levers until a key is struck.

## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Improves Pratt's earlier all-types-in-one-carrier machines (Serial Nos. 540,168 and 553,460), mainly the sideways movement and locking of the type-plate and the raising/lowering and locking of the paper carriage.

**Solution:** A celluloid type-plate with molded type slid on a guide rod by a rocking shaft whose arms are locked by interlocking levers until a key is struck.

**How it works:** Types are molded on the rear face of a thin celluloid plate in horizontal rows, sawn apart into vertical triplets (lower case, capitals, figures) so each springs clear. The plate slides on a guide rod held by flexible strips so plate and rod withdraw for changing or cleaning, and a V-shaped extension with a yoke fits a pin on an arm of a rocking shaft. Front arms act as drivers and rear arms as stops. Key levers lift transverse levers whose fingers raise small pivoted levers into the driver arms, with adjusting screws in wood slats to set each key's travel; a locking arm between uprights holds the shaft until a key is struck. The plate is brought into contact with the platen at the same time as the hammer stroke, but not by it, and ribbon feed and impression are as in the earlier applications.
