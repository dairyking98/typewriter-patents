## Reading (full text, OCR poor)

**Problem:** In front-strike typebar machines the key levers all pivoted on one level gave keys inclined, unequal paths and uneven touch across the typebar segment.

**Solution:** Key levers pivot on stepped fulcrum rods for vertical, equal key travel, and push links drive sub-levers and links to the typebars so that leverage grows through the stroke, more so for the middle bars, all in a removable, shiftable sub-assembly.

**How it works:** Key levers sit on three fulcrum rods at different heights and depths, one per key row, so every key moves vertically with equal leverage. The rear end of each first-order key lever pushes an upright link up to a sub-lever pivoted on a straight wire. A second arm of the sub-lever links to the typebar above its pivot; the arm geometry gives accelerated motion, with the leverage rising towards the end of the stroke. Links to the middle bars slope downward and those to the end bars slope upward, giving the middle bars higher initial leverage against gravity and an even touch. The segment, sub-levers and cross bar form one rigid shift frame pivoted to the machine frame, shifted by a bell-crank with a cam slot and rollers, with adjusting screws, stop screws, and a cork-padded rest.
