## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** In typebar machines with hangers in a circle or oval, the ribbon guides had to sit outside the ring, far apart, and the ribbon rubbed on the hangers and journals, coating them with ink and dust that impeded the typebars. Ribbon winding also needed gears that burdened the carriage spring.

**Solution:** Two facing curved rows of adjustable typebar hangers leave a central gap where the ribbon runs without touching them, and the ribbon winding spool sits on the carriage spring-wheel shaft so carriage motion drives it directly.

**How it works:** Key levers act through connecting rods on typebars of unequal length pivoted in adjustable hangers arranged in two concave rows, all striking a common impression point. The ribbon runs between the two rows over close-set guides from a delivering spool to a winding spool. Each key release lets the spring cam-wheel pull the paper carriage one letter space; a pawl on the cam-wheel hub turns a ratchet on the winding-spool shaft, advancing the ribbon. On carriage return the cam wheel reverses and a second pawl holds the spool still. A latch lets the operator slide the shaft to disengage the pawls and rewind the ribbon by a hand crank.
