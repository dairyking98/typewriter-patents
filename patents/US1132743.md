## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Cutting continuous sheets or tally strips on a typewriter with an ordinary blade gives ragged cuts once the edge dulls.

**Solution:** A shearing cutter in which an unsharpened pivoted blade is swung through a transverse slot in a hinged bar, shearing the paper against the slot edges.

**How it works:** The paper strip passes up through a gap between a vertical slotted bar and a blade, held by feed rollers above and below. The blade is pivoted at one end and has a finger piece at the other; pulling it forward swings it through the slot, its inclined end pressing the paper against the bar and starting a progressive shearing cut. A spring returns the blade. The bar is hinged at the opposite end and held by a spring detent in a notch, so releasing the detent swings the bar forward to make loading paper easy.

**Features:**

- **Paper and carriage:** [Feed rollers and paper release](../features/pap-feed-rollers.md), [Paper table and guides](../features/pap-paper-guide.md)
- **Frame and body:** [Few parts, low cost](../features/frm-few-parts.md)

**Parts:** slotted bar; cutting blade; finger piece; restoring spring; detent spring plate; platen carrier; tally platen; feed roller

**Improves on:**

- ordinary cutting blade or edge on typewriters: shearing action against a slot does not depend on blade sharpness
