## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Ordinary typewriters strike types one by one against the platen, noisily; the inventor wants a quiet machine that sets a whole line of characters first and prints it at once.

**Solution:** A row of side-by-side type wheels is each released in turn by a key stroke, spins under a weight, and is stopped at the chosen character by a key-raised pawl catching a projection on the wheel; the finished line is then printed at once by one lever.

**How it works:** Pressing a key raises a universal bar and a needle in a stepping carriage, which pushes up a friction-held pawl under the next type wheel. The carriage advances one tooth by a rocking rack and pawl, and in doing so frees that wheel, which turns under its counterweight until a long or short projection meets the pawl; short-stroke and long-stroke keys, plus a reversing key that kicks the wheel the other way, give four characters per pawl. When the line is set, the print lever first lowers a knife into notches to align the wheels, then presses the paper roll onto the whole row. On return a collector comb takes up the wheels and returns them all to zero, a strip drops the pawls and the carriage returns by a spring drum, while springs between the discs space the unused wheels to give a blank. A sideways-moving ink or carbon ribbon presents fresh surface to each wheel, and an error can be corrected by pressing a key that resets the wrong pawl and wheel.

## Description (English translation of the Japanese text, opening)

> Specification. "Typewriter" or similar machine. Nature and object of the invention. This invention relates to improvements in the arrangement of typewriters or similar machines. The type wheels can move angularly, one step after another, round the shaft on which they are mounted, and the limit of that angular movement depends on the particular type or character to be brought to the printing point. Operating keys are provided to regulate the amount of angular movement corresponding to each such character. The amount of rotation of a type wheel, and so the particular letter or figure, is decided by pawls mounted to work in a body such as a quadrant; when a pawl is set it comes into contact with a projection on the type wheel. The machine includes a suitable number of ordinary [universal] bars running the whole length of the machine, and a movable flange or carriage, capable of intermittent sideways movement, carrying a set of vertically movable needles, the number of needles being equal to the number of universal bars.
