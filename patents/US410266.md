## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Improving the Boston Type Writer Co. index machine (US 1886 patent) to prevent blurring, give even letter spacing and variable line spacing, add characters, and make parts adjustable and cheap.

**Solution:** A two-row type wheel driven by a sector and crown gear is shifted axially for case, and the carriage rack is advanced by two alternating pawls (one on the downstroke, one on the return) to space evenly without blur.

**How it works:** The operator swings an operating lever on a sector over a curved index plate; the sector meshes with a crown gear on an axle carrying the loosely mounted type wheel, which is keyed to the gear by a guide pin and can slide axially. A spring-loaded lever in the wheel hub groove holds the wheel back so the front row (small letters) prints; pressing the lever slides the wheel forward to use the rear row (capitals and other characters). Pressing the frame carrying the lever and index down prints the type through an inking ribbon, with a guard that keeps ribbon off the paper except at the type, and a rubber strip in the printing bar takes the blow; springs and a screw limit the depth of impression. A pawl rigid with the frame advances the carriage rack on the downstroke, and a second adjustable pawl on a spring lever completes the step on the upstroke, with a double rack of two pitches for open or close spacing. Lines are spaced by a lever and pawl that turn feed rolls by three teeth, or two teeth when a swung stop pin limits the lever; a bell warns of the line end.
