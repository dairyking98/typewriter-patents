## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Ordinary typewriters were complex, heavy and costly; a simpler, cheaper, lighter, portable one was wanted.

**Solution:** A hand-held stereotype disc or curved bar with characters on its edge is positioned over a slot in a plate and pressed down onto the paper, with a notched rod giving the letter spacing.

**How it works:** Paper passes between a base plate and a slotted plate, with corrugated rollers feeding it for line spacing. The operator holds the type bar or disc by a handle and places the wanted letter over the slot. A spring-raised rod with notches at letter-width intervals lies along the slot and is pressed down with the other hand, pressing the type onto the paper. The bar's inner edge is serrated and engages the notches, so after each print the bar is moved one notch along. Ink pads beside the slot take up ink when the type is lowered on them. In the modification the disc is free, with type on its periphery, and one or two rods fit between the type to keep them in line.
