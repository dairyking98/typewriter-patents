## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** On a mixed kana and Latin typewriter, Latin letters need closer spacing than kana, and kana and Latin letters run together and get misread.

**Solution:** Mount a kana escapement wheel and a finer-pitched Latin escapement wheel on one sliding shaft that the type-position (shift) shaft moves sideways, so the right wheel engages the rocking escapement lever's dogs.

**How it works:** A carriage plate is pulled by a draw band, and a pinion on a shaft meshes with its rack. The shaft carries both a kana escapement wheel and a Latin wheel with smaller tooth pitch, and a rocking lever with inner and outer dogs releases one tooth per keystroke. The type plate has three rows (two kana, one Latin), and the type-position shaft turns to three positions. Turning it between kana and Latin moves a spacing-change lever, which slides the escapement shaft so the other wheel engages. The wheels are spaced so that a Latin tooth engages the outer dog just before the kana tooth leaves the inner dog, which makes the carriage move on without printing and leaves a distinguishing gap at every kana/Latin change.

## Description (English translation of the Japanese text, opening)

> Japanese–Western typewriter. Nature and object of the invention. This invention relates to a typewriter that prints both Japanese and Western characters on one machine. It is characterised as follows. A driving gear for moving the platen, fixed on one end of a shaft that can move left and right, always meshes with a rack on one side of a carriage plate that is always pulled one way by the pull of a draw band. On the middle of that shaft are fixed a kana escapement wheel and a Latin escapement wheel; in particular the tooth pitch round the Latin wheel is smaller than that round the kana wheel. The distance between the two wheels is such that, just before a tooth of the kana wheel leaves the inner dog on the top of the rocking escapement lever, a tooth of the Latin wheel engages the outer dog on the top of that lever. And only when the type-position shaft turns from the Japanese position to the Western position, or from the Western to the Japanese, is the escapement shaft moved left or right, moving the kana and Latin wheels with it.
