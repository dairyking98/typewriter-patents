## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Printing on ruled paper or printed forms is hard because the platen turns only fixed ratchet steps.

**Solution:** An attachment for the Smith Premier that lets the ratchet pawl be tripped out and the platen held at any position by a locking disk at the other end of the cylinder.

**How it works:** The standard platen has a ratchet wheel with a spring locking pawl, turned by a rock shaft with a handle and a spring-loaded dog. The attachment makes the tie rod tubular and lengthens its slot, adding a sliding sleeve and a tripping dog that holds the locking pawl out of the ratchet teeth. With the pawl held out, the platen can be rotated freely, then held at any desired point by a penetrable disk on the other end of the cylinder and a sliding frame with a locking disk. A cam lever works the locking disk, and a lever holds the tripping dog out of the pawl when ordinary stepwise spacing is wanted.
