## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Typists had to calculate by hand the starting point that centres a heading, or the column positions of a table.

**Solution:** A double carriage scale with a second set of graduations at twice the pitch, reading in the reverse direction, so spacing the heading's length shows the centred start position directly.

**How it works:** A strip screwed to the typewriter frame carries the ordinary scale (0 to about 86) and a second scale whose divisions are twice as long and numbered in the opposite direction. The operator starts at zero, presses the space bar once per letter and space of the heading, and reads the number on the doubled scale above the pointer. That number is the start position on the ordinary scale, where the carriage is set before typing the heading. A celluloid or enamelled paper strip beside the scales takes erasable pencil marks for column and heading positions. One variant has the second scale zeroed at the centre.

**Features:** [PAP:line-indicator](../features/pap-line-indicator.md), [ESC:space-key](../features/esc-space-key.md), [USE:accessory](../features/use-accessory.md)

**Parts:** scale strip; pointer; doubled-pitch scale; ordinary scale; erasable writing strip

**Numbers:** ordinary scale 0 to 72 or so, second scale 0 to 86 in one example; graduation ratio 2:1; Norfolk example: 7 spaces gives 31, start at 32

**Improves on:**

- ordinary typewriter carriage scale: adds a halved-reading scale so centring needs no arithmetic
