## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typewriter carriage escapements are complicated, slow, and hard to adjust for wear.

**Solution:** A simple spring-driven ratchet escapement with a push pin and a lever-arm dog, adjustable by thumb screw for wear and depth.

**How it works:** A key-driven shaft turns an arm whose pin lifts a lever and a push pin; the push pin enters a ratchet tooth while a bell crank swings the holding dog arm away from the ratchet. A flat spring on the crank snaps the dog arm up as soon as its point clears the tooth, and a rubber bumper quiets it. A spiral mainspring on a shaft drives the ratchet, which is geared to the carriage rack; on carriage return a pawl on the ratchet rides over a second ratchet fixed to the shaft, winding the spring. A thumb-screw bracket adjusts the dog tip for wear and tooth-engagement depth.
