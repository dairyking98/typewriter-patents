## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Typebar pivots need low friction and a bearing that can be adjusted at assembly and later for wear, while thin enough to pack a full set of typebars into under half a circle in a front-strike machine.

**Solution:** Each typebar eye has a ball race running on balls held between two conical plates on a cross pin in a U-shaped hanger, one cone adjustable by upsetting the pin head.

**How it works:** The typebar has an eye with a V or round ball groove. A U-shaped hanger has two jaws and a riveted cross pin carrying two bearing cones that together form a V raceway facing the groove in the typebar. A ring of balls sits between the groove and the cones, so the bar swings entirely on balls without touching the hanger. One cone has a boss that fits one jaw hole and slides on the pin. Tapping or upsetting the pin head pushes that cone toward the other to adjust the bearing at assembly and again as it wears. Key levers act on the bar through a rod and bell crank.
