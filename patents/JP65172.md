## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Japanese typewriters with types spread over a flat surface are heavy to run and slow to search; types thrown out by the hammer or not returning cause trouble.

**Solution:** A drum carries several thousand short spring-retracted types in its rim, with a matching dial and comb pointer so the operator finds a character's column number, and pressing the numbered key slides the paper cylinder to that column while a hammer inside the drum pushes the type out to print.

**How it works:** The operator frees the drum with a foot lever and turns a handle that rotates drum and character dial together to bring the wanted row to the front, then reads the column number off the comb pointer. Pressing the key with that number swings a stepped lever system whose pivots lower from left to right, so the key stroke moves a rack, pinion and sector gear that slide the paper cylinder carriage sideways to that column. The same key stroke rocks a flap and lever so a hammer inside the drum pushes the type, held in a spring-returned box, out through the rim onto the paper cylinder. On release springs return everything, the type sinks back into the rim, a pawl turns the paper cylinder one step for character spacing, and a toothed gear raises the inked cloth one tooth to present fresh ink. A far-right key only advances the cylinder for a blank space, and a mirror lets the operator check the print.

## Description (English translation of the Japanese text, opening)

> Specification. Japanese-script printing machine. Summary of the nature and purpose of the invention. This invention concerns a Japanese-script printing machine with a type drum carrying several thousand types round its whole circumference. Each type fits into a type box; a plug in the bottom of the box is fixed to the rim by a pin, and a spring between the inner bottom of the box and the plug keeps the box always drawn automatically into the rim. On the same shaft as the drum is mounted a character-dial wheel on which the same characters as the drum's types are written in the same order, and a comb-shaped pointer faces this dial wheel so that the position of a character can be recognized. In front of the drum is a paper cylinder that can turn and also move horizontally. By pressing the key whose number equals the column number shown by the comb pointer, the paper cylinder is moved to the required position, and at the same time a hammer pushes the type out from the inside of the drum and prints it on the paper cylinder.
