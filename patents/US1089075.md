## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** A typewriter with a single shift key cannot easily shift and lock the platen for a line of capitals while also allowing a quick temporary shift from either hand.

**Solution:** Provide a temporary shift key at each end of the keyboard plus a separate shift-and-lock key, where either temporary key releases the lock dog.

**How it works:** Typebars carry upper and lower case types and the platen frame is lifted by a rocking frame. A left temporary shift key on a pivoted lever lifts the frame while held and drops it on release. On the right, a second lever lifts the frame, and a spring-loaded dog on a rock shaft catches a pin on it to hold the platen in upper case. A short auxiliary lever with the locking key pivots on that right lever; pressing it shifts and locks. Depressing either temporary key rocks the shaft and moves the dog off the pin, releasing the lock, and the right auxiliary key doubles as a temporary shift because its finger silences the dog.
