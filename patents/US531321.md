## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** The range of type on a typewriter is limited by the size of the keyboard.

**Solution:** Let one key operate several typebars by shifting an apertured segmental plate that brings a chosen group of rod-hooks over the key lever.

**How it works:** Typebars are pivoted at the bottom of the frame, each joined to a spring-returned rod that rises through an aperture in a movable segmental plate and ends in a hook. A fulcrumed lever with a spoon end lies near the hooks, and each lever can serve for example three rods. Moving the plate swings a different rod over the spoon end, so striking the key pulls that rod and its typebar down to print. Three fonts can be used by shifting the plate, which is held by a spring lever in a recess and fixed with a thumb nut.

**Features:**

**Parts:** typebar; rod; hook; apertured segmental plate; spoon-ended lever; key; key rod; return spring; detent lever; thumb nut

**Numbers:** three type bars per key lever
