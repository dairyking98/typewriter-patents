## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Independent-key machines are quick to use but need a multiplicity of keys and type levers, which is complicated and expensive.

**Solution:** A rocking keyboard plate tilting on two axes positions a single swinging flexible type-sheet by key position, and one hammer prints the selected type.

**How it works:** Banks of character-marked plates are pivoted on a cross-arm fixed to a rock-shaft that also carries a type-carrier holding a flexible rubber type-sheet in three rows. Pressing a character tilts the plate until two of the circular pins beside it bottom on a universal bar; the sideways component of the tilt turns the rock-shaft and swings the type-sheet so the chosen type lines up with a hole in the ink pad plate. The same depression of the universal bar throws a weighted hammer whose stop-flange first locks into a notched comb (locking the carrier) and then strikes the back of the type, pushing it through the hole onto the platen. A shift key, pressed left or right, stops at two depths to raise or lower the carrier and select the middle or bottom row, held by a spring wedge in notches. The universal bar spring return also steps the carriage by a pawl on a rack.
