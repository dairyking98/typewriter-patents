## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Existing index type-wheel machines print every letter at the same advance, giving uneven spacing, and are slow to set.

**Solution:** A hand-turned type wheel on a pivoting lever is pressed down to print, and notches of varying depth on its rim let a locking cone advance the carriage by the letter's width.

**How it works:** The type wheel with a hard-rubber handle sits on the end of a spring-lifted lever above a carriage running in a dovetail guide with a rack. The operator turns the wheel until a pointer on a toothed pointer wheel and chart plate shows the letter, then presses the handle down to print. While down, a fixed tooth enters the ratchet wheel fixed to the type wheel and locks rotation. A swinging ink disc with felt rides over the types as the wheel turns and swings aside on printing. Between letters on the rim are notches of different depth; as the lever descends a locking cone is pulled back over the rack until its heel meets the notch, and on the return stroke it pushes the carriage forward by that many teeth (1 to 4). A toggle lever button makes spaces of up to the maximum width, and pushed fully down frees the carriage for manual positioning.
