## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A typewriter with one key per character is complex; the inventors want a single multiple-character key that selects a character and prints it with one finger pressure.

**Solution:** A single nutating disc key with all characters marked on it carries a rigid spherical type-ring beneath it; pressing the edge at a character tilts the ring until a stop pin seats in a recess, and continued pressure drives the ring straight down to print and release a spring carriage for one space.

**How it works:** The disc key is held on a ball joint so it can tilt in any direction but not rotate, with a central spring returning it. A hollow type-ring with up to three concentric circles of type (lower case, capitals, figures) hangs from the key, its convex outer face on a sphere centred on the pivot. Pressing the key at a character tilts it until the inner wall of the ring meets a tapered stop pin, which enters the semiconical recess behind that type and holds the ring rigid and aligned over a platen block with a guard plate opening. A lever turns a cam to set the pin at one of three heights, so it meets one of three steps inside the ring and selects the lower, capital or figure circle. Further pressure moves the key and ring bodily in a straight line on a jointed rectangle (Roberval balance) against a return spring, printing through carbon paper or ribbon. The same stroke lifts a pawl rod that frees a double gear with lost-motion slot, so the spring-barrel-driven paper roller carriage advances one space.
