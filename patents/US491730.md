## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Earlier Wenz machine (US438965) needed better impression and spacing with a simple type wheel.

**Solution:** A hand-turned type wheel with elastic arms, a centering pin dropped into holes, and a bank of hammers on one impression lever.

**How it works:** The tilting frame slides on a toothed rod and travels automatically left to right while printing. A type wheel on a spindle has a slitted metal disk whose elastic arms carry the types on the underside, with a ring of holes above each type and the character printed beside each hole. The operator turns the wheel, then presses a pin into the hole to centre the type. Pressing the wheel plate drives an impression lever whose forked arm straddles the plate edge. Three hammers on the lever, one fixed and the others loose and raised or lowered, give different strikes or cases. A pointer in a heart-shaped hole reads a scale on the bar.
