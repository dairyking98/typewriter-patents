## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typewriters stop the type carrier for every letter, which limits typing speed.

**Solution:** A compressed-air typewriter whose type holder rotates continuously, so that a key press sets a pin that fires the printing and carriage-spacing pistons at the instant the chosen type passes the print point.

**How it works:** An air-driven piston and pawl turn a ratchet wheel and with it a casing that carries sliding type plates around a vertical axle, running continuously while air is supplied. Each character has a key on an air pipe; closing the pipe with a finger pushes up a plunger and pin into the path of a star wheel on the rotating arm. When the star wheel strikes the pin, a valve opens air to two cylinders: one pushes the type plate then passing the print point against the paper cylinder, the other raises a friction disk that touches the rotating casing and drives a toothed wheel and rack so the paper carriage advances one letter at the same speed as the type. The star wheel then turns on, the valve resets and the springs retract the pistons.
