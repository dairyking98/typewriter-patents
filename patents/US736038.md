## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** On a platen-shift (Underwood-type) machine, a printing-line indicator fixed to the platen would move with the shifting platen and get into the path of the typebars, and would indicate a different line for upper and lower case.

**Solution:** A weighted scale bar linked to the carriage travels with it sideways but rests on the fixed typebar segment, so it stays put when the platen is raised or lowered for case shift.

**How it works:** The carriage has a fixed rear section and a front section carrying the platen on rollers running on a vertically movable track; the shift key first unlocks a latch and then lifts the track. A scale bar marked in letter-spaces extends the length of the platen and rests by its own weight on top of the typebar segment. One end is pivoted to a link on the platen-carrying section, the other end connects through a pin-and-slot to a link on the carriage, so the bar moves along with the carriage but not up and down with the platen. The operator reads the scale to reinsert paper in the same place and to count how many spaces to go back to a correction.
