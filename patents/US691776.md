## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** A compact, light typewriter needs a large character capacity, a simple carriage feed and reliable paper holding, using type at several points along each type arm.

**Solution:** The platen carriage is lifted or lowered by shift levers between three locked heights so different type on the same arm strike the paper, with a spring-drum rack-and-ratchet carriage feed and a pawl-and-hole line-space button.

**How it works:** Key levers operate type arms that carry type at several points along their length. Left and right shift levers carry a slide with a cam slot: the first motion unlocks notched rock arms from pins on the carriage standards, and the further motion rocks a shaft that raises or lowers the carriage to the upper or lower position. Releasing the lever lets a spring plunger return the carriage to its middle position, where it is locked again; the right-hand levers instead lock the carriage in the raised or lowered position until the opposite lever is struck. A universal bar under the keys drives a tilting lever with a dog that engages a face ratchet on a pinion meshing with a rack under the carriage, while a second laterally tilting pawl takes the next tooth, and a spring drum drives the carriage. Spring-pressed rollers hold the paper on the platen, and a button with a cam slot and gravity pawl engages a ring of holes in the platen plate to line space, with a stop for single or double spacing.
