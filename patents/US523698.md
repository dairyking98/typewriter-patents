## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typebar machines need a quick, sharp print stroke, reliable case shifting, and ribbon, feed and bell mechanisms that are simple and linked to the type action.

**Solution:** A typebar machine whose whole platen carriage rides on vertically sliding legs for case shift, with typebars driven through a toothed link that shortens the leverage as the bar swings, and ribbon frame, carriage feed and ribbon spool feed all driven off the typebar's withdrawing link.

**How it works:** Each key drives a bell-crank and a link with several teeth that engage recesses in the typebar's tail piece one after another, nearer and nearer the fulcrum, so the bar accelerates and strikes sharply; the bar enters a forked guide to land on the print point. A separate withdrawing link on the tail moves a spring-loaded frame that swings the ribbon frame up to the print line, and oscillates a feed rack against a fixed stop and a spring-backed dog so the spring-drum carriage steps one tooth per stroke. Swinging the ribbon frame also ratchets either ribbon spool through pawls, whose direction is chosen by a sliding rod. Case shift is made by raising the legs that carry the platen carriage and rack using two independent levers, one raising and latching and the other releasing, with springs easing the drop. Further parts are a bell with adjustable slides that also act as carriage travel stops with pointers on a scale, a lever to free the feed dog, and a lever that ratchets the platen for line spacing.
