## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Common letter groups (th, is, it, the, for) take several keystrokes and the carriage steps one tooth per key, making typing slow.

**Solution:** Keys carry two or three letters in a row on one typelever and move the carriage two or three rack teeth accordingly.

**How it works:** Special keylevers have multi-letter type faces printing in the longitudinal direction. Their action, through a bar under all levers, oscillates the spacing-bar levers so the ratchet-toothed spacing bar and pawl advance the carriage by a number of teeth matching the letter count. Single-letter keys still advance one tooth. Described on a Remington-class machine with modifications for Hammond type machines; separate feed-roll devices are also shown.

**Features:** [ESC:pawl-rack](../features/esc-pawl-rack.md), [ESC:variable-spacing](../features/esc-variable-spacing.md), [ELEM:typebar](../features/elem-typebar.md), [SEL:keyboard-full](../features/sel-keyboard-full.md), [PAP:platen-roller](../features/pap-platen-roller.md)

**Parts:** finger lever; typelever; spacing bar; ratchet rack; pawl; carriage frame; spring drum; paper roller; ring frame; ribbon

**Numbers:** two teeth for two letters; three teeth for three letters

**Improves on:**

- Remington typewriter: multi-letter types and multi-tooth carriage advance
