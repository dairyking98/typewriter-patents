## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** On a folding typebar machine with a platen shifted to three printing levels, locking only one end of the carriage bed lets the other end sag and spoils vertical alignment.

**Solution:** Swing the carriage on side bars pivoted at the frame's upper middle, which is also the fold pivot, with two shift keys setting two raised levels through cam-selected stop screws and a locking cross rod that supports both ends.

**How it works:** Side bars pivoted to the frame carry the carriage bed and platen. Bell cranks on a cross rod, linked to the side bars and balanced by coiled springs, lift the carriage along an arc when the cap-shift key is pressed. The cap key lifts to an intermediate level that ends against one adjustable stop on a pivoted stop carrier. The figure-shift key lifts higher: a pin rides a cam face on the carrier and swings the carrier so a second, shorter stop is in the path of the shift link's shelf. A locking cross rod with wedge-faced arms at both sides engages pins on both bell cranks and so holds each end of the carriage in the chosen level.

- **Shift and cases:** [Shift by moving the platen](../features/shift-platen.md), [Three or more cases](../features/shift-triple.md), [Shift lock](../features/shift-lock.md)
- **Inking:** [Ribbon feed](../features/ink-ribbon-feed.md)

**Parts:** side bar; carriage bed; platen; bell crank; shift link; cross rod; counterbalance spring; stop carrier; adjustable stop screw; cam face; locking lever; locking pin; cap-shift key; figure-shift key

**Numbers:** three printing levels; arc radius about one-half the frame length

**Improves on:**

- earlier folding platen-shift typewriters (e.g. Corona): locks both ends of the carriage bed so it does not drop, and uses the fold pivot as the shift pivot
