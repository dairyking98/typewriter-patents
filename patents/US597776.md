## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** The earlier Kirby combination keyboard (Serial 567,989) was complex and less durable.

**Solution:** Letter-bars act only as triggers releasing spring-driven triple levers that push rods under a common striking bar.

**How it works:** Keys in banks rock shafts moving notched combination bars. Letter-bars resting on the bar stubs drop when their notches align and trip a spring-loaded triple lever. The released lever's toe pushes its push-rod beneath the transverse striking bar. The striking-bar frame, buffered by rubber, swings and operates the typewriter's key-lever through the rod's cam.

**Features:**

- **Selection:** [Small keyboard with shift rows](../features/sel-keyboard-few-keys.md), [Keys that only set the element](../features/sel-dummy-keyboard.md)
- **Impression:** [Spring-powered blow](../features/imp-spring-energy.md), [Separate print key or lever](../features/imp-print-lever.md)

**Parts:** combination bar; lock-bar; key; rock-shaft; letter-bar; triple lever; push-rod; trigger-bar; striking bar; rubber buffer

**Improves on:**

- Kirby keyboard 567,989: letter-bars become triggers; simpler and more durable
