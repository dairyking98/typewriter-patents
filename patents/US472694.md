## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** Operators wanted to columnate figures exactly and quickly without reading the carriage scales.

**Solution:** A swinging catch-lever on the carriage, set in a numbered notched quadrant, meets a graduated stop-block on the frame to halt the carriage at a chosen letter-space.

**How it works:** A catch-lever is pivoted on the carriage and sprung so its point normally clears the fixed stop-block. A notched quadrant marked 1, 2, 3 and so on lets the operator set the lever so its point lines up with a chosen notch of a graduated stop-block on a stop-bar fixed to the frame. Notches stand for units, tens, hundreds. The operator sets the lever to the number of digits in the amount and moves the carriage until it stops against the block, then releases the lever and types the number. The block can be slid along the bar to place the column on the sheet. Variants have two blocks with oppositely graduated notches or a block with ten stops.

**Features:**

- **Spacing and escapement:** [Margin stops](../features/esc-margin-stop.md), [Tabulator](../features/esc-tabulator.md)
- **Frame and body:** [Adjustment and wear take-up](../features/frm-adjustment-wear.md)

**Parts:** carriage frame; catch-lever; spring; notched quadrant; stop-bar; graduated stop-block

**Numbers:** five or ten stops on the block

**Improves on:**

- Blickensderfer applications 410,893 and 398,672: variable lever and graduated block instead of a row of side-by-side stops
