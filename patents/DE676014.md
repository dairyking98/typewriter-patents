## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Keyboard-operated embossing machines with a revolving die carrier handle upper and lower case with several concentric rings of dies, which complicates guiding the punch drive to the chosen ring and aligning the plate with it.

**Solution:** Put the upper and lower case die pairs alternately in one single ring of the carrier and give the carrier a shift: a partial rotation relative to its continuously turning shaft equal to the spacing of two neighbouring dies, so one stop per two dies selects either of the pair.

**How it works:** A vertical shaft driven in continuous rotation carries the die carrier, a drum with hub, upper and lower discs and rim flanges with axial slots holding vertically sliding die pairs. A keyboard brings one of a series of stops into the path of the revolving carrier, stopping it with the chosen pair over the plate held in claws on a carriage. A plunger drive then forces the pair together to emboss the plate. The carrier is coupled to the shaft through an inclined-slot guide, so a shift key turns it by one die spacing relative to the shaft, so the stop catches either the first die of the pair (one case) or the next (the other case). Existing single-set machines can be converted, and speed is unchanged.
