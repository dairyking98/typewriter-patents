## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Poster typewriters of the time did not let the operator see the writing while typing, and could not switch between letter sizes easily.

**Solution:** A poster typewriter with large types on an endless cloth band moved past the print point by a pointer lever over a letter scale, struck from the front by a hammer so the writing is visible.

**How it works:** Large types sit on a flexible cloth band with drive holes in the centre line, running over support rollers with driver teeth and past a permeable ink container. The operator slides a feeler lever sideways until its pointer is over the desired letter on a scale on the front shield; a chain or rack-and-pinion drive turns the rollers and brings that letter's type to the print position. Pushing the lever downwards presses a bar that pivots levers and fires a spring-returned hammer, striking the band against the paper held on a platen carriage. A crank raises or lowers the whole band assembly by a letter height to switch between small and large letters. The carriage advances by a rack, which is a rotatable rack cross with several pitches for types of different widths.
