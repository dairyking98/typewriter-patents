## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** The earlier Pastre and Lambert multiple-key typewriter (US607270) needed less key tilt and vibration, better access to the paper, a steadier key and a better inking pad. The key also had to work with a spring-driven carriage escapement.

**Solution:** A single tilting disc key ('nutating key') carries a spherical type block underneath; tilting selects a type, and pressing the key straight down prints it and releases one escapement step.

**How it works:** A hard-rubber disc key with marked pressure points sits on a ball joint and tilts in any direction. A type block with staggered concentric rows of type, struck as a sphere about the tilt centre, hangs from it. Tilting brings one type to the printing line, where a fixed stop pin drops into a matching recess on the inside of the block to lock it, and a cam sets the pin to one of three heights for the three series. Pressing the whole key down on a Roberval-type parallel linkage moves the type straight onto the platen through a pivoted inking pad. The same down-and-up key movement raises and lowers a rod with a two-finger pallet, which lets a spring-driven escape wheel advance the carriage one unit space. The standard carrying the key, block and pad is hinged to the base and can be thrown back to expose the paper.
