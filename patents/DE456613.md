## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Line shift wheel clutches with spreader linkages are bulky, need heavy axial pressure by hand (pushing the carriage unintentionally) and need two hands to operate.

**Solution:** A rotatable screw bolt moves a non-rotating threaded piston that spreads friction jaws, with the jaws and spring plates sitting in grooves of the roller flange that keep them rotationally fixed.

**How it works:** The platen, its axle and a rotating part form a unit with a flange carrying two circular sections and a groove in which two friction jaws slide. A piston-like part with grooves moves in a bore, and spring plates lie between its grooves and the jaws. The line index wheel is pushed over the jaws. A knob turns a threaded bolt with right-hand thread screwed into the piston; turning left pushes the piston, spring plates and jaws outward against the ratchet wheel, coupling the roller, and turning right releases it. A pin in a nut cutout limits knob travel, and the jaws wear can be taken up.
