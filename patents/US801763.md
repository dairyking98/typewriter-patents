## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** Direct-inking typebar machines give each type its own ink pad, pushed aside by the rising type, which is complicated and heavy to strike.

**Solution:** Group the type and give each group one curved felt ink pad that is moved out of the way by the key levers, not by the typebars.

**How it works:** The typebars are arranged in four groups (front, rear, two sides) so rest-position type faces form a circle. Each group has a bowed, felt-covered pad on arms of a rock shaft with a counterweight that holds it against the type. A lever per pad has a friction roll on the counterweight and a cross-bar under the group's key levers. Beveled fingers on the key levers push the cross-bar as the key goes down, tilting the pad away before the typebar rises. The key lost motion can be lengthened so the first travel moves only the pad.
