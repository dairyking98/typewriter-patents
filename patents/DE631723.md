## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Quiet typebar machines with two-group type bars needed complex changeover members that wore quickly and were costly.

**Solution:** Each type bar, carrying two types and driven by its own key, hangs on two links forming a four-bar linkage and is thrown to the platen by inertia along a near straight short path.

**How it works:** A key lever drives a transmission lever through a drive member; an intermediate link with a swing weight acts on the rear link of the type bar. The key withdraws and the type bar flies on by inertia, guided by the two links so the type moves almost straight to the print point. At rest all type bodies lie in a horizontal plane through the platen axis on a roughly 90 degree arc, as close to the print point as possible. The short path lets the type strike at low speed, reducing noise, and the drive parts lie below the type bars.
