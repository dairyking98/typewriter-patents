## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** A book and register typewriter that steps by pawl on a toothed guide rail has a fixed letter spacing, which should be adjustable without changing gears.

**Solution:** The print button moves a spring-tensioned slide whose pawl catches the rack, and the slide's stroke is set by an adjustable stop to vary the step.

**How it works:** A housing travels on toothed guide rails laid on the book, carrying a type wheel with rubber types on brackets around its rim. Pressing the button lever swings thumbs that slide a slide through arms and tension springs, engaging a pawl in the rack. Another projection on the button presses a finger and lever that push the bottom type bracket down to print. On release, the springs pull the housing forward along the rack until the slide hits its second slide, advancing one step. The stroke of the slide is set by a threaded bracket and nut with a lock nut, which sets the letter spacing.
