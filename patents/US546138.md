## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typebar machines were limited in speed because types collided, the carriage lagged and the stroke was heavy.

**Solution:** A fast plunging-stroke typebar machine with a quick two-dog escapement, a type-head shape that cannot catch its neighbours and a free-turning line-space platen.

**How it works:** Typebars make a horizontal plunging blow at a common print point, passing through a hollow triangular guide-shifter whose apex slot gives alignment and whose raised positions select one of three characters on each type-head. The escapement has two dogs: one holds a slotted rack bar, the second displaces it as the bar advances and holds the carriage until the type begins to retreat, then the carriage jumps forward a letter space before the type is a quarter inch from the platen. The platen turns for one or more line spaces with a pawl that normally clears the ratchet, so the platen turns freely both ways without a check spring. The carriage rides on ball bearings.
