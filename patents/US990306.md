## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** In the Yost typebar machine, adjacent keys worked adjacent typebars that collided and interlocked, lower-case types exhausted their part of the ink pad, and 85 key actions were hard to fit in without strain or side binding.

**Solution:** Connect adjacent keys to widely separated typebars on opposite sides of the type circle, using two banks of bell cranks at different heights, long horizontal draw links and adjustable hangers so every action works in one plane.

**How it works:** Typebars are arranged in a circle on a type ring, resting their types against a central inking pad. Each key stem is hung on two swinging arms rather than sliding in guides, and pushes a flattened-tube actuating lever pivoted on a rod. A near-horizontal draw link joins the lever to a bell crank in a front or rear bank at different heights, and a vertical draw link joins the bell crank to the typebar driver. Hangers are rotatable and clamped between segmental plates so each bell crank can be turned into the plane of its links. Upper and lower case types are interspersed around the pad for even inking. A universal ring lifted by the typebar guide links rocks a dog rocker to work the escapement, and the space bar acts on the same rocker through a rod.
