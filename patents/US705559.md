## Summary

**Kind:** typebar machine · **Subject (patent classes):** Typewriters or selective printing mechanisms characterised by the mounting, arrangement or disposition of the types or dies with types or dies carried on sliding bars or rods on side surfaces thereof, e.g. fixed thereto the types or dies being movable relative to the bars or rods

**In the patent’s own words:** My invention relates to the paper-carriage, and has for its object to simplify andimprove the construction of said carriage, and particularly the means for shifting the same from the lower-case tothe upper-case position, or vice versa;

**How it works (reading):** A yoke that rides and pivots on the back rod carries the platen frame, and a transverse shift fork acts directly on the frame in line with the print point.

## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Earlier shifting carriages used a front shift rod with the shifting force applied at points that varied relative to the print point as the carriage traveled, hurting alignment and adding parts.

**Solution:** A yoke that rides and pivots on the back rod carries the platen frame, and a transverse shift fork acts directly on the frame in line with the print point.

**How it works:** A back rod serves as both hinge and guide. A wheeled yoke-bar travels along it and the platen-carrying frame is pivoted to the yoke, so the platen stays parallel to the rod while tilting between lower and upper case. A wheel at the front of the frame runs on a roll that turns when the platen shifts. A key (the space-key in the example) works a shifting fork through a rod; the fork acts on the back bar of the platen frame and rocks it to the upper-case position against an adjustable stop. A contractile spring returns the frame to lower case. In a variant the fork acts on the front of the frame.
