## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** On index typewriters with a plate or wheel moved to the character and struck by a hammer, the hammer stayed in the type slot until the print key rose, slowing writing.

**Solution:** A jointed arm and spring-returned plate let the hammer strike and withdraw at once when the print lever is pressed, regardless of how long the lever is held.

**How it works:** A hand lever travels across a character index and, through a segmental gear and a gear pivoted to the frame, swings a disc that carries flexibly mounted types; the disc is slotted to guide the hammer to the back of the chosen type. A separate print key lever presses a bent lever whose inner end rides up an incline on a plate attached to the hammer arm, throwing the hammer forward against the back of the type. At the end of the incline the plate drops into a channel and the spring returns the hammer immediately, freeing it from the slot so the index lever can move on. Paper is in a carriage moved by an ordinary ratchet and pawl.
