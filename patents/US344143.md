## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Wants a practical typewriter with as few parts as possible, at low cost, but with the essential capabilities of dearer machines.

**Solution:** A single-casting frame carries a hand-turned dial whose type disk is pushed down by hand to print, the same motion feeding the platen.

**How it works:** The frame is one casting of bed, end brackets and tie-bars. A platen with pawl-and-ratchet line spacing and a guide rod to hold the paper sit in the brackets. A feed rack-bar is pivoted between the tie-bars and carries a bracket whose hub holds a dial (or the bracket is itself a disk). The dial has characters near its periphery and is rotated to bring the wanted one to the printing field, then actuated by hand to give the impression. The impression motion also feeds the platen to a new printing field, with an inking mechanism.
