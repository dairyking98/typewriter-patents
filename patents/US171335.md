## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Not stated beyond improving typewriters; offers a different type-rod arrangement and spacing.

**Solution:** Type rods radiate from a common centre and are pushed obliquely then vertically onto the paper by key rods, and a central key releases a rack on each stroke.

**How it works:** Type rods stand in three concentric rows around a common centre, bent so they move obliquely down and then vertically onto the paper. Vertical key rods linked to the type rods return by springs. A central key depresses a tilting table lying under the key buttons, which rocks a shaft and a lever carrying a double pawl; it releases one tooth of the carriage rack per stroke. A spring draws the carriage forward and a cord pulls it back. A roller on a square journal shifts the paper line by line using a toothed wheel stepped by a pin on the carriage.
