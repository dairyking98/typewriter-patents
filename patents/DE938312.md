## Summary

**Kind:** typebar machine · **Subject (patent classes):** Type-lever actuating mechanisms

**How it works (reading):** Design the typebar gearing so its transmission ratio starts near zero, stays low, and then rises sharply (to about 4 times the mean) at the end of key travel.

## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typebar linkages give a hard, uneven keystroke because their transmission ratio over the key travel does not match the natural motion of the hand and finger.

**Solution:** Design the typebar gearing so its transmission ratio starts near zero, stays low, and then rises sharply (to about 4 times the mean) at the end of key travel.

**How it works:** The inventor analyses impact energy of the typebar and finger dynamics: the mass reduced to the key must be about 25 g so the finger impact is not felt, so the initial ratio must be about zero and only the key links move at first. The ratio curve oscillates around the mean ratio and the area under it equals the type path. Existing machines are criticised for too high an initial ratio, a ratio peaking mid-travel, and side forces from side levers. The text shown covers the theory and stops before the actual lever construction.
