## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Earlier electromagnetic typewriters used one magnet and lever per character, making them complex and costly.

**Solution:** One type carrier carrying the characters in rows is moved horizontally and vertically by a few electromagnets, then struck by a hammer driven by a printing magnet, all controlled by key circuit-controllers.

**How it works:** The characters are arranged in horizontal rows on a type carrier that moves in a substantially horizontal path, and also in a vertical or axial path, to put the wanted character at the hammer. Movement is made by mechanism positively driven by electromagnets, including elevating and pin magnets and a mechanism to revolve the carrier. Circuit-controllers operated by the character keys energise these magnets and the printing magnet, whose hammer or striker makes the impression. Carriage feed and line spacing are also provided. The device serves as an office typewriter or as a transmitter or receiver in a typewriter intercommunication system.

**Features:** [Multi-row type cylinder](../features/elem-cylinder-multirow.md), [Full keyboard, a key per character](../features/sel-keyboard-full.md), [Electric drive](../features/imp-electric.md), [Hammer behind the paper](../features/imp-hammer-behind.md), [Printing telegraph](../features/use-telegraph-printer.md), [Pawl-and-rack escapement](../features/esc-pawl-rack.md), [Line spacing](../features/pap-line-space.md)

**Parts:** type carrier; printing magnet; hammer; elevating magnet; pin magnet; circuit controller; character key; carriage; feeding mechanism; line-spacing mechanism

**Improves on:**

- electromagnetic typewriters with a magnet per character: one carrier moved by a few magnets replaces a magnet and lever per letter
