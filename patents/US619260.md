## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** A typist must space twice after a word, usually by a separate space-bar action.

**Solution:** A loose dog on a sliding escapement plate has two stops, so a longer rocking-lever stroke permits a double-tooth step in the same action as the last letter.

**How it works:** The escapement wheel has a fixed dog and a loose dog both mounted on a plate that slides with the rocking escapement lever. The loose dog moves sideways under spring pressure to a first stop J for a single space. If the lever stroke is long enough, the plate strikes a trip that lifts stop J, and the dog goes on to stop K for two teeth. The fixed dog is adjustable in a slot for wear. An alternative linkage between keys and escapement is shown in Fig. 9.

**Features:** [ESC:escape-wheel](../features/esc-escape-wheel.md), [ESC:pawl-rack](../features/esc-pawl-rack.md), [ESC:print-and-space](../features/esc-print-and-space.md), [ESC:variable-spacing](../features/esc-variable-spacing.md), [FRM:adjustment-wear](../features/frm-adjustment-wear.md), [ELEM:typebar](../features/elem-typebar.md)

**Parts:** escapement wheel; fixed dog; loose dog; sliding plate; rocking escapement lever; stop J; stop K; spring; spacing lever; rack

**Improves on:**

- conventional fixed/loose dog escapement: single movement gives one or two spaces
