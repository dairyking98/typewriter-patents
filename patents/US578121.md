## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Remington-type typebar machines had a monolithic frame that was lost on breakage, a heavy sliding shift frame, and fiddly ribbon, platen and carriage arrangements.

**Solution:** A simplified Remington-type typewriter with a sectional frame, a rocking-roller-frame shift, tilting platen, adjustable keyboard lock and automatic ribbon reverse with lateral shift.

**How it works:** The frame is cast in three separable parts (front, body, rear) screwed together so a broken part can be replaced. Typebars are hung two per sheet-metal hanger on cone-pointed journals, one end adjustable by a threaded nut to take up wear. Case shift is done by rocking the platen carriage on its front guide rod via a shift key and a spring-loaded lever with shoulders that hold either position, instead of a sliding frame. Ribbon spools are driven by bevel gears from a shaft that slides endwise when pressure rollers on a rocking frame sense a full spool, reversing the ribbon, and a ratchet-and-cam advances the spools sideways at each reversal so the whole ribbon width is used. The platen is detachable and can be tilted by a key to inspect the work, returning and locking automatically; guide springs and a small roll let writing run to the paper's lower edge. Margin stops with rack and spring pawl, a bell stop and a column jump-feed are included.
