## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** An earlier addressing machine by Dart rode on a fixed supporting frame, so the length of a printed line was limited by the frame length and the machine had to be lifted and reset.

**Solution:** A wheeled carriage carries a spring-lifted frame with a rubber type drum turned by a dial knob and geared to it, and each press of the knob prints a character and rolls the machine forward one step along the surface being marked.

**How it works:** The carriage runs on two side wheels and a steerable rear roller, and carries a vertically sprung frame with a type drum on a cross axle. A plunger shaft with a handle knob and a dial lettered like the drum turns through a pair of gears to the drum, so when the dial character sits under a fixed pointer, the matching type sits at the bottom. A circular ink pad on a spring arm rolls against the type as the drum turns. Pressing the knob lowers the frame, the drum's rim hole slips over a fixed post to lock it, and the type prints through an apertured spring guard plate that shields the neighbours. As the frame rises, a spring-loaded triangular pallet on the frame cams a pin on a side wheel forward to roll the machine one character space, with a click stopping any backslip; turning the offset rear roller makes the line print on an arc.
