## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Violent operation of the line-space lever shocks the machine, and the line-space wheel can overthrow, so line spacing is neither gentle nor accurate nor adjustable.

**Solution:** Connect the platen to the loose line-space wheel through spring-loaded bell cranks and an adjustable eccentric abutment, with an overthrow stop on the driving pawl.

**How it works:** The line-space wheel turns loose on the platen axle, driven by a pawl on a slide worked from the line-space lever. A stop on the wheel pushes one arm of a bell crank pivoted on an arm fixed to the platen axle; a spring between the bell crank and the fixed arm then pulls the platen around after the wheel instead of the wheel jerking it. The platen catches up and its arm comes to rest against an abutment on the wheel. The abutment is an eccentric-headed screw, so turning it changes spring tension and the platen-to-wheel alignment, which also shifts the wear zone on the platen. An extension on the pawl rides under a projection of the shiftable frame at the end of its stroke so the wheel cannot overthrow, and a spring detent settles the wheel. The text, though headed Petermann/Corona, is Brown's Underwood patent.

**Features:** [Line spacing](../features/pap-line-space.md), [Roller platen](../features/pap-platen-roller.md), [Adjustment and wear take-up](../features/frm-adjustment-wear.md), [Shift by moving the platen](../features/shift-platen.md)

**Parts:** platen; platen axle; line-space wheel; driving pawl; pawl slide; line-space lever; bell crank; platen-driving spring; fixed arm; eccentric abutment screw; overthrow stop; spring detent; shiftable platen frame

**Numbers:** two symmetrical springs and bell cranks

**Improves on:**

- Underwood line-spacing mechanism with rigid wheel-to-platen drive: a spring coupling absorbs shock and an adjustable abutment aligns the platen
