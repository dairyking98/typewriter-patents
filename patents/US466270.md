## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Earlier flexible-plate machines made one hand do both positioning and printing.

**Solution:** A flexible sector-shaped type plate on a pivoted telescopic arm positioned by one hand and a plunger pressing the type, worked by the other.

**How it works:** The type-plate is a diaphragm in a rigid sector frame with type in two curved lines (lower and upper case). The arm shank slides in a guide pivoted on the carriage, a spring stop engaging recesses to hold a type line concentric with the pivot. A box carriage with a bottom partition and a printing opening only lets the selected type touch the paper. The impression plunger works the type down and feeds the carriage on a rack and track over the paper.

**Features:** [ELEM:sector-segment](../features/elem-sector-segment.md), [ELEM:rubber-type](../features/elem-rubber-type.md), [SEL:arc-arm](../features/sel-arc-arm.md), [SEL:selector-locking](../features/sel-selector-locking.md), [IMP:press-through](../features/imp-press-through.md), [IMP:print-lever](../features/imp-print-lever.md), [ESC:moving-print-head](../features/esc-moving-print-head.md), [ESC:print-and-space](../features/esc-print-and-space.md), [PAP:flat-bed](../features/pap-flat-bed.md)

**Parts:** carriage; type plate; frame; arm shank; guide; spring stop; plunger; bottom plate; printing opening; rack; track

**Numbers:** two curved type lines

**Improves on:**

- earlier flexible type-plate machines: split the work between two hands
