## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** Typewriters need to carry several alphabets plus figures on one letter bar, print capitals and small letters alternately, and let the operator reach the paper to insert it or correct.

**Solution:** A single letter bar carries two or more alphabet rows that a thumb lever shifts into printing position, while the whole printing mechanism with its felt ink rollers hinges up away from the paper roll.

**How it works:** The handle e is pressed down guided by grooves in the front of letter register d, printing the letter on the paper roll c. The same stroke pushes a lever g that advances the carriage one tooth of rack i, and word spaces are made by pressing lever g directly. A lever n acts through an angle lever and spring on a three-armed piece to shift roller t carrying the letter bar v, so the small alphabet (row VI) prints at rest and the capitals (row VII) when the lever is held down. Small felt-covered ink rollers press on the letter bar and are moved sideways to ink the rows as the bar travels. The entire printing unit tips back on hinges, secured by a snap spring, to bare the roll. Line feed is by a handle with a hook that turns the roller one tooth per stroke.
