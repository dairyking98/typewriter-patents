## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Automatic ribbon reverse by shifting the spool drive shaft was either too slow to engage (thread-tooth variants) or compressed into too short a time with high force (wedge thumb), so the typist felt it.

**Solution:** A cam disc with a single full-circumference screw pitch on the drive shaft is engaged by a feeler-lever-driven intermediate lever and moves the shaft a little more than half its travel in one revolution, with very little force.

**How it works:** Each ribbon spool on a fixed axle is driven through a toothed ring and gear from an axially shiftable main shaft via bevel gears. A spring-pressed feeler lever rests on the ribbon winding. When one spool runs out, its feeler lever moves a right-angled arm and a double lever whose other arm carries a nose. The nose moves into a helical curve on the cam disc on the drive shaft, which then pushes the shaft sideways, so the other spool is driven. A weak spring holds the double lever in rest, and an adjustable coupling sets the feeler spring just strong enough to overcome it.

**Features:**

**Parts:** ribbon spool; spool axle; feeler lever; spring; coupling half; double lever; cam disc; helical curve; drive shaft; bevel gear; bearing plate

**Improves on:**

- thread-tooth shaft shifting reverse: smooth screw cam avoids jumping and sudden shift
- wedge thumb shaft shifting reverse: longer switching stroke with less force
