## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** With carriage shifting the shift movement disturbs the other drives; complex linkages carry motion from frame to carriage for shifting, ribbon lift and key lock.

**Solution:** Every drive from frame to carriage works by loose pressure contact on surfaces running in the shift direction, so the carriage shifts freely and can be lifted out without disconnecting anything.

**How it works:** Keys act through parallel links; a lower bell crank link drives the type lever and the universal rail on side arms on a shared axis. That axis carries the locking arm, the ribbon-lift arm and ribbon-travel arm, and a shift arm that pushes a pin on a fixed shift tooth, which drags a loose shift tooth to switch. The space key acts on the same rail. An accent key swings the shift arm sideways by a cam so the ribbon lifts and moves but the carriage does not shift. Working surfaces are inclined at angle alpha matching the shift direction, with dead motion to absorb small path differences.
