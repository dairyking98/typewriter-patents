## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** The Hammond shuttle carrying all rows of type is heavy to move for every impression, and hammer blows vary when the carriage steps in proportion to character width.

**Solution:** A separate small shuttle for each row of type, with only the shuttle in the impression line moved and the others locked, plus a hammer giving uniform blows.

**How it works:** A non-rotating vertical shaft carries the anvil cylinder, which has a circumferential slot for each shuttle. A shuttle operator on the shaft has a stop arm meeting key-operated stop pins and a rear arm that engages whichever shuttle is in the impression line, normally the upper one. Shift keys rock a bar that raises the shaft by a greater or less distance to bring a chosen shuttle to the impression line, as in the commercial Hammond. A locking device holds the other shuttles still.

**Features:** [ELEM:sector-segment](../features/elem-sector-segment.md), [ELEM:interchangeable](../features/elem-interchangeable.md), [SHIFT:axial](../features/shift-axial.md), [SHIFT:triple](../features/shift-triple.md), [IMP:hammer-behind](../features/imp-hammer-behind.md), [ESC:variable-spacing](../features/esc-variable-spacing.md), [SEL:keyboard-full](../features/sel-keyboard-full.md), [SEL:selector-locking](../features/sel-selector-locking.md)

**Parts:** shuttle; anvil cylinder; vertical shaft; shuttle operator; stop pin; key lever; shift key lever; pivoted bar; hammer

**Numbers:** generally three rows

**Improves on:**

- Hammond machine: one light shuttle per row instead of one shuttle carrying all rows
