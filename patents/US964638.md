## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** At the end of a line the carriage stops but the keys can still be struck, printing extra letters over the last one.

**Solution:** A pivoted locking bar, dropped by the margin bar when the carriage reaches the margin stop, intercepts steps on the universal bar so the key levers and typebars cannot move.

**How it works:** A curved channel-section universal bar is supported by rocker arms on two rock shafts so it moves rearward in parallel when any sub-lever is pressed, and also drives the escapement rocker. A locking bar is normally held out of the path of stops on the universal bar by an elbow lever. A carriage-mounted stop meets the inclined surface of an adjustable margin stop on the margin bar: first a shallow incline rings the bell (lost motion keeps the lock off), then a deeper incline tilts the margin bar enough to release the elbow lever. The locking bar then drops by gravity onto the steps and blocks the universal bar, locking the sub-levers, typebars and keys. A key raises the margin bar to release the lock and print beyond the margin.
