## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Carriage escapement releases only after the typebar has printed, which has numerous disadvantages; operators differ on preference.

**Solution:** A dog assembly that can be adjusted by a cam lever to release the carriage either before the impression or after it.

**How it works:** On a Remington, a universal bar rocks an arm that carries an upright dog-holder pivoted on trunnions, returned by a volute spring. The holder carries a two-part spring-closed dog, a fixed dog, and a third pivoted dog, all working on the feed rack. A cam lever with two flat faces on a bracket pushes against the back of the dog-holder to shift it and the dogs relative to the rack. One setting gives the old plan, with release after the type-bar prints. The other gives the new plan, in which the carriage is released while the typebar is moving and printing occurs as the carriage travels from one notch to the next.
