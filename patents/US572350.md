## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typebar machines are heavy, costly, noisy and impact-printing hurts the ribbon; the inventor wants a light, cheap sheet-steel machine with uniform, noiseless impression, many characters in a small type field, and variable letter spacing.

**Solution:** All the types sit as sliding stocks in one type-mount that is moved in its own plane to bring the wanted type to a fixed print point, where a toggle-jointed plunger pushes it against an adjustable platen with gradual pressure, in one key stroke that also sets and releases a variable-space escapement.

**How it works:** Each key is a bell crank joined to a wedge slide; its stroke first moves two transfer bars through adjustable wedges, which swing the type-mount horizontally and vertically (an oblique resultant) until the chosen type stands at the fixed print point. The same wedges set a variable-space pawl for wide, medium or narrow letters. Further key travel presses down a pivoted key-plate that trips the carriage escapement and, through a lever and rod, collapses a toggle joint that drives a plunger against the end of the type stock, pressing it through a guide orifice onto the paper and platen; the platen is screw-adjustable to change the impression for manifolding. Double-ended type cylinders can be turned over with a hook, giving 84 or 96 characters from 42 or 48 stocks. The plunger's back stroke also winds the ribbon between spools. The carriage is spring-driven through a rack, pinion and ratchet escapement and is hung on a parallel-motion linkage.
