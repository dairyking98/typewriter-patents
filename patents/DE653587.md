## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** When the platen carriage is moved toward or away from the type to set impression strength or copy count, the line-height pointer and ribbon fork that travel with it change their clearance to the type guide and can jam in the narrow space.

**Solution:** Mount the line-height pointer and ribbon lifter on parts independent of the in-and-out adjustment of the platen carriage, so they keep a constant distance from the type guide.

**How it works:** The platen sits on a shift part that is raised for capitals by a pivoting rail acted on by a roller. The carriage rides on two runner bars, and the rear bar is on pivoting support arms, tilted by an eccentric from the front so the whole carriage moves toward or away from the type; the wide rollers slide sideways on the shift rail and front bar. The line-height pointer is carried on support rods and an intermediate frame, moved by angle levers from the shift rail and guided by rollers on the machine frame, so it ignores the carriage adjustment. The ribbon lifter and fork are linked likewise to the shift rail and guided by rails on the type guide, and the ribbon lifts at each type stroke.
