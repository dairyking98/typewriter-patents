## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Wheel typewriters need a way to stop a continuously spinning type wheel at the chosen character and strike the paper with it.

**Solution:** A treadle-driven spinning type wheel is stopped by a keyed pin that closes an electric circuit through a spinning contact flipper, and an electromagnet then pulls the wheel on its tilting joint against the paper.

**How it works:** A treadle and belt keep a compound shaft, a universal joint and the type wheel rotating at a steady speed. Pressing a key lever pushes up one spring-returned pin in a non-rotating casing that surrounds the shaft. A resilient insulated flipper on the shaft strikes that pin, which closes a battery circuit through brushes, the pin, the casing and the shaft to an electromagnet. The magnet attracts a soft-iron member on the wheel standard, which has a hollow face for it. That stops the rotation at the exact angle and tips the wheel on the ball-and-cup joint to strike the paper roll. A spring then returns the wheel when the magnet is released. A second key lever raises the casing and the sliding shaft section to bring capitals or figures into line.
