## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** The roller platen of a Japanese typewriter (written in vertical columns) must be fed one column-pitch at a time exactly, without the toothed disc running on by momentum, and the feed should be workable from either a hand lever or the print lever.

**Solution:** A spring-returned swinging frame carries a pitch-setting pawl slide and a locking catch, so each swing feeds the platen gear by a set number of teeth and the catch stops the gear dead at the end of the feed.

**How it works:** The platen shaft carries a disc with a pawl that engages a toothed ring on a loose gear disc; a release plate withdraws the pawl so the platen can be turned freely by its knobs. A swinging frame sits on the shaft and can be driven by a hand feed lever or, through a sliding sleeve and bent arm, by the print starting lever. On the swing a catch tooth meshes the gear and holds it while the pawl slide stretches back; at the end of the swing a pin and bulged slot rock the catch clear. A contraction spring then returns the frame, the slide pawl turns the gear by the allowed number of teeth, and the catch re-engages to stop it exactly. The slide travel, and so the feed pitch, is set by a polygonal adjuster on an eccentric spindle turned by a knob.

## Description (English translation of the Japanese text, opening)

> Specification. Paper-cylinder vertical-feed device for a Japanese typewriter. Nature and object of the invention. This invention concerns a paper-cylinder vertical-feed device for a Japanese typewriter. It has a swinging frame connected separately to a cylinder vertical-feed starting lever and to a printing starting lever, so that it can be swung by either. At the upper part of its rear end a pawl-carrying slide is held by a pin, a slot and a spring, with a suitable amount of free play. At the lower part of the frame's end a catch with a locking tooth is fitted. Worked by a pin fixed on the cylinder support frame, a slot with a bulge and a spring, the catch meshes with the gear while the frame swings, holding the gear still while letting the pawl slide fall back. It moves to a clear position away from the gear as soon as the swing ends, and meshes with the gear again as soon as the frame returns, so that the toothed disc cannot overrun by its momentum. The frame is also drawn by a contraction spring against the fixed part of the main frame, so that it always tends to return.
