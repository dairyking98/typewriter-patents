## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** The roller bearings that carry a typewriter platen carriage need their rollers kept apart and correctly placed along the rails, and prone to slipping so the carriage ends up unsupported.

**Solution:** Rollers with reduced ends are held by a slotted separator plate whose slot edges engage the shoulders, with two rollers crossed at right angles per slot so they bear on all four faces of the square rail channel, and a tongue and stops keep the separator at half the carriage travel.

**How it works:** Two pairs of grooved rails, one pair on the frame and one on the carriage, form a square-section channel. Rollers with reduced ends and shoulders run in it. A separator plate with lugs reaching into the channel has slots; the slot edges enter the shoulder angles on opposite sides of each roller axis, and rollers are sprung in and retained. Each slot holds two rollers with axes at right angles in parallel planes, so one roller bears on one pair of opposite channel faces and the other roller on the other pair, holding the rails in correct relation. The separator should travel half the carriage travel; a tongue on it meets two stops on the frame rail to correct any slip.
