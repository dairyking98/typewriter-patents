## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** High-grade keyboard typewriters have many parts and adjustments; the inventor wants a type-wheel machine with few parts that is automatic and positive, with an even impression and little wheel momentum.

**Solution:** A full keyboard turns a three-row type-wheel through a rock-shaft and segment-gear linkage, locks it with a notched wheel and pawl, then releases a spring-driven hammer so every blow has the same force.

**How it works:** Each key lever first engages the underside of twisted (inversely inclined) rocking bars, so the key's position rocks a shaft and a segmental rack that turns a pinion and the type-wheel to that character. A separate key lever (shift) raises or lowers the wheel shaft to bring the capital, lower-case or figure row to the print point. Late in the key stroke a catch frees a spring-loaded hammer shaft, whose locking arm first enters a notch in a notched wheel on the wheel shaft to hold the wheel rigid, and then the hammer, with a rubber pounding face, strikes the paper against the type. A centering spring returns the wheel to its middle position so it never turns more than half a revolution. Releasing the key retracts the hammer and a gravity pawl on a spring-driven carriage ratchet advances the carriage one notch. The ribbon feeds on two spools with a U-shaped lever sensing spool fill to reverse the feed automatically, and a bell on the adjustable hammer stop warns at line end.
