## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Improve the single-index-lever class of typewriter, where one lever swung on a pivot turns a head to bring the type into position, with exact centring, automatic spacing and a case change.

**Solution:** A circular head carrying spring-held two-character type bars is turned by an index lever through a toothed segment and gear; pressing the lever down at the chosen letter in a V-notched rack centres it, strikes the bar at the print point and indexes the carriage one space.

**How it works:** The index lever moves horizontally over a semicircular index plate, turning a toothed segment that meshes with a gear under the rotatable type-bar head, so the chosen bar's foot comes under an overreaching arm. Pressing the lever down makes its wedge seat in a V-notch of a rack at that letter, centring it, and through rods and levers pushes that arm down on the bar's foot so the type strikes the paper roll. The same motion tips a pawl lever that, on release of the lever spring, steps the carriage one tooth along a ratchet bar. A blank place on the index prints nothing but spaces. Each bar carries lower and upper case; a key rocks a shaft to lift the paper-holding rolls so the upper letter prints, and a swivel post holds it for all capitals. Ink rolls at both sides of the hood ink the bars as the head turns.
