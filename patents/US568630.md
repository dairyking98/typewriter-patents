## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Typebar machines of the time were tall, bulky and hard to inspect or correct; their key levers, type-bar pivots and platen needed to be simpler, more compact and more accessible.

**Solution:** A low typebar machine with elliptically arranged typebar pivots, tubular sheet-metal key levers on fulcrum posts, a hinged top frame for access, and a platen paired with a swing-up paper clamp so the line being typed can be viewed.

**How it works:** Typebars of varying length hang from slotted clips in an elliptical opening, and spring-armed bifurcated rear ends grip conical pivot arms so wear is taken up. Each folded-tube key lever rocks on a shouldered post passing through it, with a push-bar linking to its typebar, giving every key identical leverage. A felt-faced board under all key levers swings a rack bar, so any key or the space key advances the carriage one tooth between a rigid dog and a spring dog, driven by a spring-case and strap. A spring-loaded shield clamps the paper to the platen and can be flipped up with a finger-piece to show the line, a spring roller and apron returning it. A hand lever line-spaces by one or two ratchet teeth and also trips the dogs for carriage return. The inking sheet winds transversely between two rolls, advanced at each line space, and a bell sounds near the line end.
