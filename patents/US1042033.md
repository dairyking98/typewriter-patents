## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A power-driven carriage needs a simple back-space device, easily fitted to existing machines.

**Solution:** A key-operated dog lever engages the escapement pinion and then a pin swings the lever and its link to turn the pinion back one tooth.

**How it works:** The carriage is pulled by a spring barrel and strap; its rack meshes with a pinion linked by pawls to the escapement wheel, so the carriage can run back freely. Pressing the back-space key rocks an arm, pulling a link that rocks a dog lever about its pivot, so its dog enters a tooth of the pinion. A pin on the lever then strikes a slot edge in a spring-held pivoted link, and lever and link swing together, turning the pinion back one tooth and moving the carriage back one letter space. An extension of the lever engages a tooth of the escapement wheel to lock it against backlash. Springs restore everything on release.
