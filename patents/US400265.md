## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Typebars flex or wobble at their pivots, so the type lands out of line at the print point; fitting and aligning each type on its bar is also slow.

**Solution:** A fixed locking plate with slotted ribs close to the print point catches and aligns the typebars as they arrive, with several bent bars sharing one slot, plus soldered adjustable type plugs and a gravity-pawl-rotated ink disc.

**How it works:** Typebars are pivoted in brackets set in concentric grooves of a plate around the print point. A locking plate carries one or two ribs concentric with the print point, each cut with slots wide enough for one typebar; the rib edges are beveled so a slightly misaligned bar is guided into its slot. Because the plate sits near the print point, pivot looseness is not magnified at the type, and by bending neighbouring bars by different amounts three bars can share one slot. Each type plug with a roughened shank sits loosely in a slotted block on the bar, is adjusted for position and vertical alignment, and then fixed by pouring molten solder down the sides of the bar. The inking disc is pushed down by the arriving type and a gravity pawl follows it back, turning the disc a little each stroke to present a fresh surface.
