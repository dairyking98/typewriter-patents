## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Earlier lateral carriage feeds drove the paper cylinder with a strong wound spring, which made it hard to stop or reverse the feed and sometimes flew back and broke parts.

**Solution:** Move the cylinder carriage along a fixed saw-tooth rack by a pair of opposed spring-loaded feed pawls, one of which a tilting presser can idle to pick the direction, so the carriage advances one set pitch per print stroke with no stored spring drive.

**How it works:** The cylinder carriage runs on balls on the machine frame. On its back wall two opposed bell-crank arms each carry a feed pawl that engages a fixed saw-tooth rack, with a spring pulling the arms together. A T-shaped presser block pressed down by the print-lever linkage (or by hand through a striker) holds the arms apart; when it rises the arms close and the active pawl bites the rack, and when it falls the arms are pushed outward and the pawl slides along the saw teeth, shifting the whole carriage one pitch. A tilting presser with a hand knob holds one pawl idle so the other drives, setting direction, and in the neutral position holds both idle so the carriage stops. A raisable plate with sloping sides limits how far the arms can swing, so a scale on its handle sets the feed pitch in rack teeth. A locking tooth on the block holds the carriage still when the block is down.

## Description (English translation of the Japanese text, opening)

> Specification. Paper-cylinder lateral feed device for a Japanese-text typewriter. Nature and object of the invention. This invention relates to a paper-cylinder lateral feed device for a Japanese-text typewriter, made up as follows. At about the middle of the rear wall of the cylinder carriage frame (紙筒支持框), which holds the paper cylinder and is carried on the machine frame so that it can move sideways, two bell-crank swinging arms are set facing each other in opposite positions; each carries, free to rise, a feed pawl that can engage a fixed rack on the machine frame. Springs always urge the two arms to swing toward each other. In the middle between the two arms a tilting presser is pivoted, which can hold either arm, together with its pawl, in an idle position, alternately one or the other. When the presser is tipped to the left or right, the arm and pawl on the side to which it is tipped are kept idle, out of the rack, and the action of the arm and pawl on the other side moves the whole carriage frame in the direction in which the presser is tipped.
