## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** The specification does not state a problem outright, but it describes the difficulty of a movable type-block machine: the block's momentum swings it past the desired point and ruins alignment and spacing.

**Solution:** A single convex type-block carrying all the characters is tilted in two planes by two pivoted frames, each key-lever with a specially shaped lower edge acting on both frames so the chosen type lands under the print point, and then pressing the whole block down onto the paper.

**How it works:** A convex-faced type-block hangs from a swinging frame under a pair of nested oscillating frames, one swinging it fore-and-aft and the other sideways, both carried on arms pivoted on a rear cross-shaft. Each key-lever rests on the front rods of these frames and its lower edge is shaped so the downward stroke first tilts the block one way, then the other, until the right type is in the vertical line, and further travel presses the whole block down to the paper. Pins on the frames drop into narrowing notches on a fixed segment, which correct any overswing of the block and so align it. The same arm movement drives a double pawl on a ratchet bar to space the carriage one tooth after each letter, a central blank spot on the block giving a space key, and a spring-and-cam drive pulls the carriage at constant force with a bell near the end of travel. A ribbon between two spools on a shaft that shifts to connect either spool, driven by a ratchet from the arm movement, is led round oblique wires, and the paper feeds between a plain roller and a corrugated roller on a swinging frame.
