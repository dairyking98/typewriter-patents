## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** General improvement of a typebar typewriter with a shifting platen: simpler key-to-typebar linkage, visible writing, a gentler carriage stop, and a platen that can be removed with its sheet.

**Solution:** A typebar machine whose platen roller is lifted independently of the carriage by shift keys, with a ribbon guide that follows the platen, a three-tooth feed dog, and a removable, self-locking platen.

**How it works:** Keys act through bell-crank levers on typebars pivoted near one end, so a short key stroke gives a long type swing. Each typebar heel pushes a universal bar that rocks the feed-dog support; a slidable tooth between two fixed teeth lets the carriage step one space per stroke from a mainspring drum. Two shift keys raise a track under the platen frame (one momentary, one latched and released by the other) so each typebar can print two characters. The ribbon guide rides with the platen track and also lifts to the print line from the feed-dog motion, otherwise sitting below the line to keep the writing visible. A bell and a key lock near the line end work off lips on the carriage, and a spring-buffered stop arrests the carriage when it is released.
