## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Japanese-script typewriters with many loose types are heavy and awkward to operate; the inventor wants a simple, light-working machine that picks out the selected type easily.

**Solution:** An endless chain of type frames, each one piece with an index-character plate; the frame tilted at the chain corner opens its lid so the wanted type can be pushed up, gripped and printed.

**How it works:** U-section type frames, each bent out into an index plate, are linked by screw rods and links into an endless chain on sprockets, with rollers running in rails; a hand wheel turns the chain. The operator slides a holding frame left-right with a pressing lever to pick the character at the tilted corner. Pressing the lever grip swings a rocking lever that drives a push-up rod and piece into a hole in the bottom of the frame, lifting the type out. In the same stroke a bent lever and triangular plate close the presser frame of the 'type bar' on the type, then swing the whole type bar to the paper cylinder to print. Releasing the lever reverses everything and returns the type to its frame; an ink pad inks the type.
