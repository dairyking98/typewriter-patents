## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** In Blickensderfer's earlier power carriage drive, one swinging block served both directions, so operating one key strained the other key's linkage and could prevent the friction wheels engaging properly; the carriage also needed a way back a space when overshot.

**Solution:** Give each carriage running direction its own independent swinging block and key linkage, tie the column-stop key into the leftward drive, and add a hand-slide pawl-and-ratchet to jog the carriage back one space at a time.

**How it works:** A rack on the carriage meshes a gear on a vertical shaft whose bevel gear sits between two bevel gears, each fast with a friction wheel. A constantly running motor turns two friction disks; a Right or Left key swings its own block to press one friction wheel against its disk, running the carriage continuously in that direction. A spring between the blocks returns both to neutral. The Col key positions a column-stop arm to be struck by stops on a notched bar on the carriage, and also rocks the left block so the carriage runs to the stop. Normal letter spacing is a cam-driven toothed lever that engages the rack and advances it one space, leaving the carriage free otherwise. A key-operated slide with a pawl on a ratchet disk on the vertical shaft jogs the carriage back one or more spaces, with a locking projection preventing overthrow.
