## Summary

**Kind:** index machine · **Subject (patent classes):** Typewriters or selective printing mechanisms characterised by the mounting, arrangement or disposition of the types or dies with types or dies mounted on endless bands or the like; Others

**In the English translation of the patent’s own words:** This invention relates to a Japanese typewriter in which endless type belts set side by side are turned in step with the in-and-out movement of a key lever that moves left and right, and by working the key lever a type belt is moved and struck on the paper cylinder to print.

**How it works (reading):** Rubber endless type belts, one per row, are shifted by a single key lever that also slides on an operating box to choose the row; pressing the lever strikes the chosen character on the cylinder, takes up belt slack and advances the paper.

## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Existing Japanese typewriters are heavy, bulky and slow, and types can work loose; a light, small, portable, cheap machine was wanted.

**Solution:** Rubber endless type belts, one per row, are shifted by a single key lever that also slides on an operating box to choose the row; pressing the lever strikes the chosen character on the cylinder, takes up belt slack and advances the paper.

**How it works:** Endless rubber belts bearing a line of types run side by side over a rear drum and a roller on each print lever. Moving the key lever in and out turns a toothed shaft through a rack and drives the drum, moving all belts, while sliding the lever left and right moves the operating box to select a belt. A pointer on a toothed plate follows both motions over a pull-out index chart showing the same characters. Pressing the lever down lifts the print lever so the belt touches an ink pad and strikes the paper cylinder, while a bending bar presses the belt to keep it taut and a pawl plate readies the ratchet. On release a spring returns everything and the cylinder advances one tooth.
