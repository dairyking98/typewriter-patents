## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** On power-driven Blickensderfer wheel machines the key stays engaged until it returns, so the operator must wait, and the constant return spring is at full tension just when the type-wheel must deliver its soft momentum blow.

**Solution:** Per-key connectors that are released by the machine before the key rises, plus a returning lever that rides a circular holding surface during the impression so the return spring does not act until the wheel rebounds.

**How it works:** A constantly running gear drives a clutch that is engaged by a slide-bar when any key rocks a common shaft through that key's sliding connector. The driver moves an actuator bar, which rocks the action-shaft that turns and then swings the type-wheel to the paper through sectors, tumblers and a pinion. As the lever moves, a lifter on it raises the connector off the key-lever so the common shaft can return and another key can be struck before the first key has risen. For the return, an arm on the action-shaft has a returning surface and a circular holding surface concentric with the shaft. A spring-loaded two-armed lever with a roller is carried on a support that moves with the actuator, so the spring is slack during the stroke and the roller runs on the holding surface while the wheel hits the paper. After the rebound the spring is fully tensioned and the roller acts on the returning surface to throw the wheel back positively to its start.
