## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** The sideways feed of a Japanese typewriter's paper-support frame needs a simple, sure ratchet that can change its step between two lengths.

**Solution:** Two working levers linked by a guide rod rise and fall to stop the ratchet wheel's teeth alternately, and one lever can be held or slid along the rod to give a full or half step.

**How it works:** A spring-pulled rack on the paper-support frame always tends to turn a ratchet gear and wheel. When the sideways-feed hand lever raises a working piece, its projection enters between ratchet teeth and stops the wheel after a small gap; at the same time a second working lever, linked on the curved guide rod, lifts its step out of the teeth. On release the first lever drops out and the second drops in, so the wheel advances a second gap before being stopped again; the two gaps together are the full step. Turning a stop piece by hand so its fork holds the second lever stops it sliding to the side, so the wheel advances only half the distance. An adjusting nut on the rod sets the sliding distance and so the step.
