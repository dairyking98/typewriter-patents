## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Pointer-and-chart type cylinder typewriters of this kind are bulky; the inventor wants a particularly narrow machine built simply.

**Solution:** Mount the character chart and the type cylinder together on one frame that oscillates about an axis parallel to the cylinder axis, so pressing the pointer down swings the whole frame and strikes the cylinder at the platen.

**How it works:** A type cylinder turns and slides on an axis carried in a frame that pivots about a second parallel axis above the platen and is held about 1 cm clear by a spring. The operator sets a pin (pointer) over a character field on the frame. Cords from the pin run to a rack that engages teeth coupled to the cylinder; the rack is spring-tensioned so the cords stay taut, and its motion rotates and axially displaces the cylinder to the chosen character. A guide wheel with radial notches locks the cylinder position precisely. Pushing the pin down to the stop swings the whole frame down so the preset cylinder strikes the paper on the platen.
