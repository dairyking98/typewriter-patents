## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A Japanese typewriter needs hundreds of characters but should be typed nearly as fast as a Western machine, with the most common kana and kanji printed by one plain keystroke.

**Solution:** A polygon-shaped wheel carries sets of multi-type typebars and is turned by a handle to bring the wanted set to the keys, while the key also drops the paper cylinder to pick the row of type on the bar.

**How it works:** Typebars are pivoted round the rim of a turnable polygonal wheel, one set per side. A handle with a pointer, linked by bevel gears, turns the wheel to position 1, 2, 3 or 4, and a spring detent pin in side recesses holds it. The most common characters sit in the first position and are printed directly with the keys like a Western machine. A key lever pulls a link and arm that raise the bar of the set in front of the keys to the paper cylinder. Each bar carries four rows of types; the keys of the third to sixth rows also press a universal crank that pulls the spring-lifted cylinder frame down by one or more type pitches so a lower row prints. The cylinder slides sideways rather than turning and the characters print sideways, reading vertically on the sheet. An arm swings an ink pad over the type faces with each stroke.

## Description (English translation of the Japanese text, opening)

> Specification. Typewriter for both Japanese and English text. Nature and object of the invention. This invention relates to a typewriter that has a paper cylinder, typebars, a keyboard and so on, like those of the ordinary Western typewriter most used today. The typebars are mounted in a polygon round a wheel that can turn about a fixed shaft, one set making one side. The wheel is turned by a handle or by a push button to bring the set of typebars that has the wanted character into the printing position, and printing is done by pressing a key. Its object is to print a large number of type characters quickly, not much differently from an ordinary Western typewriter. Brief description of the drawings.
