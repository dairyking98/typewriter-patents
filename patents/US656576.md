## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typebar machines need hard, uniform finger blows, which tires the operator and makes print weight depend on touch and on which finger is used.

**Solution:** A key only connects its typebar to a single common motor (an electromagnet-driven frame), and the motor, not the finger, throws the typebar so print force is uniform.

**How it works:** Each key pulls a cord and bell-crank that thrusts a swinging arm on the typebar lever in under a light vibratory motor frame. The same key press also depresses a rocking rail under all keys that closes a battery circuit through one electromagnet. The magnet pulls the motor frame down, which presses the engaged arm and lever, and a pull-down wire throws the typebar to the platen. On release the circuit opens, springs return the frame and the arm is withdrawn. Spacing is left to any known mechanism, optionally a second magnet in the same circuit.

**Features:** [ELEM:typebar](../features/elem-typebar.md), [IMP:electric](../features/imp-electric.md), [SEL:keyboard-full](../features/sel-keyboard-full.md), [IMP:element-to-platen](../features/imp-element-to-platen.md), [FRM:few-parts](../features/frm-few-parts.md)

**Parts:** key lever; swinging arm; bell-crank; flexible cord; motor frame; electromagnet; armature; rocking contact rail; battery; typebar; pull-down wire; felted stop bar

**Improves on:**

- Remington, Smith Premier and Caligraph typebar machines: print force no longer comes from the operator's finger
