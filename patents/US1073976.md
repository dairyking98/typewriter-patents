## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** A music typewriter must print all notes and symbols in two sizes, letters, and note stems of correct length on, above or below the staff.

**Solution:** A 33-key piano-type keyboard raises the paper carriage by degrees via a toothed-segment divider and a stepped cylinder, while typebars print the notes.

**How it works:** The paper carriage runs on racks that rise vertically, geared to a transverse shaft. 33 key levers (4 octaves and 4 degrees, 11 below, 9 on, 13 above the staff) have toothed segments with 0 to 32 teeth that engage a fluted pinion shaft, turning it in proportion to the key. Through bevel gears this rotates a divider, a cylinder with 33 steps of 1 mm depth, whose steps arrest a sliding rack geared to the carriage shaft, so the paper is raised by 1 mm per degree to the right height. Typebars carry the notes, with special bars and arresting devices for notes outside the staff and for stem length.

**Features:**

- **Selection:** [Full keyboard, a key per character](../features/sel-keyboard-full.md), [Keys drive the element by racks or gears](../features/sel-differential-drive.md)
- **Type element:** [Typebars](../features/elem-typebar.md)
- **Uses:** [Special scripts and characters](../features/use-special-script.md)

**Parts:** paper carriage; rack; key lever; toothed segment; fluted pinion shaft; bevel pinion; stepped divider cylinder; sliding rack; typebar

**Numbers:** 33 keys; 4 octaves and 4 degrees; step depth 1 mm; 33 horizontal diagram lines 1 mm apart; segments 0 to 32 teeth

**Improves on:**

- earlier music typewriters raising paper by octaves or intervals: raises by single degrees
