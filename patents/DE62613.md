## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** A typewriter with a type roller needs one set of ten-per-row key levers to reach all three type sets (capitals, small letters, figures) while positioning the roller exactly.

**Solution:** A type roller with three sets of ten-by-three types is moved axially for the column and rotated for the row/set by shafts and cams worked from the keys, with locking bolts entering teeth on the roller, and a stamp pressing the paper and inked ribbon against the chosen type.

**How it works:** Thirty key levers in three rows act on ten horizontal shafts; the pressed key turns its shaft, whose arm swings a lever with a curved track to slide the type roller on its axis to one of ten positions. Two other shafts, driven by cross strips over the key rows, turn a lever that shifts a spirally toothed roller against a toothed arc, so the type roller turns one row forward or back from its middle position. A separate pair of case keys turns the roller 120 or 240 degrees by a toothed segment to switch between the three type sets. A bolt linked to each shaft rises into a gap between teeth at the roller ends to fix both the axial and angular positions before the stamp presses the paper against the ribbon and the type. Return springs recentre the levers after each key. The paper frame is pulled stepwise by a spring drum through an escapement, a worm drive winds the ribbon between two drums, and a ratchet gives line spacing.
