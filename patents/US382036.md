## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Typewriters of the day hid the writing from the operator, needed long typebar travel and heavy touch, and did not let the keyboard control carriage motion entirely.

**Solution:** Horizontal sliding typebars converging from guide plates strike a vertical platen facing the operator through a rotating ink disk, so the writing is visible at once, with a spring carriage stepped and returned by keys alone.

**How it works:** Typebars slide in square holes in two plates arranged so all converge on one point on a vertical platen plate. Each key lever works its typebar through a bell-crank; keys also depress vibrating rods whose pendants rock a rock-shaft near the base. The rock-shaft pawl steps the carriage along a ratchet bar against a barrel spring (wound on a ribbon from a central post), and a space bar rocks the same shaft without printing. A key lifts the pawls so the spring returns the carriage. A vertical disk of ribbon, edge covering the print point, is turned by a pawl on the rock-shaft. Paper is deflected up the platen face and line-spaced by a cam-guided clamp whose pins ride cam plates, a key raising the clamp frame; swapping plate edges varies line space.
