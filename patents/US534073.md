## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** The earlier pneumatic typewriter (Serial No. 404,504) is bulky; the inventor wants fewer keys, a more compact machine and better platen control.

**Solution:** Type-bar pistons in radial ducts are driven by air from chambers formed by an elastic plate over recesses, with two sets of bars per key combined with shifting levers so fewer keys are needed.

**How it works:** Ducts in a block converge on the printing point on the platen, each holding a piston-type-bar whose front end is guided in a frame. A rear block with passages registering with these ducts has chambers closed by an elastic impervious plate with dome projections, which a metallic plate holds in place. Fluid forced from a chamber when its dome is depressed drives the piston forward to print. The two upper rows of ducts are angled slightly above the radial lines so their type strike a little lower, letting descenders align without tall type faces. A transversely adjustable block and shifting levers provide the reduced key set.
