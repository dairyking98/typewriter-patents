## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** In electric type-cylinder machines with parallel key levers connecting to radial stops, the key-lever linkage made the machine long, needed fussy alignment, and the stepped keyboard slowed typing.

**Solution:** Lay all key lever rear ends in one transverse plane and link them to the radial stops by inclined intermediate levers with loosely fitted, conically collared pushers, so the stops and cylinder move far forward and assembly tolerances do not matter.

**How it works:** A key press raises a contact plate that energises one of two electromagnets, which turn a square shaft and, through a toothed sector and gear, the type cylinder, until a stop arm meets the radial stop bracket advanced by the key via an inclined intermediate lever. The impression magnet is energised in parallel but its swing is held by a pawl until just before rotation ends; the cylinder then pivots on pointed screws against the paper roller. Key lever and intermediate lever are split so the key lever is guided between them, and a U-shaped rail limits both strokes; lever lengths are proportioned so key depth is equal for every key. Just before the type reaches the paper a bracket lifts the pawl so the linkage resets and the next key can be struck at once, and the keyboard is stepped so a finger can slide from one row to the next. Keytops clip on removable tongues and the cylinder is interchangeable on its shaft for other character layouts.
