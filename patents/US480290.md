## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** In the Remington machine the beveled yoke-blocks that straddle the shift rail slide against it during carriage feed, adding friction, and as they wear they bind, abrade the rail and spoil printing alignment.

**Solution:** Replace the sliding yoke-blocks with rollers on opposite sides of the shift rail, the front ones spring-mounted so that they take up wear automatically.

**How it works:** Two fixed rear rollers on slotted arms on the platen carrier roll against the rear side of the shift rail, with cylindrical faces and a beveled lower end. Two spring-mounted front rollers on an inclined support plate bear lightly on the front of the rail, their bevelled ends rolling on the side diametrically opposite the rear rollers. When the shift key moves the rail rearward it pushes the carrier back through the rear rollers to upper case; the return spring brings the rail forward and the front rollers carry the carrier to lower case. The bevels let the carriage lower onto the rail after inspection, and slotted plates allow lateral adjustment of the front rollers for larger wear. Wedge pieces are kept to return the carrier after lifting the carriage.

**Features:** [SHIFT:platen](../features/shift-platen.md), [FRM:adjustment-wear](../features/frm-adjustment-wear.md)

**Parts:** main carriage; platen carrier; shift rail; rear roller; front roller; roller spring; yoke-block; slotted arm; inclined support plate; return spring; shift key lever

**Numbers:** 2 rear rollers; 2 front rollers

**Improves on:**

- Remington platen-carrier yoke-blocks: rollers give rolling friction and spring-taken-up wear instead of sliding beveled blocks
