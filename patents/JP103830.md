## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Braille shorthand writing is slow and clumsy because the writing box must be moved separately after each character.

**Solution:** Moving the stylus box on by one saw-tooth pitch automatically, through a lever under the key levers and two alternating catches, as the fingers leave the keys.

**How it works:** A stylus box slides on rails under a receiving plate, pulled one way by a spring. Pressing key levers lifts upright styluses through paper held between plate and grooved lid, embossing braille dots. The keys also depress a lever that swings a shaft carrying two catches set at different angles, which engage saw teeth on the plate's back wall alternately. When the keys are released the shaft turns back, the other catch engages and the stronger box spring pulls the box on one tooth pitch.

## Description (English translation of the Japanese text, opening)

> Specification. Braille shorthand machine. Nature and object of the invention. This shorthand machine has a stylus box mounted on rails so as to slide left and right with its top touching the underside of a receiving plate; the two catches of a feed device at the back of the box engage saw teeth cut in the back wall of the plate. Key levers that move the styluses in the box up and down are pressed and swung to make the required braille dots in paper held between the receiving plate and a lid, and as soon as this is done a lever linked to the key levers, acting through the catches of the feed device engaged at the back, moves the box on by one pitch of the saw teeth. Its object is to do this accurately and quickly. Brief description of the drawings.
