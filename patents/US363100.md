**In the patent’s own words:** This invention relates to that class of typewriting machines in which a revolving hub carrying radial flexible arms bearing type is employed for making the impressions by depressing the flexible arms as they reach the impression-point;

## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typewriters with a revolving hub of flexible type arms needed a simpler, easier arrangement of inking, printing and paper feeding.

**Solution:** A revolving printing wheel whose radial flexible arms are clamped between hub plates, each arm depressed at the impression point to print, with a gelatinous-ink pad and a paper holder and feed.

**How it works:** A cast metal arm bolts to a base board and carries a spindle for the printing wheel. The wheel is a shell-shaped hub whose flanges clamp the inner ends of the flexible type arms against radial ribs on a bottom plate, with an elastic packing ring to take up uneven arm sizes, all drawn together by a nut on a central tube. As the wheel revolves, the arm at the impression point is pressed down onto the paper. The pad beside the wheel is a metal shell filled with ink-bearing gelatinous mass, covered with an arched fabric clamped at edge and centre so the types ink well, and it is pivoted so it can be turned and refilled. A paper holder and feed device completes the machine.
