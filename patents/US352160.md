## Summary

**Kind:** type-element machine · **Subject (patent classes):** Types or dies fixed on wheel, drum, cylinder, or like carriers with one or more carriers travelling across copy material in letter-space direction

**In the patent’s own words:** My invention relates to that class of typewriters having a rotary type-wheel and atwirling-handle which is revolved to bring the desired type to the printing-point, and in which a reciprocating motion of the type wheel or carriage to or from each other effects theprinting of the selected letter.

**How it works (reading):** Two stacked type-wheels on a fixed vertical shaft, slidable by a shift key, are turned by a twirling handle and printed by tilting a rocking paper carriage against the wheel by pressing that handle down, with the feed stroke limited by variable-depth stops on the wheel.

## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Twirl-handle type-wheel machines had only one wheel, so no capitals or other characters; the inventor also wants proportional carriage feed matched to letter width.

**Solution:** Two stacked type-wheels on a fixed vertical shaft, slidable by a shift key, are turned by a twirling handle and printed by tilting a rocking paper carriage against the wheel by pressing that handle down, with the feed stroke limited by variable-depth stops on the wheel.

**How it works:** Two metal disc wheels with soldered types (small letters above, capitals and numerals below) ride on a sliding hub keyed to a vertical shaft by a pin. A vertical twirling handle is geared to the shaft with equal-tooth gears; an index finger over a fixed dial shows the selected letter, and a spring click-lever holds each position. Pressing the handle down pushes an elbow arm that rocks the cradle carrying the sliding paper carriage so the platen roller meets the selected type; at the same moment the cradle edge hits a set-screw on the click-lever and locks the wheel. A shift key held with the other hand lowers the hub so the capital wheel is at the print line. The rocking cradle also drives a feed pawl whose backward stroke is stopped by a stop-lever dropping into a recess of the wheel rim, so the forward feed equals the width of the letter. An oscillating inker (band, or a hinged felt ink disc) swings away from the type just before impression.
