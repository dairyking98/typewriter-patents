## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Mistyped characters, often adjacent keys or look-alike signs such as n for m, must be erased or visibly corrected, which is slow and unsightly.

**Solution:** Design the shapes and placement of the type characters so a wrong character can be overprinted with a second character in the same position and be converted invisibly into the correct one, with a fine lateral adjustment of the type or stop point where needed.

**How it works:** Letters that resemble each other in cursive, such as n and m, are designed so that printing the second over the first yields a clean correct letter. Where the fix needs a small sideways shift, an adjusting device moves the type position or stop point by a part of a letter width, for example by an inclined surface in the lever system. The normal keyboard stays; either special correction types are used, assigned to special keys or attached to normal type levers and brought into position by a switching device, or the existing characters are redesigned. The idea is claimed for any character-generating machine, including calculators, typesetters and telegraph devices.

**Features:** [ELEM:typebar](../features/elem-typebar.md), [ESC:variable-spacing](../features/esc-variable-spacing.md), [ELEM:element-construction](../features/elem-element-construction.md)

**Parts:** type; type lever; special correction key; switching device; inclined surface; stop point adjuster

**Improves on:**

- Continental type set: systematic overprint-convertible shapes instead of a few accidental ones
- white-paint erase type: no extra stroke with the correct type needed
- hand-shifting the platen to convert characters: mechanical, reliable adjustment instead of by eye
