## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Writers and copyists need a compact typographical machine; earlier layouts of types are bulky and awkward to play.

**Solution:** Reciprocating spring-loaded types in a hemispherical shell all converge on one point on a paper drum, each type's stem end being its own finger key.

**How it works:** Types slide in square holes in a hemispherical metal shell set in a box lid, each held out by a coil spring and ending in a lettered knob that the finger presses to drive the type to the paper. The shell is clamped on a rubber washer with set-screws so all faces are parallel to the drum surface and converge to one point. A blank central type is struck after each word and advances the paper without printing. The paper is wrapped on a drum on a horizontal shaft that rotates and also slides endwise, moving its surface in a spiral, worked through worm gearing on a rack, with an electromagnet and escapement in the drawings. Frequent letters are placed for the strongest fingers, vowels left hand and consonants right.
