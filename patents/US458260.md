## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Improves the Hammond keyboard typewriter: the key-levers must reach closely spaced stop-pins yet present a comfortable three-row keyboard, and the type-wheel shift, key tops and ribbon need better arrangements.

**Solution:** Key-levers converging rearward each raise a stop-pin on an arc and also drive a type-wheel segment rotarily until a stop-lever hits the pin, with a separate lever lifting the wheels vertically for case shift.

**How it works:** Two type-wheel segments with several rows of characters sit inside a cylinder behind an inking ribbon, with a hammer behind the paper. Pressing a key-lever rocks an intermediate lever whose finger pins on a wheel hub turn the wheel, and the same lever lifts one stop-pin from a curved row; the pin stops a stop-lever that arrests the wheel at the chosen character. The key-lever also trips a frame lever that releases the spring-driven paper carriage one space, and a space bar releases it without firing the hammer. A pivoted shift lever raises the vertical shaft carrying the wheels to bring another row (capitals, or figures with a deeper stroke) to the hammer, with detents to hold the shift. The ribbon spools have bosses of unequal projection so they can be turned upside down to present fresh ribbon surface. Wheels are removable for other typefaces and key tops are detachable.
