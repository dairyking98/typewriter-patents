## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Stylus-type-wheel typewriters were awkward because the stylus had to set rotation and type row separately.

**Solution:** A guide lever coupled to the stylus lever carries a wedge that raises the type wheel to the selected row as the stylus moves over a three-ring character plate.

**How it works:** The stylus lever, coupled by a pin to a guide lever with a front opening, is moved sideways and inward until the opening sits over the desired character on the plate. Sideways motion turns the type wheel through a toothed segment and small gear. Inward motion moves a wedge on the guide lever against a crosspiece of a swinging bracket that holds the wheel axle in a groove, lifting the wheel to the second or third row. Pressing the stylus lever lifts a plate and swings the wheel against the paper roller to print, and the same press advances carriage and ribbon via pawls and ratchets.
