## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Wheel typewriters needed a simple, cheap and durable way to turn the type wheel to the chosen character.

**Solution:** Each lever key has a pin that rides a cam slot in a sliding frame, so the key itself moves the frame by a distance unique to that key, and a rack and pinion turns that into the wheel rotation.

**How it works:** Pressing a key drives its pin down a cam slot in a cam plate on a sliding frame, shifting the frame a distance set by the cam slope. A rack on the frame turns a pinion on the type wheel shaft, rotating the wheel to the character. When the pin reaches the straight extension of the slot the frame is locked, and further key travel works the space bar and impression lever. That feeds the carriage one tooth with a pawl carrier, locks it with a locking tooth, and rocks the carriage so the platen strikes the type wheel. Springs centre the frame and return everything on release. The wheel carries three fonts on different arcs; a spring shift key lets the rack bar slide to one of three notched positions for lower case, upper case or figures.
