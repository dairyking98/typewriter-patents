## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Setting the line spacing on a typewriter quickly and to any chosen amount, while keeping the platen easy to remove from its frame.

**Solution:** A rock shaft with a pin moves a line-space lever carrying a feed pawl; an adjustable gauge hook limits the stroke to set the line space, and the pawl is drawn clear of the platen ratchet at rest so the platen lifts out freely.

**How it works:** The operating handle turns a rock shaft whose side pin sits in a slot of a line-space lever pivoted below the platen. The lever's front end carries a spring-loaded feed pawl; at rest a tail on the pawl meets the front cross-bar and holds the pawl clear of the platen ratchet. When the handle is moved, the pawl leaves the cross-bar, swings onto the ratchet teeth and turns the platen. A friction-held gauge lever with a hook passes under the pin and limits its travel: hook in the path gives the smallest spacing, swung away gives the largest. A spring-arm detent roller on a detent ratchet holds the platen, and a release lever pushes the roller away. The platen shaft has a reduced section so it drops through slotted bearings.

## Description (English translation of the Japanese text, opening)

> Specification. Line-spacing adjusting mechanism for a typewriter. Nature and object of the invention. This invention relates to a line-spacing adjusting mechanism for a typewriter having a platen frame; a platen in the frame, with a feed ratchet wheel; a rock shaft pivoted on the frame and carrying a projection; a line-space lever set crosswise parallel to that shaft, mounted on the frame below the platen, and engaging the projection; and a feed pawl mounted on the free front end of the lever and engaging the ratchet. Its object is to set the distance between one line of type and the next to any spacing, very quickly and surely, by a simple and suitable construction. Brief description of the drawings.
