## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Front-strike typebar machines suffer lateral wobble of the bars and non-uniform key leverage and key dip across the bars and key banks.

**Solution:** Ball-bearing typebar hubs between cone plates in forked hangers, with bell-cranks of uniform size and graduated key levers and links giving equal leverage and key travel.

**How it works:** Rearward-striking typebars pivot on bearing balls running between a V-groove in the bar hub and a groove formed by two bevelled cones riveted in a forked hanger, so they swing in one plane. Each bar is linked to a uniform bell-crank hung in an open diagonal socket and held there by a draw-spring. A second link joins the bell-crank to a key lever at its midpoint. Key levers get longer toward the front bank and pivot on four transverse rods, so every link point moves equally. A universal bar under the link line rocks a dog-rocker that steps the carriage by its feeding and detent dogs. Bars carry two types, with the platen shifted for upper case.
