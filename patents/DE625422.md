## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** In key-stopped type wheel machines the keyboard sits on a swinging bearing, so striking one key moves the others, which makes typing and reading the letter field difficult.

**Solution:** Fix the keyboard on an immovable board and let a separate drive lever, worked by the other hand, set and print the type wheel against a stop bar that rests under the struck key.

**How it works:** Three rows of keys sit loosely in holes of a fixed board, set at slight different distances from the platen. The drive lever pushes a sliding board carrying the toothed wheel and type wheel toward the platen. A spring-held toothed board meshes with the wheel and turns it, while a linked rod system moves a stop rod (restraint rod) sideways under the keys. The stop rod passes under unstruck keys, lifting them slightly, but is halted under the struck key; that halts the rods and the wheel, which then stops rotating at the chosen character. The sliding board, carried on by the now stationary wheel against the spring, brings the type to the paper roller. Releasing the lever lets the spring return everything to rest.
