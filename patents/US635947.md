## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** Operating typebar keys takes force that tires the typist over long use.

**Solution:** A motor-driven gear roll runs continuously and each key only throws a rack on the typearm lever into mesh with it, so the motor supplies the stroke.

**How it works:** Type-arms in opposite pairs are linked to evener levers, and a lever for each key is linked to them. A rack bar pivoted on the free end of each lever lies just above a gear roll driven by a spring or other motor through a gear train. Striking a key pushes a rod that throws the rack into mesh with the roll, which draws the lever down and swings the type-arm to the platen. Just before the stroke ends the beveled rack end hits a wedge-shaped bar and is disengaged. A slotted notched wheel and detent on a universal bar stop the roll when no key is pressed and let it run only long enough to finish the stroke.
