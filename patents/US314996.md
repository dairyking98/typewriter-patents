## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A typewriter cheap enough to sell for a few dollars, with few parts and little to go wrong, usable by young people as an instructive pastime.

**Solution:** A single slide-bar of type slides in a hinged frame beside a fixed letter-plate; a stylus dropped into a hole by the wanted letter aligns the matching type, and pressing the whole frame down prints and advances the paper by one tooth.

**How it works:** Two hinged side bars carry a fixed letter-plate with a row of letters, each with a recess beside it, and a slidable type-plate with a row of type on its underside in reversed order. A stylus hinged to the type-plate is set into the recess by the wanted letter, which slides the type-plate so the matching type is over the print line. Pressing the stylus downward swings the whole frame down so the type strikes an ink ribbon and the paper over an impression roller; springs raise it again. A pawl on the letter-plate steps a ratchet rack on the sliding paper platform one tooth per stroke for letter spacing. For new lines the pawl is lifted, the platform is pushed back, and a knob turns the roller to feed the paper between wires. The ribbon is threaded on a pivoted wire fork that is swung to present fresh ink.
