## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Ordinary typewriters cannot print the many variable special characters (formulas, symbols, accents) so they are written in by hand.

**Solution:** A thin embossed plastic shield bearing the special character is placed behind the ribbon at the stop point and printed by the blow of an ordinary typebar.

**How it works:** A shield of thin acetyl cellulose sheet is embossed with the character by heating and pressing a type against a yielding pad, optionally stiffened from behind with varnish. Ribs or flanges guide it on the machine's lever guide or paper rail, and it sits behind the ribbon at the print point. The typist strikes a key (often a broad letter such as m) and the lever drives the shield's character onto the paper. The shield is then removed, and shields are stored in a labelled strip box.
