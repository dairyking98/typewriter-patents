## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Typing speed is limited because the carriage must stop and be returned at each line end; the inventor wants substantially continuous writing.

**Solution:** Two interchangeable type-wheel carriages travel in an endless track along a stationary platen, one writing while the other is dismissed and returned, so the idle one starts at the left margin the moment the active one is dismissed.

**How it works:** Each carriage holds a type-wheel on a spring-returned shaft in a slide, and is fixed to an endless band driven by pinned wheels. Pressing any key trips a spring-wound shaft that makes one revolution per impression; its cam first pushes the slide so the wheel's pinion meshes with a positioning rack, then a spring-driven rock arm turns the rack and wheel until it meets a stop pin raised by the pressed key, selecting the character. A lug engaging radial guide surfaces on the wheel then locks it accurately, and the cam releases the slide so springs snap the wheel against the paper for the impression, after which the wheel retracts. A trip pawl prevents the impression until positioning has finished, a latch holds the key down until the stroke is done, and a repeat key lets the same character print again. After each impression an escapement with two pawls steps a scape-wheel to advance the carriage one space; at the end of a word at the right margin both pawls disengage, spring motors run the carriage back round the endless track, and the second carriage takes over, with the platen line-spaced at the changeover.
