## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A folding Hammond typewriter must stay rigid when open, and its loose key levers and shift linkage must survive folding.

**Solution:** Rigid fingers on the hinged keyboard extension seat in grooves in the base and are held by a spring catch, while a spring-loaded bar locks the key levers automatically on folding and a rock-shaft arm carries the shift keys across the fold.

**How it works:** The keyboard extension is hinged at the front of the base. In the flat position, backward fingers on the extension drop into grooves in the base corners, and a spring-loaded hook catch with a cam edge snaps over a pin on one finger. A finger piece releases the catch to fold. The key levers sit loosely on a knife-edge support, so a locking bar with springs drops onto them as the keyboard folds up. Fingers on the base lift the bar again when the keyboard returns. Gravity legs on the extension fall into support position on lowering, and the shift keys act through a rock shaft and arm that can swing free when the keyboard folds.

## Description (English translation of the Japanese text, opening)

> Specification. Improvements in folding typewriters. Nature and object of the invention. The invention relates to a folding typewriter combining: a rigid main base frame with a pair of grooves in the low surface of its front corners; a keyboard-frame extension, movably joined to the main base frame for folding; and an automatic catch working with several frame pieces to hold fingers in the grooves. When the extension is in working position relative to the main base frame, a pair of rigid fingers extending backward from its lower surface fit into the grooves.
