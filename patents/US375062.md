## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** On a Hall-type index typewriter every letter takes a separate impression, which is slow.

**Solution:** Enlarge the type plate and index plate with extra rows of whole common words of two, three and four letters, and make the carriage feed stop vary automatically so it advances by the length of the word printed.

**How it works:** The Hall machine's type plate and index plate carry rows of single letters, then rows of common two-letter, three-letter and four-letter words. The finder arm drops its pin into an index hole to print the chosen letter or word in one impression. A slotted arm on the finder arm drives a rack bar as the arm moves between rows, which turns a pinion whose hub has notches of different depths. The notch facing the spring arm sets how far the spring arm can swing out, so it engages the carriage rack one, two, three or four grooves ahead and the spring-driven carriage steps that many spaces. A finger piece lets the carriage be advanced several spaces without printing.
