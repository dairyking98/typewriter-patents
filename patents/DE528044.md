## Reading (short summary from the opening, OCR fair)

**Problem:** On machines with very small type, the letter spacing (about 1.5 mm) is finer than the usual 3 mm tab rail pitch, so a tab cannot land on the next but one column without pressing the space key.

**Solution:** The excerpt sets out the pitch mismatch and the bumper behaviour; the remedy itself is not visible in the first 3000 characters.

> Description Translated from German Decimal tab for typewriters with particularly small fonts On typewriters with tab facilities and normal font, the letter spacing is usually 2.6 to 3 mm, and accordingly the slots and tabs in the rider rail attached to the carriage are spaced this distance apart. In the case of machines with very small letters and very small letter spacing, this is only about 1.5 mm, and accordingly the rider rail would have to have such a division. However, it is technically not possible, while maintaining the required strength ratios, to keep the sheet thicknesses of the tabs and the web thickness remaining between two slots of the tab rail so low that a division of 1.5 mm of the tab rail can be carried out. You therefore have to use the usual material thicknesses and tab rails with their z. B. maintain a division of 3 mm, which does not matter in relation to writing in the respective adjacent columns, but when moving from one column to the next but one requires pressing the space key after releasing the label key, as can be seen from the following consideration emerges.
