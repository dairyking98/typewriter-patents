## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Reporters' shorthand notes must be written out in longhand before they can be used; a fast printing machine would give legible copy directly for the printer or publisher.

**Solution:** A type wheel runs continuously on a power-driven shaft with a slotted cylinder beside it; pressing a key makes its end enter its slot in the cylinder when that slot comes round, which lifts the paper-bearing platen against the one type that is then at the print point.

**How it works:** A treadle or hand-driven main shaft carries a type wheel, a slotted cylinder with one slot per character (arranged in half-spirals) and a cog cylinder. Keys like melodeon keys rest on fulcra with their inner ends bearing on the slotted cylinder; a pressed key drops into its slot at the instant its character is at the bottom, and a bail lifts a hinged frame carrying a rubber-covered hollow platen against that single type. At the same instant the cog cylinder engages a ratchet wheel on the platen, advancing it one tooth, and the slot's curved edge then pushes the key back out and the frame drops. A blank on the wheel with its own key spaces without printing. A cam on the ratchet wheel at line end lets a weight pull the platen one line, and a long tooth skips the crease where the paper is wrapped, so a page is one revolution of the platen; several sheets with carbon give copies.
