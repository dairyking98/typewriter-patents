## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** A simpler domestic printing machine than the inventor's earlier one (US8980), where the lever that turns the wheel also locks its position, prints, and moves the paper by the right amount for each letter's width.

**Solution:** One hand lever turns a notched-rim type wheel to a letter on a stationary index ring, then pressing it drops an arm into the tooth above that type, pushing the wheel down to print while a second, width-profiled wheel moves the paper carriage the matching distance.

**How it works:** A horizontal wheel with type around its rim turns on a vertical axis and can also move along it. A lever fixed to the wheel by a bracket turns it until a pointer is opposite the wanted letter on a fixed index ring, then pressing the lever drives an arm into the tooth of the rim above that type, which both aligns the wheel exactly and presses it down so the type prints. The same press, through rock shafts and rods, shifts a second wheel on a hollow center shaft whose rim projections match each type in number and thickness; these push a rod that moves the paper carriage forward by that letter's width plus spacing, and springs return everything. A finger lever moves the carriage extra for word spaces. The paper is on an endless band on the carriage, which is turned by running the carriage back so lines are spaced. Two ink rollers ink the type as it passes.
