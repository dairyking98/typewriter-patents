## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Key-wheel typewriters need a simple, positive way to stop the rotating type wheel at the right character so figures print accurately and cleanly, without the wheel hiding the printed line.

**Solution:** Pressing a key swings a toothed segment that turns the key wheel through a gear, while the same key lever stops a sliding rack geared to the wheel shaft, so the wheel halts at that key's position and then swings against the platen.

**How it works:** The key wheel sits on a vertical shaft at the top of a swinging bar, with a gear and pinion on the shaft. Pressing a key lever pushes a vertical lever, which swings a toothed segment arm against the gear and turns the wheel. As the wheel turns, its pinion slides a toothed rack sideways, and a pin on the rack end meets the top of the pressed vertical lever and stops the wheel at that character. Keys on the right swing the segment bar and the wheel; keys on the left swing the wheel's own bar so the gear walks along a fixed segment; either way the wheel then swings against the platen to print. Springs return the bars, and a rear stop block limits travel. A spanner on the stem shifts the wheel for capitals or figures, and a ribbon guide with forks moves with the bar. A variant has horizontally pressed keys, halving the width.
