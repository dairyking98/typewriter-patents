## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** One-piece typebar machines must be sent in whole for repair and cannot easily be adapted to other languages or wide carriages.

**Solution:** Split a visible-writing typewriter along a transverse vertical plane into two complete frames, keyboard-and-typebar front and carriage rear, joined by a light hand-releasable coupling.

**How it works:** The front frame holds the key system with shift and back-shift keys, the type levers on a curved segment plate, the ribbon spools and their transport and lift. The rear frame holds the carriage with its bearings, feed and guides, shift and line-limit devices and the release bridge. Four screws with cylindrical necks and cones join the halves exactly, with guide pieces to aid matching. Type levers sit in an upper segment and intermediate levers in a lower segment, joined by pull rods and a tension spring that stops rebound; a nose on each lever hits the release bridge to step the carriage. A whole front frame can be swapped for another language, or a rear frame for a wide carriage, and either half sent for repair or cleaned.
