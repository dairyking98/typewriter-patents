## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Two tooth sectors driving a type cylinder wheel must keep the wheel centered at rest and block the idle sector, without tooth wear.

**Solution:** A disc with a ring, stop and recesses on the cylinder axis, working with pins on the two tooth sectors, centers the wheel and lets only one sector engage.

**How it works:** Drive arms with tooth sectors engage a gear wheel on the cylinder axis for clockwise or counter-clockwise turning. A disc on the axis has a central stop that sits between pins on the two sectors at rest, locking rotation. Recesses in the ring let the pin of the pressed sector pass so its teeth mesh, while the other sector's pin rides the outer ring surface and cannot engage. On return the stop catches the pins, and its rounded side pushes the disc to the centre if the key is released slowly. A variant has the sectors swinging parallel to the axis.
