## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Index machines with a flexible type plate needed a simpler type-plate guide and setting pin, and a single action that both spaces the letters and prints so letters cannot overprint.

**Solution:** A setting pin dropped in a hole of an index plate positions an elastic type plate under a hammer lever, and a single key press steps the carriage along a rack while the same movement drives the hammer down.

**How it works:** The operator drops the setting pin into the hole of the index plate for the wanted letter. The pin's lever ends in a ball sliding in a guide groove across the carriage and carries a fork that holds the elastic type plate, so the matching type comes over the recess of the inking plate under the printing lever's hammer. Pressing one of two keys at the ends of a serrated rack bar pushes the rack sideways; a spring-loaded pawl on the printing lever rides in its teeth and is forced along, so the carriage moves one letter space. A screw in the printing lever, which turns as the pawl moves and bears on a fixed set screw, tilts the lever so the hammer descends and prints at the end of the carriage movement. For a word space the pin goes in a central hole whose type position is empty.
