## Summary

**Kind:** type-element machine · **Subject (patent classes):** Typewriters or selective printing mechanisms characterised by the mounting, arrangement or disposition of the types or dies with types or dies mounted on carriers rotatable for selection the plane of the type or die face being parallel to the axis of rotation, e.g. with type on the periphery of cylindrical carriers

**In the patent’s own words:** My invention relates particularly to typewriting machines employing a rotary or pivotal type-wheel or segment with the characters for impression mounted thereon and a depressible key-board or movable member for actuating and controlling the type-wheel or segment to bring the characters corresponding to the keys touched by the fingers into proper position for striking or imprinting on the paper.

**How it works (reading):** A pivoted keyboard plate carries a hammer-like type-wheel, which a rack-and-pinion train spins until a stop-plate, arrested by the pressed key pin, halts it, after which a friction clutch gives a compensating resistance while a toothed ring shifts and locks the wheel axially and the wheel strikes the paper.

## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A compact, light, cheap travelling typewriter with a small one-hand keyboard, where the stroke feel stays constant even though the type-wheel is stopped part-way through the key stroke.

**Solution:** A pivoted keyboard plate carries a hammer-like type-wheel, which a rack-and-pinion train spins until a stop-plate, arrested by the pressed key pin, halts it, after which a friction clutch gives a compensating resistance while a toothed ring shifts and locks the wheel axially and the wheel strikes the paper.

**How it works:** The whole mechanism is on an actuating plate hinged at the front and sprung upward; the small hexagon-cell keyboard is at its free end and the palm rests on the hinged end. Pressing the plate turns a pinion on a fixed segmental rack, which works a crank and pitman rack that turns a driving pinion, clutch drum and shaft geared to the type-wheel shaft. The shaft also draws a slidable stop-plate with an oblique edge toward the spring-held key pin that was pressed, and when the stop-plate is arrested the wheel stops with its character over the printing space. A drum with two friction springs then slips and keeps resisting so every stroke feels equal. Further pitman travel pushes a toothed ring against a double-armed shift lever that turns with the wheel, sliding the wheel axially to the front or back row or leaving it on the middle row, and locking it in position. The wheel, carried on the plate, then strikes the raised printing space of the paper support; a reversing bevel gear turns the wheel the opposite way (left-hand lever) to print capitals, and the same motion spaces the carriage one step and feeds the ribbon.
