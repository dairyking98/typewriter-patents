## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** A typebar machine needs many bars and keys for many characters.

**Solution:** Each type-bar carries up to three types, and the stroke distance chosen by shifts decides which type prints, with a hammer driving the bar and a detent locking it.

**How it works:** A key moves a link through a bell-crank toward the platen, the link holding an eye on the type-bar. Shifts lie in the path: the link's inclined end rides along them and lifts out of the eye, leaving the bar stopped with the outer type, middle type or innermost type in line. Lugs and a detent lock the bar while a hammer strikes it toward the platen. An escapement wheel advances the carriage.

**Features:** [ELEM:typebar](../features/elem-typebar.md), [SHIFT:triple](../features/shift-triple.md), [IMP:hammer-behind](../features/imp-hammer-behind.md), [SEL:keyboard-few-keys](../features/sel-keyboard-few-keys.md), [ESC:escape-wheel](../features/esc-escape-wheel.md), [ELEM:alignment-lock](../features/elem-alignment-lock.md)

**Parts:** type-bar; key; link; bell-crank; shift; hammer; detent; lug; escapement wheel; platen

**Numbers:** three types per bar
