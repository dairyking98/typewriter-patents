## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** In electric typewriters a continuously turning drive roller drives each typebar by friction, so reliable drive needs high contact pressure, which wears the parts quickly.

**Solution:** Replace the friction drive with a clamping coupling: a stirrup spanning slightly more than half the smooth drive roller is clamped to it by the self-tightening effect of static friction when the key is pressed.

**How it works:** A smooth steel roller turns continuously over all typebars. Each typebar has a stirrup with a pin in a slot of a bearing lever, which acts through an intermediate lever on the typebar. Pressing the key moves an angle lever with a push tooth that turns a star disc and lifts the stirrup onto the roller. The stirrup, of slightly smaller radius and elastic, clamps on the roller and is carried along, swinging the typebar toward the platen. Just before impact it hits an adjustable stop and the mass of the moving parts frees it from the roller, and it returns to rest. A second version uses an eccentric disc on a larger-radius stirrup to make the clamp.
