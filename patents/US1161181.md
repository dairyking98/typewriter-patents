## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** The balancing spring on the platen-shift rock shaft of a Noiseless typewriter is hard to tension accurately; long helical springs change tension too much as the carriage shifts, and adjusting normally means loosening nuts and re-locking.

**Solution:** A worm-and-pinion on the rock shaft turns a sleeve that anchors one end of the balance spring, so tension is adjusted finely with a screwdriver and self-locks.

**How it works:** A rock shaft turned by cam mechanism from two shift keys raises or lowers the carriage (platen shift) from a neutral middle position. A sleeve on each end of the shaft carries one end of a balancing spring whose other end is fixed to the frame. A pinion on the sleeve meshes with a worm carried in a bracket on a bushing fixed to the rock shaft. Turning the worm head with a screwdriver rotates the pinion and sleeve, raising or lowering spring tension. The worm and pinion hold each other against movement, so no separate locking is needed. Shifting both up and down from a central neutral position avoids over-tensioning the spring.

**Features:** [SHIFT:platen](../features/shift-platen.md), [FRM:adjustment-wear](../features/frm-adjustment-wear.md)

**Parts:** rock shaft; shift key; cam; sleeve; balancing spring; pinion; worm; bushing; return spring

**Improves on:**

- common helical balance spring in typewriter shift: adjustable tension with self-locking worm drive; neutral centre position prevents overtension
