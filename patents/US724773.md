## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typebar and key-lever assemblies are hard to adjust, assemble and replace without disturbing other parts.

**Solution:** Typebars, angle levers and finger levers hook onto fulcrum rods with outward-opening notch bearings and are held by a spring wire, so each can be removed or adjusted individually.

**How it works:** Each typebar is two sections joined by slotted screws, so its length can be adjusted to strike at the right place on the platen. Its hook-shaped fulcrum bearing snaps onto a fulcrum rod, held by a spring-loaded curved wire that also acts as a yielding abutment. A headed pin on a rearward arm of the bar sits in a slot in an angle lever; the slot curves at its upper end to give a quick final motion. The angle lever is driven by a sheet-metal finger lever on its own rod, with an adjustable return spring. The typebar returns onto a felt cushion for quiet, and a lug on the bar keeps neighbouring type from hitting it. A stop screw prevents the period from punching the paper, and ball-bearing tubular guides in front of the platen align the bar.
