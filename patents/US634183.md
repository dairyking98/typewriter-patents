## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A very low cost typewriter is wanted, with a single font-head holding a full font and a key action with little friction that cannot be jammed by pressing two keys.

**Solution:** A single segmental barrel font-head carrying six rows of type is turned to the character by a pair of reverse-moving differential cam-bars acted on by all the keys through a rack-and-pinion, raised to the row by a multi-pivot shift lever, and swung to the paper on a universal-jointed shaft.

**How it works:** Each key lever has a primary and a secondary finger-piece. Pressing a key forces its differently inclined cam face on a pair of parallel cam-bars suspended on hangers, moving them equally in opposite directions; their racks turn a pinion on a vertical shaft, which through a knuckle joint oscillates a shaft carrying the font-head to the angle for that character. At full depression the key enters aligned notches in both bars, giving exact positioning and locking the bars so a second key cannot be pressed. The secondary finger-piece depresses a bail that raises a shift lever and a frame that lifts the font-head to the second row; a second bail or the lever's own end gives rows three and five, and combining them gives rows four and six. The key stroke also throws the arm so the font-head shaft pivots on the universal joint and the selected type hits the roller through an endless ribbon; the carriage escapement (pawl and spring finger on a ratchet bar, driven by an escapement bar under all keys) acts after the impression. The ribbon is stepped by two sheaves with opposite-acting ratchets driven by the font-head's motion.
