## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Steel typewriter type made by forcing a small metal block into a matrix is difficult, expensive, heavy and uneven in quality. Embossing type in sheet steel failed when the matrix walls were steep-angled at 20-30 degrees to the surface.

**Solution:** Emboss shell type in sheet steel with a male force die and a matrix whose side walls are inclined at about 45 degrees, so the sheet fills the matrix sharply.

**How it works:** A matrix for each character is engraved in a steel block, with side walls at about 45 degrees. A male force die carries the same character, smaller by the sheet thickness. A steel sheet is laid over the matrix and driven in by one or more press strokes, giving a sharp type face; many characters can be struck at once in rows. The back may be filled with a low-melting hard alloy if the sheet is thin. The sheet is then cut into a blank with apertured projections and scored. It is folded into a hexagonal multi-faced carrier that rotates on a type-bar, with a tooth engaging a rack to bring one of three faces to the platen.
