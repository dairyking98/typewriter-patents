## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** In earlier type-wheel machines the wheel was first set to the letter and then printed in two separate operations, which is slow and heavy to operate.

**Solution:** Setting the wheel and swinging it to the platen are combined in one key stroke: a planetary-style drive rotates the wheel while the same motion swings its axle toward the paper, and a step wheel with a pawl stops the rotation at the chosen letter.

**How it works:** Each key lever has a gear sector that turns one of two toothed rings; the ring turns a pinion on the type wheel axle and, since the other ring stays still, the pinion rolls on it and so swings the axle about a transverse shaft toward the platen. A step wheel on the same hub, whose steps are graded in radius, is caught by a pawl whose engagement depth depends on the depth of the key's sector cutout, so the wheel stops at that letter while the swing carries on and prints. A second opposing pawl prevents the step wheel from rebounding, and a ratchet pawl locks the wheel just before impact. Two latching keys raise the wheel axle to bring the second or third type ring into print position. The same swing shaft advances a ratchet that spaces the paper carriage one letter, with a separate space key and ratchet for word spaces. An inking roller is rolled over the type as the wheel swings, then is lifted out of the way. Separate marking wheels underline, strike out or draw margin lines. A second variant uses lifting thumbs instead of sectors and a notched bar to hold the raised axle position.
