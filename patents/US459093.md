## Summary

**Kind:** type-element machine · **Subject (patent classes):** Typewriters or selective printing mechanisms characterised by the mounting, arrangement or disposition of the types or dies with types or dies mounted on carriers rotatable for selection the plane of the type or die face being parallel to the axis of rotation, e.g. with type on the periphery of cylindrical carriers

**In the patent’s own words:** My invention relates to type-writers, and particularly to that class or type of such machines as have an axiallyanoving type-wheel which is propelled against the platen to impress the type;

## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Blickensderfer's key-driven type-wheel machine lost time and effort because the wheel had to rotate, stop, then move to the platen in separate motions, and its stop pawl could let the wheel rebound onto the wrong tooth; the paper carriage feed and roller handling also needed improvement.

**Solution:** A single key stroke rolls a pinion between two racks so the type-wheel rotates and swings to the platen at once, a graded eccentric ratchet gripped by two opposed pawls stops it exactly at the chosen character, and the same stroke steps the carriage by pawl and ratchet.

**How it works:** A key-lever acts through a geared, variable-leverage tumbler on one of two segmental racks; the pinion on the type-wheel shaft rolls on the stationary rack, so the wheel turns and advances toward the platen simultaneously. The pinion sleeve carries an eccentric ratchet with teeth graded in length from a start point; the tumbler's slot depth moves a pawl onto the matching tooth, and a second pawl on the opposite side grips an oppositely pointed tooth so the wheel locks and cannot rebound. A concentric ratchet is also caught by a fixed pawl for steadiness before impact. The wheel carries a single peripheral row of characters, and shift keys lift its shaft to bring other rows into play. An ink roller swung only by the wheel's own motion inks the type. A pawl on the rock shaft with a cam face and a locking flange steps the carriage rack pinion via a ratchet and locks it before printing, and the spacer bar has its own pawl on the same ratchet shaft so letter and word space can be combined. The carriage has removable, hinged feed rollers, a presser plate, and adjustable underscore and rule-marking wheels.
