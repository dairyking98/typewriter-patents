## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A sheet used as an inker (instead of a ribbon) would wear through at one spot unless a fresh area is presented to the type after each impression.

**Solution:** A sheet inker, carried on the paper carriage, is automatically stepped across the carriage's travel and at right angles by the platen's line-feed, then restored by a mutilated pinion.

**How it works:** A flat inker sheet is held taut by springs at the front of a pivoted inker-carrier and by claws on a rock-shaft arm at the rear, lying between the types and the paper. Moving the carriage back and turning the platen for a new line operates the line-space lever, whose toe pulls a rod and pawl that steps a ratchet wheel joined to a mutilated pinion. The pinion swings a toothed segment on the rock-shaft, drawing the sheet back a short distance so a fresh strip faces the type after every line. When the pinion's toothless part reaches the segment, the springs snap the sheet back to its starting position and the cycle repeats. The inker-carrier can also be swung away independently to reach the types for cleaning.
