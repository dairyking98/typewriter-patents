## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typebar machines need considerable finger force, causing fatigue, and the strike force varies with how hard the key is pressed.

**Solution:** A motor-driven roller supplies the typebar energy and the key only trips a lock that releases an elliptic wheel into contact with the roller.

**How it works:** A driven roller turns continuously. Each key has an elliptic wheel on a bell crank lever, held stationary by a sliding bar that engages a pin on the wheel. Depressing a key rocks a lever that withdraws the bar, and a spring turns the wheel until its edge touches the roller, which then rotates it. Because the wheel is elliptic, the bell crank swings and pulls a bar that works an Underwood-style typebar. After half a revolution the second pin hits the bar, which has returned, and stops the wheel. Holding the key repeats the stroke, and the strike force is the same however fast the key is pressed.

**Parts:** driven roller; elliptic wheel; bell crank lever; locking bar; rock lever; key lever; pull bar; typebar; tension spring
