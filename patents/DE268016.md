## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** In typewriters whose paper roller is raised for capitals, the guidance of the roller was not secure, and parts that must keep their working position relative to the roller (line spacing, paper feed) shifted when the roller was lifted.

**Solution:** Mount the paper roller in a frame that is guided in grooves in the carriage side walls and lifted by arms engaged under the roller axis, and carry the line-space ratchet, stops and paper feed on that same frame.

**How it works:** A frame inside the paper carriage is connected to it by parallel links and guided by grooves in lugs on the carriage side walls. A yoke with arms ending in grooved ends receives the platen axis, held down by spring-loaded arms on the frame. Its cross rail runs the full carriage length between rollers on a vertically sliding rod in the machine frame, so the engagement holds at any carriage position. Pressing either shift key lever turns a square shaft and pulls the rod down; the rollers push the rail down and lift the platen with its frame to the capital position, partly balanced by a spring. The line-space lever, pawl and ratchet, the adjustable line-spacing stop, the paper-insertion plate with feed rollers and the paper holding arms all ride on the frame, so their relation to the platen is unchanged. The same shift shaft also moves the line pointer and ribbon guide.
