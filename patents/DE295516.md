## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** In pocket typewriters with a fixed paper carrier and a single movable type bar, the lines must stay perfectly straight and the characters upright, without smearing, while the type bar is guided on a carriage.

**Solution:** The longitudinally sliding type bar is carried in a rotatable support disc inside a carriage, and engages a fixed rail running the machine length as line guide while the carriage steps along toothed rails by half-tooth shift shoes.

**How it works:** A type bar with types on several side faces slides along its axis through an opening in a round support disc. Folding L-shaped guide tongues with ribs engage grooves in the bar sides to guide it, and fold flat when the bar is removed. The disc turns in a retaining ring fixed to a carriage on guide rails, so the bar can be rotated to bring a chosen side face down, held by a flat spring with lugs in grooves of the ring. A fixed rail along the machine engages the bar during printing as a line guide. Spacing uses two half-ring shift shoes whose teeth are offset by half a tooth on the guide rails, pressed by a ring spring and alternately released by lips on the ring when the bar is lifted and lowered, so the carriage advances without a pull wire and big spring drum (a lighter spring may still assist). Pressure brackets lift the shoes out for free carriage movement.
