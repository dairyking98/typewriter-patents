## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Ribbon mechanisms on visible-writing typebar machines hide the printing point, take their feed power from the carriage spring or the operator's fingers, and put the spools where the ribbon ends cannot be seen to reverse in time.

**Solution:** A ribbon vibrator on a curved guide lifts the centre of the ribbon to the printing point, while separate springs, cocked by the carriage return and released as the carriage steps, wind the ribbon through clutched spool shafts that can be switched by hand.

**How it works:** Two ribbon spools stand in front of the platen on vertical shafts, with the ribbon running straight between them through a bifurcated vibrator. Pressing any key lowers a universal bar that raises the vibrator along curved slotted guides, so it moves up and back and tilts the ribbon over the printing point; releasing the key drops it and uncovers the printing point. Returning the carriage drives an oblique cam bar on it against a rocker arm, which pulls two toothed racks forward and tensions springs. As the carriage then steps along, the springs draw the racks back, turning a pinion and a one-way bevel-tooth clutch on one spool shaft, so the ribbon winds evenly with no extra force from the operator. A thumbwheel and cams lift one clutch sleeve and drop the other, reversing the direction of ribbon travel by hand. The guides are attached to the obliquely shifting carriage rail, so the vibrator shifts with the platen between cases.
