## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Circular-basket typebar machines were costly and hard to align because the typebars, intermediate levers and their rings were separate parts on separate mountings.

**Solution:** One specially shaped carrier disk holds the typebars, swinging arms and intermediate levers, with spring-mounted carriage guides.

**How it works:** Typebars lie radially in a circle between two ribs of the carrier disk and are driven by swinging arms with toothed arcs. Key levers push rods that lift intermediate levers whose toothed ends rock the arms, throwing the bar to the common print point. A sleeve on a central pin is pressed down by each intermediate lever and works a universal lever that releases the carriage pawl on its rack. Springs return the levers, and the carriage runs on spring-mounted adjustable guides.
