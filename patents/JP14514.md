## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Braille slates need a separate guide board and backing board and the paper must be re-inserted to write the other side.

**Solution:** Two hinged metal plates, each pierced with writing windows on one face and stylus pits on the other, so paper between them is written on both sides in one setting.

**How it works:** Plates P and P' are hinged and laid together so each window faces a pit group in the opposite plate. Four pins pierce the paper corners to hold it. The user writes dots with a stylus through the windows of one plate, turns the frame over and writes through the other plate. A line marker sliding on two wires on each plate shows the next unwritten line.

**Features:**

- **Frame and body:** [Portable or compact](../features/frm-compact-portable.md)
- **Uses:** [For the blind](../features/use-blind.md)

**Parts:** metal plate; hinge; writing window; stylus pit; locating pin; stylus; line marker; guide wire

**Numbers:** 4 locating pins; 2 plates

**Improves on:**

- Braille slate with separate guide board and pitted backing board: each plate serves as both guide and backing, so the frame is smaller and lighter and writes both sides at once

## Description (English translation of the Japanese text, opening)

> Braille writing frame. This device consists of two metal plates joined by a hinge so that they open and close freely. The plates are pierced with many writing windows (紙穴, "paper holes") and stylus pits (錐受穴, "stylus-receiving holes"). If Braille paper is put between the plates, both sides can be written at once in the usual way. Since each plate serves, on its two faces, both as the guide board (紙板) and as the pitted backing board (木板, "wooden board") of the usual slate, both can be made the same size as the paper, the device can be made smaller and much lighter, and it is simpler to use and easier to carry.
