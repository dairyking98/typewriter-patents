## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** The latch-and-escapement of Richards' earlier mechanical movement (US401904) needed improvement: a more reliable latch release that holds when the key stays down, with wear taken up and the parts easy to assemble and remove.

**Solution:** A one-piece spring-steel escapement with two resilient arms carrying three stop faces is tripped by a key lever to release a spring-driven latch, which is then caught by a second stop face and cannot re-cock until the key is released; the escapement pivots on a removable fulcrum support.

**How it works:** A turret frame carries a reciprocating latch thrown forward by a spring around a plunger stem when released. A forked spring-steel escapement, pivoted ahead of the latch, has an upper arm with a primary catch that normally holds the latch catch, and a lower arm with a limit catch and a rear supplemental catch. Depressing the key lever flexes the upper arm up, freeing the latch; the spring throws the latch forward until the limit catch stops it, where a starting arm can push it back by toggle action. If the key is held down, the supplemental catch holds the latch against return until the key rises. A cam face on the plunger head and on the escapement front tip returns the escapement to the primary stop position. The spring arms take up wear by moving toward each other, and the escapement with its fulcrum support is a unit that snaps into a recess in the frame and is held by a screw.
