## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** On a katakana, Burmese or European typewriter, a dot or double dot must be struck at the right shoulder of a character already printed, but the platen normally advances after every stroke.

**Solution:** Pressing a dot-key lever trips a linkage that holds the escapement regulator out of action so the platen does not advance for the following stroke.

**How it works:** Each typebar, through a link joined to its key lever, pushes an arc piece that carries a sliding piece whose stud pushes a bent plate on the wheel regulator, releasing the detent so the platen gear advances one tooth per stroke. Under the key levers of characters that take dots, a link from the key lever turns a lower shaft, which through a link turns an upper shaft carrying a weighted hook lever. The hook lever pushes the bent plate down, opening a gap so the sliding piece's stud no longer reaches the regulator. The detent stays in the wheel, the platen stays put, and the dot key struck next lands at the same place, making for example ガ from カ.
