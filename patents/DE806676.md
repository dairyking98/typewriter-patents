## Reading (short summary from the opening, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Ribbon ends get soiled fingers, and lacquer end-protection layers let the oil-soluble ribbon dye through during drying and storage.

**Solution:** Ribbon ends are covered with a water-soluble layer (e.g. glue) that does not mix with the oil-based dye and so keeps the colour out.

Soiling of the fingers cannot be avoided when clamping ribbons in the typewriter or when removing them unless special protective measures are taken. For this purpose, paper finger cots were added to the ribbon packs early on. However, this measure does not represent a technical solution. It was therefore proposed to cover the ends of the ribbon against color, either by a covering of cellophane or other foil or by attaching an uncolored piece of ribbon by means of adhesive or sewing. All of these measures are cumbersome and expensive to manufacture. It has therefore been proposed to cover one or both ends of the ribbon with a protective layer of lacquer or the like. With the "pale of this material" two considerations were obviously critical, namely to choose a protective layer that both dries quickly and, due to the density of its application, does not allow the color of the ribbon to pass through.
