## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** A matrix-making machine, using separate keys to bring type-dies to the printing point, needs a way for each key to stop a power-driven type wheel at its die.

**Solution:** Radial key-levers whose arms of three different lengths enter circumferential recesses on a rotating clutch-box and stop its rotation on a lug, which positions the type-die wheel and drives a downward thrust.

**How it works:** A pulley and flywheel continuously rotate an upper clutch-box member. A lower member has three circumferential recesses of diminishing diameter with lugs. Pressing a key moves its lever's short arm into one of the recesses; the arm length (three per group) picks the recess, and the lug stops the lower member, so the type-wheel stops with the chosen die over the printing point. The clutch faces have inclines, so the stopped lower member is forced downward, pressing the die into the matrix body, then springs lift it back. A spring cushion returns the key levers. Type-dies are held in a circle by flat spring arms on a disk under a hub that nuts adjust for height, and a spring moves the matrix carriage.
