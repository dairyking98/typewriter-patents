**Kind:** a part or mechanism (any kind of machine) · **Subject (patent classes):** Ink-ribbon guides

**In the patent’s own words:** My invention relates to type-writers, and 10 more particularly to those machines where a ribbon is employed for effecting the impression, and has for its object to provide a simple and eflicient means for reversing the direction of the ribbon.

**How it works (reading):** A spring-loaded reverse lever is held by a stop that the ribbon's end projection trips, shifting a sliding pawl bar so the other spool's feed pawl engages; the ribbon guide swings on a horizontal pivot so it also moves back and forth.

## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Underwood typebar machines needed a simple, reliable way to reverse the ribbon direction automatically, and the ribbon guide moved only vertically, risking tearing the ribbon when the carriage travelled or paper was released.

**Solution:** A spring-loaded reverse lever is held by a stop that the ribbon's end projection trips, shifting a sliding pawl bar so the other spool's feed pawl engages; the ribbon guide swings on a horizontal pivot so it also moves back and forth.

**How it works:** Each keystroke or space moves the universal bar rearward, which swings the ribbon guide carrier up and back and drives two feed pawls on a sliding bar; only one pawl engages its spool ratchet wheel at a time. When the ribbon unwinds fully from one spool, a projection on the ribbon end hits a ribbon guide on a rockshaft, whose arm releases a spring-pressed lever from its shoulder. The lever shifts the pawl bar sideways, disengaging the old pawl (and its retaining pawl) and engaging the other, reversing the ribbon. Each carriage shift lifts a bar that resets the lever against its stop, ready for the next reversal. A spring latch on the spool holder retains the spool and lets it be detached.
