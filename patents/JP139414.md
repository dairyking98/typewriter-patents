## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Belt fasteners for flat and V-belts are rigid, strain going round pulleys and wear out quickly.

**Solution:** Stack several thin spring plates of different lengths in a stepped pile, rivet them across the butted belt ends, and for V-belts clamp a further plate on the back.

**How it works:** Thin spring plates of graduated length are piled like a trapezoid and laid over the joint of the belt's two ends, then riveted through. The stepped stack stays strong yet each plate flexes, so the fastener follows the belt round a pulley or along a straight run. For a V-belt a single plate is put on the belt's back and the belt is clamped between it and the stack.

**Parts:** spring plate; rivet hole; rivet; belt; pulley; backing plate; V-belt

## Description (English translation of the Japanese text, opening)

> Belt fastener. Nature and object of the invention. The invention relates to a belt fastener made of several thin spring plates of different lengths, stacked in a stepped (trapezoid) pile, laid on top of the two butted ends of a belt and pierced with rivet holes so that it can be riveted to the belt. Its object is a fastener that makes good use of the flexibility of thin spring plates, so that it moves with the belt's shape both when the belt runs round a pulley and when it runs straight between pulleys.
