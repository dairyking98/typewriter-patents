## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Writing by hand is slow; the inventors want a simple, cheap machine that prints neat writing with types instead of a pen.

**Solution:** Piano-style key levers throw radially arranged typebars up against a central point under a platen, while a weight-driven paper carriage is stepped one tooth per keystroke by a forked lever and ratchet bar.

**How it works:** A brass annular disk holds as many steel typebars as there are characters, each pivoted on a wire around the rim and lying in a radial groove, all of equal length so every type meets a single central point. A piano-like keyboard has finger ends under each bar; pressing a key throws its bar up so the type strikes the paper held under a fixed anvil/platen with a spherical cavity, and the bar then falls back onto a cushion. A bar lying across all the keys behind the fulcrum is raised by any key and works a forked lever whose saw-toothed forks engage a double-sided ratchet bar on the carriage, so each keystroke releases the weight-pulled carriage by exactly one letter space. The paper sits in a nested two-frame carriage: the outer frame moves left-right for the line, the inner frame is stepped by a pawl over a row of pins for line spacing. An inking ribbon is carried on two spools, the take-up spool driven by a cord and cone pulleys from the carriage motion.
