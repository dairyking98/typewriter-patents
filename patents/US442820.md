## Summary

**Kind:** type-element machine · **Subject (patent classes):** Typewriters or selective printing mechanisms characterised by the mounting, arrangement or disposition of the types or dies with types or dies mounted on carriers rotatable for selection the plane of the type or die face being parallel to the axis of rotation, e.g. with type on the periphery of cylindrical carriers

**In the patent’s own words:** The present invention relates more especially to the operating of the type-Wheel, to the locking of the same in its exact position for printing, to the spacing of the words and characters, to the locking of the platen, to the raising of the platen to print, to the subsequent automatic release of the same, and to various details of construction.

**How it works (reading):** Each key-lever of equal travel sets a type-wheel by striking a point at its own depth, a tooth-and-arm locking system pins the wheel (and stops rebound), the platen is spaced and locked by racks, and the lever's last portion of travel raises the hinged platen bed to print and lets it drop away.

## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Improves Myers' key-lever wheel typewriter in how the wheel is operated and locked at its exact position, how characters and words are spaced, how the platen is locked, and how it is raised to print and then released.

**Solution:** Each key-lever of equal travel sets a type-wheel by striking a point at its own depth, a tooth-and-arm locking system pins the wheel (and stops rebound), the platen is spaced and locked by racks, and the lever's last portion of travel raises the hinged platen bed to print and lets it drop away.

**How it works:** The type-wheel has three parallel circles of type (capitals, small letters, figures) and is pushed along its shaft by a button to choose a circle, held by a spring catch. A key-lever strikes an inclined rib or adjustable screw on a hinged frame, which through rack, pinions and quadrant rotates the wheel to that key's angle. The same lever pushes a transverse lever that swings a locking arm into a tooth of a stepped locking wheel on the shaft; five narrow wheels with offset teeth give a coarse enough pitch, and the arm stops against a fixed abutment as the right type arrives. The lever also depresses a spacing frame that moves a spacing rack and the sliding platen one character; a pawl then locks the platen. A sliding bar frees the lever shaft to lift, so the lever's remaining travel depresses a platen-raising frame that lifts the hinged bed and presses the paper to the type, then a toe slips off a salient projection so the bed falls before the key is released. A second set of reverse-facing teeth with an arm prevents wheel rebound; an inking roller on spring-pressed arms inks the type.
