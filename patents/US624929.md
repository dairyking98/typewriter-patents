## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** A typebar typewriter in which the platen is vertical, so the line of writing runs down the sheet, with the aim of a compact machine and every typebar striking the same point.

**Solution:** Typebars with two slanted characters each, strung on an arc-shaped shift rod, strike a ribbon holder against a vertical platen cylinder that is rotated by a spring-driven escapement for letter spacing and slid up or down for line spacing.

**How it works:** Each key lever pulls a typebar, strung on an arc-shaped rod, over to strike through a ribbon holder against the paper wrapped round a vertical rubber platen. Shift tilts the arc rod on bell-crank levers to a new centre so the upper character strikes, and a hand finger can cam and lock the shift. After each key the platen is turned a step by a toothed wheel and dogs on a vertical square shaft, driven by volute springs in shell pulleys and a belt, with a margin regulator on a stop wheel and a bell. Line spacing slides the platen carriage vertically on notched side bars with dogs, raised by a spring and cord and released by a finger. Paper is wrapped by turning the platen against spring-band clamps and a rubber feed cylinder. A ribbon on two vertical spools is wound and reversed through miter gears on a sliding cross shaft.
