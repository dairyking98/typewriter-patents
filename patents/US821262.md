## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** On platen-shift typewriters the shift key is stiff because it moves a heavy frame through a rigid linkage, so weak fingers struggle and strong fingers slam the frame against its stops.

**Solution:** Put a spring between the shift key and the shifting frame so the key can be depressed at once and the frame follows under spring force.

**How it works:** Each shift key carries a plate with an oblique cam slot that engages a roller between two arms on a rock shaft. The rock shaft is built as a spindle with two loose sleeves, each carrying an arm and limited in turn by elongated pin holes. A helical spring joins each sleeve to a collar on the spindle. Depressing a key turns its sleeve and winds the spring, and the spring then turns the spindle and the connecting rod to the shifting frame as fast as the frame's inertia allows. Springs are long and weak enough that their force barely rises with wind-up, so the key feels light and the frame cannot gain enough momentum to hammer its stops. With a single-direction shift one spring can be left out.
