## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** After a line is typed the carriage has to be pushed back by hand to its starting position, and the line spacing is a separate operation.

**Solution:** A spring motor with a key-released clutch and strap returns the carriage to the start, and the carriage itself works a line-spacing mechanism.

**How it works:** A spring motor in the rear of a Remington-type frame winds on a vertical shaft, with its outer end tied to a brake-regulating lever so brake pressure on a flanged gear tracks the weakening spring. A stop disk on the shaft is held by a hook lever that a key lever releases. A pulley with the circumference of a line travel is attached by a strap to an arm on the rear carriage bar, so one revolution of the pulley returns the carriage. A single-tooth clutch with spiral surfaces and a shifting lever couples the pulley to the shaft, and a smaller pulley serves shorter lines. A light ribbon spring takes up slack when the carriage is moved by hand. A carriage-operated line-spacing mechanism is also described.
