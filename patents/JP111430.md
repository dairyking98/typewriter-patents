## Summary

**Kind:** index machine · **Subject (patent classes):** Typewriters or selective printing mechanisms characterised by the mounting, arrangement or disposition of the types or dies with types or dies arranged in stationary or sliding cases or frames or upon flexible strips, plates, bars or rods; Japanese-language typewriter

**In the English translation of the patent’s own words:** The object: in a Japanese printing machine with an index plate, to make the machine small, with little sticking out of it in use, simple in construction, and able to print on wide paper although the machine is small.

**How it works (reading):** Link the index pointer to the type drum with paired four-bar linkages, and hold the paper in a curved, two-part meshed frame that swings between the index plate and the machine rear.

## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Earlier Japanese index-plate printing machines were complicated, large or stuck out in use, and could not take wide paper such as Mino paper without folding it.

**Solution:** Link the index pointer to the type drum with paired four-bar linkages, and hold the paper in a curved, two-part meshed frame that swings between the index plate and the machine rear.

**How it works:** The operator moves the index handle so the pointer lies over the wanted character on the index plate. Four-bar linkages above and below the plate, fixed to a shaft and a concentric hollow shaft, move pointer and type mechanism together. A slide piece tied to a bar of the linkage pulls a cord over pulleys onto a cord drum, turning the type drum, and moving its rail carries the drum sideways on a rail. Pressing the printing handle drives a striker that hits the type through a carbon ribbon and the paper; the holder slackens the ribbon and friction against a feed plate on the index plate advances it. The paper lies between upper and lower curved frames carrying meshes with bars half a character wide every 5.5 character spaces, which sets half, one, two or three character spacing; the frame rides on rollers held down by magnet steel and brings the printed face up before the typist's eyes.

## Description (English translation of the Japanese text, opening)

> Specification. Japanese-script printing machine. Nature and object of the invention. A Japanese-script printing machine having (i) a paper frame, curved in an arc centred on the upper front of the machine, which moves between the lower edge of the index plate and the upper rear of the machine and is carried at the rear so that it can move back and forth; and (ii) a connection between the index pointer and the type, made by four-bar linkages above and below the index plate which move together, each fixed at one end to a shaft on the machine and joined at the other end, above to the index pointer and below to the device that moves the type. The object: in a Japanese printing machine with an index plate, to make the machine small, with little sticking out of it in use, simple in construction, and able to print on wide paper although the machine is small.
