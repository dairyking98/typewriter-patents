## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typebar machines with a finger key and type lever per character needed a simpler carriage drive, a tamper-proof removable carriage, and a square platen independent of the feed rolls.

**Solution:** The universal-bar spring also drives the carriage, with a push pawl and retaining pawl on oppositely inclined racks, plus a removable locked carriage and square platen.

**How it works:** Each finger key lever connects through an adjustable serrated connecting rod and bell crank to a type lever pivoted in a bearing on a ring-shaped top frame. The spring that works the universal bar also propels the paper carriage. A push pawl acts on a rack with teeth inclined one way for letter spacing, while a retaining pawl tied to the universal bar acts on an oppositely inclined rack. A follower dog holds the carriage still while a key is struck and locks it when it is lifted out of the machine. Line-space, paper-feed and ribbon-moving mechanisms are also described.
