## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** The operator of typebar machines had to raise the carriage or platen to see the line just printed, interrupting typing.

**Solution:** Mount the ribbon in a tilting frame raised to the print point at each stroke, so the print is exposed after every impression.

**How it works:** Typebars lie in a curved plane below a common centre and swing up to print through an aperture in a plate on a tilting ribbon frame. Levers above the typebars carry the plate, and are tied by rods to the feed lever bar so the key stroke raises the ribbon in front of the print point and then lets it fall, exposing the writing. A light-spring pawl on a ratchet wheel advances the ribbon spools at each throw. The carriage is spring-drum driven, with a rack and feed lever, and paper held by tapes and rollers.
