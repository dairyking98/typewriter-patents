## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Returning typebars rebound off the type rest in a front-strike machine and clash in the basket; resilient rests make this worse.

**Solution:** A curved, pliant but non-resilient tubular pad (wire helix with woven sheaths and leather facing) is cradled in a sheet-metal holder, stretched endwise against an inner rod so it cannot bounce.

**How it works:** The pad is built like flexible gas tubing: a wire helix core, an inner woven sheath, a non-fibrous intermediate layer and an outer woven sheath, with a leather strip cemented on top where the type ends rest. Wooden end plugs are screwed to end tabs of a segmental sheet-metal holder, which also has curved fingers that cradle the pad and stop fore-and-aft sway. A thinner segmental rod runs inside the pad; tightening the end screws stretches the pad so its lower inside surface is drawn against the rod, which backs the pad and stops any part lifting when a typebar hits another part. A stout thread sewn along the underside of the outer sheath helps keep the pad tied to the rod. The holder is screwed to forward arms of the typebar segment.

**Improves on:**

- resilient type rests/cushions on typebar machines (Monarch): non-resilient stretched pad over an inner rod prevents rebound of returning typebars
