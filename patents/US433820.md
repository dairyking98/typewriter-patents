## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** The inventor wants a simple, cheap, durable typewriter whose alignment is practically perfect.

**Solution:** A rocking keyboard swings a type segment through gearing until the depressed key's stem stops it with the right character at the print point, then fires a hammer and steps the carriage.

**How it works:** A segmental disc on a vertical shaft has a downward flange with one rubber or rubber-backed metal type per finger key, set in front of apertures whose inclined partitions guide a hammer. The keyboard is pivoted and held level by a spring stiffer than the key springs, so the first press of any key pushes its stem down below the plate before the board itself tips. As the board tips, a gear train (segment gear, crown gear, segment gear and gear on the shaft) rotates the type segment forward until a yielding arm on its shaft hits the depressed key's stem, which acts as a stop, leaving that key's character at the print point. Further travel moves a lever that throws a spring-retracted hammer through the aperture to press the type against the paper backed by the platen, then pushes a pawl to step the carriage one rack tooth. A separate spacing lever acts on the same lever to advance the carriage without printing, and an inking pad lies in front of the segment, with a bell at line end.
