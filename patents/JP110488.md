## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** In a spring-fed machine whose escapement frees one ratchet tooth per stroke, pushing the carriage back by hand one character is awkward, unmeasured and error-prone.

**Solution:** A spring-returned arm carries a weakly sprung pawl that is held clear of the feed gear until the arm is pressed, then turns the gear back exactly one tooth.

**How it works:** The feed shaft carries a ratchet wheel engaged by a clock-type escapement and a gear on the base rack, driven by a strong feed spring. A bent arm pivoted on the sliding frame is held up against a projection by a strong spring; a pawl pivoted mid-arm has a weak spring toward the gear, but its tail is pressed by the projection so its tip clears the gear. Pressing the arm lets the pawl drop onto the gear and push it round one tooth backward while the escapement's separately sprung hook opens over a sloping tooth face and stops the shaft one tooth later. Releasing the arm lets the strong spring throw it up and the projection lifts the pawl clear again, with no separate release. Finger force on the arm never pushes the frame directly.

## Description (English translation of the Japanese text, opening)

> Specification. Back-spacing device for a printing machine. Nature and object of the invention. This invention relates to a back-spacing device for a printing machine of the following type: a ratchet wheel is fixed on a feed shaft that a spring urges to turn in one direction, and a clock-type intermittent escapement engages it; at each printing stroke the escapement releases the ratchet once, letting the feed shaft turn by one tooth, and so feeding the printing-mechanism part by one character space. The device is characterised in that, beside a gear fixed on the feed shaft, an arm is pivoted on the shaft's bearing frame, sprung strongly in the gear's radial direction and held against a stop; a pawl is pivoted on the middle of the arm and given a weak spring tending to make its tip mesh with the gear; and when the arm is in its position pressed against the stop, the tail of the pawl is pressed by that stop, or by another suitable stop, so that its tip is held clear of the gear's circumference.
