## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Key-wheel typewriters had uneven key touch and stroke, and were heavy, large and made of many parts.

**Solution:** A key stops a scissors pair of stop levers whose combined travel is constant, so the type wheel is turned by a bevel sector during its single advance to the platen, with a star wheel locking it before impact.

**How it works:** The type wheel (three rows) sits on a shaft in a bracket guided at one end by a slot and carried at the other by a bell-crank. A radial key lever raises its inner end into the path of one of two stop levers. As a single driving lever turns the bell-crank, the wheel advances to the printing point. The stop levers swing scissors-wise, linked to a bevel sector that gears with a bevel wheel on the wheel shaft; whichever stop lever the key end blocks, the sector rotates the wheel by a corresponding amount to either side, up to about half a revolution. The two stop levers pinch the key end, preventing rebound. A star wheel with slotted teeth then engages a fixed blade to guide and lock the wheel. The key levers have different end lengths acting on curved bars of the driving lever to equalise stroke, a space lever feeds the carriage by a pawl-and-rack when the key is released, a spring-loaded ink roller is pushed aside by the descending wheel, and two keys move the carriage sideways to select the second or third row.
