## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Earlier pocket typewriters needed both hands, or their type wheel could not rotate fully and had to be reciprocated through large angles.

**Solution:** A continuously rotatable finger-type wheel on a hinged plate; pressing the plate down prints and a toothed disc with ratchet gear locks the wheel in exact position.

**How it works:** A bed-plate carries a hinged plate holding the type wheel on its underside, the wheel's rim divided into radial fingers with rubber types on their lower faces. The knob turns the wheel and an indicator over a stationary disc to bring a character to the printing position. Pressing the plate down presses the wheel against an annular ink pad (cut away at the print place) and a slotted lever on the bed-plate, engaged by a pin on the plate, is depressed against the finger at the printing position so its type prints. The same motion drives a slide through a link whose pointed end enters the deep teeth of a spur wheel on the wheel shaft, correcting the position exactly. Paper is advanced by a roller; the machine is held in the left hand by a handle.
