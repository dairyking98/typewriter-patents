## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Typebars in a downstrike typewriter need a simple, cheap bearing and an easy way to adjust the throw of each bar.

**Solution:** Thin plate typebars pivot in slots sawn into a segment plate and are linked to key levers by threaded, hooked pull-rods that can be unhooked and lengthened or shortened to adjust the throw.

**How it works:** Thin plate typebars stand obliquely in slots cut into the top of a segment plate, their journals resting in the lower ends of inclined slots. A spring from an outer horn to the segment flange holds each bar up. An inner horn takes a hooked rod whose lower end screws into a threaded socket with a hook engaging a strap on the key lever. Pressing a key pulls the bar down to print. The bar is unhooked by hand and the socket screwed in or out to adjust throw. A modification shows two superposed sets of bars on a double segment.

**Features:** [Typebars](../features/elem-typebar.md), [Full keyboard, a key per character](../features/sel-keyboard-full.md), [Element struck to the platen](../features/imp-element-to-platen.md), [Adjustment and wear take-up](../features/frm-adjustment-wear.md)

**Parts:** typebar; segment plate; key lever; pull rod; threaded socket; return spring; type
