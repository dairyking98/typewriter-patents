## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Compact travel typewriters put the printing point far back, so the writing is poorly visible, and the platen needs near-horizontal shifting.

**Solution:** Arrange the typebar pivots on an arc in a plane inclined about 30 degrees from vertical, near-tangent to the platen, so printing is near the front and case shift only needs lifting or lowering the platen.

**How it works:** Key levers converge at a point and connect through angle levers and pull rods to short typebars with at least three types each. The pivots lie on a 90-degree arc in the inclined plane, so the striking point is just above the platen's horizontal midplane. The platen is raised or lowered between three positions (double shift) using its weight. Angle levers are mounted on brackets raised outward to compensate height, and the lever ratio is large at the start of the stroke and smaller at impact.
