## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Ingersoll wants a simple, cheap, effective type-writer in which selecting and printing a character is done by one hand.

**Solution:** A slide with pointer is moved along an index plate, turning a type-wheel on a spirally grooved rod to the chosen character, and pressing the hinged frame down locks the wheel with a pin, prints, and advances the paper one step.

**How it works:** A hinged frame, held up by a spring over a bed plate, carries a spiral-grooved rod with a rubber-type wheel at one end and an index plate with the characters. An indicating slide with a pointer and handle runs along the rod and its pin in the spiral groove turns the rod and wheel as it moves, so the wheel position follows the pointer along the index. Pressing the frame down brings the wheel against the paper on a support in the bed; as it descends the wheel teeth engage a rigid locking pin on the bed to align the type exactly, and spring arms on the slide let the wheel give a little to find the pin. An ink roller on a spring arm inks the wheel. The descent also drives a spring dog on the paper holder ratchet to move the paper one step. A blank space on the wheel lets the pointer off the characters feed the paper without printing.
