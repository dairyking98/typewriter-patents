## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Ordinary typewriters are poorly suited to writing on bound book leaves, pamphlets, postal cards and envelopes, or on paper of any width.

**Solution:** A small print head travels over a clamped flat plate: a pivoted curved type segment with a type band is slid by an indicator arm over a letter chart, and one printing lever punches the type down through a slot onto the paper while also stepping the head along a toothed bar.

**How it works:** The material is clamped between a plate and a frame, and a transverse frame carrying a toothed feed bar is locked at the chosen line by cam-faced rock-shaft clamps on guide flanges. A standard travels on the feed bar and carries a semicircular index plate above a hinged type segment with a slotted flange, over which a type band with a raised line of type is held by clasps. The operator moves a finger-piece on the indicator arm over the index plate letter, which brings the matching type under the punching bar. Depressing the printing lever pushes a gravity feed dog one tooth along the bar and presses the punching bar through the slot onto the type and paper. A rod beside the punch drops into a notch in the flange, one per type, to hold the segment still, and also limits punch travel against the plate. Spring return restores everything, and inking pads on a frame that travels with the head ink the neighbouring types; a half stroke or a blank in the band gives a space.
