## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A Hammond-type machine with an oscillating type segment needs a simple way to keep its inking rollers supplied with ink and to stop the paper touching any type but the one being printed.

**Solution:** Hammer-synchronised inking rollers sweep across the character at the impression point and then press on a ratcheted rotating ink platen, while a spring-lifted thin shield with a window protects the paper.

**How it works:** The type segment turns to bring a character to the impression point, as in the Hammond. The rock-shaft that carries the impression hammer also swings a bell-crank that lifts a yoke of inking rollers from below the type, across the face of the selected character, and up against a rotary ink platen above the segment. As the lever returns, a shoulder on an arm strikes a pin on a pawl carrier, so a spring pawl turns the platen's ratchet and presents fresh ink surface. A thin metal shield with an opening is held up by a spring behind the selected character, and the roller lever pushes it down to its rest position. The ink is lithographic transfer ink, so the output can be transferred to a stone or aluminium plate.
