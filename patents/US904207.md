## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** On Oliver machines with extra-long carriages for wide paper, the long carriage guide-bars project beyond the frame and flex under the carriage weight.

**Solution:** Auxiliary standards with oscillating support arms hold the guide-bar ends, rising and falling like the shift-frame's suspension links, and oblique brace rods stop sideways flexing.

**How it works:** The paper carriage rolls on two guide-bars fixed to a shift-frame that swings fore and aft on pivoted links, rising slightly at each end of its swing. Four standards on a base-board each carry a vertically adjustable head with a pivot pin. An oscillating arm, pivoted to the head, supports the outer end of a guide-bar. Two set-screws at the arm's foot, spaced either side of the pin, make the arm rock on one then the other, so it lifts as it swings, matching the shift-frame's rise. Oblique brace rods from the rear of the shift-frame guide stem to the front guide-bar prevent horizontal bending.
