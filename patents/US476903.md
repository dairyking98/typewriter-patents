## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typebar machines needed an inking ribbon, and each typebar normally carried only one or two characters.

**Solution:** A typebar that slides and rotates in a pivoted carrier turns its type against an ink pad on every return stroke and can present one of several characters selected by a plate.

**How it works:** Each typebar runs through a pivoted carrier and a pin riding in a diagonal groove rotates the bar as it moves lengthwise, so the type faces an ink pad on its way back from the platen; no ribbon is used. A spring around the bar returns it lengthwise. For multiple characters, a shiftable selecting plate with curved paths in different planes decides how far the bar projects, or in a second form a mitre pinion on the bar meshes a fixed sector and a stop lever limits its turn, bringing one of several types on a segmental block to the print point. Locking pins catch lateral projections on the bar near the platen for alignment. Side details cover a removable platen with a screw-and-journal mount for swapping hard and soft rubber, and a zinc cup-and-celluloid key mounted on a bifurcated spindle.

**Features:** [ELEM:typebar](../features/elem-typebar.md), [SEL:keyboard-full](../features/sel-keyboard-full.md), [INK:pad](../features/ink-pad.md), [ELEM:alignment-lock](../features/elem-alignment-lock.md), [IMP:element-to-platen](../features/imp-element-to-platen.md), [PAP:platen-roller](../features/pap-platen-roller.md), [SHIFT:axial](../features/shift-axial.md), [FRM:cleaning-access](../features/frm-cleaning-access.md)

**Parts:** typebar; typebar carrier; diagonal groove; guide pin; return spring; ink pad; selecting plate; segmental type block; mitre pinion; fixed sector; stop lever; locking pin; removable platen; key-lever; zinc key cap

**Numbers:** three pins and teeth on the mitre pinion; two characters per bar illustrated

**Improves on:**

- ordinary ribbon typebar machines: prints direct from inked type with a rotating bar
