## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** On ordinary typewriters a wrong key prints at once and has to be erased, and letters of different widths on block-letter type cannot be spaced evenly or inked cleanly.

**Solution:** Selecting a letter and printing it are split into two steps: the key only sets the type wheel and the width-proportioned carriage stop, and the letter is not struck until the next key is touched, so a wrong key can be cancelled first.

**How it works:** Two stacked type discs (small letters above, capitals below) sit on one shaft and are lifted by a capital key to choose the case. A spring drum turns the shaft until an arm hits a stop pin that the pressed key has pushed up in a fixed disc, so each key sets a different angle; a toothed frame then clamps the shaft. A second disc of stop pins, whose pin positions differ by letter width, similarly lets the spring-driven paper carriage advance a width-proportioned distance. A horizontal plunger, triggered when the next key is struck, presses the paper against the wheel, which is inked by a roller in an ink cylinder rather than a ribbon. A correction rod holds the carriage and wheel so the wrong pin can be lowered and the right key hit. A hand lever re-tensions the spring drum, returns the carriage and advances the line.
