## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typewriters print one letter per key; the inventor wants keys that print whole syllables or two or three letters at one stroke, which needs the types to print evenly across a wider face.

**Solution:** Keys that print one, two or three characters at once act on wheel sectors (or one integral frame) whose multi-letter rows have small flat facets, with a sliding aperture plate and a hammer of adjustable width matching the number of letters struck.

**How it works:** The type frame is made of horizontal wheel sectors mounted on a vertical axle; each sector's hub has a hollow extension in which a ball-ended rod slides, engaging a triple-armed angular lever so a key swings that sector left or right to bring the chosen character to the print position. Balancing support levers under the key rows lift the sectors to the correct writing height, and the same lift raises a plate with apertures of one, two and three letter widths so the right aperture sits at the print point. The single-letter rows have curved printing faces, while syllable and multi-letter rows have small flat facets so that the middle letter does not print harder than the outer ones. A hammer made of one rigid piece and two tilting pieces, each one letter wide, strikes one, two or three letters according to a notched bar that either tilts the extra pieces out of action or lets them strike. A variant uses a single integral type frame with a horizontally sliding plate, and carriage shift by lever and detent gives capitals and figures.
