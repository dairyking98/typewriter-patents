## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A pad-inked, upstrike typewriter of the cheap class needs a way to see the writing, a steady carriage drive, and typebars that print at the same spot without lateral play.

**Solution:** A circular-frame typebar machine in which the paper carriage tilts back on a key press to expose the writing, a cam-wheel spring gives even carriage pull, and a rack-and-dog escapement is tripped by a universal bar.

**How it works:** Typebars are pivoted on removable radial blocks around a circular support; each bar is two sheet-metal straps straddling its block, so the block guides it sideways, with a type-carrying arm pivoted at the free end, driven by a link from a key lever. Eight key levers are nested concentrically as tubes on one axis to save space. Pressing any key depresses a universal bar that rocks a tilting bar and pushes a rod up under a toothed rack hinged to the carriage, shifting the rack from the movable dog to the fixed dog and letting the carriage step one tooth under its spring pull. The spring acts through a cord, a wheel and a cam wheel so the pull stays uniform as the spring weakens. A key near the keyboard tilts the whole carriage on its rod so the printed line shows, and a weighted pointer marks the print point. The platen is stepped by a spring plunger and dog on a ratchet, double or single space with a latch.
