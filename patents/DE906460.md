## Summary

**Kind:** typebar machine · **Subject (patent classes):** Character-by-character adjustment

**How it works (reading):** Make each drive unit self-contained: swivel arms and grippers are carried on a height-adjustable cross rail and are not coupled to the intermediate levers when at rest.

## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** In power typewriters the swivel arms with cam discs were fixed to the intermediate levers, so drive units could not be installed or aligned independently.

**Solution:** Make each drive unit self-contained: swivel arms and grippers are carried on a height-adjustable cross rail and are not coupled to the intermediate levers when at rest.

**How it works:** A continuously turning drive roller faces cam discs on swivel arms. In rest position grippers, released by the key levers, hold the cams away from the roller. The swivel arms are carried on a support axis of the cross rail, and grippers on articulated arms forming a knee joint, supported on a support rod of the rail. When a key is pressed the cam engages the roller and the arm moves the intermediate lever toggle to drive the typebar. Raising the rail adjusts the type stroke for all units from one point. Two kinds of release devices make the stroke depend on or independent of key hold time.
