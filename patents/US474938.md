## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** On the Yost typewriter the carriage could not be freed from the letter-spacing dogs quickly, either to run it back under its spring or to push it right without the rack riding over the feed dog.

**Solution:** A release rod hung from the feed rack by two oblique links moves down and sideways when pushed, depressing the feed dog out of the rack.

**How it works:** A key or space-bar press rocks a frame so a rigid holding dog enters the rack while a spring-loaded loose feed dog is withdrawn and steps one tooth right; on release the stronger carriage spring pulls the carriage left by one tooth. A release rod hangs parallel to the vertical feed rack on two oblique links, with a push-button stem through a slot in a frame and a return spring. Pushing the button moves the rod right, and the oblique links make it also drop, bearing on the feed dog and forcing it out of the rack. A projection on the dog slips behind a stop so the dog spring cannot drive it forward, so it re-engages at once when the button is let go. The release works with any dogs that vibrate in two perpendicular planes.

**Features:** [ESC:pawl-rack](../features/esc-pawl-rack.md), [ESC:carriage-release](../features/esc-carriage-release.md), [ESC:spring-drum](../features/esc-spring-drum.md), [ESC:space-key](../features/esc-space-key.md)

**Parts:** release rod; oblique link; feed rack; feed dog; holding dog; rocking frame; dog spring; push-button stem; slotted frame; carriage spring drum

**Numbers:** dog spring moves feed dog one letter space

**Improves on:**

- Yost typewriter (GB 5136 of 1889): adds a release key acting directly on the feed dog
