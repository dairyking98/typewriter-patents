## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Typewriters have too many complicated parts and cannot travel over a book or ledger, so they cannot be used for bookkeeping on bound or fixed material.

**Solution:** A light hand-held casing with endless type bands, selected by a key moved over a dial plate against stops, that is pressed down onto the paper to print and travels on parallel rods over the work.

**How it works:** A metal casing open at the bottom carries three endless type bands (capitals, small letters, figures/punctuation) on pairs of wheels. A key rising through a circular opening in a dial plate printed with characters is moved laterally and down until its pointer meets a stop of graded height under the character; this drives a rack bar and pinion that turn the selected band wheel until that character is at the bottom opening. A shaft with splines, moved by a lever, clutches one of the three wheels to the pinion, and friction springs hold the unused bands still. Pressing the key further pushes the whole casing down against springs until the type touches the paper; spring rubber cushions return the key. On rising, a pawl turns a pinion on a toothed rod to space the carriage a word space, or a thumb lever limits the pawl to a letter space. Spring-loaded ink rollers bear on the bands, and the casing runs on rods resting on the paper with a straight-edge guide.
