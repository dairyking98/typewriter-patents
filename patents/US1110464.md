## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Stencil-cutting typewriters for address cards needed a quick way to feed separate flat cards from a hopper to the printing line, space them, and discharge them without bending.

**Solution:** A Remington typebar stencil machine whose carriage carries a card hopper and two endless spring-armed bands that carry each flat card to the platen, step it line by line from the carriage-return handle, and shoot it out into a receptacle.

**How it works:** Cards stand on end in a sloped hopper on the carriage and a spring-driven follower pushes them toward a discharge opening. Two endless bands over two drums carry spring arms with hooks; guides flex the arms so the hooks engage the bottom of the foremost card and carry it flat round the drum to a felt platen, where needle-faced typebars punch the stencil. The bands are turned by a finger-piece rock shaft, link, bell crank and rack acting on a pinion, crank arm and pawl, which engages a mutilated line-spacing wheel (two sets of three teeth with dwells) and a spring-loaded controlling disc. The disc changes the pawl's lost motion, so successive pulls of the handle give line spaces between lines and a longer feed to bring in a new card, and the last advance releases the arms so a finished card is shot out. A pawl on the carriage and a toothed stop bar arrest the carriage return at progressively different points on successive lines for address blocks.
