## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A motor-driven Japanese typewriter cannot vary striking force to suit dense kanji versus sparse kana, so printing is patchy.

**Solution:** A ball governor on the motor shaft, set by a hand-worked regulating handle, changes motor speed and so the printing lever's momentum.

**How it works:** The motor shaft drives a sliding-clutch print mechanism that is latched and released by a push-rod after each revolution. A ball governor on the motor main shaft has its opening set by a sliding regulating plate, pushed by a bell crank linked to a handle at the side of the frame. The typist moves the type tray with the right hand, presses the push-rod to print, and swings the handle with the left palm according to character density. Moving the plate one way slows the motor, the other way speeds it, so the strike is lighter or harder.

## Description (English translation of the Japanese text, opening)

> Nature and object of the invention. An electric typewriter characterised in that a regulator is fitted to the motor drive shaft and linked to a regulating handle, and by working the handle to control the regulator the speed of the motor shaft is quickly raised or lowered, changing the working inertia of the printing lever, so that the striking force can be made stronger or weaker at will. The object is an electric typewriter in which, just as in the hand-worked Japanese typewriter, the striking force can be adjusted deliberately to suit the density of a type's strokes, to give clear printing.
