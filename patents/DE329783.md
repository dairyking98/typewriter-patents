## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Type wheels are held on the axle by a threaded nut and rubber washer plus a driver; changing a wheel is slow, and a loose nut lets the wheel turn and print wrong characters.

**Solution:** A cross wedge pin through the top of a plain axle engages a groove in the wheel top, with a compression spring pressing the wheel onto the wedge, so wheels are changed by pushing down and turning, with no nuts.

**How it works:** The steel axle has no lugs; a collar is pinned to it and a hole drilled across its top holds a wedge pin. The type wheel has a cross groove: one groove runs through the full bore length, the other is a shallow cut in the top face that receives the wedge. A compression spring on the axle sits on the collar and pushes the wheel up against the wedge, so the wedge in the shallow groove prevents rotation. To remove the wheel, push it down against the spring until the shallow groove clears the wedge, turn it so the through-groove lines up with the wedge, and lift it off. Fitting is the reverse.

**Features:** [Element mounting on its hub](../features/elem-hub-keying.md), [Interchangeable element](../features/elem-interchangeable.md), [Cleaning access](../features/frm-cleaning-access.md), [Few parts, low cost](../features/frm-few-parts.md)
