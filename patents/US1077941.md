## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** On shifting-platen typebar machines, the platen carriage binds, makes noise and tilts when shifted, so the two ends do not move equal distances.

**Solution:** Carry the platen carriage on an oscillating ball rail with a rock-shaft and paired arms so both ends shift equally, with a swiveling feed rack that stays meshed with the escapement pinion.

**How it works:** Typebars carrying three types each strike a flat vertical platen strip on the platen carriage. The carriage rides on a middle oscillating rail via balls in V-shaped runways between two spaced carriage rails, one with a curved bearing face so the rail can rock without binding. A rock-shaft with arms at both ends is attached to the oscillating rail and forces equal movement of both carriage ends when a central shift lever lifts or depresses a rod. An elliptic spring under the rod balances the carriage weight. The feed rack hangs on swinging arms and rides on a roller so it stays in mesh with the escapement feed pinion in every shift position.

**Features:** [Shift by moving the platen](../features/shift-platen.md), [Typebars](../features/elem-typebar.md), [Pawl-and-rack escapement](../features/esc-pawl-rack.md), [Roller platen](../features/pap-platen-roller.md), [Adjustment and wear take-up](../features/frm-adjustment-wear.md), [Three or more cases](../features/shift-triple.md)

**Parts:** platen carriage; flat platen strip; oscillating rail; ball runway; rock-shaft; balancing arm; tie rod; shift lever; elliptic spring; feed rack; feed pinion; roller

**Numbers:** three types per typebar

**Improves on:**

- earlier shifting platen carriages: no lost motion or binding, equal movement at both ends, quieter
