## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** Margin gages and similar adjustable stops on a rack need a simple, cheap, easily applied way of locking and releasing them.

**Solution:** A T-lever carrying a rack-engaging dog is rocked either way on a stud to free the dog, and continued push slides the stop along the rack.

**How it works:** A rack on the carriage carries slidable margin-gage members, each with a dog that fits between the rack teeth. The dog sits on a bar loose on two tapered studs, with a handle forming the stem of a T. Pressing the handle left or right rocks the bar about the stud on that side, lifting the other end and the dog out of the rack, and further pressure slides the member. Compression springs around the studs return the bar. The members carry stops for letter-feed and return travel limits, a bell striker tongue and a key-locking tongue.

**Features:**

- **Spacing and escapement:** [Margin stops](../features/esc-margin-stop.md), [Line-end bell](../features/esc-bell.md), [Pawl-and-rack escapement](../features/esc-pawl-rack.md)
- **Frame and body:** [Few parts, low cost](../features/frm-few-parts.md)

**Parts:** rack; margin-gage member; dog; T-lever; handle; stud; compression spring; stop; bell striker; key-locking member

**Improves on:**

- existing margin-gage locking devices: single T-lever both releases and slides the stop
