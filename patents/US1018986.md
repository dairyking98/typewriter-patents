## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typebar bearings loosen with wear, causing rattle and misalignment, and the return-spring tension needs adjusting without threaded parts so the bar returns at the right speed (too strong and it returns before printing, too weak and bars pile up).

**Solution:** Typebars pivot on adjustable cone bearings in upper and lower rails, and each return coil spring is wound or unwound by turning a friction-held split pin in a channel rail that also stiffens the frame.

**How it works:** Each typebar sits in a cone bearing in an upper and a lower rail, and a cone can be adjusted to a firm seat as it wears. A slotted stem at the bar's lower end hooks one end of a helical coil spring, whose other end hooks into the notched reduced stem of a pin standing in a channel rail across the bottom of the frame. The pin has a split body whose two spring leaves press against the rail holes, holding it by friction against the spring's pull. To change return speed, a tool turns the pin in the rail, winding or unwinding the spring while the bar is held, with no screws needed. The channel rail runs side to side and reinforces the type-supporting frame.
