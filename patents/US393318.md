## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A simple index-type typewriter needs a way to bring any of several rows of a rotary type wheel to the print line accurately, with printing, letter spacing and word spacing from few parts.

**Solution:** Finger-pieces on a rack slide are moved along an index board above a depressible key-bar; the rack turns a multi-row type wheel by gear, and pressing the key-bar swings the platen carriage to the wheel while a taper pin locks the gear in a guide hole.

**How it works:** The operator puts a finger between two finger-pieces on a slide and moves them along an index board of characters. The slide carries a rack that meshes with a gear on the vertical shaft of the type wheel, so the wheel turns to approximately the chosen character. Pressing the key-bar (which has a depression under each character) swings the hinged platen carriage against the type wheel, and a taper pin on the lever frame enters one of a circle of holes in the gear to correct the angular position exactly before the impression. A pivoted single-tooth feed dog on the carriage engages a fixed rack and steps the carriage one tooth on each print stroke; a separate spacing lever vibrates the carriage against an adjustable stop so it spaces without printing. The wheel has four rows (lower case, capitals, italics, figures and punctuation) and is raised or lowered on its shaft by a lever and a cam-driven rocking frame operated by two buttons. Two spring-held ink rolls ride on the type and move up and down with the wheel.
