## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Key-to-typebar linkage in a front-strike machine should be simple, easy to take apart, and give an elastic, uniform touch across the keyboard.

**Solution:** Each key lever drives its typebar through a tilting lever and an L-shaped lever with a slightly compressible link, all pivoting on removable slotted beams.

**How it works:** A key lever's rear end raises the front end of a tilting lever, which depresses the horizontal arm of an L-shaped intermediate lever. The L-lever's upright arm swings forward and pushes a link that lifts the typebar, which has a rearward and downward extension, to the platen. Play in the joints and the compressibility of the link give an elastic touch. Key levers, tilting levers and L-levers each pivot on a pin through slots in their own removable beam, so a whole set or a single lever can be lifted out. The L-levers differ in length from center to edge to match the typebar fan.
