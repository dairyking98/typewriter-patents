## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Silent typewriters with few keys and grouped type need to shift the carriage to several positions without the noise of parts striking stops.

**Solution:** Shift the carriage through a lever whose rollers run in a fixed cam groove with a dwell, so the parts stay in constant engagement and the cam itself fixes each shifted position.

**How it works:** The carriage slides vertically in guides and is held up by a spring. A rock shaft driven by the shift levers swings a double-armed lever through a link. The long arm carries a roller that runs in a fixed cam groove with a slight dwell, moving the carriage to the second position, where the dwell stops it. Further rotation by the other shift lever lifts that roller out and engages the short-arm roller in the groove, giving the third position. When the levers are released the spring returns the carriage. Because carriage and shifting parts stay engaged throughout, there is no impact, and the groove and steel rollers are hardened to cut wear.
