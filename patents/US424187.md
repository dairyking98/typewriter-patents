## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** On Hammond-type machines, shifting characters by hand takes a hand off printing, wastes time and obstructs the view of the keyboard.

**Solution:** Knee-operated levers under the typewriter stand work the capital shift, spacing and figures shift, and a foot treadle returns the carriage.

**How it works:** Bell-crank levers hinged under the stand are connected by rods to the capital-shift key and the space key, and are pressed by the operator's knees. A looped rod lets the space lever return slowly. A third horizontal lever with adjustable fulcrum and link holes is raised by the knee to pull down the figure-shift key. A floor treadle with cord, sheave and clamp standard pulls the carriage tripping lever to return the carriage.

**Features:** [Attachment or accessory](../features/use-accessory.md), [Three or more cases](../features/shift-triple.md), [Space key or bar](../features/esc-space-key.md), [Carriage return](../features/esc-carriage-return.md)

**Parts:** bell-crank lever; connecting rod; looped link; knee lever; treadle; cord; sheave; clamp standard; return spring
