## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** Typewriters need many keys; fewer keys or wider use of each key would speed up writing.

**Solution:** A compound key-lever carries a spring type at its rear end plus extra types on the lever, so a light press prints one letter and a harder press prints a short word.

**How it works:** The key lever of a Sholes and Glidden or similar machine has a recess at its rear end and a steel spring attached beneath it, whose movable end carries a type. The lever end carries one or two types beside the spring type. A light touch prints only the spring letter, while greater force pushes the spring down so the combination spells a common word, for example f on the spring with o and r on the lever gives for. Spacing for the extra letters is arranged by lever bars. The keys are labelled with the initial letter large and the complement small.
