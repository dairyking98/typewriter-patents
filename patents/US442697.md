## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typewriters hide the writing; the inventor wants an upstrike-free machine that leaves the top of the platen open and in view, with a direct vertical blow and a smooth carriage.

**Solution:** Carry type blocks on horizontal bars hung from two unequal links (parallel-ruler motion) so each lifts from an ink pad and comes down vertically on top of a shifting platen mounted on a single ball track.

**How it works:** Each key lever swings the rear link of a type bar, which stays horizontal while it lifts, projects and drops its three-type block onto the top of the platen; return springs and counter-springs keep the linkage taut and the touch soft. Type blocks rest on horizontal ink pads on either side of the platen, which is itself a rubber-covered roller. Shift is done by tilting the platen on oscillating standards with a shift key, so the lower, middle or upper type of each block strikes. A spring-driven carriage is released a tooth at a time by a pivoted rack and a pair of pawls that the typing keys and a space bar rock, with a bell near the line end. A thumb-piece on the platen both releases the rack and feeds the paper line to line, and sprung wire cages hold the paper.
