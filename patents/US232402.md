## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Hammond's earlier machines moved the type segment through lifting frames and arrested it with serrations on the segment edge, which was complicated and less certain in holding the segment exactly at the hammer blow.

**Solution:** Notched key levers act as eccentric cams on an oscillating rod to rotate the type segment, and a pin raised by each key stops an arm on the segment shaft so the segment is held exactly where the letter faces the hammer.

**How it works:** Each key lever carries a notch whose incline crosses a fixed cross rod at a slight angle, so keys further from the centre push the rod further, right-hand keys one way and left-hand keys the other. The rod swings a vibrating shaft whose arm and rod turn a lever with a toothed sector, meshing with a gear on the vertical shaft of the type segment and rotating it from its zero point to the key's letter. The same key raises a spring-returned pin into the path of a stop arm on the segment shaft, which halts and holds the segment; springs and a solid backing piece quiet the lever. A bar lifted by any key trips a dog that cocks and releases a spring hammer, which strikes the paper against the type and rebounds; a screw adjusts the spring and so the blow force. The bar also rocks the escapement pawl for step-by-step carriage spacing, a central key spaces without printing, and the ribbon winds between two spools with a reversing gear. Letters on the segment are arranged by frequency outward from the centre.
