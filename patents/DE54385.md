## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** The blind need an easy apparatus to produce raised letters on paper that is also better than a typewriter where every letter has the same centre-to-centre pitch.

**Solution:** Two coaxial discs, one with raised letters on its upper edge and the other with matching matrices, are turned by the finger of the blind user to a stop and then self-centred and embossed through the paper by a lifted punch, the paper advancing by each letter's width.

**How it works:** A lower disc (male) near its edge has holes guiding punches; an upper disc (female) has the matrices opposite, and on its top edge raised letters that the blind person feels. The finger finds the letter and turns the disc until the finger meets a fixed stop. A step lever raises a rod whose angled cutout plate first nudges the nearest punch pin sideways by its sloping surface, so the wheel is exactly aligned. The punch then rises, and a claw swinging in a slotted link takes it through the paper clamped between punch and matrix, embossing the letter. The paper is advanced by a device that moves according to each letter width, making proportional spacing possible. The same machine could be built as a die-stamping or ordinary type machine.
