## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Choosing characters is hard on Japanese typewriters with very large character sets; loose heavy types are cumbersome and the machines heavy and complex.

**Solution:** Keep all characters right-reading on one flat stereotype-like plate, move a pointer frame over it by hand, and print through an endless transfer band that takes ink from the type face and deposits it on the paper cylinder.

**How it works:** A flat type plate lies on the base with characters in rows and columns. A frame sliding forward-back and left-right on rails carries the print handle, a pointer, an ink roller, the transfer lever and the paper cylinder. The operator slides the frame until the pointer sits over the wanted character and presses the handle: the pointer lever swings away, the ink roller inks the face, and the transfer lever descends so an endless band on its tip picks up the inked character. A one-way grooved wheel with ratchet makes the band step along as the lever rises and falls, so the character is carried to the lever's upper face and struck onto the paper on the next rise. A cleaner and wipers clean the band. In a variant a toggle-like arm and spring snap the lever down and up so the blow is spring-driven and independent of hand force; the paper cylinder is stepped by a pawl on each stroke and a line-space handle advances a toothed bar.

## Description (English translation of the Japanese text, opening)

> Specification. Transfer printing machine. Nature and object of the invention. On the base of this machine lies a flat, level type plate (印盤), like a stereotype plate, on which many characters are engraved right-reading, not in mirror image. Above it is a framework that can move forward, back, left and right, carrying the printing mechanism, the other mechanisms and the paper-cylinder frame. When the handle of this mechanism is pressed, transfer ink is spread on the type face; and a transfer lever, which carries an endless transfer band, moves down, touches the plate and prints the character onto the band. When the handle returns, the lever moves up and strikes the paper wound on the paper cylinder, transferring the character printed on the band to the paper. The object is to remove the difficulty of choosing characters, make the machine easy to handle, light and simple, and yet fit it to carry a very large number of characters.
