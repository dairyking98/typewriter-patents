## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Platen shift mechanisms driven by a swinging shift key lever were noisy and gave no way to adjust the shifted platen position without disturbing the normal position or the key height.

**Solution:** Shift keys whose free-end rollers run in cam grooves on a cam piece rotate a shaft that raises or lowers the platen rail, with key-lever pivots (eccentric rolls on slidable blocks) that can be adjusted to vary the shift distance without moving the key.

**How it works:** Two key levers carry rollers at their free ends, set in two cam grooves of a cam piece fixed to a shaft. Depressing one key swings its roller through the groove and turns the cam and shaft. A lever on the shaft pushes a link up or down to a vertical rail that carries the platen carriage on ball bearings. One key raises the platen to the second character of a three-character typebar and the other lowers it to the third, while the neutral position prints the first. Each key lever is carried on a fixed pin and an eccentric pivot roll that can be set or slid in a curved slot. This shifts the curved path of the roller, changing the shift distance without changing the key level. Further modifications use other cam and lever arrangements, the whole being designed to work quietly.
