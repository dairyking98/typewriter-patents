## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** On a type-wheel machine with two sets of key levers each driving a wheel-mover, keys of both sets or several keys of one set pressed together can jam or mis-set the wheel; wheel gearing also wears when the wheel is raised for case shift.

**Solution:** Two toothed wheel-movers joined by a central pinion lock each other out, a flier limits same-set interference, a star-wheel and dog snap the wheel exactly into position, and the type wheel lifts on a splined sleeve for capitals without moving its gears.

**How it works:** Each key lever has a cam end that pushes a roller on a lateral arm of its wheel-mover, farther out for keys farther from the centre, turning the mover's toothed segment and, through a central segment and pinion, rotating the type wheel to the right character opposite the hammer. When one mover is pushed back, a segmental disk on the pinion swings over a pin on the other mover and locks it, so keys of the other set are dead until release. A flier on the pinion strikes the raised end of the pressed lever, locking keys beyond it and removing the arm from the path of the others in the same set. A dog operated by a cam plate through the key lever's continued travel engages a star-wheel on the wheel's pinion to lock the type wheel exactly in place and correct slight over-run. The type wheel sits on a splined sleeve lifted by capital and figure keys through a yoke, so it rises without sliding on the gears.
