## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** Front-stroke typebar machines need a light key touch with a type-bar that starts slowly and accelerates to the printing point.

**Solution:** A flexing toggle connection from each type-bar heel to a fixed point, operated by the key stem, throws the bar by a pull; variants let a quick key impulse print before full depression.

**How it works:** Type-bars pivot in a segment and lie nearly horizontal. A two-link flexing connection runs from the bar heel to a fixed point, and the key stem's eye embraces one link near the hinge. Depressing the key flexes the joint and the bar is pulled to the printing point, with minimal initial resistance and rising velocity. In Fig. 6 a roller in a loop with free margin lets a quick impulse throw the bar before the key bottoms; in Fig. 7 a swivelled link follows the arc of the front link. Also described: shifting type-bar support for case, universal bar, movable type-bar guide, and a vibrating ribbon carrier.
