## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Yost typebar machine whose type strike the underside of the platen so the writing is hidden; also criticises line-space regulators that disturb the platen, uneven carriage draft, and scales hidden under the platen.

**Solution:** A full-keyboard typebar machine with a hinged platen axle/guide rail, a lever that rotates the platen to bring the hidden line into view, a central-draft spring carriage with a hinged-dog rack escapement, and side-by-side reversed scales.

**How it works:** Key levers raise typebars of unequal length, arranged in two rows, to strike a ribbon against the underside of the platen at a common point. Pressing any key also vibrates a frame carrying a hinged knife-edged dog that moves from a movable rack to a fixed rack, so on release the carriage advances one tooth under a spring-cam and strap acting in the plane of the platen axis. A lever with pawl rotates the platen a little over a quadrant to show the line being written, and can return it exactly. A carriage lever with a two-position regulator rotates the platen by one or two ratchet teeth for line spacing. The ribbon spools lie parallel to the platen and are driven by a pawl and ratchet on the spring-cam, with a gear shaft for choosing direction and a handle for traversing the ribbon sideways.

**Parts:** typebar; key lever; platen; guide rail; paper carriage; fixed rack; movable rack; hinged dog; vibrating frame; spring cam; carriage strap; ratchet wheel; carriage lever; line-space regulator; platen-rotating lever; ribbon spool; bell; scale; pointer

**Numbers:** line-space regulator gives 1 or 2 ratchet teeth; platen rotated slightly more than a quadrant to view line; movable rack travels one tooth pitch

**Improves on:**

- scales under the platen: reversed scales placed side by side in view
