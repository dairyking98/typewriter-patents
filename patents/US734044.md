## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Keyboard typewriters with a moving type body were bulky, hid the writing and had many parts; the inventor wants a compact, visible-writing machine with fewer parts.

**Solution:** A tubular type sleeve on a pivoted arm is slid axially to pick the row, rotated to pick the column, and swung down onto the platen, all driven by the one key lever that is pressed.

**How it works:** Key levers press down a rocking frame carried in a universal bail; levers sit either side of the frame's axis so the frame tilts forward or back, and a stop-bail on the frame is arrested by a projection on the pressed lever, so the driving arm moves a variable distance and slides the type sleeve axially along its arm to the chosen row. The same lever deflects a toothed comb sideways, which turns a helical-toothed segment and pinion, and through a rod and tongue rotates the sleeve to the chosen column. The bail also swings the impression arm down so the sleeve meets the paper. During the final descent an oscillating rack drops into a notched flange and ratchet teeth on the sleeve base to lock it exactly in alignment. An inking roller on a compound-pivoted arm sits in the path of the descending type and swings aside across the character face. Shift keys move the segment endwise so the helical teeth rotate the sleeve to another of three type fields (lower, capitals, figures).
