## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Earlier Japanese typewriter type magazines took up a large area and were slow; the inventor wants a compact magazine, close to the paper cylinder, in which individual types can be swapped at will.

**Solution:** Type cells are arranged lengthwise around a rotating cylinder, with the types held in by flexible strips carrying cover plates that are spread apart only at the top position so the chosen type can rise and be struck.

**How it works:** Pairs of wall plates and partitions fixed around a hollow cylinder form many type cells, with a hole through the cylinder wall under each cell. Flexible strips fixed in the gaps between cell groups carry cover plates that overlap the cell mouths so loose types cannot fall out as the cylinder turns. The operator turns the cylinder to bring the wanted cell group to the top, then presses a knob that drives a claw lever between two strips, spreading the cover plates and locking the cylinder against shake. Pressing the key lifts a type pusher through the cell hole, then a striker passes up through the pusher and drives the type about half out of its cell against the paper cylinder. On release springs return the levers and the type drops back by its own weight.
