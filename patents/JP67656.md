## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Travelling typewriters were made small only by folding the upper part down over the keyboard, which costs money and reliability.

**Solution:** Short typebars swinging about 120 degrees backward from a back-tilted segment let the platen sit very low behind the keyboard, so a standard-keyboard machine is portable without folding.

**How it works:** Each key lever runs back under the typebars and carries a pin in a slot of a crank lever. The crank's other arm drives a link to a short arm at the rear of a short typebar, which carries a three-type head. Crank and link stand at an obtuse angle so the key action is quick at the start and gentler at the end, like a cushion. Links, cranks and key-lever rears converge toward the narrow typebar segment, which is tilted back about 30 degrees. The bar swings backward and downward from its highest position to strike the upper front of a low platen, which shifts almost vertically for case. A spring finger catching a lug on the type head returns the bar, and one saddle bar carries all the key-lever coil springs.

## Description (English translation of the Japanese text, opening)

> Specification. Typewriter. Nature and object of the invention. This invention relates to a typewriter in which the platen is placed behind the type-bar pivots, level with the top row of keys; the key levers [sic: the type bars] are mounted to swing more than 90 degrees from the horizontal; although the keyboard is of standard size, the type bars converge rearward and fill an arc of about 90 degrees; the plane of the arc of the type-bar pivots is tilted back about 30 degrees so as to be tangent to the surface of the platen; and when a key is pressed the type bar swings from its highest position backward and downward to strike the printing line on the upper front face of the platen.
