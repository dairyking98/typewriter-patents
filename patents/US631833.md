## Summary

**Kind:** type-element machine · **Subject (patent classes):** Typewriters or selective printing mechanisms characterised by the mounting, arrangement or disposition of the types or dies with types or dies mounted on carriers rotatable for selection the plane of the type or die face being parallel to the axis of rotation, e.g. with type on the periphery of cylindrical carriers

**In the patent’s own words:** My invention relates to that class of typewriting machines in which a rotary typewheel is employed which is rotated differentially by the keys of the machine to bring the proper type into alinement with the paper roll and is then moved laterally to strike said type against the paper.

**How it works (reading):** Each key first turns the type-wheel by a key-specific amount of lost motion through racks and bevel gears, then locks the wheel and unlocks its rock-frame so the same stroke swings the wheel to the paper.

## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A keyboard-driven rotary type-wheel machine needs a way to turn the wheel differentially to each key's character, stop it, and then throw it against the paper, together with case shift and carriage feed.

**Solution:** Each key first turns the type-wheel by a key-specific amount of lost motion through racks and bevel gears, then locks the wheel and unlocks its rock-frame so the same stroke swings the wheel to the paper.

**How it works:** Keys carry fingers set at different distances from universal bars, so each key lifts a segmental rack after a different lost motion, turning the wheel through bevel gears by a different angle. Further travel of the key hits a second universal bar that lifts a rod, freeing a detent and letting a spring wedge lock both bevel gears, which stops the wheel's rotation. The same motion rocks the frame carrying the wheel down against the paper roll, and a spring lifts it back and then restores the wheel to zero. Two case keys move a sleeve and cam that slide the wheel along its shaft to pick one of three circumferential rows. Each key stroke also pushes a bar whose roll drops down a step in a zigzag slot on the carriage bar, so the carriage advances one space when the key is released; a lever lifts a pin to free the carriage for return. An ink roll is drawn across the type each time the wheel descends.
