## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** On a postal telegram typewriter where some type bars carry one character and others two, the clerk can strike a letter key while the carriage is shifted for figures (or the reverse), which prints a bare type head smudge.

**Solution:** A single sliding lock bar with prongs and cutouts alternately locks the letter keys or the figure keys, while leaving the two-character keys free, and it is moved by the figure shift key.

**How it works:** A lock bar on a spring-loaded rod runs along the intermediate levers. Its prongs block either the intermediate levers of the figure keys or those of the pure letter keys, and wide cutouts always let the two-character keys through. A cone on the rod rests against the key lever of the figure shift key, so pressing the figure shift shifts the bar to block the letter keys; a release key drops the carriage and lets the spring return the bar to block the figure keys. The spring is on the rod's right end and a guide screw in a groove stops rotation. The keyboard has a figure row, a letter row and rows of letter plus punctuation keys.
