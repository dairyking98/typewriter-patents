## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A key-driven type wheel normally has to return to a fixed starting point after every character, which needs return mechanism and complexity.

**Solution:** Each key stroke drives the wheel always in the same direction through a friction clutch that can slip, and a key-raised stop pin catches a latch on the wheel spindle so the wheel stops at the right character from wherever it was.

**How it works:** A key lever raises a common cross bar that moves levers and a pawl, stepping a ratchet wheel whose face is a rubber or leather friction surface. This face bears against a friction wheel on the type-wheel spindle, so the type wheel turns forward in the same direction every time, by more than a full revolution if not stopped. Each character has a stop pin; the key raises its pin into the path of a spring latch fixed to the spindle. When the latch hits the pin the wheel stops, and the ratchet face slips under the friction wheel while the key finishes its stroke. If the pin is too close to rise ahead of the latch, the wheel makes a full extra turn first. The pin is pressed down by a spring on release and stops are covered in rubber to deaden noise. Friction contact is adjustable by an endwise-moving cross shaft with spring and thumbscrew.
