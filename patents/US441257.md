## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Inventor wants an inexpensive, simple, durable typewriter, simpler than his earlier application (Serial 310,393) which used travelers between keys and the type-wheel shaft.

**Solution:** Each key directly rotates a type-wheel shaft by pressing on its own angled position-plate fixed to the shaft, locking the wheel in place as the key continues down to fire a hammer and advance the platen.

**How it works:** Keys in two banks slide vertically in rails; each has a straight striking edge. The wheel shaft carries one flat position-plate per key, each set at a different angle, so a key pressing its plate flat against its edge turns the shaft and type-wheel to a unique angle (front bank turns it one way, rear bank the other). Once the plate is parallel to the key edge the shaft is locked while the key continues down. Lost motion then lets the key lug depress a bail on a hammer rock-shaft, which pushes a hammer slide whose hammer strikes the back of a rubber type band on the wheel rim through slots, printing against a platen. The same stroke retracts a spring-loaded pawl carrier so that on key release a double-toothed pawl advances the rack-bearing platen carrier one tooth. Two shift keys slide the wheel along a feathered sleeve to bring one of three type rows to the hammer; a return plate with a spring recentres the shaft.
