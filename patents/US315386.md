## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** An inexpensive, few-part typewriter that needs as few movements as costly machines and can print on paper of any length without coiling or folding it.

**Solution:** A stationary letter-plate with tapered holes and a sliding type-plate with a rigid stylus pivot on hinged arms; the stylus point in a hole aligns the type, and pressing it down prints through a perforated ribbon guard onto a fixed impression stud while a pawl and rack space the paper.

**How it works:** A stationary letter-plate on overhanging brackets has letters in one line, each with a tapering socket. A type-plate slides parallel to it in hinged arms and has a rigid stylus with a tapered point; the type are in reverse order. The point is put in the socket for the wanted letter, and the taper corrects fine alignment. Pressing down drives the type through a perforation in a plate beneath it, with an ink ribbon between type and plate, onto the paper over a small stationary impression stud. The ribbon slides with the type-plate so each ribbon spot inks only its own type. A pawl on the hinged arm engages a toothed rack on a sliding paper frame, moving it one tooth per stroke; springs lift the arms. A knob-turned roller with a hold-down strip feeds the paper between lines, and the overhanging brackets leave free passage for paper of any length.
