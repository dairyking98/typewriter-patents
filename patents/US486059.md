## Summary

**Kind:** a part or mechanism (any kind of machine) · **Subject (patent classes):** Typewriters or selective printing or marking mechanisms characterised by the purpose for which they are constructed for embossing, e.g. for making matrices for stereotypes

**In the patent’s own words:** The object of my invention is to produce on a type-writer by the use of its ordinary type or hard fiat-faced type-letters what may be called a stencil or a printed sheet, the printed letters or contents'of which shall be pervious, while the remainder of the sheet is impervious, to ink, whereby duplicate prints thereof may be made.

**How it works (reading):** Back the paper with a roughened or fine-pointed platen surface so hard flat-faced type bruise and perforate the fibre without cutting out pieces.

## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** Making a duplicating stencil on an ordinary typewriter normally cuts out the centres of letters like O or leaves dotted letters.

**Solution:** Back the paper with a roughened or fine-pointed platen surface so hard flat-faced type bruise and perforate the fibre without cutting out pieces.

**How it works:** The platen surface is roughened or covered with closely set fine points. The stencil sheet, such as loose-fibre paper waxed or soaped, is typed on in the usual way. Each type blow crushes the paper against the rough backing, so the points perforate it and the hollows macerate it to the shape of the type face. Ink later passes through these areas and soaks into the macerated paper, so duplicates show full letter outlines. On a flat platen where paper is drawn over the surface, an elastic rubber strip stretched over the points yields under the blow and then lifts the paper clear so it can feed freely. A cylindrical platen that turns with the paper needs no rubber cover.
