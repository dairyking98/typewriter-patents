## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Typebar machines are complex; the inventor wants a simple keyboard typewriter with few moving parts, with the electric stroke separated from the choice of character.

**Solution:** Each row of keys rocks a fan-shaped plate carrying spring type-plates so the chosen type lies at a common aperture, and an electromagnet hammer behind the paper strikes it.

**How it works:** Under each row of keys is a pivoted lever; pressing a key near the lever's pivot moves the lever end less, one far away moves it more, so the lever's travel encodes which key was pressed. A strap connects the lever to a pivoted fan-shaped plate carrying a row of riveted spring type-plates, each with a character at its tip, and the plate rocks by an amount that brings the chosen type opposite an aperture in a back plate. The same key stroke touches two wires running round the keyboard, closing a battery circuit through an electromagnet. The magnet pulls an armature whose hammer, behind the aperture, strikes the back of the type-plate and presses its type against the paper on its roller. The types brush an inking pad on the back plate as they swing, with pivot pins angled so plates approach the plate face near the aperture.
