## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** On the Remington typewriter the platen shifting frame rides against the shift-rail with lost motion, so the cylinder vibrates, alignment suffers, and the rail needs constant oiling.

**Solution:** Pull rollers on the frame arms against the rear of the shift-rail with springs, so the platen frame is always in contact with the rail and has no play.

**How it works:** The shifting frame carrying the platen and band-pulley shaft has two forward arms overlapping the shift-rail. Each arm carries a horizontal-axis-free roller, turning in a horizontal plane, mounted on a stud held in an adjustment slot by a screw. Two spiral springs between the shaft and the carriage front bar pull the rollers against the rear of the rail and hold the frame forward. Depressing the upper-case lever throws the rail back, shifting the platen against the springs, which return it on release. Conical stud heads below the rollers cam the frame back when the raised carriage is lowered.
