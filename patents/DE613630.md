## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** In portable typewriters the platen release clutch must not stick out beyond the carriage ends, and earlier single-sided friction clutches clamped the line-space ratchet wheel too weakly so the platen could over-rotate.

**Solution:** The line-space ratchet wheel is clamped between a fixed flange and a sliding washer, pressed by two wedge levers inside the platen end that a cam on an axially sliding shaft pushes apart.

**How it works:** The platen is carried on two shafts, the left one sliding axially and ending in a knob. The line-space ratchet wheel sits loose on a threaded nut inside the platen end, between a fixed flange on the nut and a washer on guide pins. Pushing the knob in drives a tapered cam between the long arms of two levers, whose short arms act as self-releasing wedges and press the washer against the wheel, locking the platen to the ratchet wheel. Pulling the knob out frees the cam, the levers fall back, the wheel is released, and the platen turns freely while the spring roller holds the wheel. Turning the flanged nut with a key adjusts the clamp force without disassembly, and a lock nut fixes it. The platen can be tilted and lifted out for service.
