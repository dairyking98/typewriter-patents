## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typing on stencils (wax-coated) needs an even stroke: too heavy cuts out letter centres, too light does not break the wax for ink flow; ordinary key force varies.

**Solution:** An electric attachment where a light key touch closes a circuit through a solenoid per key that rocks the typebar shaft with a constant stroke, plus a solenoid to turn the platen and space lines.

**How it works:** Each key is in two hinged sections with contact points held apart by a spring; pressing closes a battery circuit through a solenoid for that key. The solenoid core is pivoted to a crank on a rock shaft which, through rods with a turnbuckle for wear take-up, tilts the type-carrying lever against the platen. A variant uses a relay-type magnet attracted to a metal field plate, and another a rubber cap with a contact that slips over an ordinary key. A further solenoid on a pivot is connected to the platen/carriage-return handle (as on Smith Premier machines), so that a key closing its circuit turns the platen for line spacing and returns the carriage.
