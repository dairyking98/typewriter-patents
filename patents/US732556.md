## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Driving the type-wheel of the Harrison-Hill electric typewriter needed a simpler, cheaper, durable mechanism with few parts.

**Solution:** A constant-travel solenoid lever drives a roller through an angled slot in a cam on the type-wheel shaft, and the roller's starting side sets the direction and amount of wheel rotation.

**How it works:** Any key depresses a universal bar that closes a contact plate, energising a solenoid whose core always travels the same distance. The core swings a lever whose roller runs in a cam slot extending both sides of a central point; the slot's inclination turns a shaft carrying the type-carrier arm and wheel. Which side of the slot the roller starts on gives the direction of rotation, chosen by the key, so a constant motor travel yields differential wheel travel and brings the character to the centre-line printing point.

**Features:**

- **Selection:** [Keys drive the element by racks or gears](../features/sel-differential-drive.md), [Full keyboard, a key per character](../features/sel-keyboard-full.md)
- **Type element:** [Type wheel (types on the rim)](../features/elem-wheel-rim.md)
