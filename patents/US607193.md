## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typewriters with a cylindrical type-holder need a simple way to use the key levers to both position the character and then print.

**Solution:** Key levers first turn the cylindrical type-holder through cams and gearing to the desired character, then by continued travel oscillate it into the print position.

**How it works:** Each sheet-metal key lever has a bifurcated front end with two oblique cam faces. One arm rocks a bail which sets a stop arm into the path of a rotary stepped cam (cut obliquely so contact points lie at different heights). The other arm moves a plate with oblique slots that drives rack segments and a pinion to turn the shaft carrying the type-holder, which has three circumferential rows of type. A locking dog and toothed disk lock the holder at the chosen position, and the swinging frame carrying the shaft then oscillates to bring the type to the platen.

**Features:** [SEL:dummy-keyboard](../features/sel-dummy-keyboard.md), [SEL:differential-drive](../features/sel-differential-drive.md), [SEL:selector-locking](../features/sel-selector-locking.md), [ELEM:cylinder-multirow](../features/elem-cylinder-multirow.md), [IMP:element-to-platen](../features/imp-element-to-platen.md)

**Parts:** key lever; oblique cam surface; bail; stop arm; stepped rotary cam; type-holder cylinder; pinion; rack segment; locking dog; locking disk; oscillating frame

**Numbers:** 3 circumferential rows of type
