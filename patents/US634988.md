## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Transmitting key movements to a type-wheel so that it is positioned accurately, with low operating force and no springs to overcome.

**Solution:** Each key lever has a fork that pushes a wedge-shaped frame fixed at a different place on a rocking shaft, turning the shaft by a key-specific angle that a bevel sector passes to the type-wheel, which then stays put until the next key.

**How it works:** Keys sit on double-armed levers on a common axle; the rear end of each lever is bent into a U-shaped fork that straddles a shaft carrying one wedge-like frame per key. Because each wedge sits at a different position on the shaft and each fork engages it differently, pressing a key rocks the shaft in one or other direction by an angle unique to that key. A bevel sector on the shaft drives a bevel wheel on the type-wheel axle, so the wheel turns to the right letter. A rod under all the key levers is linked to a weighted hammer lever that strikes the paper against the type-wheel. The wheel does not return to a home position after printing, which reduces the force needed; weight alone returns keys and hammer. Two shift keys raise the wheel axially for further type rows.
