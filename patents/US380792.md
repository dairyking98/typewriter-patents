## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Typewriters are complex and costly, and few print capitals, small letters, figures and punctuation in one simple machine.

**Solution:** A simple index machine in which a hinged type disc carrying a ring of types on its underside is turned by a knob arm until a pointer sits over the marked hole in the top plate, and pressing the arm down prints the letter and feeds the paper one tooth.

**How it works:** A disc hangs from a spring-lifted hinged frame in a large opening of the top plate, with its underside carrying a ring of types, half capitals and figures and half small letters and punctuation. Two arms with knobs and pointed ends sit on the disc, one for each half. The operator turns the disc by the knob until the pointer lies over a hole in the plate marked with the wanted character, then presses down: the pointer enters the hole, which locates the disc, and the type is pressed onto the paper wrapped round the cylinder below. A stud on the frame pushes down a lever and pawl as the frame descends, advancing the toothed rack of the sliding foot plate one tooth so the paper moves a letter space just before the impression. Two spring-mounted inking rollers press on the type. At line end the pawl is lifted by a lever so the foot plate and cylinder can be pushed back, and a knob on the cylinder advances the line by a ratchet.
