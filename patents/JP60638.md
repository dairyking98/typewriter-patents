## Summary

**Kind:** type-element machine · **Subject (patent classes):** Typewriters or selective printing mechanisms characterised by the mounting, arrangement or disposition of the types or dies with types or dies mounted on carriers rotatable for selection; Cylindrical Japanese-language typewriter

**In the English translation of the patent’s own words:** This invention relates to a typewriter in which carriage legs, movable front to back, are set on a base;

**How it works (reading):** Many toothed type rings, each carrying a line of types on its rim, hang on a gear roller that turns as the carriage slides front to back, while one handle moves a lifting arm left and right to pick a ring and, pressed down, throws that ring against the paper cylinder.

## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Earlier Japanese typewriters were slow and awkward to select characters on; the inventor wants one handle to bring any type of many to the printing position and print it, with types that cannot scatter or get out of order.

**Solution:** Many toothed type rings, each carrying a line of types on its rim, hang on a gear roller that turns as the carriage slides front to back, while one handle moves a lifting arm left and right to pick a ring and, pressed down, throws that ring against the paper cylinder.

**How it works:** Carriage legs run front and back on rails over an index plate and, through a pinion, intermediate gear and base rack, turn a gear roller. Several type rings, each with a row of types outside and internal teeth meshing with the roller (or, in a second embodiment, external teeth formed by deep grooves between the types), hang on the roller side by side, steadied by support rollers and comb-like spacers. Moving the handle forward or back turns all the rings so that the wanted type shows in the window of a pressing lever; moving it left or right slides a support frame so a hook-shaped swinging arm sits under the chosen ring. Pressing the handle swings the arm, which lifts the ring until the spring-loaded pressing lever grips it, its front frames entering the partition grooves between types, then throws the ring against a paper cylinder parallel to the roller, printing the type.
