## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Letter-width carriage shifting by one pawl against many movable stops needs many light parts, finely pitched racks, and wears out easily.

**Solution:** Use several independent pawl-and-ratchet mechanisms, each with one fixed step, whose movements are summed by a differential gear into a central member that shifts the carriage.

**How it works:** Each key selects which of the independent pawl-and-ratchet devices act. Each device produces one fixed extent of movement. A central member (a differential gear) adds the movements, so two devices give three different shifts and more devices give many combinations. The result drives the paper carriage through a pinion on its rack. Shown on an Underwood-type typebar machine with key levers, bellcranks and typebars.

**Features:**

- **Selection:** [Keys drive the element by racks or gears](../features/sel-differential-drive.md)
- **Typebars:** [Typebars](../features/elem-typebar.md)
- **Spacing and escapement:** [Proportional spacing](../features/esc-variable-spacing.md), [Pawl-and-rack escapement](../features/esc-pawl-rack.md)

**Parts:** key lever; bell-crank lever; typebar; pawl; ratchet; differential gear; carriage rack; pinion

**Improves on:**

- single pawl against movable stops: independent fixed-step pawls summed by a differential, fewer and sturdier parts
