## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** The letter-space feed for a typewriter paper carriage should be simple and effective, and the carriage should not creep while a character is being struck, which would blur the impression.

**Solution:** A duplex escapement: two independent feed dogs on a common rocking trunnion engage a double rack of two toothed bars, so that one dog always holds the carriage while the other steps one tooth.

**How it works:** The carriage is pulled left by a cord from a spring drum and carries a rack of two toothed bars in one plane with teeth facing each other. Two dogs pivoted together in a trunnion engage one bar each. Depressing any type key lifts a universal ring that rocks the trunnion; the first dog enters between teeth of its bar before the second leaves its bar, so the carriage is never free during the strike. The second dog, now clear, is thrown one tooth over by a small spring, and on key release the dogs return and the carriage advances one letter space. The space key works the same lever without printing, and a spring-returned release key tips both dogs out of the rack to let the carriage run freely. The type bars swing from a ring-pad to a platen above, printing on the underside of the paper.
