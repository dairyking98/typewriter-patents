## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typebar machines need a lever system between key and type to keep key travel short, which costs material and labour.

**Solution:** Curved, leverless type bumpers (sliding bars) in three rows, each sliding forward through guide holes and deflected up or down by a slotted guide at the print point.

**How it works:** Curved bumpers are mounted in a frame with guide holes in three rows and converge on a common anchor point at the paper carrier. The types sit on the front ends in two stacked rows. Pressing a key pushes its bumper forward; the bar's curvature makes the type move forward and also up or obliquely down. A four-sided slotted guide in front of the anchor point lines up the type so it cannot shift sideways or vertically. Springs on the frame and bars return them, and a rod under each row triggers the escapement and ribbon holder. Optionally key levers and bumpers can be separate pieces joined by an intermediate lever.
