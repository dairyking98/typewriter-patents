## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Index-type wheel machines needed the operator to place the pointer very exactly, and spacing was uniform regardless of letter width; the wheel also had to be inked without a complicated mechanism.

**Solution:** A pointer on a letter plate rotates a multi-row type-wheel by rack and pinion, pressing the same pointer down swings the whole frame to print, a V-notch wheel on the shaft snaps the type square just before the paper, and set-pins on the rack give letter-width-dependent spacing.

**How it works:** A type-wheel slides on a splined shaft inside a frame that pivots about a rod; a thumb-piece and pointer on a rack moves along an index plate printed with rows of letters, rotating the wheel through a pinion until the chosen letter faces the paper. A knob lever slides the wheel along its shaft to select the row, held by a notched spring catch. Pressing the pointer down swings the frame so the wheel first meets an inking roller and then pushes the roller aside with a pin as it descends to the paper on the platen. A notched wheel on the shaft drops onto a V-shaped anvil just before impact so the type lands squarely, so the operator need not align the pointer exactly; in a variant a tooth on the pointer enters a V-toothed rack on the frame. Set-pins on the rack depress a spacing lever by different amounts depending on the letter, and on release a spring drives a pawl along a ratchet bar on the platen carriage by that amount, giving variable spacing. A notched wheel and spring click the platen for line spacing.
