## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typebar machines of the time hid the printed work, needed heavy key pressure and had escapement pawls that vibrated together. The inventor wants a rapid, visible-writing machine with few parts and light key action.

**Solution:** A downstroking typebar machine with top-of-platen printing, knife-edge key levers, and a carriage escapement of two pawls oscillating in opposite directions about a common pivot.

**How it works:** Each key lever rests on a knife-edge bearing and is held up by a coiled spring; a connecting rod joins it to a bellcrank-like typebar whose trunnions are at the bar end, so the free end swings down onto the top of the platen and leaves the work visible. A lug on each typebar passes between pins on a plate to align it at the print point. Every key depresses a bail on a rock-shaft, which drives a fixed pawl and a movable cushioned pawl on a common pivot alternately against a carriage rack, giving step-by-step spacing under a spring barrel and cord. The same rock-shaft steps a ratchet and a sliding long pinion that winds the ribbon onto either spool. The carriage has a ratchet-and-handle line spacer, margin stop pins on a diagonal, a bell and a release lever.
