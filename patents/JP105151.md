## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Earlier amount-printing machines need a sliding carriage and other complicated mechanism, making them hard to build and use.

**Solution:** A tilting round box holds a hand-turned disc carrying a half circle of swinging type keys, one of which is aligned with a notch to be pressed, and each press-and-release spaces the whole box one ratchet step along a bar over the fixed paper.

**How it works:** A round support box sits tippably on a grooved bar over a window in the cover plate, with paper and carbon paper or ribbon beneath. A cover disc and seat disc turn together on a central vertical axle, and on a ring axle of the seat disc hang many key bars side by side over about half the circumference, each carrying one figure or letter type. The operator turns the discs until the wanted type is under a pointer on the axle, which puts its key under the single notch in the box's ring flange; elsewhere the flange holds the keys up, so only that key can be pressed. Pressing it tips the whole box down and the type prints on the paper in the window. Two spring strips under the box, one on a crosswise ratchet bar and one on the plate, lift the box back on release and advance it one fixed step sideways, so the next character prints beside the last.

## Description (English translation of the Japanese text, opening)

> Specification. Printing machine. Nature and object of the invention. A printing machine in which a support box with a round flange is hinged on top of the machine case so that it can tip up and down; a round cover disc and a seat disc turn freely on a vertical axle fixed in the centre of the box; a ring axle is fitted to the seat disc, and in a curved cut-out of the seat disc several printing keys are hung on the ring axle, guided by vertical slots in the cover disc and normally held up by the round flange; the flange has a single notch, so that only the key that has come to it can be pressed down; and two spring strips under the box, one engaging ratchet teeth laid crosswise on the frame and the other bearing on the top of the frame, so that each time the box has been pressed down and springs back up, it moves sideways one fixed step at a time.
