## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** Back-spacing by hand is slow and imprecise on carriage-spring machines.

**Solution:** A key-operated dog engages the carriage rack and draws it back until the escapement dogs catch the previous tooth, where a positive stop halts it and makes it rebound, giving exactly one space per press.

**How it works:** A key lever rod rocks a bell-crank dog on a pivoted plate so its tooth enters a notch of the rack bar. The plate swings, drawing the rack back two to five teeth until the escapement dogs engage the next tooth, then hits a stop. A lug on the dog rests on a stop so the dog cannot withdraw, abruptly stopping the carriage, which rebounds against the escapement dogs. Several forms are shown for Remington, Densmore, Smith Premier, Caligraph and Yost style machines.
