**Kind:** type-element machine · **Subject (patent classes):** Typewriters or selective printing mechanisms characterised by the mounting, arrangement or disposition of the types or dies with types or dies mounted on carriers rotatable for selection the plane of the type or die face being parallel to the axis of rotation, e.g. with type on the periphery of cylindrical carriers

**In the patent’s own words:** My improvements relate more particularly to the mechanism for positioning the typewheel to make an impression, and to effect this in the known machines the following, among other parts, have been necessary, namely: a ratchet or sprocket wheel;

## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** The positioning and stop mechanism of a keyboard type-wheel machine (Blickensderfer-style) needs about 29 separate parts: ratchets, front and back stops, bails, springs and adjusters.

**Solution:** Replace those 29 parts with six: a crown-ratchet stop-wheel on the type-wheel spindle, a locking pawl that moves parallel to the spindle, a cam-lever, a lever driven by it, a stop-guide and a fulcrum screw.

**How it works:** Key levers in banks press short bails whose toothed racks turn sector levers, and the sector levers rotate a pinion fixed to the type-wheel spindle, so the wheel turns to the chosen letter. A crown stop-wheel with one tooth per key lever, split into two oppositely bevelled series by a short home tooth and a long tooth, turns with the type-wheel. As the key goes deeper it presses the long bail, whose toothed arm turns a cam-lever. The cam-lever lifts a pivoted lever under the pawl flange and drives the locking pawl parallel to the spindle into the gap between two stop-wheel teeth. This locks the wheel before the rock-shaft carries it to the platen. A spring returns the pawl, and a light retarding spring rubbing the stop-wheel flange slows a wheel spun by a sudden stroke. A torsion spring mounted on the rock-shaft returns it, in place of the old long radial arm and spring.

**Numbers:** 29 parts replaced by 6; three banks of key levers; wheel turns up to half a revolution either way
