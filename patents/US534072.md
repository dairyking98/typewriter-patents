## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** Typebar machines need heavy keystrokes and are noisy and non-uniform in impact; the inventor wants rapid, quiet, uniform printing with an exceedingly small expenditure of force.

**Solution:** Type-bars that slide as pistons in radial cylinders are driven by air sent through rubber tubes from rubber bellows-domes pressed by finger-key stems.

**How it works:** A block is drilled with cylinder ducts on radii converging at the printing point, each holding a type-bar whose rear head acts as a piston. Rubber tubes connect nipples at the block's rear to chambers in a second block, closed by dome-shaped sheet rubber bellows pierced by key stems. Pressing a key compresses its dome and drives air to propel the type-bar to the platen. A frame with cross wires guides the type-bar front ends. Worn type can be serviced by disconnecting the tubes and removing the block bodily, and the machine also has ribbon control, carriage feed and a platen turning attachment.
