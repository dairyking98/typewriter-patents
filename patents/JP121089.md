## Summary

**Kind:** a part or mechanism (any kind of machine) · **Subject (patent classes):** Fluid-pressure power drives

**In the English translation of the patent’s own words:** The invention relates to a typewriter in which the printing mechanism is worked by air power, such as compressed air.

**How it works (reading):** An air valve serves as the print key, so a fingertip press admits compressed air that lifts the selected type and then swings it against the platen.

## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Hand force on the print stroke tires the typist and cannot be varied easily; the inventor wants the blow powered by compressed air while still adjustable.

**Solution:** An air valve serves as the print key, so a fingertip press admits compressed air that lifts the selected type and then swings it against the platen.

**How it works:** A flat type tray is slid so the wanted type sits under the slot of a pivoted type lever. Pressing the valve button sends air into a first cylinder whose piston, via a rod, lifts the type through the slot, inking it on a pad on the way. At the end of that stroke a branch passage feeds a second cylinder whose piston straightens a toggle, swinging the first cylinder and lever about the shaft so the type face hits the platen. Releasing the button exhausts both cylinders and springs return the pistons, a spring catch holding the first piston up until the second has returned.

## Description (English translation of the Japanese text, opening)

> Specification. Pneumatic typewriter. Nature and object of the invention. The invention relates to a typewriter in which the printing mechanism is worked by air power, such as compressed air. It is characterised in that its control device, that is the printing key or button, is an air-valve control device, mounted movably or fixed on the paper-cylinder mechanism, the type-lever mechanism, the type tray or part of the base, and that the valve is worked as a push button to make the desired printing stroke. The object is a typewriter in which the power for the printing stroke comes from compressed air, so that the user does no more than work the control valve with a fingertip, while the force of the blow can still be adjusted freely for types with more or fewer strokes, no differently from an ordinary hand-worked machine; so that printing is light and quick, without effort, and as strong or as light as wished.
