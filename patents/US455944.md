**Kind:** type-element machine · **Subject (patent classes):** Typewriters or selective printing mechanisms characterised by the mounting, arrangement or disposition of the types or dies with types or dies mounted on carriers rotatable for selection the plane of the type or die face being parallel to the axis of rotation, e.g. with type on the periphery of cylindrical carriers

**In the patent’s own words:** The object of my improvements is to render the machine more nearly perfect in its operation, less likely to get out of repair, to simplify its mechanism, and lessen the cost of its manufacture.

**How it works (reading):** A refined hand-lever index typewriter: one lever swings a crown-gear segment to turn a type-wheel, locks on a curved rack, and is pressed down to swing the wheel frame onto the paper, with many detail improvements to the wheel rim, shaft, escapement, ribbon, anvil, and paper handling.

## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** The earlier Kempster / Currier / Blount type-wheel machines (Columbia Index type) needed to be more accurate, less liable to repair, simpler and cheaper to make, with type that is easy to adjust and a type-wheel shaft with fewer joints.

**Solution:** A refined hand-lever index typewriter: one lever swings a crown-gear segment to turn a type-wheel, locks on a curved rack, and is pressed down to swing the wheel frame onto the paper, with many detail improvements to the wheel rim, shaft, escapement, ribbon, anvil, and paper handling.

**How it works:** The type-wheel A has a central rib and a circle of holes on each side holding loose square-headed type (lower case on one side, capitals on the other), each seated on a sub-rim and clamped with a set screw. The wheel and its spur gear are fixed on a shaft; a spur gear meshes with a crown-gear segment that is swung by the horizontal movement of the type-wheel lever U. A pin under the lever drops into a curved rack to hold the wheel at the chosen letter, and further downward movement of the lever depresses the swinging wheel frame against balance springs so the type strikes the ribbon and paper above an anvil. Thumb pressure on a push-rod slides the whole shaft and wheel forward to bring the second row of type into line (shift), and a spring returns it, with an eccentric stop to adjust the return. The same stroke trips an angle lever that works the carriage escapement for one letter space; a separate thumb rod spaces between words. Two ribbon spools are stepped by pawls on each up-stroke, and a sliding rod swaps pawls to reverse the ribbon; the carriage is driven by a spring and chain with cam equalisation.
