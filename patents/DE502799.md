## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typewriter-style machines for making typographic type-setting stencils (clichés) advanced the carriage by whole tooth multiples, so character spacing could not be fine enough for modern fonts.

**Solution:** The carriage advance is driven through a friction gear, so each key can give any spacing, set by the height of its stop on a shift rod.

**How it works:** Type levers carry two characters each; the upper part of the carriage is lifted or lowered by rods and forks to choose which one hits the sheet. Each key lever's rear end lifts against a stop of different height on one of two shift rods, so each oscillates by a different amount. A slider rides the rods and, through levers, controls a friction transmission of discs and a pulley that advances the carriage by a variable amount matching the character width. Two rods are needed because the two characters on one lever have different widths. The carriage runs on ball bearings.
