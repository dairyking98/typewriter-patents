## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** A typewriter whose ribbon is traversed to spread wear (Smith Premier) cannot easily use a two-color or two-ink ribbon with the ribbon stripes side by side.

**Solution:** Halve the crank throw so the ribbon spools reciprocate only the width of one stripe, and give the pitman two spring-clip bearings for the crank pin so a keyboard-side key-lever can move the spools to the other stripe.

**How it works:** The carriage mainspring casing drives a ratchet and a pair of elliptical gears through a pawl, so a crank turns only as the carriage steps. The crank pin drives a spring pitman that rocks a shaft; slotted arms and yokes slide the ribbon spools axially on transverse shafts, giving the ribbon a crosswise traverse. A ratchet and pawl on the spools wind the ribbon lengthwise as the spools move forward, so the ribbon feeds in two directions at right angles. The pitman has a solid lower part and an upper looped spring of one strip with two outward-bent semicircular bearings. A key-lever on the rock shaft pushes the pitman so the crank pin springs from one bearing to the other, shifting the spool range to the other color field. More colors would need a shorter crank throw and more bearings.
