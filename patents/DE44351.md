## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Segment-plate typewriters normally need separate mechanisms for choosing the letter, printing it, and advancing one space, which forces the operator to use both hands.

**Solution:** One two-armed lever, turned to pick the letter and then pressed down, both prints it and advances the whole writing apparatus one rack tooth, so a single hand does everything.

**How it works:** The writing apparatus rides on a toothed transport rod over the paper, its underframe marked with the letters and resting on rails. A rubber-type segment plate rotates on a fixed pin with a two-armed lever above it. The operator turns the lever until its pointer is over the desired letter, then presses it down so it engages a toothed sector of the base, locking the angle. Through ball-jointed rods this moves a second two-armed lever that pushes down a base lever. That lever's transport pawl moves the whole apparatus sideways by one rack tooth, and at the end of the stroke a pressure pin presses the chosen rubber type onto the paper on its backing. A spring returns the lever and type plate and a locking pawl drops into the next rack tooth. The text breaks off before describing the plate's other end.
