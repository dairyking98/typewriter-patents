## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A simple, cheap pointer type-wheel machine needs the pointer set exactly on a character, which slows typing.

**Solution:** A V-toothed wheel coupled to the type wheel and a V-shaped tooth on the print-lever axis snap the nearest character exactly to the print point, so the pointer only has to be near the letter.

**How it works:** The type wheel (several rows) is clamped to a shaft; a V-toothed wheel B is carried round with it by a driving pin but stays vertically in place when the shaft slides up. A pivoting arm with a button and toothed segment meshes with a small gear on wheel B, so swinging the arm over the index plate turns the type wheel. The left hand presses a button lever that swings a rocking axis carrying the paper-bearing frame to the type wheel. On the same stroke a V-shaped tooth on the axis drops into a notch of wheel B and centres the nearest character. A pawl on a rack steps the frame one tooth on its return, giving letter spacing. A second button slides the shaft axially to bring another type row to the print point. A word-space lever swings the frame forward just enough to step the pawl without touching the types. The paper support is a roller, and the types are concave to match its curve.
