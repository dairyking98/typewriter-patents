## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A hand-set typewriter with a type wheel is wanted that holds both lower and upper case and symbols, sets each letter exactly, inks it and spaces the carriage automatically.

**Solution:** A type wheel and a partly overlapping type sector turn together on a swinging frame that one handle pulls out to select the character, then presses down to print, and the downswing steps a screw-nut carriage along the line.

**How it works:** The type wheel (small letters and punctuation) and a loosely mounted sector (capitals, figures, other signs) sit on a hollow axle in a frame that swings about a fixed lead screw. A double-toothed rack pulled out by the handle first turns only the wheel; after a full turn of the wheel its second row of teeth also picks up the sector, a pawl keeping the sector still until then. The writer pulls the handle until a pointer riding in a spiral groove on a letter disc shows the wanted character, then pushes the handle down, swinging the frame so the character presses the paper against the rod beneath. Holes in the wheel rim, one per letter, slide over a fixed mandrel and give a small correcting turn so each letter registers exactly; an ink roller on a forked carrier is pushed aside past the lowering type and wiped across a pen-like dye reservoir. As the frame rises, a pawl turns a ratchet joined to the nut on the lead screw one tooth, moving the whole frame one letter along; a hand lever gives a word space without printing, and paper line feed uses two rollers with a ratchet and a lever that also frees the screw for return to line start.
