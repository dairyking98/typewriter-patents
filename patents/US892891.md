## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Impression-device carriers need to be simple yet hold a large number of types, and a way to bring any one to the working point and strike it.

**Solution:** Mount the types in three tiers on a spherical turret that a continuously friction-driven shaft rotates and a rock-frame tilts, with key-raised stop pins arresting the rotation and the same stop impact tripping a spring-loaded hammer.

**How it works:** A turret with types on a spherical outer surface is joined to a rotating shaft by a gimbal (universal) joint, so it can turn about the shaft and also tilt. The shaft turns continuously through a belt-driven friction pulley. Each key lever raises a spring-returned stop pin (one pin per axial group of three types, shared by three keys), putting it in the path of a finger carried by the shaft; the finger is stopped and the shaft halts with the chosen axial row at the working point. The key's boss also presses a cross bar that moves a rod and rock-frame to tilt the turret to the upper, middle or lower tier. The same keystroke compresses a hammer spring through bell-crank levers and a pitman, and the finger being thrown back at the stop frees a catch so the hammer strikes through the paper guide with a movable platen between hammer and die. The same pitman also operates a two-tooth escapement on the paper slide for letter spacing, and the paper is fed from a supply roll through gripping rollers.
