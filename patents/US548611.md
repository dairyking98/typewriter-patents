## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** Pivoted key levers are costly, wear and have side play.

**Solution:** Key bars with a parallel vertical movement guided by balls in V-grooves, and typebar return springs grouped radially around a common centre.

**How it works:** Each key sits on the end of a flat rigid plate in a vertical plane, with a slotted yoke that straddles a vertical bridge across the machine. V-grooves on the bridge and matching stamped U-shaped grooves on the yoke hold steel balls, so the bar moves up and down with little friction or sideways motion. Rods link the bar's rear extension to the typebar butts. The return springs radiate from one centre so they can be tightened or loosened together. Variants make the yoke from two riveted plates.

**Features:** [Full keyboard, a key per character](../features/sel-keyboard-full.md), [Typebars](../features/elem-typebar.md), [Few parts, low cost](../features/frm-few-parts.md), [Adjustment and wear take-up](../features/frm-adjustment-wear.md)

**Parts:** key bar; yoke; bridge; V-groove; ball; connecting rod; typebar; return spring; cross-bar stop

**Improves on:**

- pivoted key lever: parallel ball-guided motion with less wear
