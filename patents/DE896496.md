## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Conventional typebar machines are noisy, wear heavily and cannot be made much smaller; earlier single-plate or button-and-pinion carrier schemes were too slow or too primitive to compete.

**Solution:** A light, short shuttle carrying a row of side-by-side type blocks is shifted horizontally in front of the platen by the ordinary keys, stopped at the chosen position, and the selected block is pushed to the platen.

**How it works:** A shuttle on a guideway in front of the platen holds a row of small type blocks, each with a character on its front, held in place by interlocking projections and returned by an elastic band or small springs. Each key lever raises a plunger that strikes the edge of a pivoting toothed segment geared to the shuttle, so the shuttle moves a multiple of the letter spacing that depends on the key. The key also raises a stop rod into the path of stops at the shuttle ends, which locks the shuttle exactly and absorbs its momentum. Once the shuttle is locked, an electromagnet circuit (closed through key, rod and shuttle contact) drives a bumper rod into a centering notch in the block and pushes it to the platen. A second embodiment uses a toggle-lever linkage and pivot lever with height-adjustable stops to move the shuttle left or right from a middle position. The shuttle only travels half its length at most from the centre.

**Parts:** shuttle; guideway; type block; elastic return band; toothed segment; plunger; key lever; stop rod; end stop; electromagnet; bumper rod; centering notch; toggle lever; pivot lever; return spring

**Improves on:**

- typebar typewriters with key levers and intermediate rods: replaces many typebars with one light shuttle, reducing noise, wear and space
- older plate-in-front-of-platen machines with type pins in superimposed rows: single row shuttle is shorter and lighter and moved only horizontally
- button-and-pinion carrier setting: keys move the shuttle directly to a stop-defined position
