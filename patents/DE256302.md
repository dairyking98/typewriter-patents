## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Existing devices for turning a type carrier from key movement are complicated and inaccurate, and need intermediate links to produce the impression.

**Solution:** A helically wound plate (screw) fixed to the type carrier shaft is turned to a key-dependent angle when one of several equally spaced vertical keys hits it, and the same key push presses the shaft down to print.

**How it works:** A screw-shaped helical plate is fixed to the vertical axis of a semi-cylindrical type carrier, which carries letters around its half circumference. Vertical keys, one per letter, sit over the screw. Pressing a key strikes the helix at a point that depends on the key and turns the screw until the key touches it at two points and locks it, so the matching letter faces the printing surface. The key keeps moving down, carrying screw and type carrier with it to print, with turning and lowering effectively simultaneous. A spring restores both on release. The screw can be shifted lengthwise so the same key strikes a different helix point, printing a different letter, which gives a changeable cipher. A variant replaces the screw with a toothed cylinder and the keys with racks.
