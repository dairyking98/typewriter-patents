## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Officers need a way to exchange cipher letters with a machine small enough to hide in a pocket, which can also make copies.

**Solution:** A pocket machine, 8 cm across, in which a type disc is turned by a removable spindle and, as the spindle is pressed, a lever strikes a thin paper strip against the selected type.

**How it works:** On a base filled with hectograph mass, a hinged upper part holds a rotatable disc H whose types are in a ring, plus a toothed ring. The operator turns the disc with a removable spindle M (hollow, containing a screwdriver) while a pointer and a fixed indicator show the letter. Pressing the spindle spring-pushes in a bolt K that locks the disc, and tips a T-shaped lever U which drives three levers: one presses the paper strip against the type, one feeds the ink ribbon, one advances the strip by a ratchet when the spindle is released. The strip, wound on a foldable drum, runs between rubber-covered rollers. The pointer is coupled to a cipher mechanism that offsets it from the disc, and the hectograph ink on the ribbon allows copies. A double-series version exists.
