## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Type-lever and type-cylinder typewriters have many entangled parts, friction and costly machining; the inventor wants a cheap, simple machine that stays accurate with a light keystroke.

**Solution:** A single type segment with three rows is carried on an angle lever, and each key's pivot pin enters its own hole in the lever so the segment swings about a different pivot for each character.

**How it works:** Each key lever carries an upright guide piece whose round top end acts as a pivot pin. Pressing a key pushes its pin up into the matching bore in the leg of the angle lever, which is part of the type segment plate. At the same time the key lever lifts a cross-machine axle carrying small rollers; a roller strikes a cam on the type segment axis and swings the segment about the chosen pin against the platen. On release the pin leaves the bore, and a light tension spring pulls the segment plate back against its stops. The segment has three type rows; case change is by known means such as raising or lowering the platen or the segment. An ink pad sits beside the segment.
