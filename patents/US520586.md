## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Platen-shifting carriages of typebar machines were heavy, sliding and hard to adjust or reach; the rack and pinion of the escapement also wore from pressing hard together.

**Solution:** Carry the platen carrier on a pivoted link (coupler) behind the guide rail and a vibrating shift rod in front, so the shift is a swing on pivots, not a slide, and give the feed rack a bearing bar and roll so it cannot bottom in the pinion.

**How it works:** The platen carrier rides on rolls on a shift rod carried by rocking levers at the front, and at the rear is pivoted to a link that hangs from a yoke travelling on the guide rail. Pressing the shift key rocks the levers and the carrier swings rearwards about the link pivots for upper case. The yoke carries a feed rack meshing with a step-by-step pinion and ratchet with spacing dogs, which limit the carriage to one letter space at a time. A bearing bar on the rack rests on an anti-friction roll on the pinion shaft, limiting how deep the rack meshes. A release key rocks the yoke on the rail to lift the rack out of the pinion for free carriage travel, and the whole carrier can be hinged up for inspection against stops.
