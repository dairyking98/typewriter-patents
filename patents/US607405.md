## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Margin stops on typewriters should be simple and have an easy release.

**Solution:** Adjustable margin stops slid on notched rods, with a pawl release on each stop and a keyboard key that vibrates the notched rod to release the carriage at the margin.

**How it works:** A rod notched at letter-space intervals (two rows of notches) hangs from a crank arm and is vibrated sideways by a finger key through a connecting rod and lever. An adjustable margin stop slides on this rod and a second rod, with a spring-loaded pawl whose tooth engages a notch; a push button lifts the tooth so the stop can be repositioned. A weighted pivoted arm on the stop yields in one direction, and a stop on the carriage meets it to halt the carriage at the margin. A bell hammer lever is also associated. Shown on a Remington.
