## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Earlier type-wheel machines needed better construction of the parts that stop the wheel at the right letter and press the paper on.

**Solution:** A type wheel on a continuously spinning spring-driven shaft is stopped by a key-raised stop meeting a pin on a spiral stop cylinder, then a swinging elastic roller carrying the paper strikes it.

**How it works:** A spring barrel and gear train turn a shaft carrying the type wheel and a stop cylinder with a spiral row of pins. A fly balance on multiplying gearing through a ratchet absorbs inertia and prevents rebound. Pressing a key raises its stop into the path of a pin, halting the wheel with the chosen letter at the print position. An escapement lever then releases an impression lever that swings a rubber-faced roller and paper up against the type. The roller frame slides on a screw bar to space, the roller turns a notch for each new line, and a felt roller inks the revolving types.
