## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** On a machine whose whole carriage shifts to change case, the feed rack, feed pawls and their locking must travel with the carriage while staying engaged for letter spacing.

**Solution:** A single link carrying both the reversing (sliding) feed pawl and a lock that keeps the feed rack engaged is attached to the oscillating shaft and shifts with the push rod and paper carriage.

**How it works:** A paper roller rides on a carriage on ball tracks and is pulled by a drum spring. A feed rack on spring-held arms is engaged by two pawls on a block on an oscillating shaft that every letter or space key moves: a sliding pawl and a fixed feed pawl. On a key stroke the sliding pawl leaves the rack, the second pawl enters, and the sliding pawl is advanced by a tension spring to the next tooth, so on release the carriage moves one letter space. If the carriage is returned without disengaging, the loose pawl slides over the teeth. For the case shift the platen carriage rails, feed rack and rocking shaft sit on a sliding sheet-metal frame carried by crank arms and links on a rocking shaft, so the whole carriage moves front to back to write the upper row of letters while the type lever segment stays still. The frame is a removable inner frame inside a sheet-metal outer frame.
