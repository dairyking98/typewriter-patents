## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Ordinary typewriters hide the last letters typed and batter the rubber platen, which then has to be replaced often.

**Solution:** An oscillating type segment strikes the back of the paper through a slot in the carriage, against a small replaceable ivory block in front, with a narrow inking band running vertically over the block so the line stays visible.

**How it works:** Three rows of keys act through bent levers on two-armed levers whose rear arms have hooks of different heights. Depressing a key first presses one of two cranked rods, which turns toothed segments and a pinion to swing the type segment on its shaft. The hook then trips a rod that drops an elastic arm into the teeth of a second pinion, which stops the segment with the chosen type opposite the printing block. Further travel of the lever swings the segment forward on a hollow lever, so the type strikes the back of the paper and prints through the inking band onto the front. The type segment carries two rows of types, and a shift key with wedges moves its frame forward to bring the second row to the block. On release a double rack and pawl advance the carriage one type width, and a wire from the cranked shafts ratchets the narrow ink band one tooth, so the printed letter has left the band and is visible. The paper runs in a trough with a slot, and a hinged rear wall and liftable plate allow corrections.
