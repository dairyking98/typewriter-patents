## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A type-wheel index machine needs a way to select among several rows of type and to locate each type exactly in the printing line, with one hand doing both selection and impression.

**Solution:** A single key on a hollow shaft turns the multi-row type wheel by a pin-and-slot, presses down to swing the platen to the type, and drops a tooth into a tapered notch to centre the letter, while a separate lever on a cam lifts or lowers the wheel to change row.

**How it works:** The type wheel, with one to three horizontal rows of type, turns on a hollow shaft and also slides vertically on it. A key whose shank passes through the shaft has an arm and pin engaging a slot in the wheel, so turning the key turns the wheel while its pointer moves over an alphabetical index plate on the hinged lid. Pressing the key down pushes a lever that swings the hinged platen carriage against the type, and a tooth on the key enters a downward-tapering notch in a guide plate, centering and holding the selected letter. A finger piece, held against one of two thumb-pieces with adjusting screws, swings a cam that raises or lowers the wheel and the ink rolls together, putting the lower, middle or top row on the print line; a spring returns it to the middle row. A gravity spacing dog with two teeth catches the back rack so the platen carriage advances one tooth when the platen falls back. A spring stop rod prevents the platen reaching the type so that the key press only spaces between words. A clearing lever lifts the dog to free the carriage.
