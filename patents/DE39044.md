## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** A cheap typewriter is wanted in which one rotating type disc serves for capitals and small letters and the paper advances automatically after each letter.

**Solution:** A hand-turned disc carrying two hooks (one for capitals and figures, one for small letters) is turned over a ring of marked holes and pressed down so the hook enters the hole and the type beneath prints.

**How it works:** A spring-lifted disc hinged on a support lies under a perforated plate with a ring of holes each marked with a character. The disc's lower face carries two rows of letters, capitals and numbers on one half and small letters and punctuation on the other, inked by two spring-mounted ink rollers. The operator turns the disc until the capital hook C or small hook S is over the wanted hole and presses its button; the hook drops into the hole, which stops the disc and brings the matching letter to the paper. A pin on the pressed disc depresses a lever whose pawl steps a rack on the paper-roller bearing by one tooth, so the paper moves on a letter space after each print. A comb wheel and pawl set the line spacing, and a lever frees the pawl so the roller slide can be pushed back for a new line.
