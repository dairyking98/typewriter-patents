## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** On pointer typewriters one hand sets the pointer and the other presses a separate print lever (and another for spacing), which is slow and makes the machine unusable by someone with one arm.

**Solution:** Put a finger ring on the pointer handle so one finger sets the letter, presses the fingerboard to print, and presses a spacing rail to feed without printing.

**How it works:** A vertical shaft carries the type cylinder at its top and a toothed wheel meshing with a sector on the scale pointer, so swinging the pointer over the arc-shaped scale turns the type wheel. A finger (index) goes through a spring-mounted ring on the pointer. Pressing down on the ring presses a curved fingerboard running the length of the scale, which swings an axis carrying the paper-roller carriage against the type wheel to print. A nose on the axis enters a notch in a toothed disc on the type-wheel shaft and locks the selected letter during impression. As the carriage swings back, a pawl on a rack steps it one letter space. A spring-loaded rail on top of the fingerboard works as a space key: pressed alone it makes only a small swing that steps the carriage without touching the type.
