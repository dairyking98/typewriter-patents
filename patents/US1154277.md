## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** On a shifting-carriage Royal machine the back-space pawl may fail to engage the rack in both upper and lower case positions.

**Solution:** A cam-slotted plate and latch compensate for the carriage's vertical shift so the back-space key moves the same distance in either case position and always engages a rack tooth.

**How it works:** The back-space key moves a bell crank and rod that rock an arm carrying a pawl lever with a pawl. A pin on the pawl lever rides in a cam slot in a plate on the pinion bracket, which moves up with the carriage in upper case. In upper case the pin changes position and lifts the pawl; a latch resting on the shift shaft overlaps the pin in lower case to take up the difference in travel. The pawl then sets the rack and carriage back one tooth space, and an eccentric rack-locking arm holds the rack against the pinion.
