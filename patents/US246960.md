## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Typewriters were costly and complex, and the printing was often hidden so errors were not seen as they were made.

**Solution:** A cheap index typewriter with a fixed horizontal type wheel, an alphabet circle above it and a hand lever that swings over the circle and presses down, moving the paper roller against ribbon and type and spacing the carriage in the same stroke.

**How it works:** A fixed annular type wheel lies horizontally with types projecting radially from its rim, and a fixed alphabet circle with a notched guide sits above it. The operator turns a lever on a vertical pivot to the desired letter on the circle and presses it down into the notch over that letter, which locates the lever over the type. The downward motion of the lever's shaft rocks a crank and rod that swing the paper roller toward the type wheel, pressing the paper and ribbon against the chosen type to print. The same stroke works a second crank, link and dog that advances the carriage one tooth on a ratchet bar. The ribbon moves a type width per impression by ratchet mechanisms on two spools, a hand ratchet line-spaces the roller, the carriage is returned by hand after releasing the pawl, and a bell rings near line end.
