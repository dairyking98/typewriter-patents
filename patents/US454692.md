## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typewriters need a type carrier that can be positioned exactly opposite the platen from an ordinary key-per-character keyboard with few parts, and printing must not happen until the carrier has stopped on the right type.

**Solution:** A keyboard whose key levers have different throws drives a sliding actuating bar and rack-slide that rotate a single type cylinder to the chosen type, after which a key-selected link couples the bar to a printing bar that pushes the cylinder against the platen.

**How it works:** Each key depresses a bell-crank whose arm length gives a different throw, so the actuating bar moves a different distance and its slide (with teeth engaging lugs on a toothed wheel on the carrier shaft) rotates the type cylinder to that character. The same bell-crank lifts one link whose shoulder sits at a distance matched to that throw; when the bar reaches the shoulder it pushes the link and printing bar, which moves the carrier shaft toward the platen to print. A fixed stop engages the toothed wheel during the print stroke to hold alignment and prevent rotation, and a universal bar withdraws a locking stop so printing cannot occur before the proper moment; the same stop motion works the space feed-dog on the rack of the spring-drum-driven carriage. Raising the carrier shaft with a lever or button brings a second row of type into the print line. Spring-mounted inking rollers ride on a common support with the carrier.
