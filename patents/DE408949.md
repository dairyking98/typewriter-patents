## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Electrically driven rotating type wheels stopped by a magnet-pulled hook in a ratchet wheel cannot reach normal typing speed, because the hook is hit too hard at about 1000 rpm and cannot be pulled in accurately.

**Solution:** Before the hook engages, a friction clutch shifts the type wheel shaft from a fast gear to a slow gear, so the hook catches a slowly turning ratchet wheel.

**How it works:** A type wheel on a spring-loaded shaft normally presses a friction plate against a fast-driven gear and spins. When a letter contact closes, a rotating brush on a collector ring sees the live lamella and energises a pot magnet, which pulls the shaft down so the plate drives from a slower gear. A delayed relay contact then lets a second brush energise the hook magnet, so the hook drops into the ratchet wheel only after braking is done. A resistor reduces the pot magnet current to soften impact, and a three-brush variant uses a relay for the same sequence. Releasing the key frees the hook and returns the wheel to fast speed.
