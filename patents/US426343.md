## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** Matrix-making machines and typewriters need a way to justify lines by adding or removing small amounts of space across a line.

**Solution:** A pivoted guide rail set by a dial lever changes the ratio of carriage movement to escapement movement by a chosen percentage.

**How it works:** An escapement controlled by a magnet and armature steps a rack driven by a spring or motor. A lever pivoted on the rack has a cam slot engaging a pin on the carriage and another pin riding in a slot of an adjustable guide rail. When the rail is angled to the carriage path, each feed step makes the lever rotate and add or subtract motion to the carriage. The setting lever with thumb piece and an index on a dial sets the unit-spaces to add or take away; at zero the rail is parallel and movements are exact. A second form uses an elbow lever and adjustable link.

**Features:**

- **Impression:** [Electric drive](../features/imp-electric.md)
- **Spacing and escapement:** [Proportional spacing](../features/esc-variable-spacing.md), [Pawl-and-rack escapement](../features/esc-pawl-rack.md)
- **Uses:** [Printing telegraph](../features/use-telegraph-printer.md)

**Parts:** carriage; spring motor; rack; escapement; armature; magnet; guide rail; setting lever; dial; cam-slot lever; elbow lever; adjustable link

**Numbers:** unit-space based widths (i = 2 units, n = 3, m = 5)
