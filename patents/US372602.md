## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A ribbon on a typebar machine must cover the print point only at the instant of impact and otherwise stay clear so the operator can see the writing; it also needs feeding and reversing.

**Solution:** A ribbon strung between two side reels is carried over the print point by a reciprocating pointer bar driven by the common detent lever, which also advances the ribbon reels by ratchet.

**How it works:** The ribbon is stretched between two reels beside the print point so it normally lies off the line of writing. Pressing any key depresses a common detent-lever pad; a pin on the pad runs in an inclined cam slot with a dwell in a swinging block linked to a sliding index bar. The bar thrusts forward early in the stroke, its guide carrying the ribbon over the print point before the type strikes, and the pointer on it marks the print position. On release the bar withdraws the ribbon, exposing the imprint. A feed lever, slotted onto a pin of the detent lever, hooks a ratchet on a reel to turn it each stroke. Two identical feed devices are joined by a rod so a handle engages one and disengages the other, reversing the feed.
