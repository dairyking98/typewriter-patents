## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typebars in a segment must sit very close together yet have wide, stable bearings; ordinary hangers force a trade-off, and bearings are exposed to dust.

**Solution:** Each typebar has an offset hollow cone-shaped pivot part whose rounded tip nests in a cup inside the neighbouring bar's cone, so adjacent bars serve as each other's bearings.

**How it works:** Each typebar has a hollow cone-like pivot part projecting sideways, with a rounded ball tip on one side and a cylindrical flange on the other. The cylindrical flange runs in a bearing eye of a hanger fixed in a slotted segment, and the ball tip sits in a cup inside the cone of the adjacent bar, so each bar is supported at two widely spaced points. The bars are in two sets nested one in another from the centre outward; a double-ended central bearing member keeps the sets apart and adjustable end screws with lock nuts press on the outermost bars to take up play for the whole set at once. The bar bodies are offset so the types meet at the printing point. The nesting also covers the bearings against dust.

**Parts:** typebar; cone-like pivot part; ball bearing tip; bearing cup; typebar hanger; segmental support; central double-ended bearing member; end adjusting screw; lock nut

**Improves on:**

- conventional typebar hangers: bearings are wide and mutually supported, adjustable in one or two operations and shielded from dust
