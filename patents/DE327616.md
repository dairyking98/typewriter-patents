## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Key typewriters with a striking, liftable and rotatable type cylinder need long key travel, differing keys and many different parts, making them costly to build.

**Solution:** All keys have the same short stroke and a single shift key; identical key groups act through slotted cam levers that rotate and lift the type cylinder by amounts set by the slot shape, with a hammer swinging the cylinder to the platen.

**How it works:** The type cylinder on a vertical spindle has eight ring fields (small letters in the top four, capitals and others below) and is rotated, lifted and swung out to the platen roller. Forty-four keys sit in eleven groups on four stepped shafts; each group pushes a common rail and a lever whose end has a slot (concentric, rising or falling). The slot engages a second rail that turns a shaft, a lever and a toothed sector which rotates the spindle left or right by an amount set by the slot slope, with the concentric slot part leaving the cylinder still during the last part of the stroke. Each key shaft also moves an arm whose slot lifts a lever by one to three field steps, transmitted to the cylinder sleeve by a forked lever. A shift key toggle coupling changes the rest position by four fields to reach the lower case rows. A hammer driven from the same rail strikes a ring on the spindle and swings the cylinder against paper, ribbon and roller; a centering pin enters one of a ring of holes to locate it exactly, and the cylinder then springs back.
