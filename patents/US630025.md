## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** The usual escapement of the Remington Standard wears its dogs and rack teeth because the points of the stop dog and spacing dog grind past each other as they swap.

**Solution:** Make the stop dog a rotatable beveled disk and the spacing dog a sliding dog with a concave face and lead, so one dog fully engages a tooth before the other releases.

**How it works:** A carrier vibrates at right angles to the rack bar, rocked forward by the space bar and returned by a coiled spring. On the carrier sits a rotatable hardened-steel disk as stop dog, spreading wear and cutting friction. A spacing dog on a foot slides in a groove parallel to the rack and is returned by a spring against a yielding finger. Its face is concaved to receive part of the disk, and its point leads, so on depressing the space bar the disk fully engages a tooth before the spacing dog is disengaged. The spacing dog then slides back into line, and on the reverse movement the carriage advances one tooth.
