## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** A key-driven type-wheel machine should position the wheel quickly and strike with enough force, yet a long print stroke would be slow and the key would still be connected during the stroke.

**Solution:** Keys pick a stop that limits the electromagnetically rotated type wheel, then an electromagnet throws the wheel at the platen on a short stroke while the key linkage is automatically disconnected well before impact.

**How it works:** Each key lever, through a push rod, rocking lever and bell crank, depresses a rocker. The rocker closes a contact that energises one of two electromagnets, which rock a toothed segment and turn the type-wheel shaft left or right by at most 180 degrees until a stop arm on the shaft hits the depressed rocker. A subsidiary magnet then closes the circuit of a printing magnet that attracts a rocker plate and throws the wheel against the platen. The same rocker plate motion works the escapement to move the carriage one space and, through a U-shaped frame, kicks the bell crank off the rocker so the key connection is broken before impact. Springs return the wheel and the rockers to rest. Shift keys slide the wheel shaft axially to bring one of two type rows to the print point, and a spring collar lets the wheel be lifted off and swapped.

**Numbers:** type wheel turns no more than 180 degrees either way; two rows of types on the wheel

**Improves on:**

- earlier key-driven type-wheel machines: key linkage is disconnected before impact so the stroke can be short yet forceful, and wheel travel is limited to 180 degrees
