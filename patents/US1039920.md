## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Printing-wheel typewriters need an extremely simple and cheap ribbon feed, with simple low-cost spools and a manual reversal that is convenient to reach.

**Solution:** The ribbon is fed by the type wheel spindle's own to-and-from movement relative to the platen, with a manual key to swap which spool is driven.

**How it works:** The type wheel is positioned by rotation, then its spindle moves bodily toward the platen to print. A feed cam above the spindle is held up when the spindle is retracted; when the spindle advances to print, the cam drops, oscillating a pawl carrier rod that draws two feed pawls back. On retraction the spindle lifts the cam and the carrier advances the pawls, so the engaged pawl turns its spool's face ratchet one step. The pawls are spaced so only one engages at a time, and a reversing key shifts the carrier to change the driven spool. Spools are cup-shaped casings on posts hung from the top plate, held by a retaining screw.
