## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Wants a segment-type typewriter that is extremely simple, durable, cheap to build and reliable.

**Solution:** Key levers with notched inclined rear arms swing a pivoted horizontal type segment on a vertical rock-shaft to the printing point, and a hammer presses the paper against the type.

**How it works:** The type segment sits on top of a vertical rock-shaft above the front feed roller, in front of the paper path. Two radial shifting arms on the rock-shaft lie over the rear arms of the key levers, which are in two groups either side. Each lever's rear arm has a notch and front and rear inclines; pressing a key raises the rear arm and an incline pushes the shifting arm, turning the shaft and segment until the arm seats in the notch, which positions the segment. A hammer (details lost in the truncated text) then presses the paper against the type through an ink ribbon wound between two spools. The paper carriage is a transverse plate with scroll guide arms and feed rollers carrying the paper upward past the type.
