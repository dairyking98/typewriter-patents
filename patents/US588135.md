## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Typebar machines normally shift the platen or carriage to print capitals versus small letters; the inventor wants case change made at the typebar itself, simply and with light touch.

**Solution:** Each typebar carries a small pivoted plate with a small and a capital letter on radiating fingers, flipped by an arm striking a common ring stop that is raised by a shift key.

**How it works:** Typebars arranged in a ring strike downward at a common point on a fixed book or sheet. At the end of each bar a plate pivoted on a transverse pin carries two types on radiating fingers; a spring holds it with the small letter active, and pins and stops limit its two positions. An arm on the plate points inward toward the bar pivot. A single ring-shaped stop around the printing point normally lies out of the arm's path, so the lowercase prints. A shift key lever lifts a frame carrying the ring into the path of the arms, so as each bar swings down the arm hits the ring and turns the plate, bringing the capital into position.

**Features:** [ELEM:typebar](../features/elem-typebar.md), [SHIFT:rotary](../features/shift-rotary.md), [IMP:element-to-platen](../features/imp-element-to-platen.md), [PAP:flat-bed](../features/pap-flat-bed.md), [SEL:keyboard-full](../features/sel-keyboard-full.md)

**Parts:** typebar; key lever; type plate; type finger; shift arm; return spring; stop ring; ring frame; shift key lever; limit pin

**Improves on:**

- Remington type of machine: case is changed by flipping the type plate at the bar instead of shifting the printing surface
