## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typewriters are complicated and apt to get out of order.

**Solution:** A typebar machine whose single rocking bar under the keys spaces the carrier, feeds the ribbon, and a cylinder carriage that shifts for capitals and numerals.

**How it works:** Type levers with knife-edge fulcrums on segmental standards are driven by three banks of key levers. A rocking bar resting on the key levers moves a shaft with arms carrying pawls. One pawl steps a rack on a carrier that moves the impression cylinder along its length. The other turns ratchets on two ribbon spools, with the ribbon led through a triangular head on a pivoted guide arm that also has an aligning pin device. The cylinder carriage moves across the carrier to shift for capitals, small letters and numerals. A spring, a bell and an adjusting bar are shown.

**Features:** [ELEM:typebar](../features/elem-typebar.md), [SEL:keyboard-full](../features/sel-keyboard-full.md), [SHIFT:platen](../features/shift-platen.md), [SHIFT:triple](../features/shift-triple.md), [INK:ribbon](../features/ink-ribbon.md), [INK:ribbon-feed](../features/ink-ribbon-feed.md), [ESC:pawl-rack](../features/esc-pawl-rack.md), [ESC:bell](../features/esc-bell.md), [PAP:platen-roller](../features/pap-platen-roller.md)
