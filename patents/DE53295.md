## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Keyboard type-wheel machines of the time were complicated and slow, and needed an accurate stop for each of the many characters; the paper carriage was also tied to its feed and could not be swapped for wider paper.

**Solution:** A hollow hard-rubber type wheel that is both rotated to the selected character and thrown against the paper as a hammer by one key stroke, with a graduated eccentric ratchet wheel to stop it and a concentric locking wheel with a fixed finger to correct its final position.

**How it works:** Each key lever, depressed, first moves one of two push frames (left or right) that engage one of two opposed circular-arc racks meshing with a gear on the type-wheel shaft, so the wheel turns up to half a revolution either way from a zero position (the key's step on a graduated flange sets how far). A second feed frame, acting inversely, then meets a tooth of an eccentric ratchet wheel on the shaft to stop the rotation at the right moment and, by continuing, tilts the frame carrying the wheel so the wheel strikes the paper like a hammer. A concentric locking wheel with V-notches on the shaft meets a fixed finger as the wheel advances, correcting small errors from wear. A catch lever releases the tilting frame only when the wheel has been set. The wheel has several rows (small letters, capitals, figures, and a row of whole words on flat facets) and is slid along its shaft by shift keys (a locked key holds capitals). The same key stroke advances the free-running paper carriage by pawl and rack, steps a two-spool ribbon whose feed reverses automatically, and the space bar and word keys use a separate double-pawl feed.
