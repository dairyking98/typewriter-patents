## Summary

**Kind:** typebar machine · **Subject (patent classes):** Typewriters or selective printing mechanisms characterised by the mounting, arrangement or disposition of the types or dies with types or dies carried on sliding bars or rods on side surfaces thereof, e.g. fixed thereto

**How it works (reading):** A single pivoted lever on a sliding carriage carries a movable type rod (or segment/wheel) that the hand positions with a pointer key over a rack, then presses to print.

## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** Earlier typewriters needed a separate lever or key per character; the inventor wants one lever to serve every character.

**Solution:** A single pivoted lever on a sliding carriage carries a movable type rod (or segment/wheel) that the hand positions with a pointer key over a rack, then presses to print.

**How it works:** Lever D is pivoted on carriage C which slides on rod B along the bed. A type rod G (types arranged to match a rack) slides in a groove in the lever; the operator moves key H to the wanted character, its pointer e dropping into rack f on the lever. Pressing down brings the type onto the paper held between rollers K and backed by a pressure body. As the lever descends a locking cone E drops into rack F on the bed plate and moves the carriage one step sideways. Word spaces are made by pulling the rod back so nothing prints, or via a lever E1 and button o that advances the carriage alone. At line end the carriage is returned and pressing lever M turns the paper roller through a ratchet for line feed. Variants replace the rod with a segment lever or a type wheel turned by the key.
