## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Characters of differing widths (European letters) printed on a Japanese-style character plate or cylinder machine come out with uneven spacing because the paper cylinder always advances by a fixed step.

**Solution:** A rule plate with a sloped or stepped face and a spring-loaded roller on a lever push a flexible shaft in or out under the feed pawl, so the pawl advances the paper cylinder by one, two or more teeth depending on where on the character plate the character was picked up.

**How it works:** The paper cylinder rides on a sliding frame that moves fore and aft over the base while the operator positions over the character plate. A lever pivoted on the sliding frame carries a roller held down by a spring in a groove along a rule plate fixed to the base; the plate's upper face is stepped or inclined to the sliding line. As the frame moves, the roller height changes and the lever pushes or pulls a flexible shaft in a flexible tube. The shaft's end sits under the feed pawl; the pawl's stroke is constant, but the shaft end lifts the pawl clear of the ratchet wheel beyond a certain point, so one tooth, two teeth or more are fed per print. Characters are grouped on the plate by width so each zone gives its own spacing. Slotted screw holes let the rule plate height and slope be adjusted.
