## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Improves Seifried's earlier Munson type-sleeve machine (US 410,066) so that the sleeve is placed and registered accurately in both longitudinal and radial position, with a simpler common return and ribbon handling that keeps the printed line visible.

**Solution:** A nine-faced type-sleeve is slid and rotated at once by one of thirty keys, and a set of ten vertical stop-pins entering cam-shaped registering notches in the sleeve ends stop and lock it both axially and rotationally before a hammer strikes it.

**How it works:** Thirty keys in three rows of ten work levers pivoted on common shafts; each set of three contiguous levers (one per row) shares a rock-shaft that, through side levers and a cam lever, slides the splined type-sleeve to the left or right and also pushes one stop-pin up. Notches in the lever rows decide which of two bars is pushed, and those bars move a sliding spiral gear in a slotted guide to rotate the sleeve shaft, so the first row only slides it while the other two rows slide and rotate. The semicircular, cam-shaped notches at the sleeve ends engage the stop-pin, which both limits and aligns the sleeve axially and radially. A single spring-driven returning treadle restores the cam lever, side levers, stop-pins and spiral-gear lever, and its rock-shaft also drives the impression hammer that strikes the sleeve from behind through an opening in a curved ribbon guide. A cap key or figure key rocks the segment-gear guide to turn the sleeve one-third or two-thirds of a turn to bring the capital or figure faces into use. A narrow ribbon runs transversely round the sleeve on pivoted spools with a clutch reversing drive linked to the spring-drum escapement, so the ribbon continuously creeps; the paper carriage runs under a spring drum with a pin-wheel escapement.
