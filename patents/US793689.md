## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** Type-heads on bars that shift between upper and lower case must be held rigidly in position during printing.

**Solution:** Give each typebar a pivoted two-type head with a trigger that both shifts and positively locks the head, operated by a movable trip and restored by a buffer.

**How it works:** Each typebar ends in a bifurcation holding a pivoted head with an upper-case and a lower-case type at an angle, banked against stops at each position. A projection with a cam end on the head sits in the hook of a trigger pivoted on the bar; a shoulder on the trigger locks the head. A movable trip is shifted into the path of the trigger as the bar swings, flipping the head to the other case and locking it. A buffer at the end of the return stroke restores the head to lower case. Shown on the Fisher flat-bed machine.
