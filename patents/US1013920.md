## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Front-strike typebar actions should feel soft at the start of key travel yet throw the bar to the platen with growing speed, resist rebound from the type rest, and fit many actions in a standard-size machine.

**Solution:** A compound lever of two third-order levers, one pivoting and one both turning and sliding over a fixed guide, gives continually diminishing leverage so the typebar accelerates throughout the stroke.

**How it works:** Each key drives a bell crank, a pull link and a sub-lever pivoted on a wire in a sub-lever segment. The sub-lever's top pivots to a nearly horizontal rod that slides through a hole in a fixed angle bar, so the rod slides endwise first and then increasingly turns about this fulcrum. The rear end of the rod pulls a radial link that swings the typebar, so at first the rod mostly slides and the bar barely moves, but leverage falls and the bar speeds up as the stroke proceeds. Short and long typebars alternate on two segments, with longer rod lever arms for the short bars so key dip is equal. In the rest position the linkage sits at a dead centre and locks the bar against rebound, and a spring per action returns it, while felt-and-metal key-stem stop pads silence the keys.
