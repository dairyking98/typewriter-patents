## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** The platen shift key is heavy because of the platen frame's inertia and rigid linkage; weak fingers tire, strong fingers slam the frame, and an earlier locking device (US504713) must be adjusted exactly or it fails to lock or locks early.

**Solution:** An elastic connecting rod lets the shift key move fully at once while the platen follows, and a locking bar on the key's rock shaft holds the shifted position until the next type key or space bar releases it.

**How it works:** Two shift keys turn a rock shaft whose arm drives the shifting frame through a connecting rod made of a main piece and a pivoted bell-crank held at right angles by two leaf springs, so the rod can change effective length. The rock shaft also moves a locking bar endwise; a spring lifts one of two shoulders on the bar against a stationary guide plate, locking the shaft in the shifted position regardless of how far the frame has moved. The next stroke of a type key or space bar presses the universal bar, which pushes a pivoted plunger down onto the locking bar and releases it. Coil springs then return the shift keys and frame to normal. The type, already at speed, strikes before the slow-starting platen has moved appreciably.

**Features:** [SHIFT:platen](../features/shift-platen.md), [SHIFT:lock](../features/shift-lock.md), [ESC:space-key](../features/esc-space-key.md), [FRM:adjustment-wear](../features/frm-adjustment-wear.md)

**Parts:** shift key; rock shaft; shifting frame; platen; elastic connecting rod; bell-crank lever; leaf spring; locking bar; guide plate; unlocking plunger; universal bar; type key lever; space key; return spring

**Improves on:**

- [US504713](../patents/US504713.md): that patent also locks the shift key down and releases it on the next stroke but needs exact adjustment of the lock timing; here the elastic rod decouples locking from frame travel
