## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Front-strike typebar actions had crowded, sloped sub-levers and unequal key leverage, and left the mechanism exposed to dust.

**Solution:** Key levers with rear pivots in four banks drive upright sub-levers through short links, and the sub-levers push the front-strike type bars through push links attached below the bar pivots, all under a plate-and-top-plate arrangement that keeps dust out.

**How it works:** Horizontal first-order key levers pivot on four wires (one per key row) so every key has the same dip and leverage; short links at their rear ends rock upright sub-levers pivoted on a flange of the base top plate. Push links from the sub-lever tops enter the type-bar heels below their pivots, so the sub-lever tops are as wide as the key system and all stand vertical. A curved universal bar, carried parallel on two rock shafts, is struck by the sub-lever tips near the end of the stroke and works the carriage escapement dogs on the crown escapement wheel; the space bar also works it. The type bars pivot on ball bearings in a grooved curved segment that swings down on pivoted arms for upper case, with a type rest following it. The carriage runs on balls on an inclined rail, with a clutch and release keys on the feed pinion.
