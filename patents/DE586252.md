## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** Syllable typewriters cannot easily press three adjacent keys with one finger, which common German combinations such as sch, cht, ung need.

**Solution:** A honeycomb key layout allows one, two or three adjacent keys to be pressed with one finger, with superposed keys sharing one print point.

**How it works:** Four rows of honeycomb-arranged keys sit under the fingers. Vertically paired keys of rows one and three, and rows two and four, strike the same point on the paper, and they lock each other so only one can be pressed. Letters are placed in syllable order (initial consonants, vowels, final consonants, endings) so syllables print side by side. A paper tape feeds a line after each release and a space bar is above the keyboard.
