## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Typebar machines with radially arranged levers are complicated, the levers can foul each other at speed, and to save space they carry only capitals.

**Solution:** A single type wheel is rotated to the right letter by a quick-pitch screw shaft driven by a frame that each key lifts to a different height, then a spring-released hammer strikes the paper against the type.

**How it works:** Each key lever lifts a vertical frame whose lower edge is an exact incline, so keys lift it by different amounts. A nut on the frame engages a quick screw thread on the type-wheel shaft, so the lift turns the wheel to the character matching the key. A tilting platform turns a star wheel one tooth, which pushes a slotted strip back and carries the hammer away; when the tooth reaches the slot the strip recoils and drives the hammer against the paper and type. A second tilting platform, whose travel is set by the bevel on each key's heel, rakes a gravity pawl on the horizontal paper frame to space by a predetermined amount. At line end a bolt trips both pawls so a spring or weight retracts the frame, and a heel-and-toe pawl on a pinned vertical frame lets the sheet rise one line space.
