## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** In a keyboard-driven type-wheel machine the wheel must not dip to the platen until it has stopped at the chosen character; the printing escapement needs to be released at exactly that moment.

**Solution:** Link the printing escapement (lock pawl and hook holding the wheel frame from tilting) to the wheel's stop-arm, so the escapement frees the wheel to print only when the stop-arm is arrested by the key's raised stop pin.

**How it works:** Pressing a key lifts a stop pin and, through levers and a bail, turns the type-wheel by a segment and pinion until a stop-arm on the pinion shaft meets the raised pin. A lever pivoted on the stop-arm is pressed against that pin and swings a bell-crank, which lifts a link and raises the lock pawl off the hook of the wheel frame. Continued key travel then swings the frame so the wheel dips and strikes the platen. A spring returns parts when the key is released. A shift lever raises or lowers the wheel shaft for case change. Variants use a yoke or a reciprocating rack as the stop-arm.
