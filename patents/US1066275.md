## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** In the Underwood front-strike machine the shiftable platen frame did not always settle on its front stops after a shift, and with wear the type alignment suffered, especially on wide carriages with long, weak platens.

**Solution:** Pivot the platen frame on gudgeons placed just behind its centre of gravity so its front always sags onto the front stops, which sit near the printing line.

**How it works:** The platen frame carries the platen on a letter-feeding carriage and is lifted by a shift key lever, bell crank and lifting rail acting on a roller, with a counterbalance spring. Rock arms from a rock shaft on the carriage hold gudgeons on the frame ends in open bearings, placed slightly behind the centre of mass and between the platen axle and the rock shaft. Guide links at each end also guide the frame. Because the frame is overbalanced at the front, it drops with its front hanging lower, so the front stops strike the carriage close to the printing line even if the rear stops do not seat. A guide fixed on the carriage engaging a slotted bushing and the hub on the platen axle stops end-wise shake while allowing the up and down shift.
