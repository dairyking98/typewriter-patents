## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typebar sub-lever linkages should start the stroke easily, accelerate toward the platen and return dead without forward rebound, while keeping the writing visible.

**Solution:** Bell-crank sub-levers whose type-bar link lies in line with the sub-lever pivot at rest, with the second link to the key lever at right angles, and typebars inclined at about 45 degrees.

**How it works:** Each typebar sits in a semicircular frame at about 45 degrees to the horizontal. A right-angled bell-crank sub-lever links one arm to the typebar and the other arm, through a link at right angles to it, to a key lever pivoted at the rear and sloping down toward the front. At rest the typebar pivot, the link joint and the sub-lever pivot are nearly in one line, so the typebar is locked back and does not rebound off its rest stop. As the key is pressed the link joint swings out and gains leverage, giving an easy start and growing speed that peaks at the platen. The frame is shaped with a sloping keyboard, a horizontal typebar deck and a rearward sloping carriage rail.
