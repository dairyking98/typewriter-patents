## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Keyboard music typewriters need a key and a type for every note in every octave, and earlier stylus-and-search-board drum machines had no means of centering the notes exactly on and between the staff lines.

**Solution:** A stylus-and-search-board type drum machine that takes two interchangeable drums (letters or music), with a height-centering device for the music drum that magnifies the tiny staff-line spacing through a transmission so larger teeth and a knife can centre it.

**How it works:** The type drum sits on a shaft moved by a stylus: moving the stylus in one direction shifts the drum lengthwise, perpendicular to that it turns the drum about its axis, bringing any character to the writing position. Striking a key pivots the drum about a transverse axis against the paper on the platen of a carriage. For music, a second drum replaces the letter drum, with the same note type usable for several octaves because the height position is not a drum row but is set by the centering device. The drum's height shift is carried by a transmission gear into a mutual shift between a toothed wheel and a knife along the teeth, so the small pitch difference between line and space positions becomes large, coarse teeth. The knife engages the teeth to centre the note exactly on or between staff lines. A separate circumferential centering device serves the ordinary letter drum.
