## Summary

**Kind:** typebar machine · **Subject (patent classes):** Typewriters or selective printing mechanisms characterised by the mounting, arrangement or disposition of the types or dies with types or dies carried on sliding bars or rods on side surfaces thereof, e.g. fixed thereto the types or dies being movable relative to the bars or rods

**In the patent’s own words:** My invention relates to that class of typewriters in which are embodied the features of a traveling paper-carriage, a platen-roll mounted thereon around which the paper is carried, arranged to be swung transversely across the line of travel of the carriage, and a set of type-bars, each carrying a plurality of types.

**How it works (reading):** A shift key rocks a shaft whose hooked arm pushes a pivoted shift-block roller against the traveling platen frame, swinging the platen into upper-case position.

## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** On a typebar machine whose bars carry two characters each, the platen roll must be swung sideways to bring lower or upper case to the print point; the inventor wants this done from the regular keyboard.

**Solution:** A shift key rocks a shaft whose hooked arm pushes a pivoted shift-block roller against the traveling platen frame, swinging the platen into upper-case position.

**How it works:** Two key arms on a rocking shaft sit beside the keyboard. Depressing one swings a link and an upright shaft with an L-shaped hooked finger, which strikes the hook of a pivoted shift-block. The block's roller, placed directly opposite the print point, pushes the platen frame sideways against a stop so the upper-case letter meets the paper. A coiled spring returns everything to lower case when the key is released, and a knob can latch the shift in the upper position. Lifting the same keys instead tilts the platen roll forward so the operator can see the printed line.
