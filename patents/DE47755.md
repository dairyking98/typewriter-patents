**How it works (reading):** A stylus button is pressed into one of 72 holes in a button plate, each hole over a type on a rotating disc, so the disc turns to that type, and the depth of the stroke sets the carriage advance.

## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** Typewriters need a simple way to bring any of many types to the print point and to space by letter width.

**Solution:** A stylus button is pressed into one of 72 holes in a button plate, each hole over a type on a rotating disc, so the disc turns to that type, and the depth of the stroke sets the carriage advance.

**How it works:** The button plate, hinged and spring-held, sits over a type disc whose types are on resilient radial springs. A cranked lever G, ending in a fork carrying a steel-cone button M, turns the disc; the button is pressed into a hole (letters marked beside holes on radii matching the types, wider holes toward the centre for wide letters). Pressing down moves a pin L, and pushes the button plate, whose arms drive a U-shaped pressure lever with a pressure pin that goes down faster than the plate and prints the set type onto the paper above a push rod. A pivoted ink pad lever keeps the types inked and is cammed clear of the type about to print. Pin L's stroke depends on hole size and so on letter width; it acts through a lever and bridge with three screws of different length that engage and release pawls on the carriage rack, so the spring-driven carriage moves by an amount tied to the letter.
