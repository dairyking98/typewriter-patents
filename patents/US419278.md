## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Radial type-lever typewriters used a secondary key lever between finger and typebar; the inventor wants the operator to press the type-carrying lever directly, in a strong, simple machine.

**Solution:** Elbowed type levers pivoted round a circular base are pressed directly by the finger, and each stroke also drags down a ring on a feed lever that turns the paper roller one letter space.

**How it works:** Elbowed levers, each with one type, are pivoted in a circular cast-iron rim so all type ends converge on one print point on a cork paper roller. The operator presses a finger plate on the lever until it hits a rubber-buffered guard plate. The lever's end passes through a ring on a feed lever, pulling it down and, through a forked lever and rod, a pawl turns the roller by one tooth, which is the letter space. A separate spacing lever with its own key does the same for word spaces. At line end the roller is turned back by a hand wheel and the whole roller carriage slides axially on a spring-pinned set of holes, one hole per line. A bell on the roller spindle rings near line end, the paper is held by two eccentric clips, and ink comes from two small rollers swung by a button over the type, or from a ribbon.
