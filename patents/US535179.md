## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** Telegraph signalling with typewriting had required specially constructed machines; the inventor wants an attachment for standard typewriters.

**Solution:** A signal-making device mounted on a key lever makes Morse dots and dashes in a telegraph circuit as the key prints its letter.

**How it works:** A rod mounted on insulating hard-rubber brackets lies across the machine, with a conducting plate above it, each wired to binding posts. Signal-producing devices on the rod are shaped (indented) for each letter's Morse code and operated by the key lever motion. When the key lever moves, it strikes the type bar to print as usual and also closes the circuit to a telegraph instrument connected to a battery and ground, sending the message to a distant station. A pivoted switch lever cuts the telegraph out so the typewriter can work independently.
