## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** Gear-shaped escapement teeth touch at a single point, so they wear fast and the carriage escapement loses its freedom of movement.

**Solution:** Use straight saw-tooth racks whose working faces are square to the carriage travel so every tooth bears over its whole face, with a sliding short rack as the second stop.

**How it works:** A long rack hangs from the carriage on pivots at each end and can be tilted between two short stop racks. One short rack is fixed to the frame; the other slides on it, guided parallel, and is pulled by a spring against the carriage direction. Tilting the long rack onto the fixed rack releases the sliding rack, which the spring throws forward a set number of teeth until a stop pin; letting the long rack rise meshes it with the sliding rack and the carriage spring pulls both back until the slot ends. The throw is changed by moving the stop pin on a rock shaft between seats, via a key and lever.

**Features:** [Pawl-and-rack escapement](../features/esc-pawl-rack.md), [Mainspring-drawn carriage](../features/esc-spring-drum.md), [Adjustment and wear take-up](../features/frm-adjustment-wear.md)

**Parts:** long rack; fixed short rack; sliding short rack; spring; stop pin; rock shaft; stirrup; lever; guide slot

**Improves on:**

- Double pawl escapement with gear-form teeth: straight saw teeth with full-face contact instead of single-point contact
