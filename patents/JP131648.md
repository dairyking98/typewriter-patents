## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Japanese typewriters for right-to-left vertical writing step the paper cylinder with a toothed rack and pin that make a high-pitched rubbing sound, and horizontal writing is hard.

**Solution:** A key stroke slides a lever and link that turn a small gear, while a notched brake plate and detent release and re-catch a gear so it turns a fixed amount, moving the character-spacing rack by one or two teeth.

**How it works:** Striking the key presses an L-shaped lever, sliding a slide and an L-shaped pawl lever. The pawl turns a small gear, which turns a gear meshing with the character-spacing rack carrying the paper cylinder. The slide also pulls a link that turns a notched brake plate, lifting the detent pin out of the notch and the detent pawl out of the gear for an instant, so the gear advances a set distance. Springs return the plate and detent so they re-engage exactly. A cam and adjusting plate limit the pawl lever's stroke, choosing one tooth or two or more per stroke.

## Description (English translation of the Japanese text, opening)

> Sideways-feed device for a Japanese-script typewriter. Nature and object of the invention. This invention relates to a sideways-feed device for a Japanese-script typewriter, characterised in that the turning of a lever slides an L-shaped lever and a link; through a small gear, these turn a gear held loosely on the same axis as a brake plate; the link turns the brake plate, which has a notch, and so frees the pawl of a detent from the gear, and at the same time takes the detent's pin out of the notch, so that the gear is given a fixed amount of turn. The object is a sideways-feed device for a Japanese-script typewriter, simple in construction and easy to work, which moves the character-spacing rack carrying the paper cylinder accurately to the right by one or two teeth at a time for vertical writing; which, after the vertical feed of the cylinder has been stopped, also makes right-to-left horizontal writing easy; and in which handling the paper cylinder does not give the unpleasant high-pitched sound of earlier machines.
