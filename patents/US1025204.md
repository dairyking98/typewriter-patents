## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** A spring-actuated impact hammer needs its blow force adjustable to suit light work or manifolding.

**Solution:** A pivoted hammer is cocked by a key-driven arm that stretches a spring, then released by a pawl, with spring tension set by screws that slide the spring's attachment points.

**How it works:** A curved hammer pivots at its lower end and is held back by a tooth engaged in a notched pawl. Pressing a finger key moves a transverse bar down, whose first finger pushes an arm (on the hammer's pivot) down, stretching a coil spring that joins the arm to the hammer and so storing energy. Further down, a second finger on the bar depresses the pawl, freeing the hammer so the spring throws it forward against the impression surface. On the bar's return, a spring finger lifts the arm, which pushes a pin on the hammer to reset it, and the pawl spring re-engages the tooth. Force is adjusted by turning a vertical threaded member to raise or lower the sleeve holding the spring's hammer end, or by turning a horizontal threaded member so the spring end travels along its thread.
