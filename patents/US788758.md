## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typewriter escapements should release the carriage instantly when a key starts to return, hold it firmly during the type impact, and start the carriage moving before the dogs are fully clear.

**Solution:** A holding dog pivoted on an axis parallel to the rack plane, with an arm bearing on the rack side, acts as a brace against the rack tooth while a key is down, plus a feed dog that steps back one tooth on release.

**How it works:** A key stroke rocks the dog carrier. The feed dog, normally engaged, steps back a tooth on release while the holding dog engages the rack tooth. The holding dog's notch is angled so the line from its pivot to the notch bottom braces against the tooth and resists the carriage spring while the key stays down. On the return of the carriage the dog is turned by its arm and the rack to the face of the next tooth, with a stop on the carrier limiting reverse motion. Shown on a Remington No. 6.
