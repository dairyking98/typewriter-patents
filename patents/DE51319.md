## Summary

**Kind:** typebar machine · **Subject (patent classes):** Typewriters or selective printing mechanisms characterised by the mounting, arrangement or disposition of the types or dies with types or dies carried on sliding bars or rods

**How it works (reading):** A row of loose types sits in a sliding box-shaped carrier moved by a finger handle along a toothed index bar, with a universal bar and finger piece pressing the selected type to the paper.

## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** The aim is a small, cheap, durable, light and powerful typewriter.

**Solution:** A row of loose types sits in a sliding box-shaped carrier moved by a finger handle along a toothed index bar, with a universal bar and finger piece pressing the selected type to the paper.

**How it works:** A one-piece cast main frame has a channel in which the box-shaped type carrier slides; its loose individual types have a rear groove so a flange holds them at inking height and stops them falling out. A guide plate with an opening shaped like the type body guides the type for a straight print, and a spring-wire-mounted inking roller on each side touches the type face. The operator moves the carrier by a wire handle with a finger piece; the finger piece engages slots between pointed teeth on a front index bar, locating the type over the print point. Pressing the finger piece pushes a universal bar across the machine front that drives the print, while the index bar and its levers can be shifted for capitals, and for figures and punctuation. A space lever and a rear pawl for carriage guiding are described but the text is cut off. The paper carriage lifts to vertical and rides on a rail.
