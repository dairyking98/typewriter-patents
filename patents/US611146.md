## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** A cheaply made typewriter with a rotary type wheel or segment is wanted.

**Solution:** Pressing a key swings a stop arm fixed to the type wheel shaft until it hits that depressed key, so the wheel turns to a character according to the key's distance from the middle.

**How it works:** Two sets of keys lie either side of a median line. Each key arm depresses a main lever carrying a secondary lever; a loose slot-and-pin link swings a lever whose arm engages shoulders on a disk fixed to the type-wheel shaft, rotating the shaft and the loosely mounted type wheel or segment. A stop arm on that shaft rests between the key sets and swings under the raised key arms until it strikes the depressed key arm, stopping wheel and character at the print point. Keys farther from the median give larger rotation, and the paper carriage touches the paper to the type for printing, with a lever preventing contact during spacing.

**Features:**

- **Selection:** [Keys that only set the element](../features/sel-dummy-keyboard.md), [Keys drive the element by racks or gears](../features/sel-differential-drive.md), [Selection locked for printing](../features/sel-selector-locking.md)
- **Type element:** [Type sector or segment](../features/elem-sector-segment.md), [Type wheel (types on the rim)](../features/elem-wheel-rim.md)
- **Impression:** [Platen pressed to the element](../features/imp-platen-to-element.md)
- **Frame and body:** [Few parts, low cost](../features/frm-few-parts.md)
