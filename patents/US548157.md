## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A visible-writing typewriter with few type-bars, where each bar must print several characters while every key still travels the same distance so the impression is uniform.

**Solution:** Twelve horizontally sliding type-bars, each carrying six characters (three lower, three upper), are projected variable distances by groups of three key-levers against stops, and a hammer then strikes the stopped bar from above.

**How it works:** Type-bars lie radially around the print point on top of the platen and slide in T-grooves. Keys come in groups of three per bar and each group acts through one angular lever with a two-section yielding joint. Depressing a key pulls down its own movable stop (or none) so the bar's shoulder meets a stop at one of three distances, bringing one of three characters over the print point; the joint buckles after the bar stops so the key always travels the same distance. A caps key swings a sway-bar sideways so a second shoulder meets the stops and prints the upper-case character, and also moves the key-lever fulcrum for extra throw. A hammer on a spring-adjustable helve, driven by the key bail, then strikes the bar onto the paper; a felt ink roller sits over the types. The carriage runs on balls, is driven by a spring-wheel and cord, spaced by a rack with two spring pawls on a double cam, and returned by a front crank and dial, with an adjustable switch that line-spaces the platen automatically.
