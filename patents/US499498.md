## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** A type-wheel machine needs inking that does not drag on the type while the wheel turns or shifts, a way to shift between rows of type, and a carriage that spaces, signals line end and returns itself.

**Solution:** A multi-row type wheel on an upright shaft is rotated by a key-driven gear sector and lifted to the chosen row by a stepped shift lever, with a split two-part ink pad toggled away from the type during movement and the paper platen pressed through a hole in a shield onto the one selected character.

**How it works:** Pressing any key lever of a staggered set lowers a rocking bail by an amount unique to that key. First it opens the toggle joint so the two-part ink pad moves off the type, then (through a lost-motion slot) pulls a rod that turns a gear sector and the pinion on the wheel's vertical shaft to the chosen character. The same stroke draws a second bail and rod that propel the platen carriage and paper against a loose shield whose hole admits only the selected type. On release springs return the wheel, the pad re-closes on the type to ink it, and a pawl on the rod steps a ratchet barrel that winds cords to advance the carriage one space. A separate finger piece opens the pad and depresses a stepped shift lever (held in notches) to lift the wheel shaft to another row; the wheel is held on its shaft by one screw and can be swapped for other fonts. A bell rings a few spaces before line end, and at line end the next key stroke trips a sector to return the carriage and feed the paper one line.
