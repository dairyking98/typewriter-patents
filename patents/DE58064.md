## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** A type-wheel typewriter needs a simple way for each key to set the wheel to its character exactly and hold it there while the hammer prints, without a separate selector.

**Solution:** Each key first wedges an inclined plane against a plate on the type wheel shaft, turning the wheel by an angle set by the incline, and then, with the wheel locked flat against that incline, drives a hammer to print and pulls the carriage pawl.

**How it works:** Pressing a key in its first stroke pushes a nose with a particular slope against a setting plate fixed to the type wheel shaft; the shaft turns until the nose lies flat on the plate, so the angle equals the slope and the wanted type is in front of the paper. The flat contact then locks the wheel as the key continues its second stroke. That second stroke rotates a cross rod and lever that withdraw the carriage pawl from the rack and push a hammer slide, so the hammer inside the wheel drives the set type (carried on a rubber band through slots in the rim) against the paper on a flat pressure plate. Releasing the key lets springs return the nose, the hammer and the pawl, which advances the carriage one space, and a spring or counterweight returns the wheel. Variants put the slope either on the keys/levers/sliders or on per-character plates on the shaft; extra rows or alphabets are reached by sliding the shaft axially with extra keys, or by turning the hub 180 degrees.
