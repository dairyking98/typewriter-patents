## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Typewriters were bulky and costly; the inventor wants a compact, cheap, portable machine with a light type cylinder driven by a single key.

**Solution:** A single pointed key is moved by hand over a swinging chart board of conical holes, a cord turns the type cylinder and a frame slides it along its shaft to match, and pressing the key into a hole swings the board down to print and, on release, to step the carriage.

**How it works:** A curved inclined key board carries conical holes each marked with a letter. A pointed key on a sliding arm is moved over the board and its sideways motion pulls a cord that turns a pulley under the type-cylinder carrier, rotating the cylinder; its up-and-down motion tilts a swinging frame whose arms slide the cylinder along its oblique shaft, choosing the row. Pressing the key into the hole swings the whole board, shaft and cylinder toward the paper, where radial pins entering holes in a guide plate steady and align the impression onto a rubber feed roll. A fabric ink roller carried on the board rubs on the type. A separate shift key on the rock-shaft raises the frame so capitals (lower rows of the cylinder) print instead of small letters. Releasing the board lets a spring return it and rock a pawl shaft that advances the carriage one tooth; a separate space key advances it further. Flipping the swinging plate lifts the rack clear for carriage return.
