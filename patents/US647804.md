## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Ordinary typewriters need very accurate manufacture and fine adjustment, and give a weak blow unsuited to mimeograph stencil work. The inventor wants fine alignment, a powerful blow and a cheap simple construction.

**Solution:** A rotatable type-wheel is positioned by a driver lever that runs until a stop arm hits the depressed index key, then the same actuating key, through a rocker of bail and link, fires the hammer and spaces the carriage.

**How it works:** Index keys arranged in concentric arcs only mark the character; pressing one puts its stem in the path of a stop arm on the type-wheel shaft. A separate actuating key near the wrist presses a main lever onto a link of a rocking bail. Because the wheel positioning spring is weaker than the hammer and spacing springs, the link first moves a driver lever that spins the wheel until the stop arm strikes the depressed index key stem. The continued pressure then rocks the bail, which strikes the hammer arm and drives the paper against a ribbon and the type. The bail hits a stop block just before the end of the stroke, giving the hammer a sudden jerk and rebound so the returning wheel does not smear the print. The same bail tilts a dog off the carriage rack to space one step.
