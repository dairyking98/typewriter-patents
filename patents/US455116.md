## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Holding down the shift key for capitals ties up the hand; the inventor wants a cheap foot treadle that shifts the platen on existing machines.

**Solution:** A floor treadle pulls a chain to a lever clamped on the typewriter's platen-shift rock-shaft, with a spring-loaded yielding rod so over-pressing the treadle cannot strain the machine.

**How it works:** A treadle under the table pivots on a shaft in the cabinet. Pushing it down pulls a spring-loaded screw rod and a chain through a hole in the table top to a forked lever clamped to the platen-shifting rock-shaft of a Remington-class machine. The lever turns the shaft, whose arm pushes the transverse rod back so the platen shifts to print capitals. Once the platen hits its stops, the spring on the rod yields and absorbs extra foot pressure. Nuts on the rod set the spring tension, and a rubber buffer and bowed spring quiet and return the treadle. Clamp guides screwed to the table front hold the machine in place.

**Features:** [USE:accessory](../features/use-accessory.md), [SHIFT:platen](../features/shift-platen.md)

**Parts:** treadle; chain; attachment lever; rock-shaft; transverse rod; screw rod; yielding spring; trunnion block; clamp guide; rubber buffer; bowed plate spring
