## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Platen-shifting typewriters let the platen wobble or have play in its raised and lowered positions, which spoils impression.

**Solution:** Hang the platen frame from the carriage on parallel link pairs and lift it on a guided cross bar with adjustable cushioned stops, so it is held rigidly in each shift position.

**How it works:** The platen sits in a frame of end plates and cross rods, joined by pairs of equal parallel links to a carriage that runs on two guide rods. Because the links are parallel and symmetrical, the platen moves up and down while staying parallel to itself. A wheel on the platen frame rests on a transverse bar that slides in slotted guides. Shift levers at both sides of the machine lift the bar through a transverse rod, lever arms and links, and adjustable screws with leather plug cushions stop the bar at the normal and shifted positions. Line spacing uses a ratchet on the platen, a spring pawl and a rotary stop plate with three steps that gives feeds of one, two or three teeth. The same rod retracts the carriage. Key levers pivot on a knife-edge bar and are returned by springs whose tension is adjusted by sliding blocks on a grooved rod.
