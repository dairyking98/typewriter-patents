## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A keyboard typewriter with a rotating type-cylinder needs a simple way to turn the cylinder to the right character for each key without a separate linkage per key.

**Solution:** All keys press on one pivoted perforated key-plate, and collars set at different heights on the key stems make the plate move by different amounts, which a segmental rack and pinion converts into the right cylinder rotation.

**How it works:** Each key stem passes through a perforation in the key-plate and carries a collar at a height unique to its character. When a key is depressed its collar meets the plate sooner or later, so the plate is tilted by a distinct amount. A link bar joins the plate to a segmental rack that meshes with a pinion on the type-cylinder shaft, so a small plate movement turns the cylinder to the first character of a row and a full movement to the last. On release a counterweighted lever and spring return the plate and the cylinder to zero, with an adjustable set screw against a rubber buffer fixing the rest position. Cone-point pivots for the plate can be tightened as they wear.

**Features:** [SEL:keyboard-full](../features/sel-keyboard-full.md), [SEL:differential-drive](../features/sel-differential-drive.md), [ELEM:cylinder-multirow](../features/elem-cylinder-multirow.md), [FRM:adjustment-wear](../features/frm-adjustment-wear.md)

**Parts:** key-plate; collared key stem; key; link bar; segmental rack; pinion; type-cylinder; counterweight lever; return spring; set screw; rubber buffer; cone-point pivot

**Improves on:**

- keyboard machines with separate per-key rotation linkages: one common plate and graded collar heights drive the cylinder
