## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** A single-key type-wheel machine must stop the wheel exactly at the chosen character despite momentum and also give word spaces.

**Solution:** A pivoted sweep arm geared to a type-wheel is swung over a sector index plate of characters, and pressing the key button swings the platen carriage into the wheel while a detent drops into a brake wheel to lock the wheel's position.

**How it works:** A single knob and key T is on a sweep arm that swings over a graduated sector plate carrying the same letters as the type-wheel. A segmental gear on the sweep turns a pinion on the vertical shaft of the type-wheel so that one full sweep travel equals one full wheel revolution. Pressing the button rocks a shaft through a bell-crank and swings the carriage with its platen roller forward against the type and ribbon. At the same time a detent on the rock-shaft enters between two teeth of a brake wheel below the type-wheel, locking the wheel against overrun. One tooth space of the brake wheel is closed, so when the sweep is set over two stars on the index the detent cannot enter, the carriage cannot reach the type, and no print is made, which makes a word space. The carriage steps by a ratchet bar and pawl on each swing forward.
