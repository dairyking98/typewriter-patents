## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** A cheap, simple, compact single-key typewriter that a traveller can carry in a grip with other articles.

**Solution:** A row of pivoted typebars sits side by side on a sliding carrier that the operator moves by a hand pinion to bring the wanted bar under one striking key; striking also steps the head along a guide rod by a full space.

**How it works:** A base plate with feed rolls (turned by a thumb wheel) serves as the platen, with a raised plate above it and a guide rod on which a head slides and pivots. The head holds a type-bar carrier, a bar of angular typebars on a rod, that the operator slides through the head by turning a stem with a pinion that meshes with a rack on the carrier. One vertical key and stem on the head strikes whichever typebar is under it down onto the base plate; wire springs return the bars. Pressing the key also lifts a spacing bar with two inclined teeth that engage staggered recesses along the guide rod, so the head moves two half-spaces, one full space, per stroke; a finger extension on the lever spaces without printing. A detachable sheet-metal frame carries an endless ribbon around rollers between the type faces and paper, advanced by hand, and a hook locks the head down during a line.
