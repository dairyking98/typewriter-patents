## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Carriage stops for a visible front-strike typewriter should be adjustable, positive, cheap sheet-metal parts, and releasable so the carriage can travel further.

**Solution:** Several sheet-metal stops slide and snap into slots on a tiltable bar on the carriage, each hitting a fixed bracket, and a finger lever rocks the bar to clear the stop.

**How it works:** A square slotted bar is pivoted in the carriage ends and carries two to three stops, each a pair of nested U-headed sheet-metal members joined at pendent arms and pushed apart by a flat spring. A tooth on one head engages a bar slot to lock the stop, and pressing its roughened head releases it so it can be slid to a new position. A fixed bracket on the carriage bed lies in the path of the stops' pendent arms, so the carriage halts when the first stop meets it. Depressing a finger lever at the bar end rocks the bar and moves that stop clear so the carriage runs on until the next stop meets the bracket. The stops can also serve tabulating.
