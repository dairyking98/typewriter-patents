## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Ordinary typewriters are slow; the inventor wants to write words or syllables of up to six letters at once to work about four times as fast and keep up with dictation.

**Solution:** A typebar keyboard where several keys are pressed together and laterally pivoted bar suspensions are displaced cumulatively by spacer cams so the letters land side by side.

**How it works:** Each key lever moves an intermediate lever, which drives a hammer-type typebar and also a link to a spacer cam. Each typebar hangs from a suspension bar that can swing sideways. When a key is pressed its spacer cam bears on a roller of the adjacent bar and shoves it, and all later bars, laterally, so when several keys are pressed the displacements add and each letter prints one space further along. The carriage advances in proportion to the number of keys depressed, powered by a spring drum wound by a key or pedal. A commutator key shifts three-character typebars.

**Features:**

- **Selection:** [Full keyboard, a key per character](../features/sel-keyboard-full.md)
- **Type element:** [Typebars](../features/elem-typebar.md)
- **Spacing and escapement:** [Mainspring-drawn carriage](../features/esc-spring-drum.md), [Proportional spacing](../features/esc-variable-spacing.md)

**Parts:** key; key lever; intermediate lever; typebar; suspension bar; spacer cam; spring drum; commutator key; pedal

**Numbers:** up to 6 letters per stroke; about 4 times ordinary speed

**Improves on:**

- ordinary typewriters: writes several letters at one stroke
