## Summary

**Kind:** type-element machine · **Subject (patent classes):** Typewriters or selective printing mechanisms characterised by the mounting, arrangement or disposition of the types or dies with types or dies arranged in stationary or sliding cases or frames or upon flexible strips, plates, bars or rods; Japanese-language typewriter

**In the English translation of the patent’s own words:** This invention relates to a typewriter in which a type rod holding a type, on its way through the type tube, pushes up the pawl of a bell-crank lever by means of its projection, and so acts on a feed pawl to make the type tube advance.

## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** In a tube-and-rod loose-type typewriter, uneven character spacing and complex spacing mechanisms.

**Solution:** The type rod's own projection trips a bell-crank lever as it is drawn up out of the type tube, releasing a feed pawl so a spring advances the tube exactly one tooth, while the paper sits on a separate flat bed.

**How it works:** A type tube on an arm slides along two guide rods, held against a spring by a feed pawl in stop teeth. The type rod, carrying a loose type, has a projection that on the way up pushes the pawl of a bell-crank lever on the feed lever, withdrawing the feed pawl from the stop teeth. A spring then pulls the arm and tube forward one tooth, giving uniform spacing. The paper is clamped by spring strips on a flat bed on rollers and rails, moved crosswise by a rack and pinion for line spacing. A ribbon on spools at both ends of the arm runs between the tube and the paper, and the types are kept in separate boxes.
