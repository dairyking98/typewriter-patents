## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** A typebar machine that uses an inking pad instead of a ribbon needs the type face to be offered to the pad at rest and to the paper when printing, with accurate alignment, equal key leverage and cheap hangers.

**Solution:** Typebars that present the type to an inking pad at rest, sheet-metal hangers with adjustable trunnion grip, and key-to-typebar links made of equal bell-crank pulls so all keys have the same leverage.

**How it works:** Each key stem hinges to a bell-crank lever which pulls, by a rod, a second bell crank that in turn pulls a vertical pull-rod hinged to the short arm of a typebar. Keys at the front and rear of the nest use the same system, so all keys have equal leverage and travel. The typebar hangs in a struck-up sheet-metal hanger with side holders bearing its trunnions, and a screw lets the grip be tightened. The print point is beneath the platen. Pull-rods can be fitted with turnbuckles for length adjustment.
