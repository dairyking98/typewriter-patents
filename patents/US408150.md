## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** In Crandall-type machines the type-cylinder on a pivoted arm is hard to start and tends to recoil after the blow.

**Solution:** A rock-shaft cam arm with a gradually quickening curve starts the type cylinder slowly and delivers the blow at speed, then locks against recoil.

**How it works:** A key lever lifts a yoke on a rock shaft which carries a cam arm with a shallow notch. The cam arm bears on a pin and inclined face of an arm on the yoke that carries the type cylinder. Pressing the key rocks the shaft and the cam throws the type cylinder forward, slow at first and quickening as the curve works, giving an increasing leverage. At the end of the stroke the pin has left the notch and the cam end lies between pin and inclined face, a rigid link that blocks recoil until the key is released.
