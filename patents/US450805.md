## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** The space-key connection to the carriage escapement of the Yost typebar machine needed to be simpler, and its return spring needed to be adjustable without long tools or dismantling; the space key also should not bob during ordinary typing.

**Solution:** Link the space key and the circular universal bar to one lever that rocks the escapement dog through a vertical link, with a slotted coupling to the space key and a return spring whose tension is set by a thumb nut under the bed plate.

**How it works:** A horizontal lever rocks in a standard; its rear arm connects by a link to a rocker-shaft carrying the dog that alternates between the two Caligraph-style feed racks on the carriage. Its front arm connects by a rod with an elongated slot to the space-key lever, and a spindle carries the circular universal bar onto the lever. Pressing the space key rocks the lever against a coiled spring, shifting the dog; releasing it lets the spring return everything and the carriage steps one tooth. Pressing a character key pulls its connecting rod down, whose lug depresses the universal bar and rocks the same lever, so each letter also spaces. The slot in the coupling lets the lever move without moving the space key, avoiding its bobbing. The spring runs through the bed plate to a screw and thumb nut in a U-shaped bracket for tension adjustment.
