## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** Ordinary typebar machines need a long key stroke and heavy touch, batter the type faces and spring the bars, and manifold poorly.

**Solution:** An auxiliary cam-edged lever between each key lever and typebar makes the typebar move slowly at first then accelerate at the end of the stroke and return fast.

**How it works:** Each key lever presses on the cam-shaped upper edge of an auxiliary lever pivoted on a cross-bar. The auxiliary lever is coupled by a wire to the typebar. As the key goes down the cam gives a growing leverage change so the bar speeds up toward the print point and snaps back quickly. Two banks of key levers drive two circles of typebars of unequal length (shorter in the upper, smaller circle) all striking one common point. The cam may alternatively be on the key lever.

**Features:** [ELEM:typebar](../features/elem-typebar.md), [SEL:keyboard-full](../features/sel-keyboard-full.md), [IMP:element-to-platen](../features/imp-element-to-platen.md), [IMP:impression-adjust](../features/imp-impression-adjust.md)

**Parts:** key lever; auxiliary lever; cam edge; coupling; wire ligature; typebar; return spring; cross-bar

**Numbers:** two circles of typebars

**Improves on:**

- Remington / Caligraph type typebar machines: adds accelerating auxiliary levers for shorter stroke and lighter touch
