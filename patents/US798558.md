## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** In a front-strike typebar machine the key stroke is hard at the start and the typebars rebound or vibrate and can collide at speed.

**Solution:** An intermediary first-kind lever joined by toggle links to key-lever and typebar gives an easy early stroke, an accelerated final swing, toggle locks against vibration, and a common spring-loaded slider that kicks the bar back.

**How it works:** Each key-lever is linked to one arm of a lever pivoted at its middle, and the other arm is linked to the heel of a horizontally resting typebar. At rest the typebar link is in line with the fulcrum, so gravity holds a toggle lock against vibration. Most of the key travel moves the lever through the early part, and the last bit of key travel flings the typebar to the platen with built-up momentum. At the end of the stroke the key-side link comes into line with the fulcrum, which limits key travel and holds the bar just short of the platen. A sliding frame common to all levers, held by a spring, is pushed by each lever arm and on release drives the bar away from the platen at once. The slider's motion also drives the escapement.
