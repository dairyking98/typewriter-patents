## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Improve a disc type-writer with an oscillating paper roller: a simpler carriage feed, a way to ink the types, and automatic line spacing; also stop the lever being placed slightly off a letter and blur the print.

**Solution:** A lever hinged to the type disc turns it to a letter and, pressed down, tilts the paper roller to the type; a V-tipped spring-loaded guide pin seats in a serrated crown to centre the letter, and a spring-driven carriage is released one tooth at a time by an anchor escapement tripped by the rising shaft.

**How it works:** The annular type disc (three clamped thin discs, the middle one notched to grip the types) turns on a fixed plate that has a serrated crown and letter-circle. A lever pivoted to a lug on the disc is swung to a letter and pressed; its V-shaped spring-loaded projection drops into the crown serration and corrects a slightly misplaced lever, floating so it does not jar the disc during the blow. The pressed shaft tilts the paper-roller frame through an angle iron and rod against the type, with spring-backed buffers softening the stop. A coiled-spring drum pulls the carriage; its rack meshes with a gear on a shaft with an escape wheel held by an anchor escapement. When the shaft rises a pawl on it releases the anchor for one tooth, spacing one letter, and a separate button lever gives extra spaces. An inking roller on a sprung arm is lifted against the chosen type by the downward stroke. Returning the carriage lifts the rack out of mesh and a pawl-and-ratchet turns the roller one line automatically at a plate on the way-bars.
