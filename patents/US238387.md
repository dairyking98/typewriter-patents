## Summary

**Kind:** type-element machine · **Subject (patent classes):** Typewriters or selective printing mechanisms characterised by the mounting, arrangement or disposition of the types or dies with types or dies arranged in stationary or sliding cases or frames or upon flexible strips, plates, bars or rods

**In the patent’s own words:** My invention relates to type-writers of that class in which the type are carried on a plate made movable so as to bring any required letter into alignment with any desired point upon the paper.

**How it works (reading):** A flexible rubber type-plate carried on a spring-driven stepping carriage is moved by a hand arm whose spur drops into lettered holes of an index plate, and the same arm's downward push presses the chosen type through a hole in an inking plate onto the paper.

## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Typewriters of the day had too many parts, were large and costly; the inventor wants a simple, small, cheap, durable machine.

**Solution:** A flexible rubber type-plate carried on a spring-driven stepping carriage is moved by a hand arm whose spur drops into lettered holes of an index plate, and the same arm's downward push presses the chosen type through a hole in an inking plate onto the paper.

**How it works:** A rubber type-form with its characters on the underside is mounted in a rigid frame on a pair of parallelogram link sets under a hinged plate A, so it can be shifted forward/back and sideways while staying square. The operator grasps arm P, whose spur fits one of the holes in an index plate on top of plate A; each hole is lettered to match a type position, and seating the spur brings that letter under a fixed printing stud and over the opening in a lower inked plate B. Pressing down on the arm depresses plate A against springs so the stud pushes that single type through the opening onto the ink pad and the paper on a bar below. The same depression strikes the escapement lever: a mainspring barrel meshing with a round rack rod pushes the plates one notch to the right, with a stop allowing one or two notches for letter spacing. A trigger and bell warn of line end, and a clip, feed roller and lever advance the paper by line.
