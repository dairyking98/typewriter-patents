## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typebar typewriters with the types striking the middle of a ring were complicated, and the impact varied with the thickness of paper on the cylinder.

**Solution:** Pivoted type levers hung on radial tongues under a ring plate are driven by parallelogram key levers through pull wires, with a stroke-limiting crosspiece that makes the impact independent of paper thickness.

**How it works:** A top plate carries a wide ring whose underside has radial grooves holding hanging tongues. Each slotted type lever pivots on a tongue and carries a type body with a cylindrical peg, adjustable radially and by turning. Each key bar forms a parallelogram with a rear link and base so every key moves the same way, and a pull wire with a U-fork universal joint couples it to the short arm of the type lever. A crosspiece stops the lever against the tongue so it springs up and strikes the paper or ribbon directly, so extra sheets need no harder blow. Springs on a crossbar return the key bars.
