## Summary

**Kind:** type-element machine · **Subject (patent classes):** Typewriters or selective printing mechanisms characterised by the mounting, arrangement or disposition of the types or dies with types or dies arranged in stationary or sliding cases or frames or upon flexible strips, plates, bars or rods; Japanese-language typewriter

**In the English translation of the patent’s own words:** This invention relates to a printing machine in which, when the paper cylinder moving over the type magazine comes over any type-holding groove and takes up the printing position, the type in that groove points obliquely at the centre of the cylinder.

**How it works (reading):** Each loose square-bar type carries its own sunk index character on its side as well as the raised printing character on its end, and the grooves are slanted so the type under the paper cylinder points obliquely at the cylinder centre, where a single key fires it up through a guide frame.

## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** In magazine-type Japanese typewriters, the operator has to find loose types among many grooves by matching against separately written index characters, and the construction is complicated.

**Solution:** Each loose square-bar type carries its own sunk index character on its side as well as the raised printing character on its end, and the grooves are slanted so the type under the paper cylinder points obliquely at the cylinder centre, where a single key fires it up through a guide frame.

**How it works:** Types rest in slanted U-grooves in a stepped magazine, index character facing the operator. The operator holds a key and slides a two-axis carriage (lengthwise on rails, crosswise on balls) until the paper cylinder and a guide frame sit over the wanted type. Pressing the key lowers the guide frame over the type, lets the cylinder tip down toward the type, and through a bent lever and roller in cam grooves first raises a rack bar to mesh a row of teeth at the back of the magazine (fixing the position), then jerks a weighted striking lever whose beak knocks the type up the guide channel against the paper. A notch in the type catches a spring detent on the guide frame, which pushes the type back into its groove. The cylinder tilting also trips a pawl on a rack plate to feed the cylinder sideways one space per stroke.
