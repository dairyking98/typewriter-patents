## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** Shifting a typewriter carriage for case change should be easy and free of binding.

**Solution:** Ball bearings at the rear form a fulcrum while a shift rail with balls raises the front, and a spring-loaded auxiliary ball track follows the carriage to prevent binding.

**How it works:** The carriage rear rides on balls in V-grooved rails, which act as the pivot. A shift key rocks a lever that raises a front shift rail, lifting a carriage track on balls in V-grooves. A movable auxiliary track carried on pins and flat springs pushes balls upward so they always follow the raised carriage track, keeping a proper ball bearing in either position.
