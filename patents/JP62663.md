## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Finding characters on a tray typewriter for Japanese script is slow, the paper cylinder needs a wide travel, the types must be turned over by hand, and horizontal printing (for European text) is poor.

**Solution:** A ring-shaped rotating type tray has a post at its centre carrying a swinging rail, and a carriage with paper cylinder and a two-tube printing arm slides on it, pushing up an inked type, flipping it 180 degrees automatically and printing it, in either columns or rows.

**How it works:** The ring tray holds loose types radially, index face up and printing face down, and turns on balls; the left hand turns the tray while the right hand swings the carrier about the central post and slides it along the rail to bring a ladle-shaped type holder over the wanted type. Pressing the key first raises a pusher whose bristle/cloth tip pushes the type up into the holder and inks it from below, then a presser rod grips the type. Further travel turns the outer tube of the telescoping arm, so the arm rises while a gear on the tube rolls on a rubber strip and turns the holder over 180 degrees, striking the type face on the paper cylinder. On release everything returns by weight; the key movement also drives a spring post on the cylinder ratchet for character spacing in vertical columns, while a handle and rack move the cylinder one line at a time. For horizontal rows, the spring post is laid down and a saw-tooth wheel on the handle shaft is engaged by a hook on the key to space along the row.
