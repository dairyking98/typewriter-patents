## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** The ribbon shield used on the Hammond typewriter, a bow with a foil plate that keeps the inked ribbon off the paper, has a horizontal bar and upright limbs that hide the last lines written.

**Solution:** Bend the limbs of the shield bow at an obtuse angle so the horizontal bar lies back over the type-wheel and out of the operator's line of sight.

**How it works:** In the Hammond, the type-wheel segment is opposed by a hammer, and the inked ribbon runs between spools in front of the type. A thin metal foil plate with a small rectangular hole sits between ribbon and paper, held stiff between the two limbs of a wire bow. The limbs drop by their own weight into tubular sockets on the type-wheel guard flange. The hammer blow passes through the hole in the foil, pushing the ribbon and type against the paper. In the improvement the limbs are bent so the bow's horizontal part clears the paper and sits above the type-wheel, which it just misses at its highest lift; the operator can then read the latest line, and pressing the paper back from the shield is easier.
