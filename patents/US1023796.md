## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Operators cannot easily see or adjust exactly where the next character will print on the platen, and typebars are slow to return from the printing position.

**Solution:** A spring-wire pointer mounted close to the printing point indicates the print position, and the same wire doubles as a typebar repulser that helps the bars return.

**How it works:** A bent spring wire is screwed to the card-guide support so that its end edge sits directly over the printing point, with only the paper between it and the platen. The pointer lies in the same vertical line as the printing point, so any spot on the work sheet can be lined up with it. The same wire is placed so that typebars strike it when actuated, and its spring action pushes them back toward rest. Shown on a No. 10 Remington with shifting platen.

**Features:** [PAP:line-indicator](../features/pap-line-indicator.md), [SHIFT:platen](../features/shift-platen.md), [ELEM:typebar](../features/elem-typebar.md), [PAP:platen-roller](../features/pap-platen-roller.md), [FRM:writing-visible](../features/frm-writing-visible.md)

**Parts:** spring wire pointer; card guide; type bar; type block; platen; platen frame; shift frame; shift key

**Improves on:**

- Remington No. 10 indicating devices: pointer is simple and sits right at the printing point for close adjustment
