## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** Electric typewriters returned parts by springs (wearing, hard to operate) or separate magnets needing their own circuits.

**Solution:** Differentially wound electromagnets in the circuit of the working magnets return the parts, going dead at the instant the working magnet pulls, so no resistance.

**How it works:** Pressing a type key closes a contact; a rack is drawn by a magnet and turns a gear on a type-wheel shaft to the character position, then a delayed magnet hits the wheel to the platen. A shift key moves the shaft axially so a second or third type row lies over the platen. On release, differential magnets return the rack, shaft and wheel, and another advances the platen one tooth through a pawl and carriage rack against a spring.
