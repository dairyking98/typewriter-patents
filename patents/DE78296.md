## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Index typewriters with an elastic type plate needed simpler guiding of the plate and setting pin, and a way to avoid skipped letters or overprinting.

**Solution:** One key press advances the carriage by a letter width and, through a screw-and-pawl linkage, lowers the pressure lever only at the end of that advance, so printing and spacing are one action.

**How it works:** A carriage slides on a rear axis and carries an index plate with holes; the adjusting pin is inserted in the hole for the desired character. A rigid lever joins the pin to a ball running in a slot guide (held in by a guide bar) and carries the elastic type plate, so the chosen letter lies over the opening in the ink pad beneath the hammer of the pressure lever. Pressing either button on the saw-toothed rack pushes a pawl and moves the carriage one tooth. The pawl turns the screw in the pressure lever's rear reinforcement, which bears on a fixed adjusting screw, so the hammer end is forced down onto the type plate at the end of the travel. A spring returns the pawl and rack on release.
