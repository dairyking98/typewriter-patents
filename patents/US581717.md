## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A rapid typewriter with many convenient features, improving the inventor's earlier US364343 in which the two sets of type-carrier levers moved together as rocking bars.

**Solution:** One type carrier is moved in two planes (rock and slide) by two crossing sets of independent key levers, each lever fixing its own stop position, and a spring-driven hammer is released by a trigger once the carrier has stopped at the chosen type.

**How it works:** Each of the finger keys has wings that depress a transverse lever, which rocks the type carrier on its shaft to bring up a row, and a longitudinal lever, which slides the carrier along the shaft to a column. Because the keys sit at different distances from each lever's fulcrum, a uniform key stroke gives each key its own travel. Stopping fingers on the levers drop into the path of stops on two slides tied to the carrier and halt it at the exact position, and the stopping slide also pushes a trigger slide that releases the cocked hammer lever. A transverse lever draws the hammer back and cocks it on a trigger; on release the impression spring drives the hammer into the selected spring-retracted type, pressing it through the ribbon and paper on the cylinder. The same hammer frame moves an escapement lever so the mainspring-driven carriage steps one space; word spacing uses a transverse lever alone with the carrier at rest so the hammer swings idly.
