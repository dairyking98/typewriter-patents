## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** Equal letter spacing gives uneven-looking gaps because letters differ in width.

**Solution:** Fingers set by the keys limit how far the pivoted escapement dog can travel, so narrow, medium and wide letters let one, two, three or four rack teeth pass.

**How it works:** A fixed dog and a pivoted dog on an elbow lever, swung by a cross-bar lowered by any key, alternately hold the carriage rack, as in the Remington. A projection on the pivoted dog meets adjustable fingers on a rod, which limit how far the dog can swing, and so how many teeth the rack advances after the key is released. Keys of broad letters move the fingers aside, so more teeth pass. Capitals pass one more tooth than their small letters, and double or treble letters can pass the number closest to their width.

**Features:** [ESC:variable-spacing](../features/esc-variable-spacing.md), [ESC:pawl-rack](../features/esc-pawl-rack.md), [ESC:print-and-space](../features/esc-print-and-space.md), [ELEM:typebar](../features/elem-typebar.md), [SEL:keyboard-full](../features/sel-keyboard-full.md)

**Parts:** carriage rack; fixed dog; pivoted dog; elbow lever; cross-bar; gauging finger; finger rod; spring; key

**Numbers:** 1 to 4 rack teeth per letter; about 4 width classes

**Improves on:**

- Remington machines: adds width-dependent spacing to the fixed and pivoted dog escapement
