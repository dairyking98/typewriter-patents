## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Hammer-per-type machines are limited in number of hammers, inking of the type is poor, and fixed letter spacing gives wide letters like m too little room and narrow letters too much.

**Solution:** Arrange the hammer pivots in two nearly semicircular groups that all print at one central spot, give each type its own ink roller carried on the key slide, and space the carriage in four grades depending on the letter.

**How it works:** Each finger key moves a slide; during the first part of the stroke the hammer stays still while the slide carries an inking roller across the type face, then the hammer swings and strikes the paper at the common print spot. The base holds four pivoted spacing frames; a projection on each key moves one of them according to the letter width. The paper carriage is drawn by a spring but held by a pinion with a retaining pawl on a ratchet. A pawl carrier with limited freedom lets the carriage advance a set distance then stops it, and a driving pawl on a lever moved by the spacing frames makes the advance prompt. The driving and retaining pawls are linked so one engages as the other disengages.

**Features:** [ELEM:typebar](../features/elem-typebar.md), [SEL:keyboard-full](../features/sel-keyboard-full.md), [INK:roller](../features/ink-roller.md), [ESC:variable-spacing](../features/esc-variable-spacing.md), [ESC:pawl-rack](../features/esc-pawl-rack.md), [ESC:spring-drum](../features/esc-spring-drum.md), [ESC:print-and-space](../features/esc-print-and-space.md), [PAP:platen-roller](../features/pap-platen-roller.md)

**Parts:** finger key; slide; type hammer; inking roller; bell crank; spacing frame; paper carriage; pinion; rack; retaining pawl; driving pawl; ratchet wheel; comb

**Numbers:** four grades of letter spacing; ten sheets of drawings

**Improves on:**

- hammer typewriters with a limited set of hammers: two semicircular groups of pivots allow more hammers all printing at one spot
