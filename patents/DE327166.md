## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** On Mignon-type pointer typewriters every type of the cylinder has its own field on the index plate, so the plate is large and the pointer must travel far.

**Solution:** Each plate field serves two types (for example capital and lower-case letter), and a thumb button on the pointer handle shortens or lengthens the setting slide so the cylinder turns to the second type of the field; electromagnets can optionally do the printing and spacing strikes.

**How it works:** A pointer hangs in a ball joint above the type plate, and a setting slide links it by a ball joint to a gear that turns the type cylinder. The slide is in two parts with a spring, and a lever on the pointer, worked by a push button at the handle, shortens it by a fixed amount, which shifts the cylinder to the neighbouring type. Without the button the small letters, punctuation and fraction signs print, and with it the capitals, figures and signs. Striking the write key drives the cylinder against the paper and advances the carriage one tooth pitch, and a space key advances without printing. An empty field on the cylinder or a space field on the plate can also make a space. Optionally contacts in each plate field close a circuit through the pointer tip so an electromagnet works the write key, and a space contact works the space key, with hand operation still possible. A solenoid version of the shortening button also prevents striking until the cylinder is correctly set.
