## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** In the inventor's earlier type-wheel machine (US588608) the type-wheel stood in front of the printing line and hid the writing, the mechanism that swung and lifted it was complicated, and the hammer was in the way.

**Solution:** The oscillating type-wheel rests at one side of the print line, and an inclined operating blade driven from two key groups swings and lifts it against a one-sided stepped gage, with a side-swinging hammer.

**How it works:** The type-wheel is a frame of parallel type-bars set on an arc, each bar bearing two types one above the other; the frame slides and swings on a rod. Pressing a key lifts the wheel to the right row (half the keys) and swings a striker bar attached to the wheel until it hits a shoulder on a stepped gage, which fixes which bar is at the print point. An inclined blade, driven from one key section directly and from the other through a lever giving a shorter or longer movement, produces the swing. A hammer on a vertical shaft then swings horizontally onto the inner side of the bar, printing through a ribbon onto the paper on the platen roller. The same key lever that throws the hammer in also releases a pawl on the ratchet of the spring-drawn carriage, so the carriage steps the instant the hammer has acted.
