## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** In a loose-type Japanese-script typewriter the push-up rod that lifts a type into the gripper tube was lowered on the return stroke, which slowed printing and caused mistakes.

**Solution:** Shape the cam so its lifting face passes beyond the roller at the end of the forward stroke, dropping the push-up rod at once, and let the roller ride back under the cam face on the return.

**How it works:** A shaft turns through a set angle on each key stroke and returns, turning a cam through a link. A spring-held arm with a roller bears on the cam and carries a push-up rod sliding in a fixed guide tube. At the start of the stroke the cam's lifting face raises the roller, arm and rod, pushing a type up into the gripper tube of the printing arm. The roller then rides a circular part of the cam, so the rod stays up while the gripper takes the type. At the end of the stroke the lifting face passes the roller, which drops, lowering the rod while the type is already gripped. On return the roller is pushed aside by the underside of the cam flange against the spring and slips back, so the rod stays down.

## Description (English translation of the Japanese text, opening)

> Specification. Type push-up device in a typewriter. Nature and object of the invention. Beside a cam that turns back and forth with a shaft which, at every printing stroke, turns through a set angle and returns to its original position when the stroke ends, is placed an arm carrying a roller that a spring always holds raised to its limit; the roller works with the cam. The tip of the arm is joined to a push-up rod that can rise and fall along a fixed guide tube. At the end of each printing stroke of the handle the cam face turns far enough to pass the roller, so that the roller and the arm drop and the push-up rod comes down; on the return of the printing mechanism the roller goes back under the cam face. The object: in printing, after the type has been pushed up, the push-up rod comes down first, at the same moment as the type is gripped by the gripper tube or the like on the printing arm, ready for the type to drop back; this makes the printing action brisk.
