## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Book typewriters are hard to use on different surfaces; a machine is wanted that works anywhere, level or inclined, with little exertion.

**Solution:** A casing holding type levers and ribbon mechanism sits beside the key casing and is the only part moving over the writing surface, fed along the line by the key strokes and adjustable in height.

**How it works:** The main casing sits on a wheeled carriage with links that raise it to the surface height, adjusted by a hand-wheel worm and worm-wheel quadrant; the front is weighted. Keys connect to type levers by cords over sheaves on fixed and movable shafts joined by tensioning links. The type carrier alone moves over the page, advancing along the line by letter-spacing from key depression. A lever with toothed wheel and spring pawl gives line spacing by moving the carriage on its wheels. Backspace and automatic return of the type carrier at line end are provided. A modified version is shown in Figs. 12-17.
