## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** On pointer-and-index-plate typewriters (the Hall), the characters lie at the bottom of deep dark holes in a dark plate and are hard to read in shadow or dim light.

**Solution:** Colour or line the walls of the index-plate holes with a light-reflecting or luminous material, or insert white tubes, so the character on the card below shows clearly.

**How it works:** A handle carries a pointer that is dropped into any of the holes in a perforated index-plate, one hole per character. A card bearing the printed characters sits under the plate, aligned with the holes. The hole walls are painted white, gilded, enameled or coated with luminous paint, or fitted with white paper tubes, and rows can be colour-coded (numerals, punctuation, capitals, small letters). The letter can also be printed on the tube side so one need not peer into the hole. The card slides into a recess on the underside of the plate so it can be swapped for another language without loosening the platen screws that keep the plate parallel to the type form.
