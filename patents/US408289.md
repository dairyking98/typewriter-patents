## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** In Crandall machines the ribbon ran close to the platen and smeared the paper, wrapped the type cylinder poorly, and hid the last printed word unless a special key moved a ribbon carrier.

**Solution:** The ribbon is held taut between two spring-loaded oscillating take-ups on arms that straddle the type cylinder, away from the platen.

**How it works:** A spreader and holder in front of the spools has two forward arms between which the type cylinder lies. Each arm carries a stud with an oscillating take-up hub and a pin through a slot, pulled by a spring to one end of the slot. The ribbon runs from one spool round the inside of one hub, round its pin, across in front of the type cylinder to the opposite pin and hub, and on to the second spool. When a key throws the type cylinder forward it carries the ribbon with it and the take-ups turn to give slack without pulling ribbon off the spools. On return the springs draw the ribbon back taut and clear of the paper. Spring arms at the arm ends hold the paper on the impression rollers beside the printing point.
