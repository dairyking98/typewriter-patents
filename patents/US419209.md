## Summary

**Kind:** type-element machine · **Subject (patent classes):** Typewriters or selective printing mechanisms characterised by the mounting, arrangement or disposition of the types or dies with types or dies mounted on carriers rotatable for selection the plane of the type or die face being parallel to the axis of rotation, e.g. with type on the periphery of cylindrical carriers

**In the patent’s own words:** The invention relates to a type-writer having the finger-keys arranged in parallel rows and designed so as to produce a strong, simple, and reliable machine at less cost than some that are at present in the market.

**How it works (reading):** A spring-driven type wheel spins whenever a key is pressed and is stopped by the key's plate catching one pin on a disk on the wheel shaft, so the type is registered at the print point, while the paper roller rises and presses the paper against the wheel.

## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** A strong, simple and reliable keyboard typewriter at lower cost than the machines then on the market, with finger-keys in parallel rows.

**Solution:** A spring-driven type wheel spins whenever a key is pressed and is stopped by the key's plate catching one pin on a disk on the wheel shaft, so the type is registered at the print point, while the paper roller rises and presses the paper against the wheel.

**How it works:** A 75-character wheel is on a shaft driven by a clock-spring barrel and normally held by a brake block on a toothed brake wheel. Pressing any key presses its row's key lever, which pulls the brake off, and the wheel spins; keys in a row have shoulders so each descends a different distance, and a stop plate on the row's lever then reaches only one pin on that row's disk. Each row has its own disk on the shaft with pins staggered by one character pitch (1/75 of the circumference) from the neighbouring disk, so the wheel stops with the right character at the print point. A sliding key frame moved by a handle shifts the stop plates to other disks for small characters and figures. The same lever first moves the paper roller carriage one space by a rack and pawl and then lifts the hinged roller bed against the wheel, with a spring-pivoted arm limiting the pressure. The wheel is inked by a roller that touches the types as it spins; a push brake saves spring power when repeating a character; and a bell warns near the line end.
