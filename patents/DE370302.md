## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** On type wheel typewriters that shift case by raising and lowering the wheel, the wheel is not yet at the right height when the wheel swings to the paper at higher typing speed, so types print off the line or unevenly.

**Solution:** Stacked stop rollers, one per type row, that the beveled disc on the wheel shaft slides under during the swing toward the platen, so the final height is fixed by the stop and not by how far the shift key got.

**How it works:** The type wheel shaft slides axially in two ring bearings and rests on a beveled disc at its lower end, which rides on two rollers carried by a yoke lever. Pressing the single or double shift key lifts the yoke lever, raising the shaft by one or two row pitches. When an electromagnet swings the shaft toward the paper roller, the disc passes between or under one of three stacked stop rollers, whose spacing equals the type row spacing. The wedge-shaped bevel of the disc rides up on the stop roller and lifts or lowers the shaft to the exact row height even if the shift key was not fully depressed. The block carrying the three stop rollers is adjustable in height on pointed screws, so one adjustment shifts all three row heights together.
