## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** In a typewriter for oriental languages (per main patent 562 505) letters typed in the upper platen position always trigger a double carriage step, but some of those letters need only a single step.

**Solution:** A second shift rail with cutouts and projections, linked to selected key levers, swings an auxiliary stop into the path of the movable escapement pawl so that those letters give only a single step even in the upper platen position.

**How it works:** The carriage shaft carries a ratchet wheel worked by a fixed pawl and a movable pawl on a swingable pawl carrier. A stop on the carrier normally lets the movable pawl travel far enough for a double step. A forked lever with a bent end can be swung into the pawl's path to limit it to a single step. The lever is moved by an arm, an adjustable link and an oscillating shaft carrying a shift rail with cutouts and projections, so only certain key levers move the rail. The space bar acts on the rail through side lugs, so it gives a single step in the lower roller position and a double step in the upper.
