## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** On double-type Remington machines the platen shift key must be held by one hand, so capitals cost typing speed.

**Solution:** A wire attachment under the keyboard lets the operator's knee hold the platen-shift key down, freeing both hands.

**How it works:** A heavy wire is doubled into a stem with arms at right angles, and a small flat steel spring is soldered to it. A threaded connecting rod with a nut passes through the keyboard and table and loops over the platen shift key or bar. Staples under the table receive the pivot arm. Pressing the knee outward on the stem swings it up, pulls the rod and holds the platen shifted for capitals until the knee is released and the spring returns it.

**Features:** [USE:accessory](../features/use-accessory.md), [SHIFT:platen](../features/shift-platen.md), [SHIFT:lock](../features/shift-lock.md)

**Parts:** wire stem; arm; steel spring; connecting rod; nut; staple; shift key

**Improves on:**

- Remington Standard No. 2: shift key no longer needs to be held by one hand
