## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Existing Japanese typewriters are large, make characters hard to find and need practice; the inventor wants a smaller machine holding more types that anyone can use quickly and correctly.

**Solution:** Types carry three tiers of characters on one side and are lifted to one of three stop heights, while a character board of rotating three-sided prisms shows the matching tier face and sets the stop height at the same time.

**How it works:** Many types hang in side-by-side open-ended chambers on the base; a pointer with a sighting frame, carried on a moving frame that travels forward, back, left and right, is moved over the wanted character on the character board. A handle press drives a lever that pushes a type-pushing rod up, lifting the chosen type into a sliding type frame along a lifting plate until its lug meets a stop. Moving a handle-rack turns the prisms (gear and rack) to show face A, B or C for the upper, middle or lower tier, and via links it withdraws one or two of three stops on the lifting plate so the right tier stops at the print height. A cam on the drive lever then strikes a stepped lever (three steps to time the blow to the rise) that turns a striking wheel, whose projection throws the striking lever against the back of the type frame to print on the paper cylinder. The rebound turns the striking wheel backward and returns the mechanisms.

## Description (English translation of the Japanese text, opening)

> Specification. Japanese-script typewriter. Nature and object of the invention. This invention relates to a Japanese-script typewriter that has its types and a character board separately, and prints by pointing to a character on the face of the board. Types with characters engraved in three tiers (upper, middle, lower) on a side face are raised and lowered vertically. Several three-sided prisms set side by side form the character board; each prism can be turned freely to show any of its faces, each face being related to one tier of characters on the types, so that with the board showing a given face, printing is done with that tier of the types. The object is to make characters easy to find, to make the machine smaller while increasing the number of types it holds, and to let even an untrained person print quickly and correctly, so that it serves not only in offices but also in ordinary homes.
