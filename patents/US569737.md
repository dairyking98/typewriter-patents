## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typewriters need ribbons, and keeping the characters aligned is hard.

**Solution:** Typebars hang on a slotted fulcrum so that at rest the type presses against an inking pad, then lift and swing to the paper along a fixed path.

**How it works:** Each typebar pivots in a bracket with a cam slot at its upper end on a fixed fulcrum pin. A connecting rod from the type-lever lifts the bar, and the cam slot withdraws the type from the disk-shaped inking pad. As the rod rises further the bar turns about the rod's upper end to a horizontal position and strikes the platen. The rod's upper end slides in a guide slot, so each type strikes at the same distance from the pivot, preserving alignment. On return a spring or gravity drives the type against the pad's periphery to ink it.
