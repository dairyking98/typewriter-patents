## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Column tabulators needed a quick way to bring the carriage to any chosen column without a separate, differently-set stop for each key.

**Solution:** Identical column stops on the carriage are counted as they pass a rotary toothed counter-stop, so the first key arrests the first stop, the second key the second, and so on.

**How it works:** Identical, adjustable column stops clip into letter-space notches on a stop bar hung from the carriage, which is driven by a spring drum and checked by an escapement. Pressing a tabulator key lever raises a universal bar that rocks a shaft, lifting a vertical shaft carrying a toothed rotary counter-stop into the path of the column stops and, with lost motion, disengaging a clutch collar to release the carriage from the escapement. Each passing column stop strikes a tooth and turns the rotary member one step, held by a spring detent, while a rack-and-pinion drives a sliding bar leftward until its shoulder meets the raised end of the depressed key lever. Stopping there leaves the next tooth in the path of the stop for the chosen column. Releasing the key re-engages the clutch and drops the counter-stop clear; columns beyond the fourth are reached by successive key presses. Letter spacing is by a universal bar struck by typebars driving a single-tooth escapement.
