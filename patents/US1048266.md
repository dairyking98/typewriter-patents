## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Front-stroke typebar actions need an easy touch yet must still deliver a powerful printing blow at speed.

**Solution:** Connect key lever, sub-lever and typebar through links with lost-motion pin-and-slot joints so leverage is light at the start of the stroke and strong at the end.

**How it works:** A key lever drives a sub-lever through an L-shaped link that first bears at a point near the key pivot, then takes up a pin-and-slot lost motion farther out for greater leverage at the end of the stroke. The sub-lever's vertical arm moves a second link that first lifts the typebar by pushing on a shoulder, then transfers to a pin in a slot as the bar rises, shifting the fulcrum. A spring ties the sub-lever to the key lever to hold the unit at rest. A universal bar under the key levers works an escapement rocker and ribbon vibrator, and rollers align the bars at the printing point.

**Features:** [Typebars](../features/elem-typebar.md), [Full keyboard, a key per character](../features/sel-keyboard-full.md), [Element struck to the platen](../features/imp-element-to-platen.md), [Ribbon lift](../features/ink-ribbon-lift.md), [Pawl-and-rack escapement](../features/esc-pawl-rack.md), [Roller platen](../features/pap-platen-roller.md), [Visible writing](../features/frm-writing-visible.md)

**Parts:** key lever; sub-lever; L-shaped link; typebar link; typebar; typebar segment; return spring; universal bar; escapement rocker; ribbon vibrating guide; toothed rack; alining rollers
