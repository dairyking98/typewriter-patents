## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Typewriters of the time needed a positive, simple actuating movement; the inventor wants a simpler structure than typebar machines.

**Solution:** Pressing any of a set of key-spindles lets a spring-driven cord rapidly spin a flexible rubber type-disk until a stop arm meets the descending plate, so the disk is at the right character just as a hammer strikes it, and the same stroke steps the machine along a ratchet bar.

**How it works:** A row of spring-loaded key-spindles passes through the top plate, each marked with a character. A spring-supported plate under the keys is pushed down by the pressed key, which slackens a cord; a contraction spring then rotates three belted vertical spindles, one of which carries an elastic type-disk with characters on its underside. The plate keeps descending until the arm on the spindle nearest the depressed key engages it, which stops the rotation with the wanted character under the hammer block hanging from the plate. The hammer then strikes and prints the character from above. A second rod with a bevelled block on the plate slides a pawl one tooth along a fixed ratchet bar, spacing the machine, and the rising plate rewinds the cord and re-stretches the spring. A slotted lost-motion coupling on the disk spindle lets one key print either a capital or small letter.
