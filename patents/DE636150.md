## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** On single-shift typebar machines with 42-46 bars the bars need a long travel, so the guide combs either force a long machine (keyboard far from the print point) or sit too close together and guide the bar poorly.

**Solution:** Guide each typebar on its own fixed, polished guard rail with fork-like fingers on the bar, so the front guide can be moved right up to about 40 mm from the print point.

**How it works:** Each typebar carries two fork-like fingers that straddle a fixed upright guard rail seated in slots of the front and rear combs, so the bar cannot slip off. The type floats above the rail. A drive lever from the key engages a slot in the bar and pushes it along the rail to the print point, while a comb slot prevents tilting. Because the guide is no longer held behind the rest position of the type, the front guide can be brought up to the print point, keeping the overall length short and the keyboard closer to the print point.

**Features:** [ELEM:typebar](../features/elem-typebar.md), [SEL:keyboard-full](../features/sel-keyboard-full.md), [IMP:element-to-platen](../features/imp-element-to-platen.md), [FRM:writing-visible](../features/frm-writing-visible.md)

**Parts:** typebar; guard rail; fork finger; front comb; rear comb; tilt-prevention comb; drive lever

**Numbers:** 42 to 46 typebars; 28 to 30 typebars on double-shift machines; guide up to 40 mm from print point; total length 22/10 vs 26/10 in prior design

**Improves on:**

- two-comb typebar guides with movable guard rails: fixed guard rails and fork fingers let the front guide approach the print point and shorten the machine
