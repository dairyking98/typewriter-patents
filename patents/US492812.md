## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Typewriters get out of order easily and are too complicated to sell cheaply.

**Solution:** A cheap, simple keyboard machine with one three-row type-wheel turned by toothed key segments of differing tooth counts acting on a pinion, then tilted up against a paper roller by the last part of the key stroke.

**How it works:** Each of 28 keys is pivoted to a tilting lever whose rear end is a toothed segment; the number of teeth differs by key, so pressing a key turns a long pinion by a set amount, which turns the type-wheel through gears and a miter gear. Stopping against a bar fixes the wheel angle; continued key travel lifts that bar and the whole tilting frame, throwing the wheel up against the paper on a rubber roller. The frame's drop pushes a spacing lever that lifts and re-seats a dog on a rack to step the paper roller sideways. A clock-type spring returns the wheel. Two extra buttons of different stop heights swing the roller frame to bring the capital row or the figure row over the wheel. Each key carries a letter and a figure that sit on the same axial line of the wheel.
