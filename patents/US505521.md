## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Uniform carriage feed gives narrow letters such as i and l the same space as average letters and crowds wide letters such as M and W.

**Solution:** A feed rack with teeth twice as fine and a three-step variable stop tied to the keys, so the escapement slips one, two or three teeth by letter width.

**How it works:** The ordinary rack is replaced by one whose two-tooth spacing equals normal letter width. The fixed stop that limits the free pawl's travel is replaced by a stop with three steps or faces. It is connected to the keys, or those that need it, so it presents one face to the pawl according to whether the character takes one space (i, j, l, f, r, t), two (average) or three (M, m, W, w). On a Remington the dog in the rack escapes forward until it strikes the selected step, and little else in the feed is altered.

**Features:** [ESC:variable-spacing](../features/esc-variable-spacing.md), [ESC:pawl-rack](../features/esc-pawl-rack.md), [USE:accessory](../features/use-accessory.md), [ELEM:typebar](../features/elem-typebar.md)

**Parts:** finer feed rack; feed dog; free pawl; three-step stop; key connection; carriage

**Numbers:** 1, 2 or 3 spaces per character; rack teeth about twice as fine as usual

**Improves on:**

- uniform-feed typewriters (Remington, Yost, Caligraph, National, Premier): adds three spacing widths instead of one
- exactly proportional spacing machines: only a coarse three-step correction, simpler and practical
