## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** Returning the carriage by hand takes time and effort.

**Solution:** An electric motor with friction clutch and rack bar returns the carriage on a button press.

**How it works:** A motor behind an Oliver machine has a loose pinion with friction disks and inclined clutch teeth that lock the pinion to the armature shaft when it turns. The pinion meshes with a rack bar loosely connected to the carriage, with a forked end gripping the platen knob's spring-cushioned shank. Pressing a button closes the battery circuit and the motor drives the carriage back to the margin stop. The spring then expands and throws the rack back about a quarter inch, reversing the armature and separating the clutch disks so the carriage is free to step normally.
