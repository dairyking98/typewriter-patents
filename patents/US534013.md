## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Wheel typewriters need a better type movement and key-transmission construction.

**Solution:** Each key carries a cam that slides a frame by a character-specific distance, in either direction from a central position, and the frame's movement turns the type wheel shaft.

**How it works:** Pivoted lever keys, spring-returned and guided in a slotted plate, each carry a cam plate with an inclined portion and a straight slot. The incline engages a cross bar on a sliding frame and moves it a distance set by the character; the straight slot then holds the frame there for the rest of the key stroke. Cams move the frame both ways from a centre to shorten the travel. A connection from the frame turns the vertical shaft carrying a type wheel with two rows (capitals and small letters), splined so it can slide for case shift with the usual shift key. The OCR cuts off before the frame-to-shaft connection and impression are described.

**Features:** [ELEM:wheel-rim](../features/elem-wheel-rim.md), [SEL:keyboard-full](../features/sel-keyboard-full.md), [SEL:differential-drive](../features/sel-differential-drive.md), [SHIFT:axial](../features/shift-axial.md), [IMP:print-lever](../features/imp-print-lever.md), [INK:ribbon](../features/ink-ribbon.md)

**Parts:** side plate; sliding frame; cross bar; key lever; cam plate; guide plate; type wheel; vertical shaft; shift key; ribbon mechanism

**Numbers:** two rows of characters on the wheel
