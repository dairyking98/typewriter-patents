## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** One typewriter should print both left-to-right alphabets such as English and right-to-left ones such as Hebrew.

**Solution:** A hand-indexed rotatable multi-alphabet type bar in a swinging cradle prints onto a platen carriage whose single spacing lever, through a shiftable double pawl, can advance the carriage in either direction.

**How it works:** The type bar is a long bar carrying three alphabets (English, Hebrew, upper-case English) as flats; it turns in lugs on a plate that slides endwise in a swinging cradle. A handle on the plate carries an index pointer; the operator slides the bar until the pointer sits in the notch of a fixed plate (matching a character on an index bar) that aligns the wanted type, and turns the bar to select the alphabet, with springs detenting the flats. Swinging the same handle down drops the cradle so the aligned type prints through an opening in the cradle bottom onto the paper on the platen, and the pointer enters the notch to fix the position exactly. An arm on the cradle works the spacing lever, whose arm carries a double pawl engaging one of two oppositely beveled rack bars on the carriage; shifting the double pawl with a small lever makes the same stroke advance the carriage in the opposite direction. A bell hammer, a cutting blade on the platen and a line-space lever are also included.
