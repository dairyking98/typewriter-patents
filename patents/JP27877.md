## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A way to print a very large character set (kanji) with one simple mechanism, using ordinary cheap letterpress types, and avoiding the indistinct, ugly ribbon-struck impression of ordinary typewriters.

**Solution:** A flat tray of loose types lies under a movable printing unit; a single hand lever slid over the tray lifts the type beneath its tip, grips it, carries it to a large paper cylinder, inks it and prints it, while the cylinder advances automatically.

**How it works:** Loose letterpress types stand in cells of side-by-side type racks in a flat magazine, open at the bottom and held by bent lips. The whole machine slides forward, back, left and right over the base on crossed rails, so the operator holds the front of the pressing lever and slides it until the printing-lever tip is over the wanted type. Pressing the lever raises a roller that works as a cam: a pyramid-tipped arm rises into the cell and pushes the type up into the type-gripping hole of the printing lever. Further travel strikes an impulse lever, which through a link turns a working lever whose presser slides into a nick of the type, then swings the printing lever and presses the type on the paper cylinder. A soft ink-soaked roller on a vertical lever inks the type face as it rises and swings clear on a cam bump. The impulse lever also swings a bent lever that moves a feed bar and pawl, which turn a gear and advance the cylinder one character. Sideways spacing is done by sliding the cylinder frame on its shaft, held by a rack and a point.
