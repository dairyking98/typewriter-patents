## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** On a Postal-type wheel machine the type wheel is rotated by a stop-arm that strikes a stop pin; the arm rebounds and the wheel may dip before it has finished turning, blurring the print at speed.

**Solution:** A printing escapement that holds the wheel from dipping until the stop-arm has hit its stop, plus a cam-driven pawl and ratchet on the index gear that grabs the arm to kill rebound.

**How it works:** Each key lifts a stop pin and, through levers, rotates the type wheel via a rock-shaft arm, segment and gear while an index gear with an arm turns until it hits the raised pin. A hook-and-bail printing escapement, with some play, lets the rocking wheel frame dip slightly while keeping it from striking until the stop-arm hits the pin and a T-tail lever releases the lock via links. Continued key travel then snaps the wheel down onto the platen. The first dip of the frame moves a cam that presses a spring-toothed pawl onto a ratchet on the index gear, so the arm cannot bounce back off the stop. The wheel shaft slides axially for case shift and is held by an adjustable hardened lock with a roller during the stroke, kept from turning by a slotted guide plate. A ribbon spool frame is stepped by the dipping frame and can swing aside.
