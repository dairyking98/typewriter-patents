## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Ordinary typing cannot produce a stencil, so manifold copies required a separate stencil-cutting process.

**Solution:** Replace the rubber platen with a hard roller (or hanging bar) knurled with fine diagonal ribs so type impact cuts minute slits in the paper, making it a stencil for a stencil press.

**How it works:** A steel roller with spiral ribs about thirty degrees to its axis and roughly one-hundredth of an inch apart substitutes for the rubber platen. When a type strikes, the paper is forced against the ribs and a series of tiny diagonal slits is cut within each letter, the angle chosen so slits never run parallel to the long strokes. A strip of soft rubber is interposed between type and paper to protect the types and the ribs. A plain backing can be placed between paper and roller for ordinary typing; a gravity-hung ribbed bar section can serve instead of a full roller.

**Parts:** ribbed roller; ribbed bar; soft rubber strip; steel shell; backing sheet; arm

**Numbers:** ribs about 30 degrees to roller axis; ribs 0.01 in. apart

**Improves on:**
