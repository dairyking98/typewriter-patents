## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A keyboard type-wheel machine needs a simple mechanism to first turn the wheel to the chosen character and then strike it against the platen, all from one key stroke.

**Solution:** Three frames on coincident axes, a type-wheel frame and two gear-segment frames meshing on opposite sides of the wheel's pinion, are driven in sequence by one key lever: opposite motion selects, then locks, then common motion prints.

**How it works:** Each key lever has two legs of different, key-specific lengths that strike two fluted cross-bars on the two segment frames. As the key goes down, one segment turns the pinion and the other moves oppositely until its leg also meets its bar, so the wheel has turned to the character set by the difference in leg lengths. With both segments now arrested, the pinion cannot turn, so the segments, pinion and type-wheel frame lock as one body. Further key travel swings the whole assembly rearward on its horizontal pivot so the type strikes the platen. A spring returns the wheel frame; the segments stay where they are, so the last character remains at the print position until another key is pressed. The carriage is fed by a spiral-grooved shaft stepped by a ratchet wheel and universal-bar pawl after each stroke.
