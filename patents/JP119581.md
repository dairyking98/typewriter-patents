## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** In an electric typewriter the motor strikes with a fixed force, so characters with few strokes print too dark or tear the paper.

**Solution:** A slot-and-pin stroke changer in the drive link, worked by twisting the print handle, shortens the lift of the printing lever so it strikes more by its own momentum and more softly.

**How it works:** Pressing the print handle engages the motor, a disc lug turns a shaft, and through a crank arm, an adjusting plate, a cross lever and a lift rod the printing-lever link is pulled. The adjusting plate has a short and a long slot and a spring normally holds the cross lever's pin in the short slot. Twisting the handle slightly sideways pulls a chain of links and bell cranks that pushes the plate and moves the pin into the long slot. The extra play shortens the stroke of the cross lever and link, so the lever swings less under drive and more under momentum, striking with less force.

## Description (English translation of the Japanese text, opening)

> Specification. Striking-force regulator for an electric printing machine Summary of the nature and object of the invention. This invention relates to a striking-force regulator for a printing machine of the type in which, linked to the pressing down of the printing handle, the electric motor and the printing mechanism are connected and printing is done by electric power. Its feature is that an adjusting device that changes the stroke of one link in the drive between the motor and the printing mechanism is put into that drive, so that the distance through which the printing lever is lifted and swung by the drive can be made different. Its object is to change the range through which the printing lever is lifted according to how dense or sparse the strokes of the type's character are, so increasing or reducing the distance through which the lever must swing afterwards on its own momentum, and thereby to vary the force with which the printing lever strikes the paper cylinder and make the darkness of electric printing even.
