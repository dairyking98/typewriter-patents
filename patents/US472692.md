## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** A light keyboard typewheel machine needed a way to position the wheel quickly and accurately from few key motions, with carriage spacing, margins and column stops.

**Solution:** Pressing a key rotates the type-wheel through a pinion on segment racks until a graded stop ratchet halts it at the right type, then tilts the wheel to the platen, with the wheel sliding on a non-rotating spindle to choose among three type fields.

**How it works:** Keys in two groups rock one of two frames that swing gear segments, rolling a pinion that turns the wheel and a pair of ratchets. Each key has a second bearing face graded in distance, so it moves a third frame and a pawl lever when its type is in position; the pawl catches a tooth of a heart-shaped ratchet, a second pawl grips from the other side against rebound, and a fixed horn pawl steadies the wheel. The wheel then tilts forward, inked on the way by a spring-hung roller, and strikes the platen. Two side buttons lift the spindle one or two steps to bring the middle or lower type field into action, and a notched locking bar holds it there while tilted. A cam on the rock-shaft works a feeding pawl and checking pawl so the carriage steps one notch per print, with a spacer key, bell, variable margin stop and keyed column stops.
