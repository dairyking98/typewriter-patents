## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** A cheap, simple, light typewriter is wanted that avoids a separate typebar for every character.

**Solution:** Keys arranged in two rings each push a key-bar down a miter-shaped cam that turns a single elastic type disc to that character, lock it, and then drive a hammer onto the back of the disc.

**How it works:** Keys sit in two concentric rings, each on a pivoted key-bar whose inner end rests over a miter-shaped cam inside a guide barrel. Pressing a key slides its bar down the cam ledges, which turns the cam, its sleeve and the central rod carrying the type disc until the bar drops into the slot at the cam's lower end, so the chosen character sits under the hammer. Further travel pushes an arm on the rod into a lock slot on a collar, which stops rotation, and then forces the rod and disc down toward the ribbon while a collar on the rod presses the pivoted hammer, which strikes the back of the elastic disc over the letter. Hammer motion trips a two-plate escapement that lets a clock-spring-driven rack carriage advance one tooth; the spacer key depresses the rod just far enough to trip it without printing. Two case keys slide the whole key and type assembly up inclined guides to bring the second or third circle of type to the print point. The ribbon is moved by a ratchet driven from an inclined way on the carriage, with a hand-wheel reversing it.
