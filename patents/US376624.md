## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** The typewriter carriage with a rack carries extra weight and inertia, slowing return and travel.

**Solution:** Put the rack on the machine frame and swing it by the key bar between a fixed and a pivoted dog fixed on the carriage.

**How it works:** A cross-bar under the key levers is joined by a rod to a lever on a shaft under the carriage, with a hub carrying the toothed rack. On a keystroke the rack rocks from the pivoted dog to the fixed dog; on return, under spring action, the pivoted dog (pushed forward by a spring against a stop) enters the next tooth, and the carriage spring pulls the carriage one letter space. A lever with a button turns a shaft that disengages the dogs for carriage return, or the line-feed mechanism can do so.

**Features:**

- **Spacing and escapement:** [Pawl-and-rack escapement](../features/esc-pawl-rack.md), [Mainspring-drawn carriage](../features/esc-spring-drum.md), [Carriage release](../features/esc-carriage-release.md), [Prints and spaces in one stroke](../features/esc-print-and-space.md)

**Parts:** key lever; cross-bar; rack; fixed dog; pivoted dog; carriage spring; release lever; shaft; bell-crank lever

**Improves on:**

- Remington escapement: dogs on the carriage are fixed and the rack swings, so the carriage is lighter
