## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A simple, cheap, hand-held device is wanted that can print any characters on a sheet or book page (a type-writer rule).

**Solution:** A hand-held rule carries an endless type band that one plunger advances until the chosen character shows opposite an index, while a second plunger fires a striker onto the band and then steps the striker and index one space along the line.

**How it works:** The rule is set on the paper. Pushing plunger X turns the two rollers carrying the endless band through rack, pinion and bevel gears; the printed copy of the characters on the band's upper face shows through a slot, and a spring pawl holds the band at each character position while the letter lines up with the index. Pushing plunger Y turns a pinion and three toothed disks: the first teeth spin a paddle shaft whose vanes lift and release a spring striker that hits the inside of the band, pressing the type under it onto the paper. On the return stroke the next teeth turn the double-threaded screw shafts a fixed amount, which moves the striker and the index forward one space. A third plunger K, by shifting gears along their shafts, selects which disk spaces (different widths for capitals, small letters and italics), slides the rollers sideways to bring another style of type on the band over the slot, or returns the striker to line start. An ink roller inks the type as the band moves past.
