## Summary

**Kind:** typebar machine · **Subject (patent classes):** Typewriters or selective printing mechanisms characterised by the mounting, arrangement or disposition of the types or dies with types or dies carried on sliding bars or rods on side surfaces thereof, e.g. fixed thereto the types or dies being movable relative to the bars or rods

**In the patent’s own words:** This invention relates more particularly to the platen shift mechanism of visible or front strike typewriting machines in which the carriage and platen are adapted to be folded into overhanging relation to the keyboard or otherwise shifted or moved into compact relation to another part of the machine when not in use.

## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** On a foldable front-strike typewriter the carriage must be shifted vertically by levers on one side while both ends move evenly and stop positively, with the carriage support kept rigid and the folding feature retained.

**Solution:** Slides at both sides, tied by upper and lower cross rods into a rigid rectangular frame, are lifted by two levers on one side, with a sliding stop bar and stop screws of two lengths setting the two shift heights.

**How it works:** Two vertical slides run in rear corner posts and are joined by a lower rod and an upper rod to form a rigid frame that carries the pivoted carriage bars. The first lever on a pivoted cross rod lifts the lower rod directly through slots, giving the greatest shift, while a second lever linked by pin-and-slot lets it move independently. Two stop screws of different lengths on the lower rod pass through registered holes in a spring-held transverse stop bar and the corner brackets. Pressing the second lever uses a cam to slide the bar so its solid part blocks the holes, stopping the long screws sooner and giving the smaller shift. A rocking lock lever with projections holds either shift lever down or locks both, and an adjustable spring on the rock shaft offsets the carriage weight.
