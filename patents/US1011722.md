## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** A reverse escapement is fastest but gives blurred prints for typists with a short stroke and double imprints for typists who follow the key down; an ordinary escapement is slower.

**Solution:** An escapement that acts automatically as a reverse or an ordinary escapement depending on how far the key is depressed, and that uses carriage power to restore its parts.

**How it works:** In a Remington No. 10 style machine, a shallow key depression moves the carriage under reverse feed, where the carriage starts on the downstroke. A deeper depression makes the escapement work as an ordinary one with let-off on release. The spring-drum power that drives the carriage also moves the escapement parts back to normal. Details of the linkage are not in the opening text.

**Parts:** escapement mechanism; key; typebar; carriage; spring drum
