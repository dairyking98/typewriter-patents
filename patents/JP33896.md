## Summary

**Kind:** a part or mechanism (any kind of machine) · **Subject (patent classes):** Typewriters or selective printing mechanisms characterised by the mounting, arrangement or disposition of the types or dies; Western type typewriter

**In the English translation of the patent’s own words:** The object is to print such languages without complicated mechanism, and in particular to make a machine of ordinary construction fit to write them.

**How it works (reading):** Fit an Underwood typebar machine with sideways Korean types that do not trigger the escapement, plus separate space keys that step the carriage after each syllable group, with Roman letters on the shifted position.

## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Korean (and similar) syllables are built from consonants and vowels stacked in groups, which an ordinary typewriter cannot compose, and the sheet must read top to bottom.

**Solution:** Fit an Underwood typebar machine with sideways Korean types that do not trigger the escapement, plus separate space keys that step the carriage after each syllable group, with Roman letters on the shifted position.

**How it works:** Korean consonant and vowel types are set sideways on the typebars, so the impressions lie on their side and the sheet, fed in from the side, reads upright when turned. The type keys do not move the carriage; after a consonant and its vowels have been struck over one another, a space key steps the carriage one space, and a second key steps it two. Type pitch is about nine to the inch while the carriage steps one eighth inch, leaving small gaps between groups. Roman letters sit lengthwise at the ends of the same bars and are struck with the platen shifted; an interposer moved by the shift key reconnects the universal bar to the escapement dogs so English spaces automatically. The ribbon vibrator works in both shift positions from the universal bar.

## Description (English translation of the Japanese text, opening)

> Specification. Typewriter. Nature and object of the invention. To print Korean and other similar languages, this invention places each vowel and consonant letter sideways on its type, so that the printed characters appear lying sideways. Each consonant occupies almost the whole of a letter-feed step, that is, the whole of one subdivision of the writing line, and the consonant letters are all mounted to strike the same position, so that their impressions can be superimposed. A key moves the carriage one step at a time: after one consonant and its one or more attached vowels have been written, this key moves the carriage on, completing the syllable or group. A second key moves the carriage two steps. The letter-feed mechanism stays silent while a consonant and its attached vowels are written. The composite characters, made of consonants and vowels arranged vertically or horizontally, are printed sideways on paper put into the machine from the side, and when the paper is turned round the characters stand upright.
