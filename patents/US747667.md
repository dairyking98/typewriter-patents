## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Swinging or straight-sliding typebars limit how many bars fit and tend to get out of alignment; the inventor wants a visible writer with nearly twice as many bars, moving easily.

**Solution:** Use circularly curved type-bars that slide longitudinally along an arc between guide plates with ball bearings, in three rows that all strike the same point on the platen.

**How it works:** Key levers pivot on a rod; each is linked through a bell-crank lever and ball-jointed links to a curved typebar. The bars slide along their own circular arc in grooves formed between pairs of guide plates, one pair for each set of three bars lying in one plane. Balls in elongated chambers beside each bar roll as the bar moves, cutting friction, and separators hold the balls in place. The three rows are inclined progressively, so all bar tips converge on the same point of the roll. Springs return the keys.

**Features:** [Typebars](../features/elem-typebar.md), [Full keyboard, a key per character](../features/sel-keyboard-full.md), [Element struck to the platen](../features/imp-element-to-platen.md), [Visible writing](../features/frm-writing-visible.md)

**Parts:** platen roll; curved typebar; guide plate; ball chamber; bearing ball; ball separator; key lever; bell-crank lever; link; ball joint

**Numbers:** three rows of bars (four possible, two with a shift)

**Improves on:**

- swinging and straight-sliding typebar machines: nearly twice as many bars fit and guidance keeps alignment
