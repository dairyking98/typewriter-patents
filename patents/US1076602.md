## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** In a floating bell-crank key lever driving a typebar, the lever's rounded end only rests in a shallow recess in the typebar under a spring, and a hard strike can bounce it out unless the spring is stiff.

**Solution:** Shape an arm on the key lever and a heel on the typebar with matching curved faces that meet near the end of the stroke and positively lock the rounded end in its recess.

**How it works:** The typebar is pivoted to the frame and has a shallow segmental recess near its pivot. The bifurcated end of the key lever has a circular enlargement on one fork that sits in the recess, held by a spring, and the lever is guided by a pin working in a slot in the frame. The other fork has a face curved on an arc struck from the centre of the enlargement. As the typebar nears the platen, its heel slides against this curved face, which cannot move because the typebar pivot is fixed, so the key lever cannot move in the direction that would dislodge the enlargement.

**Features:** [Typebars](../features/elem-typebar.md), [Full keyboard, a key per character](../features/sel-keyboard-full.md), [Element struck to the platen](../features/imp-element-to-platen.md)

**Parts:** typebar; recess; floating bell-crank key lever; circular enlargement; bifurcated fork; curved guiding face; typebar heel; spring; guide pin and slot; platen

**Improves on:**

- earlier floating bell-crank key-lever typebar mechanisms: the typebar and key lever are positively locked at the end of the stroke instead of relying on a stiff spring
