## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Hall wants a hand-operated disk-and-sector type machine in which the letter disk and the figure sector can be brought into play independently, with letter and word spacing and a quick return of the print head.

**Solution:** A vertically rotating type-wheel plus a figure sector are turned by one sliding rack lever to the chosen character, locked by a forked stay, and pressed down onto the paper; the return stroke spaces the head along a screw roller.

**How it works:** The type-wheel and sector ride in a frame on a sleeve that threads onto a spiral-grooved spacing roller and slides on a guide rod. The operator pulls a sliding lever with two spur racks: the long rack turns the type-wheel a revolution first, and the second rack then engages the sector pinion (a spring pawl holds the sector still until then). Pins around the wheel engage a bifurcated stay to hold the chosen character in position. The lever then presses the wheel down to the paper, and on the way an inking pad is pushed clear by a tappet on the hub. On the return the spacing lever turns the sleeve on the screw and advances it one letter space, with an extra handle for word space. A release lets the sleeve slide freely back to the left like a quick screw.
