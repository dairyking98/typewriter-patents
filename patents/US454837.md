## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** In John Becker's index machine (US350717) the operator forgot to turn the feed roll, the rigid ink surfaces wore the type, and the rigid print stud needed adjusting for paper grade.

**Solution:** Advance the feed roll automatically from the index plate's return stroke, ink the type on freely turning felt discs, and make the print stud yield.

**How it works:** An index plate slides on a feed bar and carries a printing lever and a pivoted segment of rubber type. A feed dog loosely joined to the printing lever engages teeth on the feed bar. A stud on a pivoted arm on the index plate enters one of four spiral grooves on a hub on the feed roll; as the plate returns to the left the stud follows the groove and turns the roll a fixed amount, but it rides clear going forward. Two flat felt discs pivoted under the plate touch the type near their rims and revolve as the segment moves. The printing lever's stud is on a spring arm so it yields for different paper thickness.
