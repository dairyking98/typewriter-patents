## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** The inventor wants a type-wheel machine with writing visible, exact alignment of each type at the print point, letter spacing matched to letter width, automatic carriage return and an adjustable impression strength.

**Solution:** A continuously turning central shaft, driven by pedals, flywheel, hand or clockwork, is coupled by a key-selected pawl to a sleeve and gear that turns the type wheel to the chosen letter, then a knee joint pushes the swinging type wheel frame against the paper.

**How it works:** Keys held up or down by spring knee joints each have a radial arm; a pressed arm releases a pawl so that a sleeve on the turning central shaft rotates until the pawl is caught by that key's arm, and a gear on the sleeve turns the type wheel shaft by the same angle. A thrust disc on the shaft then drops the sleeve and straightens a knee joint behind the swinging wheel frame, pressing the type through an opening in a guard plate onto the paper against a rubber cushion whose position is adjustable for the number of copies. A V-shaped notch on the wheel's drive gear takes a fixed blade as the frame moves forward and so aligns the letter exactly. Two stepped rings on the sleeve, cut to letter width, set how far a push bolt and escapement wheel let the weight- or spring-driven carriage advance, giving variable spacing. A lever lifts or lowers the wheel on its square shaft to select another row of type, and the carriage can be disengaged by hand and is returned automatically at line end by a clutch driven from the flywheel. Ink comes from an inking roller or pad touched as the frame swings.
