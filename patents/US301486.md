## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Earlier Dement cylinder machines needed to be easier to operate, less likely to fail, more accurate and cheaper.

**Solution:** A continuously revolving cylinder with pivoted tilting bars, keyed to a type ring, where a depressed key tips a bar so its hammer presses paper on the matching type via cams.

**How it works:** A pulley-driven shaft carries a grooved cylinder with a bar in each groove pivoted near its centre, each reaching a particular key; a spring holds it up. A ring of types sits radially beside the cylinder's end and short hammers linked to the bars overhang it, one per type. Pressing a key moves its bar so that, as the cylinder revolves, the hammer arrives over the right type; cams then press the paper onto it, advance the paper and throw the hammer off. A toothed bar holds the key bars against the cylinder while operating.

**Features:** [ELEM:ring](../features/elem-ring.md), [ELEM:individual-slugs](../features/elem-individual-slugs.md), [SEL:keyboard-full](../features/sel-keyboard-full.md), [IMP:hammer-behind](../features/imp-hammer-behind.md), [IMP:element-to-platen](../features/imp-element-to-platen.md), [PAP:flat-bed](../features/pap-flat-bed.md), [USE:telegraph-printer](../features/use-telegraph-printer.md)

**Parts:** shaft; pulley; cylinder; tilting bar; hammer; type ring; key bar; spring; cam; toothed bar; paper guide; backing plate; band

**Improves on:**

- Dement earlier sliding-rod cylinder machine: pivoted tilting bars replace sliding rods
