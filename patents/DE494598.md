## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Typewriters whose individual types sit in rows and columns of an adjustable frame need many type bodies, a heavy frame and long adjustment paths, limiting speed.

**Solution:** Each type body carries characters on two opposite sides and the frame is turned 180 degrees to switch between them, halving the number of bodies.

**How it works:** Individual type bodies lie side by side and one above another in an adjustable frame, each limited to a short forward travel by rods. The frame is moved so the wanted type is in front of a fixed pusher (made slightly softer than the types), which advances that type by the short limited distance. Then the whole frame, with all types, is thrown by a nose pressing on a frame lever against the paper roller. During the stroke all the types are pressed together with the advanced one, so the advanced type's impact does not make the others rush forward and the pusher does not take the full blow. Unselected advanced types fall back by weight at rest, and turning the frame 180 degrees by a tension member switches to the other side's characters.
