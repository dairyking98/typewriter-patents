## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** In ordinary escapements the carriage feeds on the key upstroke, so in rapid writing it feeds too late and characters print on top of each other.

**Solution:** An attachment between keys and escapement lets either the pawl or the detent be the member normally engaging the rack, so the carriage can be made to feed on the key downstroke or upstroke.

**How it works:** Typebar keys press a universal bar linked to a pivoted arm held up by an adjustable spring. A link joins this arm to a slotted triangular plate on a rock-shaft that carries the rack bar. The pawl and detent are mounted on the carriage and engage the rack bar. Reversing which of them normally holds the rack moves the feed from the upstroke to the downstroke. Shown applied to Fox, Remington and Smith Premier escapements.

**Features:** [ESC:pawl-rack](../features/esc-pawl-rack.md), [FRM:adjustment-wear](../features/frm-adjustment-wear.md), [SEL:keyboard-full](../features/sel-keyboard-full.md), [ELEM:typebar](../features/elem-typebar.md)

**Parts:** carriage; pawl; detent; rack bar; rock-shaft; universal bar; spring; thumbscrew

**Improves on:**

- Fox, Remington, Smith Premier escapements: carriage feeds on the key downstroke instead of only the upstroke
