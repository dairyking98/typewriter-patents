## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** A foldable front-strike typewriter needs a platen shift that raises the platen carriage to two different heights (upper case and figures) using two shift levers on the same side of the machine, while still letting the carriage fold forward over the keyboard.

**Solution:** Two shift levers on a common cross rod lift a vertically sliding carriage support to different heights, with a pivoted stop closed by the second lever to limit the throw, and a single lock lever to hold either position.

**How it works:** The platen carriage rides on a support whose brackets pivot on forward extensions of two vertical slides, so the carriage can still fold forward over the keys. The figures lever is fixed to a rock shaft and lifts the slides the full distance. The upper-case lever is joined to it by a pin-and-slot (lost motion) connection; when it is pressed it first swings a pivoted stop to close an opening that a pin on the figures lever would otherwise enter, so the two levers then move together a shorter distance. A single lock lever engages slots under either lever to hold the shifted position, and an adjustable spring on the shaft assists the lift.
