## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Small pocket typewriters that travel over the paper needed separate handles for setting and printing, making them clumsy.

**Solution:** One swingable, radially pressable bracket on the housing both sets the type wheel and prints, and its return stroke advances the whole machine and the ribbon.

**How it works:** The type wheel sits on a hollow axis that can be pressed down against springs. A bracket on the axis ends swings between stops and turns the wheel through an incomplete gear and slot-and-pin guide; a pointer on the handle moves over a scale with a dimple per character. Pressing the handle lowers the wheel so the type meets the ribbon on the paper, the pointer entering the dimple. Sliding the handle in the hollow bracket pulls a cord that shifts the wheel along its axis to another type row. On release a pawl turns the ribbon drum one pitch, which also moves the machine one letter space along the paper; a transparent celluloid support lets the writing be seen.
