## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** In earlier pointer-lever type wheel machines the wheel is mounted on a swinging lever, so the setting gear rolls a little on the rack during the stroke and the character shifts; separate setting and printing devices, spring members and tooth locks cannot secure the exact position.

**Solution:** Mount the type wheel and its gear on a slide plate guided straight against the platen in the base plate, with a fixed ratchet comb that a pin on the setting rack enters during the stroke.

**How it works:** A sleeve with a setting mark slides and pivots on a guide rod and carries a rack meshing with a gear on the type wheel's axis. Moving the mark along the letter scale sets the character; pushing the lever in toward the paper moves the slide plate, with wheel and gear, straight to the platen against a return spring. During that stroke a locking pin on the rack enters a fixed ratchet comb in the base plate, fixing the wheel position before it hits. The same slide-plate motion drives a spring-loaded pawl that advances the carriage rack one letter width. Turning the adjusting knob swings a locking arm into the comb plane, which limits the stroke so the carriage advances without the wheel reaching the paper, giving the word space. Two toggle keys lift or lower the wheel on its axis for case shift, a down-shift key backspaces the carriage, and the whole mechanism sits under a roof-shaped cover so the writing and scale are visible.

**Improves on:**

- type wheel on a pivoting lever with rack setting: the setting gear rolled on the rack during the swing; here a straight slide plate keeps the geared setting fixed
- tooth and guide-tongue locking of the wheel: the lock was not under the type and hindered the swing; the comb is engaged by a pin on the rack in the base plate
- straight-slider type wheel machine with separate setting and printing devices: slider and locking combined with simplified structure and fewer parts
