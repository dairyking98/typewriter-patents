## Summary

**Kind:** a part or mechanism (any kind of machine) · **Subject (patent classes):** Guards, shields or dust excluders

**How it works (reading):** A carriage-controlled lock holds the hood bolt until the carriage has moved so far that the line-shift lever is out of the hood's opening path.

## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** A hinged protective hood over the motor and ribbon spools of a small typewriter can be opened with the carriage in a position where the line-shift lever or other carriage parts that pass over the hood would be damaged.

**Solution:** A carriage-controlled lock holds the hood bolt until the carriage has moved so far that the line-shift lever is out of the hood's opening path.

**How it works:** The hood sits in spring clips on pins at its rear edge, so it hinges up or lifts off. A spring-loaded locking bolt with an actuating button in the front wall holds the hood's underside stop. A locking rod with a bent arm rests under a control edge of the bolt and prevents it from pivoting, while a pivot lever with a nose rides on a control bar on the carriage. Only when the carriage has moved far enough left for the line-switch lever to clear the hood does the control bar release the nose, so the rod drops and the bolt can be released.
