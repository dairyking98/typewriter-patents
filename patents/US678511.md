## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** Ink-pad typewriters printing directly without a ribbon need a compact type-bar with a short, simple path from the pad to the platen.

**Solution:** A type-bar hinged on a ball-bearing hub carries a spindle with a type-block that is rotated by a cam sleeve as the bar swings, so the type face goes from lying on the pad to facing the platen.

**How it works:** A hanger carries two cones forming a ball-race with a surrounding hub, giving a ball-bearing pivot. A radial arm on the hub holds a freely turning spindle with a type-block of several types, retained by a pin in a shallow groove. Key lever, links and bell-crank swing the bar from a segmental ink-pad to the platen. As it swings, a link moves a sleeve along the spindle, and a spiral cam slot with dwell portions at each end engages a pin on the spindle and rotates the type-block between pad and print positions.
