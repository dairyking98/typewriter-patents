## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Upper and lower case on typebar machines has needed a shifting platen or a turning type block.

**Solution:** Upper and lower case types sit on a sleeve that slides along each typebar, and a cam moved by the upper-case lever pushes the sleeve to bring the chosen type into play.

**How it works:** Each typebar carries a sleeve with a lower-case and an upper-case type, free to slip between two shoulders. A vertically adjustable cam with two converging inclined faces and a central recess is raised or lowered by a rod from the upper-case lever. As the bar swings up, the sleeve meets the cam and is guided into the position for the selected case. The platen stays fixed.

**Features:**

- **Typebars:** [Typebars](../features/elem-typebar.md), [Typebar and type-head construction](../features/bar-typebar-construction.md)
- **Shift and cases:** [Shift by sliding the element](../features/shift-axial.md)
- **Impression:** [Element struck to the platen](../features/imp-element-to-platen.md)

**Parts:** typebar; type sleeve; upper-case type; lower-case type; shoulder; cam; cam rod; upper-case lever; platen

**Improves on:**

- shifting-platen machines: no platen movement, the type moves on the bar
- turning type-block machines: sleeve slides instead of rotating half-way
