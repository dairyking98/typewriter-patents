## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A typebar machine whose paper carriage is hard to control from the keyboard and which needs more characters without adding more typebars.

**Solution:** Each typebar carries two types and the whole typebar cradle rocks vertically to bring the upper or lower type to the print point, combined with finger-bar carriage control, a friction-reducing truck under the carriage, and a ribbon lifted only while a key is struck.

**How it works:** Converging sliding typebars in three banks run in guide plates on a cradle frame that pivots at its front. A cross bar lever lifts the cradle, bringing the lower of the two types on every head into line with the print point; a plunger with a notch and spring lever locks it up for capitals and a second plunger releases it. Every key lever also drives the carriage rack pawl; the pawl is held against the rack by an inclined stud during the stroke, then a spring pushes it forward to step the carriage. Two finger pieces above the keyboard move the carriage back or forward positively, while a bar releases the pawl so the carriage can be moved any distance without taking the hands from the keys. A ribbon on a spring-depressed frame is raised between type and paper only as the key goes down, so writing is visible, and the ribbon spools are wound by pawls on a shiftable cross bar.
