## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** The paper cylinder of a Japanese-script printing machine should not move at the moment of printing, and the machine also needs back-space, run-on, blank feed and fine adjustment of the sideways feed.

**Solution:** A swing frame worked by the print key alternately meshes a fixed gear (holding the cylinder still) and a rotary gear (letting a rack spring advance the cylinder after printing) with a rack on the paper cylinder, with a turning ring, pawls and detent giving the five movements.

**How it works:** A plate-shaped frame carries a fixed gear and a free rotary gear of equal size, which mesh in turn with a rack fixed to the paper cylinder. During printing the fixed gear meshes and holds the cylinder still, while a spring turns a ring that advances the freed rotary gear by the play of an arm between a pin and an eccentric character-spacing adjuster. When the key is released the rotary gear meshes and the stronger rack spring pulls the cylinder one character space. The back-space key lifts a pawl that pushes the gear back one space and locks the ring with a detent. The run-on key frees the stop pawl so the rack spring runs the cylinder to the next preset stop pin on the rack. A blank-feed key repeats the feed without printing, and a screw shifts the whole frame for fine adjustment.

## Description (English translation of the Japanese text, opening)

> Sideways feed device in a printing machine. Nature and object of the invention. This invention relates to a sideways feed device in a printing machine, whose feature is as follows. A cross shaft is set in a plate-shaped frame mounted so that it can swing, and a fixed gear and a rotary gear of equal diameter and equal number of teeth are mounted side by side on it. As the frame swings with the action of the printing key, they mesh in turn with a rack fixed to the paper cylinder. A turning ring is fitted loosely on the shaft of the two gears; a stop pawl is hinged to one of its arms and held by a spring against the rotary gear; its other arm lies, with a suitable play, between a pin fixed on the face of the frame, together with a back-space pawl linked to the back-space key, and a character-spacing adjuster. A groove is made in the lower part of the turning ring, which can catch on or leave a detent that rises and falls on the frame with the run-on key. The pivot of the plate-shaped frame is mounted on the machine frame with a suitable sliding play, and the frame's position is adjusted by an adjusting screw.
