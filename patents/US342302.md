## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A simple, efficient type-writer in which the printed work can be inspected while writing, without the operator bending over an index plate.

**Solution:** A yoke frame holding a type-wheel, its sector drive and the ribbon is hinged over a sliding paper carrier; a lever moved along a tilted index plate turns the wheel through a toothed sector, drops into a notch to lock, and a further press prints and spaces.

**How it works:** The frame swings on a guide rod above the paper carrier, and can be tipped up 90 degrees to expose the paper. The operator moves a bent operating lever along a throat between an inclined index plate and a notched ledge, which swings a pivoted toothed sector that turns a small gear on the type-wheel's axis, bringing the character to the print position. Pushing the lever down seats a V projection in the notch for that character, locking the wheel, and a further press lowers the whole frame so the type strikes the ribbon and paper; an adjustable screw stop limits the blow so soft metal type is not battered. On the frame's return stroke a pawl turns a feed wheel one tooth (or two, if a pin is pulled out) against a rack on the carrier, and other pawls feed the ribbon between spools. Individual metal types are held singly by set-screws in radial recesses. A space pin on the wheel at the centre position stops the descent short of the ribbon, giving a word space. A bell sounds at line end, and scales on the carrier help re-register for correcting a letter.
