## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** On a Hammond-type machine with two oscillating type-wheel segments, fast operators release and press keys so quickly that the type carrier rebounds or is not held still long enough for the hammer to print cleanly.

**Solution:** Put a spring between each key-driven type driver and the fingers that push the type segment, so the segment stops at a key-set stop and then dwells there while the driver overtravels, both on the way forward and on release.

**How it works:** Two type segments with pins on their hubs sit on a vertically movable shaft; a bank of finger keys each carries an arm and a vertical driver with a pair of spring-mounted fingers that push on the pins to oscillate a segment. Each key also raises a spring-pushed pin into the path of a pivoted stop lever, so the segment's advancing end hits the stop lever, which in turn is stopped by the raised pin, fixing the position for that character. The driver can move on past this point because the fingers sit on a plate with a coil spring or on a slotted plate with a flat spring, so they yield. On release the driver must first travel back far enough for the fingers to reset before they pull the segment back, giving a dwell that kills rebound while the hammer prints. At rest the finger ends stand beside the pins, locking the idle segment; an inclined face on a finger smooths the push. Outer keys raise the segments for capitals and figures and the centre key spaces.
