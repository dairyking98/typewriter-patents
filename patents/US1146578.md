## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** In a pressure-printing (noiseless) typewriter the platen needs rigid backing and its distance from the type must be adjustable, and printed paper needs a surface for erasing.

**Solution:** A carriage rides on a wedge block sliding on a beveled truss so a knob moves the whole platen carriage toward or away from the type, with a platen formed by a turned-down plate and an erasing table.

**How it works:** A rigid truss bar carries a beveled face against which a wedge block slides, driven by a threaded rod and knob. Turning the knob shifts the wedge and moves the platen carriage forward or back to set printing pressure. A graduated scale and pointer read the setting in paper-sheet thicknesses, and a gauge arm and fixed block let the operator measure the paper stack. The platen is the downturned front of a plate; paper enters through an aperture behind it, passes between feed rolls, and goes up over the platen. A flat erasing table sits on the plate.

**Features:**

- **Type element:** [Typebars](../features/elem-typebar.md)
- **Impression:** [Impression adjustment](../features/imp-impression-adjust.md), [Backing or anvil behind the paper](../features/imp-anvil-backing.md), [Platen pressed to the element](../features/imp-platen-to-element.md)
- **Paper and carriage:** [Feed rollers and paper release](../features/pap-feed-rollers.md), [Paper table and guides](../features/pap-paper-guide.md), [Line and point indicator](../features/pap-line-indicator.md)

**Parts:** truss bar; wedge block; threaded rod; adjusting knob; platen carriage; platen plate; feed roll; paper table; erasing table; graduated scale; pointer; gauge arm

**Numbers:** scale unit equals one sheet of typewriter paper thickness
