## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** Typebars need to be compact, to contact an ink pad before printing, and to allow two sets of type (e.g. different languages) striking the same point.

**Solution:** Each typebar is moved by a pair of crossed levers joined by a link, so the type touches an ink pad then strikes the platen, with two such sets stacked.

**How it works:** A typebar is pivoted to two crossed levers; one is pivoted to a Z-shaped rigid support, its free end linked to the other lever, whose free end is pivoted to a bar on a fixed pivot connected to a key lever. The type rests against an ink pad and, when actuated, travels to the platen printing point. A second set, with type of a different language, sits above with its own ink pad and strikes the same point.
