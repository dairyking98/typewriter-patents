## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** On a shift-carriage typebar machine (Remington style), the whole carriage must move to change case with the printing plane kept parallel, yet the carriage rack must stay engaged with the escapement dogs.

**Solution:** Mount the entire carriage on a pivoted two-rail shift frame that moves in parallel, and hinge the rack to the truck with a fixed rack holder so the rack teeth stay stationary during the shift.

**How it works:** Front and rear guide rails sit on rocker arms pivoted to the main frame and joined by connecting rods, so they rotate in unison. Depressing the shift key pulls the front rail's rocker through a wire and carries the whole carriage rearwards for upper case, with a spring returning it forward for lower case. The carriage has a platen frame hinged to a truck, and the rack is hinged to the truck, so the joints open and close as the carriage shifts. A spring-loaded rack guide holds the rack teeth in place against the escapement dogs in both case positions. A release key on the platen frame rocks the rack out of the dogs to free the carriage by hand.
