## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** The key-lever to typebar linkage needs a lot of leverage to overcome typebar inertia at the start yet a short key stroke, and the levers must stay aligned.

**Solution:** A key lever hung by a hook on a right-angle extension presses with a curved rocker face on a slotted type lever, so the contact point rolls from high above the pivot down toward it, giving high leverage at the start and a short stroke.

**How it works:** The key lever has a rear extension at about a right angle, with a hook at its top that hangs on a flange of a stamped guide plate and a curved outer face. A type lever, slotted at its lower end over an angle plate as pivot, bears against that face above its pivot. Pressing the key rocks the extension so the contact point descends along the type lever, and a ligament from the type lever's top pulls the typebar up to the platen. Both levers run in the same slot of the guide plate so they stay parallel, and a tension spring keeps the type lever against the key lever and returns it. A guard piece above the hook keeps the key lever seated.
