## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** A machine with its types in parallel rows on a movable holder needs a way to bring different type faces (capitals, small letters, figures) into printing and to set the holder precisely and signal line end.

**Solution:** The type holder is a tiltable bar with two angled faces of different type sets, positioned by dropping a finger-grip pin into the gaps of an interchangeable comb, and the whole holder swings down onto a rubber-covered paper roller.

**How it works:** The rubber-covered paper roller sits on a slide that moves in a guide, turned by a knurled knob or a pawl handle for lines. A lever with a pawl steps the slide one tooth per stroke, while an elastic rod keeps the paper against the roller. A pivoted frame carries a rail with guide grooves in which a slide holds the type holder, a bar turning in eyes, with two longitudinal faces set at an angle holding capitals and figures on one and small letters, punctuation and fractions on the other, a spring holding the chosen face in print position and a device tilting it. The operator grips the finger handle and slides the holder until its pin sits in a gap of the comb, whose gaps match letters on a rail 14, then swings the frame down so the type hits the roller, against a yielding stop spring. A signal sounds near the end of the line. Comb and holder can be exchanged.
