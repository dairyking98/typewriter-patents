## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Circular letters with the same body but different superscriptions need to look fully typewritten, which ordinary printing combined with typing does not manage when inks differ.

**Solution:** Combine a typebar typewriter with a rotary type-cylinder printing press so that both draw ink from one common ink fountain through a corrugated roll that the typebars strike.

**How it works:** The letter body is set in removable type and spacing bars in a recess of a hand-cranked cylinder and locked by a sliding segment. An ink fountain feeds a train of absorbent rolls to the cylinder and to a corrugated absorbent roll placed in the path of the typewriter's type-bars. The operator types the date and address, and each typebar takes ink from the corrugated roll on its downward sweep. The sheet is then removed, passed between a roll and the cylinder while turning the crank, and printed with the body. Both the typed and printed parts use the same ink so the result looks fully typewritten.
