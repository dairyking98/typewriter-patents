## Summary

**Kind:** typebar machine · **Subject (patent classes):** Typewriters or selective printing mechanisms characterised by the mounting, arrangement or disposition of the types or dies with types or dies carried on sliding bars or rods on side surfaces thereof, e.g. fixed thereto the types or dies being movable relative to the bars or rods

**In the patent’s own words:** The object of my invention is to producea simple and effective carriage-shifting device for type-writing machines which is capable of shifting the carriage backward and forward from normalcentral position to receive impressions from any one of three type characters arranged upon each of the type-bars.

## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A typebar machine with three characters per bar needs a simple carriage shift that moves forward and back from a central rest and holds position rigidly with accurate adjustment.

**Solution:** Two shift keys turn an oscillating spiral-grooved cam disk through mutilated racks and a segment gear; a roller in the groove moves a shifting arm to shift the carriage forward or back, and the groove's concentric centre locks it in the middle.

**How it works:** A rock shaft with arms is joined to the typewriter carriage, and a rocker arm with a roller engages a spiral cam groove on a disk fixed to a segment gear. Two spring-held key rods carry mutilated racks on opposite sides of the gear. Pressing one key rotates the cam one way, driving the roller outward or inward so the carriage shifts forward or back. The concentric centre of the groove (about a quarter inch long) locks the carriage in the middle position, and a single tooth on each rack holds the gear when its key is up. A centering spring with an adjustable pin returns the carriage, adjusting screws limit shifted travel, and a locking arm can hold a key down for upper case work.

**Numbers:** concentric cam portion about 1/4 inch long; three characters per typebar; cam groove extends over two-thirds of the disk
