## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typewriters of the time were complicated and costly; the inventor wants a simple index machine whose operator can see which character will print and which keeps the number of characters on the element and indicator small.

**Solution:** A rotary bell carrying a rubber type ring is turned by hand until a plunger on it drops into one of the holes of a fixed perforated circle, then pushed down to print, which also advances the paper carriage and ribbon.

**How it works:** The bell (A) with a rubber type ring (X) on its lower edge sits on a spindle that both turns and slides in a fixed tubular standard. The operator turns the bell to the wanted character, and a plunger carried by the bell drops into the matching hole of the fixed perforated circle (D) to hold it there. Pressing the bell down against a return spring prints the character through a ribbon onto paper held over an impression cylinder. A tappet on the spindle depresses a lever whose pawl engages a rack on the paper carriage and feeds it one space; the same lever steps the ribbon between two drums. Fingers on the bell point at a semicircular indicator around the holes, so one alphabet serves both halves of the ring (capitals/lower case) depending on which finger is used. Separate coupled keys print accent marks, and a gong signals the right margin.
