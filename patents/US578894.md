## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** Typebars should be light, stiff and cheap to make.

**Solution:** A typebar struck up from flat metal U-shaped in section, stiffened by a spring-steel wire held in by turned-over edges.

**How it works:** A bar of required length with downturned end and perforated ears for the journals is stamped from soft flat metal as a U section. A stiff wire or rod of spring steel is laid in and held by turning in the lower edges. A socket at the far end seats the type. The wire may project to take the blow against the frame stop or have a notch or tooth to stop longitudinal movement and prevent type sliding and blurring. Intended for the Williams typewriter.

**Features:** [Typebars](../features/elem-typebar.md), [How the element is made](../features/elem-element-construction.md), [Alignment lock at the print point](../features/elem-alignment-lock.md), [Few parts, low cost](../features/frm-few-parts.md)

**Parts:** typebar; U-shaped channel; stiffening wire; socket; ear; link; stop

**Improves on:**

- earlier solid typebars: lighter and stiffer built-up bar
