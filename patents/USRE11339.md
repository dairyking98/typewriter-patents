## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Shifting the heavy carriage and platen on a Remington, or a whole frame of key levers and typebars, makes the shift heavy and slow.

**Solution:** Key levers are fixed; each typebar is pivoted to its key lever so that only the active bar is shifted sideways by a guide that also controls its head at impact.

**How it works:** Key levers pivot on a fixed support and each typebar pivots on the end of its key lever so it can move transversely to the printing stroke. A triangular typebar guide in front of the platen has converging strips that direct the bar head to a slot. Pivoted levers with studs of different length raise the guide so the central or lower character of the bar prints. The carriage has a double rack bar and a pair of pawls with an adjusting screw and spring for a quick-action feed.

**Features:** [Three or more cases](../features/shift-triple.md), [Typebars](../features/elem-typebar.md), [Full keyboard, a key per character](../features/sel-keyboard-full.md), [Pawl-and-rack escapement](../features/esc-pawl-rack.md), [Roller platen](../features/pap-platen-roller.md)

**Parts:** key lever; typebar; typebar guide; shift lever; stud; double rack bar; pawl; spring; paper roller; base; pillar

**Improves on:**

- Remington No. 2: shifts only the active typebar rather than the heavy platen carriage
