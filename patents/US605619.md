## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** A type cylinder that must be turned, slid along its axis and swung to the paper on one key stroke tends to overshoot or fail to stop exactly at the right position before it hits the paper.

**Solution:** Each key stroke moves graded stop levers that turn a toothed segment and slide the cylinder by exact amounts and stop them, with a lost-motion arrangement so the key can finish its stroke while the cylinder swings the last part to the paper without further turning.

**How it works:** Key levers, in three groups, pull rods that rock shafts. One shaft swings the frame carrying the type cylinder up against the paper at each stroke; two others move levers that slide the cylinder along its axis by one or two sections, stopped by pins. The cylinder is turned by a pinion driven from a swinging toothed segment; five graded levers on each side stop the segment after one to five rows of turn, selected by which key frame is pulled down. The types sit in sections and longitudinal rows on the cylinder, and upper-case is reached by a shift key that moves the whole paper carriage frame rearwards. Springs or circular-shaped slots let the key continue down after turning and shifting have been stopped, so the swinging cylinder prints without moving further. A pawl and rack escapement spaces the carriage on key release, a space key skips the print swing, and a ribbon is fed by a ratchet moved as the cylinder swings back. An adjustable set screw limits the cylinder stroke to regulate impression force.
