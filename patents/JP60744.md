## Summary

**Kind:** type-element machine · **Subject (patent classes):** Typewriters or selective printing mechanisms characterised by the mounting, arrangement or disposition of the types or dies with types or dies arranged in stationary or sliding cases or frames or upon flexible strips, plates, bars or rods; Japanese-language typewriter

**In the English translation of the patent’s own words:** This invention relates to a printing machine for copying or printing in which: a frame movable forward, back, left and right is placed over a flat printing plate like a stereotype plate, on which a great many characters are formed right-reading, not as mirror characters;

**How it works (reading):** A hand-guided frame carrying a spring-struck printing lever is slid over a flat plate of right-reading characters, and each stroke prints one character onto a narrow continuous tape that feeds on automatically.

## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Earlier copying/printing machines print many lines on a wide sheet held on a heavy paper cylinder with character and line spacing gear; the inventor wants a simple, light, easy machine.

**Solution:** A hand-guided frame carrying a spring-struck printing lever is slid over a flat plate of right-reading characters, and each stroke prints one character onto a narrow continuous tape that feeds on automatically.

**How it works:** A sliding rail and sliding frame carry the mechanism over a fixed flat plate of characters in rows and columns; the operator moves the frame until a pointer (71) sits over the wanted character. Pressing the handle (6) drops a printing lever (7) through an over-centre spring (10) on its arm (9) so it strikes the plate with the tape lying over its front end; just before that an ink roller (64) is swung out of its rest and rolled over the plate, then returns. A ratchet (25) and feed wheel (24) on the lever shaft, with a pawl (26) that is lifted off at the end of the stroke by a screw (41), feed the tape one character space per stroke while the lever is at rest relative to the tape at the moment of impact. A second ratchet (42) counts characters, a peg in one of its holes rings a bell at the line length, and a knob (73) with a mutilated gear (56) feeds the tape a larger gap for the line space and resets the counter through a cam. The tape (paper, mimeograph stencil or cloth) is cut into lines afterwards and mounted side by side for duplicating.
