## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** In electric type-cylinder typewriters the cylinder rebounds at the end of its rotation (both at the stop and when returning to the middle position), which can misplace the type before the impression.

**Solution:** Use spring-loaded pawls that snap in behind the stop the instant rotation ends, plus a single-spring lever pair for centring, so the cylinder cannot bounce back.

**How it works:** Two electromagnets turn a shaft that rotates the type cylinder through a toothed sector until a stop on an arm hits the stop bracket the pressed key has advanced. Spring-loaded pawls on the arm are pushed back by the bracket and jump behind it as the stop is reached, holding the cylinder in place for the impression. After printing a spring returns the cylinder: two angle levers joined by a single spring press on a pin of the sector so both directions of return have equal force. At the middle position two pawls on a stop on the shaft catch it; the pawl on the side of the pressed key is lifted by a key linkage to allow rotation, and the other pawl limits the return and prevents rebound. Alternatively a slot in the middle position can receive a pawl.
