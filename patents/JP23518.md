## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** At the end of each line the typist must take a hand off the keys to return the carriage and turn the platen, which loses time and breaks the flow of copying.

**Solution:** An attachment for any typewriter that returns the carriage and turns the platen automatically at the end of a line (or earlier at a key), using one spring that drives the carriage both ways through a clutch and is kept wound automatically.

**How it works:** A spring drum drives the carriage left at each escapement step through an inner clutch drum and a flat band, while the usual dog and escape wheel govern the step. At the line end an adjustable reversing stop on a toothed stop bar trips a reversing bar, whose rack and pinion move a slide that disengages the inner clutch drum and engages an outer one. The same drum spring then drives a second band, a transmitter and a third band that pull the carriage back to its start. A re-engaging stop at the start position returns the bar and reselects the forward clutch. A pawl-foot on a bell crank rides a deflector on the frame to turn the platen by its ratchet and sector by an adjustable amount, a hand lever or key can do either action before line end, and a key-operated ratchet winder rewinds the drive spring.

## Description (English translation of the Japanese text, opening)

> Patent No. 23518, Class 30. Specification. Typewriter (印字器). Nature and object of the invention. This invention relates to typewriters of all kinds in which the carriage has to be returned to its starting point after each line is finished, and in which the paper roller (platen) must also be moved after each line. These operations are done sometimes separately, sometimes together; but either way the typist must take a hand off the keyboard, which loses time and interrupts the sequence of the matter being copied. In this invention, first, the carriage is easily and quickly disconnected automatically, so that the machine can be used as an ordinary typewriter while the platen-turning mechanism still works; second, a single spring moves the carriage in both directions of its travel, and the tension of that spring is kept up automatically. The mechanism can be fitted to any typewriter with only slight alteration, and is built so that it works without fault from the first line of a sheet to the last.
