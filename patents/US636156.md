## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Make a cheap, easily operated typewriter; full banks of type levers are costly and the lightly built carrier cannot be relied on for accurate type alignment.

**Solution:** A swinging segment carries slotted typebars which a hand setting-arm with a finger ring positions over a key; pressing that key locks the segment and fires a single fixed hammer.

**How it works:** Pivoted typebars sit in radial slots in a wheel-like segment that swings about a central post. The operator moves a finger-ring setting arm (linked to the segment) over the desired key button, which turns the segment to bring that bar to the printing point. Pressing the key drops its lever out of a guide comb into a forked locking arm, which holds the segment in position, and also swings a bail so a hammer strikes the bar. The bar's lower arm lands on a fixed lifter block that both steadies it and lifts it level, correcting any misalignment of the light carrier. The hammer stroke also raises a ribbon guide, advances the carriage pawl, and the segment's oscillation ratchets the ribbon spool forward. Two shift keys lift the segment and lifter to bring the middle or lower of three types on each bar into play.
