## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Syllable typewriting normally needs a wholly new keyboard that writers must learn from scratch.

**Solution:** Add syllable typebars at the sides of a conventional arc of single-letter typebars, driven from extra syllable keys by horizontal tie rods, so a familiar keyboard also writes syllables.

**How it works:** Keys act through key stems, angle levers and pull rods, via slot-mounted adjustable bearings, on intermediate levers that swing typebars mounted in slots of curved bearing carriers. Single-letter bars sit in the middle of the arc and syllable bars on the sides, and a syllable key prints the syllable while the carriage advances the matching number of letter spaces. A compression spring with an adjusting screw sets key pressure, and the intermediate lever rolls on a plate so its lever arms change length for a light early stroke and faster final approach.
