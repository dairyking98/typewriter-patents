## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** A key-driven wheel typewriter needs a simple way to turn a light type-wheel to the right letter with the operator's key power, stop it exactly, and strike the paper against the type without a heavy swinging wheel.

**Solution:** Each key throws a gear-arm that spins the type-wheel left or right from a home position (never more than half a turn) until a stop on the key halts a vibrating arm geared to the wheel, then a spring-tripped platen rod strikes the paper against the stationary wheel.

**How it works:** Keys in two halves of the keyboard push one of two pivoted gear-bars forward, which meshes with a mutilated pinion on the type-wheel shaft to spin the wheel right or left from its initial point; keys nearer the centre travel less, matching type nearer the home point on the wheel. A projection on the same key drops into the path of an oscillating arm geared to the shaft, which hits it and stops the wheel with a letter at the print point. A stop under the wheel is raised by two shift keys to lift the light gutta-percha wheel to its second row (capitals) or third (figures). A universal bar under all the keys draws back a platen rod that carries the paper and then trips off a hood, so a spring snaps the paper sharply against the type. The same bar steps the carriage with fast and loose dogs on a rack against a spring drum, and advances the ribbon by a dog and ratchet. A spring pointer on an eccentric notched disk returns the wheel to its home position and locks it there.
