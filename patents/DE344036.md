## Summary

**Kind:** a part or mechanism (any kind of machine) · **Subject (patent classes):** Devices or arrangements for controlling character selection

**How it works (reading):** A light type-wheel machine set on the writing surface, with key-lever lengths pulling Bowden cables to turn the wheel by the amount for each character and press the wheel onto the paper.

## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** Typewriters cannot write on bound ledgers or flat documents that cannot be clamped in the machine, and typebar actions are complex.

**Solution:** A light type-wheel machine set on the writing surface, with key-lever lengths pulling Bowden cables to turn the wheel by the amount for each character and press the wheel onto the paper.

**How it works:** Each key acts on a lever of a different length, so each key pull always has the same travel but gives a different Bowden cable movement. All cables wind on a common roller, which frictionally turns the type wheel by an amount unique to the key, bringing that type to the lowest point above the writing surface. A spiral spring returns the wheel and roller on release. A rod behind the levers pulls a second Bowden cable that drops the frame carrying wheel and roller onto the surface, and springs lift it again. Stepping uses a spring pin riding a ring on the wheel to index a pinion on a rack, with manual line feed available.
