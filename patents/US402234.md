## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** The earlier Richards latch needed a long latch stroke and considerable force to trip, and could misbehave when the key was held down as the latch returned; the cases also had to be removable in a close-packed set.

**Solution:** A pivoted two-catch escapement beside the spring-loaded latch, with the pivot placed to minimise latch stroke and trip force, and a spring-loaded lever pressing the latch against it.

**How it works:** A spring-driven reciprocating latch in a revolving turret is driven back by a starting arm. The latch has two catches, one ahead of the other. A pivoted escapement bar beside it has a holding catch and a detaining catch. At rest the holding catch hooks the first latch catch. Pressing the key lifts the escapement arm, which forces the latch down off the holding catch and the latch springs forward. If the key is released, the holding catch drops back to re-catch the returning latch; if the key is still held, the forward latch catch is caught by the detaining catch, and when the key rises the spring lever lifts the latch until the holding catch re-engages. The escapement pivot sits just below the line of the catch edges, which cuts stroke and trip force. Each latch sits in a sheet-metal case held by a spring detent so one can be removed without disturbing its neighbours.
