## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Ribbon mechanisms of the day needed manual reversing of the ribbon feed, a fixed ribbon throw, and no easy way to put the ribbon out of action (for stencil work).

**Solution:** An L. C. Smith ribbon mechanism that reverses ribbon feed automatically when a spool empties, lets the ribbon-carrier throw be varied by shifting a fulcrum, and can disable the ribbon lift entirely.

**How it works:** The carriage spring drum drives, through ratchets and reducing gears, a rear cross shaft that feeds two flexible shafts to the two vertical spool shafts. Only one flexible shaft is geared to a spool at a time. When the ribbon is fully unwound, a lever on the spool core swings out, depresses a cam lever and releases a latch; a cam wheel on the driving shaft then lifts it, rocking a bell-crank so the drive changes to the other spool. The ribbon passes through a sheet-metal carrier held below the print line so writing is visible. The universal bar vibrates a lever on the carrier each keystroke; a slide with two fulcrum pins engages different holes in the lever to give a short throw (upper ribbon half), a long throw (lower half or other colour zone), or no engagement so the ribbon stays down.
