## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** On a shift-key typebar machine, the keys must reach either the upper-case or lower-case key-lever of each pair without doubling the number of keys.

**Solution:** A perforated plate swung on links shifts the lower ends of the key stems over either the upper or lower key-lever of each pair, driven by a shift key and rock-shaft.

**How it works:** Key stems pass through slotted sleeves in a fixed plate and are held up by springs. Their lower ends pass through holes in a movable plate hung on links. Depressing a shift key turns a rock-shaft whose cranks and links swing the plate, tilting the stems from the lower-case lever to the upper-case lever; a spring returns it on release. Blocks on each pair of levers, with the neighbouring lever notched, make a key press only one lever.

**Features:** [SHIFT:platen](../features/shift-platen.md), [SEL:keyboard-full](../features/sel-keyboard-full.md), [ELEM:typebar](../features/elem-typebar.md)

**Parts:** key; key-lever; slotted sleeve; perforated shifting plate; link; shift key; rock-shaft; return spring; block
