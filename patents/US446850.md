## Summary

**Kind:** type-element machine · **Subject (patent classes):** Typewriters or selective printing mechanisms characterised by the mounting, arrangement or disposition of the types or dies with types or dies on spherical, truncated-spherical, or like surfaces

**In the patent’s own words:** My invention relates to that class of typewriters in which a series'of keys or levers are actuated by both hands of the operator, and more particularly to the class wherein the types are carried on a segment or type wheel, and in which a hammer is used to cause an impression to be made on paper.

**How it works (reading):** Each key's pin rides down an inclined bar on a vertical shaft, turning the type segment by an amount set by the key's distance from the shaft, then drops into a hole in the bar to lock it, and only then releases a spring-reversed hammer.

## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Wants a two-hand, fast-writing typewriter with a type segment and hammer that needs comparatively few parts and is unlikely to get out of order.

**Solution:** Each key's pin rides down an inclined bar on a vertical shaft, turning the type segment by an amount set by the key's distance from the shaft, then drops into a hole in the bar to lock it, and only then releases a spring-reversed hammer.

**How it works:** Key levers lie across an inclined bar fixed to a vertical shaft that carries a three-row type segment. Pressing a key pushes its pin down the incline, swinging the bar left or right by an amount that depends on the pin's distance from the shaft, which brings a type opposite the platen. The pin then drops through a hole in the bar (guided by a ledge), locking the segment. A cross-bar under the keys drives a bail along a slot in a pivoted hammer; a single spring flips the hammer forward once the bail passes below the pivot, striking the type bar forward onto the paper, and flips it back on release, with a dog delaying the blow until the pin has locked. Case is changed by sliding the segment along the shaft with two shift levers to bring the capital or figure row to the platen. A spring-driven carriage is stepped by two alternating dogs on a rack, with a space key, a ribbon driven by carriage-geared spools, and a pivoted rack for return.
