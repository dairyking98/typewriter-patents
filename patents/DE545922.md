## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** On stenotype machines the paper tape must advance by exactly one tooth per stroke, but the inertia of the platen makes a plain pawl overshoot.

**Solution:** A one-piece pawl with a switching tooth and a trailing safety tooth, plus an adjustable stop on the holder, moves the ratchet exactly one tooth per stroke.

**How it works:** A holder pivoted on the universal rail swings down when a key is released. A one-piece pawl on the holder has a switching tooth that engages the platen ratchet wheel and a safety tooth behind it that lands against a following tooth at the end of the stroke, preventing overshoot. An extension of the pawl rests against an adjustable stop of compressible material on a screw in the holder, so the pawl is rigid while driving the ratchet and cannot give way. A return spring on a third tooth brings the pawl back. On the upstroke the switching tooth, which has play between ratchet teeth, runs idle slightly so the safety tooth can lift off the sloping flank, and the pawl swings out of the way.
