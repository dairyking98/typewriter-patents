## Summary

**Kind:** a part or mechanism (any kind of machine) · **Subject (patent classes):** Special purpose keyboards; Human engineering on planes with different or alterable inclination, e.g. convex plane

**In the patent’s own words:** My invention relates to an improved keyboard, in which both inconveniences are obviated by arranging the keys in four curved lines, which I call guiding-lines, the two innermost of which are the guiding-lines of the thumbsand correspond to a curve described by the point of the thumb when spreading it away from the hand.

**How it works (reading):** Arrange keys on four curved guiding lines matching thumb and finger paths, with large main keys on the lines and smaller by-keys before and behind, hands resting on cushions.

## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** Rectangular or semicircular keyboards make the typist move the forearm; stenographic curved layouts have too few keys.

**Solution:** Arrange keys on four curved guiding lines matching thumb and finger paths, with large main keys on the lines and smaller by-keys before and behind, hands resting on cushions.

**How it works:** Two inner guiding lines follow the thumb tips and two outer lines follow the four fingertips. The most used characters sit on the lines in larger keys, with the less-used by-keys placed before and behind, the far ones raised. All keys are reached by finger motion with palms resting on cushions. Vowels go to the thumbs. The example has 48 keys carrying 88 types, shown with the Yost machine and usable by the blind through key size differences.

**Features:** [SEL:keyboard-few-keys](../features/sel-keyboard-few-keys.md), [SEL:keyboard-full](../features/sel-keyboard-full.md), [USE:blind](../features/use-blind.md)

**Parts:** key; main key; by-key; cushion; guiding line; Yost typebar mechanism

**Numbers:** 48 keys; 88 types; hand angle about 90 degrees

**Improves on:**

- conventional rectangular or semicircular keyboards: curved ergonomic layout reachable without arm motion
