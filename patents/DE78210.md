## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Small portable index typewriters need a way to hold the selected type exactly at the print position so careful, accurately spaced work is possible.

**Solution:** A locking pin on the pressure plate enters a hole under the selected type in the type segment during the stroke, holding the elastic rubber type carrier exactly in place.

**How it works:** Soft rubber types are moulded on a rubber backing fixed to an arcuate metal segment plate carried by radial arms on a hub; the hub axle is adjustable in a slot to set the types against the pressure plate. The operator turns a pointer over an alphabet plate to bring the chosen type to the print point. Depressing the pressure lever swings a plate carrying a type pusher, which presses the type through an opening in a protective plate onto the paper while the rubber yields. The same plate carries a pin that enters one of the holes in the segment plate, each centred under a type, locking the segment against lateral movement during impression. On release the pin withdraws and the space button's push rod advances the paper carriage (rack, pawl and tooth) one letter space.
