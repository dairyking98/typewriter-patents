## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** Power-driven typebar drives using friction segments, cams or roller-mounted bars are complicated, give uneven line height, and do not convert easily to manual operation.

**Solution:** A typebar with a fixed pivot is driven through a pull rod by a friction wheel pressed on a rotating roller by the key; shifting a rail switches to manual drive.

**How it works:** A continuously rotating roller drives a friction wheel mounted on a spring-held lever. Pressing a key pulls an arm so the friction wheel contacts the roller and pulls the typebar to the platen through a pull rod. Releasing the key returns everything by springs. For manual operation, a rail disengages the intermediate links and locks the friction lever arms, so a second pull rod on each key lever moves the typebar directly. The roller slips if the key is held too long.
