## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Typebar typewriters needed many bars and brackets for the characters printed, printed against a curved platen, and had fragile brackets, a hard-to-service spring drum and a clumsy key attachment.

**Solution:** A keyboard typebar machine in which each rotatable typebar carries two characters and is turned by its pinion rolling over pins on a shiftable pin-arc, printing against a flat rubber impression bar.

**How it works:** Each typebar holder is pivoted in a riveted closed bracket and carries two characters at one end and a pinion at the other. As the key swings the bar up, the pinion meshes with a series of fixed pins on a semicircular pin-arc and the bar rotates, bringing one of its two characters to the print point; sliding the pin-arc with a shift key puts the opposite set of pins in mesh and the bar rotates the other way, selecting the other case. Locking pins between the pinion teeth give exact alignment, and a lever or pawl on the universal bail holds the pin-arc shifted until the printing key is released. A crescent-shaped bar with a rubber strip gives a flat impression surface beside the ordinary platen. The carriage is driven by a spring drum and a two-pawl escapement worked from the universal bail, with an eccentric screw to set pawl depth.
