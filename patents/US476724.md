## Summary

**Kind:** type-element machine · **Subject (patent classes):** Typewriters or selective printing mechanisms characterised by the mounting, arrangement or disposition of the types or dies with types or dies arranged in stationary or sliding cases or frames or upon flexible strips, plates, bars or rods

**In the patent’s own words:** The object of the invention is to provide a new and improved type writing machine which is simple and durable in construction, can easily be manipulated, and arranged so as to print directly without the use of a ribbon single characters, such as letters of the alphabet, numerals, sigus,and the like, and also words composed of two, three, four, or more letters each.

**How it works (reading):** A two-axis sliding type case of vertical type blocks is moved by a pointer knob over an index chart until the chosen block sits under a fixed aperture, and one lever pushes it up through the aperture against the paper while also spacing the carriage.

## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A simple, durable typewriter that prints single characters and whole words or syllables directly without a ribbon, with the operator picking the type by hand.

**Solution:** A two-axis sliding type case of vertical type blocks is moved by a pointer knob over an index chart until the chosen block sits under a fixed aperture, and one lever pushes it up through the aperture against the paper while also spacing the carriage.

**How it works:** Vertical type blocks sit in a holder of about six rows of twenty (120 types), and the holder slides longitudinally in a carriage that slides laterally, so any block can be brought over a central pin. The operator holds a knob on the holder whose pointer moves over an index table laid out like the type rows; spring-loaded rollers in notches on a bar from the carriage hold the position. A space-lever pressed down works the printing-lever, which lifts the pin and pushes the type up through a beveled fixed opening against the paper roller; a spring returns the type. On release a spring-returned lever pulls a toothed rack on the paper carriage along by the number of teeth set by which of five notches the space-lever was pressed in (one letter, word end, 2-letter word, etc.). Inking rollers ride on the type tops as the case slides, so no ribbon is needed.
