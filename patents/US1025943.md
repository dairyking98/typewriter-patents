## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Operating the escapement, case shift and carriage return of a Remington No. 10 takes much finger effort.

**Solution:** Solenoids driven through a tripper contact power the escapement, platen shift (with hook-lock held until a character prints), carriage return and line spacing.

**How it works:** A tripper on the key-controlled universal bar closes the circuit of a solenoid that rocks the universal bar's rock shaft and thereby the escapement; the bar has slight lost motion, so key force is negligible. The tripper then trips off and breaks the circuit so the escapement spring restores the key, even if the key is held. A shift solenoid shifts the platen as the shift key starts to go down and a hook holds it shifted until a printing key is struck, when the rock shaft releases the hook. Estimates: half the key effort goes to the escapement and its spring.

**Features:** [IMP:electric](../features/imp-electric.md), [ESC:escape-wheel](../features/esc-escape-wheel.md), [SHIFT:platen](../features/shift-platen.md), [ESC:carriage-return](../features/esc-carriage-return.md), [PAP:line-space](../features/pap-line-space.md)

**Parts:** tripper; solenoid; universal bar; rock shaft; escapement spring; shift hook; shift key

**Numbers:** escapement ~ half of key labour
