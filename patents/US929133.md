## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** A key-operated typewriter must be made to run from electric circuits (for example from a stock-letter strip or remote contacts) instead of manual keying.

**Solution:** One solenoid-controlled two-part clutch per key lever grips a constantly rotating cylinder and is dragged down by it, depressing the key lever, then is released by short-circuiting the solenoid.

**How it works:** Under a standard Remington-type machine sit two geared, constantly rotating cylinders driven by a small motor. Each key lever is linked by a double-jointed link to a pair of sheet-metal clutch halves straddling the cylinders; closing that letter's circuit pulls the solenoid core to its armature so the halves grip the cylinder and are carried down, depressing the key and striking the typebar. Near the end of stroke an extension closes a short-circuit about the solenoid, held by a small holding magnet, releasing the clutch, and a spring lifts the key. The force of the blow can be governed by releasing earlier. The shift keys are worked by a powerful magnet that is latched until a second switch short-circuits it. Variants release the clutch by an escapement-lever circuit breaker or intermittently drive the cylinder with a magnet and pawl.
