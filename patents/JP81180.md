## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Typing and copying continuously (e.g. shorthand typing) with carbon paper is slow and loses clarity because the same strip of carbon is struck repeatedly; loose papers get scattered.

**Solution:** A differential platen, driven through a circulating gear with a slightly different tooth count and a prime-toothed ratchet, never repeats its phase, so the type always strikes a fresh part of a carbon surface stored inside the platen, while roll writing and copy paper are fed and taken up.

**How it works:** The platen is a hollow base tube holding a roll of carbon paper, wrapped by a springy core tube and rubber tube whose slit is kept shut by spring but opened by a cam lever to pay out fresh carbon. The platen's end cover has an internal gear (e.g. 50 teeth) meshing with an eccentrically mounted circulating gear (e.g. 47 teeth) fixed to a ratchet wheel and index wheel (e.g. 43 teeth); the line-feed pawl advances the platen via this gearing. Because the tooth counts are not whole-number related, the platen's phase takes thousands of line feeds to repeat, so the type never lands on the same spot. Roll writing paper and copy paper from a supply box pass over the platen to two take-up drums driven by friction couplings, and a slide-mounted cutter cuts either the writing sheet alone or both. Frames adapt the whole assembly to typewriters of different layout.

## Description (English translation of the Japanese text, opening)

> Specification. Continuous copying device for typewriters. Nature and object of the invention. In this invention a gear is attached to one end of the platen and meshed with a circulating gear whose number of teeth differs from it by a little, so that their ratio is not a whole number; an index wheel and a ratchet wheel are fixed on the shaft of this circulating gear. The platen is thus made differential: its rotation is given a very small lag or lead, and it presents a printing surface that is renewed without end. With it can be used rolls of spare writing paper and copy paper, a store for spare carbon paper, take-up drums for the typed writing paper and copy paper, and a cutter. The object is to type continuously (or in separate pieces, as one likes) while the carbon surface is constantly renewed and a copy taken at the same time, so as to make the work easy and quick in busy typing or in typewriter shorthand, to keep documents from getting scattered and lost, and to obtain the highest efficiency.
