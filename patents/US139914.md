## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Single-letter-at-a-time machines are slow; the inventor wants to print several letters per operation.

**Solution:** Two or more treadle-raised type levers are each arrested at a key-pin-selected height so they present different letters side by side, and the paper advances several characters per stroke.

**How it works:** Weighted levers on fixed axes are raised by a treadle through a lifter and fall under spring and weight. Each lever's long arm carries a hinged spring flap; pressing a key pin inward into the flap's path stops the lever on descent at a height unique to that pin, presenting one letter of the alphabet at the end of the short arm (directly, or through a gear segment that multiplies the motion). A collar on the pin and a lip on the flap hook together so the finger can leave and look for the next pin. The paper on a flat surface or endwise-moving cylinder is drawn against the types, and types are inked by rollers or pads. The left hand works one set of pins and the right hand the other, both levers driven by the same treadle.
