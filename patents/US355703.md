## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Simplify and improve the author's earlier index-plate machine (application 171,773): paper holding, plate setting, and feed control.

**Solution:** A hand-moved plate carrying rubber type on its underside and a matching character index on top is slid until a fixed pointer sits over the wanted character, then pressed down to print, which also steps the whole printing frame along fixed rods.

**How it works:** A frame carrying the printing and feeding mechanism slides along two parallel rods above a paper roller. A plate with a knob is moved by hand; its upper face carries an index of characters and its lower face a removable rubber type plate, arranged on arcs about the plate's oscillation centre. A fixed pointer over the index shows which type is above an opening in the inking pad plate. Pressing the plate down pushes the type through the opening onto the paper and a tapered pin enters one of a series of holes in the plate to align it exactly. The same motion, through a yoke and lever, lifts a holding tooth from a rack on a guide rod and advances a pawl one or more teeth; on release a spring drops the tooth and pushes the frame forward. A thumb lever feeds without printing for word spaces, and pressing two thumb-pieces together frees the frame. A lever shifts the type plate on slotted screws so a second type series (e.g. capitals) comes under the same index.
