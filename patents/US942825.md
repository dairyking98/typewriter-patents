## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typewriters print one character at a time; this one aims at much faster writing by printing whole words from one key.

**Solution:** A type cylinder carrying type-faced plungers, rotated and raised by keys, with a spring-driven hammer released by escapement so single keys can print characters or entire words.

**How it works:** The type cylinder or segments carry plungers or pins and can move axially and rotate. Keys rotate the cylinder to a character and shift keys raise or lower it to another row. A hammer strikes the plunger; the spring is wound by carriage return and released through an escapement operated by the character keys. For word keys, the cylinder is turned to the first letter of a word, the hammer fires, is drawn back, the cylinder rises one row and it fires again, until the word is complete. Letters of each word lie in vertical rows on the type wheel, which here has six rows, so six-letter words are the longest. Word-shift keys give two short words per key.

**Numbers:** six horizontal rows of type; words up to six letters; 14 sheets of drawings
