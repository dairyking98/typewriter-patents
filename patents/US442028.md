## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** Typewriter carriages need rigid, strong joints and good paper guidance around the platen.

**Solution:** A carriage frame whose front rail and ends are one bent piece, with a rear rod, a spring-pressed curved paper-guide plate with fingers under the platen, and long springy front fingers that follow the roll contour.

**How it works:** The front rail and end sections are one bent piece of metal; the back rail is a straight rod sprung into sockets, with a second rod ahead of it screwed to the ends against spreading. Standards on the rear rail ride the stationary rear rail. The second rod pivots a curved plate with fingers following the lower rear platen contour, held up by coiled springs. Front fingers on the front rail rise and bend back to follow the roll with a longer springy bearing. Paper entering at the rear is thus constrained around the roll, with the fingers kept clear of the type impact line.
