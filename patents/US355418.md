## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Inking devices for typewriters were complicated in how they received and presented the ink-bearing material to the type.

**Solution:** A small disk of inked paper or cloth is carried on a bracket and turned by a rack on the moving platen frame, so each impression finds a fresh spot.

**How it works:** A bracket on the machine top carries a hollow shaft with a pinion; a tapered stud on a removable disk holds an inking pad of saturated paper or cloth against the shaft by friction. A rack bar on the platen carriage frame meshes with the pinion, so carriage travel rotates the inking disk. The type strikes through an aperture in the bracket against the inked pad, then onto the paper. The bracket and rack are adjustable so type can hit nearer the disk centre or edge, using the disk longer.
