## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Carriage escapement with a fixed and a movable pawl needed separate springs for the pawl's rotation and its axial displacement, and shifted roughly.

**Solution:** A single spring wound around the pivot pin of the rotatable pawl both pulls it against a fixed stop and presses it toward the ratchet wheel.

**How it works:** A base plate swings from the universal rail when a key is pressed, carrying a fixed tooth and a second, double-armed pawl lever mounted on a pin. During the stroke the fixed tooth meets the back of a ratchet tooth while the lever's tooth swings out of the wheel; the lever stays pressed on an eccentric pin by its spring. On return the lever tooth is pushed outward against the spring over the next tooth, then drops in, giving smooth stepping at any speed. Pressing a slide strikes a nut joining the levers and lowers the pawl out of the teeth so the carriage can be pulled right to left. When the carriage is returned left to right the lever acts as a sliding pawl and rides over the teeth against its spring.

**Features:** [ESC:escape-wheel](../features/esc-escape-wheel.md), [ESC:carriage-release](../features/esc-carriage-release.md), [FRM:few-parts](../features/frm-few-parts.md)

**Parts:** escapement wheel; base plate; fixed tooth; pawl lever; coil spring; eccentric pin; angle lever; release slide; universal rail; return spring

**Improves on:**

- escapements with a fixed and a movable pawl: one spring serves both rotation and axial pressure of the movable pawl
