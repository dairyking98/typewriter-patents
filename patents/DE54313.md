## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** Hall's typewriter moves the whole printing mechanism along the line, which makes parts costly and hard to interchange, and its short lever length gives weak impressions needed for carbon copies.

**Solution:** Keep the printing mechanism fixed lengthwise and step the paper carriage instead, and suspend the type plate on four equal parallel links so a long lever gives a stronger impression.

**How it works:** A flexible type form with hard or metal types on rubber pads hangs under plate A and is moved in every direction by a pointer rigidly joined to it; the pointer end is set into a cutout of the indicator plate for the wanted character. Moving the pointer carries the chosen type across an ink pad in a support frame D, ending over a hole in pad and base plate. Pressing the pointer then pushes the type through the hole to print on the paper below. Plate A hangs from frame D by four short links of equal length so it lifts parallel off the ink pad, giving a lever length equal to the link length, with springs keeping it against the pad and an adjustable stop limiting travel. The printing frame turns in bearings without longitudinal travel while a rack on the paper carriage is driven by a pawl and held by a holding pawl.
