## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** In known type-wheel setting devices the member driven by the key levers oscillated in a plane perpendicular to the key lever motion, which pushed the levers sideways and jammed their bearings.

**Solution:** Replace the oscillating member with slides that move in the same plane as the key levers, with each key's stop arm meeting a rack that limits the type wheel's rotation, and the same key stroke then carrying the wheel to the paper roller.

**How it works:** The type wheel sits on a drive wheel with pins, on a vertical axis carried by a rail that slides front to back; the wheel can be lifted by a rod and arm to bring one of its type rows opposite the print point. A lever pivoted on the rail carries a toothed segment meshing with the drive wheel; its two arms are connected by links and rods to two ball-guided slides, one for each half of the keyboard. A depressed key's stop arm enters a slot in its slide, hits one of rollers of graduated diameter and pushes the slide back, rotating the wheel, until the arm meets a rack on the drive wheel that blocks further rotation at the right character. The arm lengths of the key levers, grouped in threes, set how far the rack can travel. Continued motion moves the rail backwards, with the lever arms riding on inclined surfaces, so the wheel swings to the paper roller and prints; a spring returns the rail and springs return the keys.
