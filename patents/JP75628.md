## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** In a typewriter where the printing mechanism carriage travels freely over a type magazine, the carriage can shift during printing so the type cannot return to its slot in the magazine, damaging types and magazine.

**Solution:** A rack-driven gear with a toothed disc on the carriage is locked by a notched stop disc that the printing action advances one pitch, so the carriage is held in place for the duration of each print stroke.

**How it works:** The carriage carries a gear meshing with a rack fixed relative to the magazine, and fixed to it a disc with forward-pointing teeth, one pitch per type column. A notched stop disc sits at right angles to it. Pressing the key lever pushes the arm of the push-up lever against a pawl lever, engaging the pawl with the stop disc; as the key returns, a spring pulls the pawl and turns the stop disc one notch, and a brake pawl detents it. While the solid part between notches passes between two teeth of the toothed disc, the disc and gear cannot turn, so the carriage is locked until the type has returned to the magazine.
