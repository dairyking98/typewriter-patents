## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Cheaper, simpler keyboard typewriter with fewer type carriers.

**Solution:** A loose type-wheel rotated by gearing from a rock-shaft whose angle is set by key levers of different leverage, with a second wheel carrying common words.

**How it works:** Key levers at the front connect to a rock-shaft so that equal key depression rotates the shaft by different amounts. Intermediate gears turn a type-wheel loosely mounted on a shaft to bring the chosen character into position. A tripping mechanism then operates an impression bar. A second independent wheel carries about 26 common words (the, and, can, have, for). Carriage is moved by a spacer wheel and ribbon shaft; detachable inking rollers are used.

**Features:**

- **Selection:** [Full keyboard, a key per character](../features/sel-keyboard-full.md), [Keys drive the element by racks or gears](../features/sel-differential-drive.md)
- **Type element:** [Type wheel (types on the rim)](../features/elem-wheel-rim.md)
- **Impression:** [Separate print key or lever](../features/imp-print-lever.md)
- **Inking:** [Ink roller](../features/ink-roller.md)
- **Spacing and escapement:** [Prints and spaces in one stroke](../features/esc-print-and-space.md)
- **Frame and body:** [Few parts, low cost](../features/frm-few-parts.md)

**Parts:** type-wheel; rock-shaft; key lever; intermediate gear; impression bar; tripping mechanism; word wheel; spacer wheel; ribbon shaft; inking roller; carriage frame; bed plate

**Numbers:** 26 words, more or less; double set of key levers; 7 sheets of drawings
