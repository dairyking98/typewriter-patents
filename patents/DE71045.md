## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** Switching the platen between front and rear positions in a double-keyboard typebar machine needed two key levers (one to lock, one to release temporarily), which is cumbersome and confusing.

**Solution:** A single key lever connected to the changeover rail, with a spring and a hand-raised lever that moves their points of attack to the other side of the shaft, serves for both permanent and temporary shift.

**How it works:** The platen on the roller carriage is coupled by a claw on a shaft arm to a changeover rail turned by two arms on a frame shaft. An extension on that shaft carries a pivoted lever linked by a rod to the key lever, and a second lever carrying a spring to the frame, each limited by slots and stop pins. At rest the platen is forward; pressing the key pulls the rail to the rear while stretching the spring, and the spring returns it on release. Raising the handle on the second lever flips both the rod's and spring's points of attack to the other side of the shaft, so the spring now holds the rail rearward and pressing the key briefly returns it forward.
