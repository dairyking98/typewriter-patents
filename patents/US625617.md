## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Improve the inventor's earlier type-wheel machine (US593789) to make it cheaper, more compact and simpler, with no separate wheel lock, a spring-released hammer, simple paper and ribbon feeds, and easy correction of wear in the key-lever pivots.

**Solution:** Two groups of keys turn the type wheel either way through two racks on one pinion, a flier arm stops the wheel against the pressed key so the geared train wedges itself rigid, and a universal bar then trips a spring-cocked hammer to print and step the carriage.

**How it works:** Each key lever has a cam end that pushes one of two light levers, which turn a vertical shaft and a small rack that drives a pinion; both racks stay meshed with the pinion, so one moves forward and the other back. The pinion turns the long pinion carrying a hollow rubber three-row type wheel, and a loose flier arm turning the opposite way meets the pressed key and stops the train, which then wedges and holds the wheel without a separate lock. The key also raises a universal bar and a weighted frame, which compresses a spring against a hammer arm and trips its hook; the hammer drives the paper, ribbon and type together at the point of writing. Capital and figure keys lift the wheel on its spindle by a plate, with locking versions that hold it up until a release key is pressed. The same universal frame cocks a lever and ratchet dog so the carriage steps on key release under a spring drum with a bell, and the space key skips the hammer. The ribbon winds by dogs on the wheel-drive levers, with a button to reverse direction, so only type keys feed it.
