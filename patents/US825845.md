## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Learners of typing or piano find keys hard to tell apart without looking.

**Solution:** Give each key a recess with a spring whose projecting end is formed differently, and vary the spring stiffness by key group, so keys are recognised by touch.

**How it works:** Each key has a recess holding a flat spring fixed at one end. The free end projects above the key surface and is shaped as a point, toothed wheel, knob, double point, curve, velvet pad, metal ball, roughened end, knife edge, small cylinder, wooden cube or concave face. The finger must press the spring down before the key, feeling its shape. Spring strength differs by group so groups are felt by resistance, and recesses and bevelled edges differ in shape.

**Features:**

- **Selection:** [Full keyboard, a key per character](../features/sel-keyboard-full.md)

**Parts:** key; recess; flat spring; projecting end

**Numbers:** 12 keys per group

**Improves on:**

- keyboards recognised only by sight: adds touch recognition
