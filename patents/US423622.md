## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Murray wants a fast typewriter with a keyboard where a key press stops a continuously rotating type-wheel at the right type, and the wheel's momentum does the printing without jarring the machine or needing to restart the wheel.

**Solution:** A treadle-driven horizontal type-wheel spins at speed, a pressed key raises a stop pin into the path of a lug on the wheel, and the sudden stop makes a loosely mounted upper disk drive the matching pivoted type down through a window onto the paper.

**How it works:** A treadle, flywheel and belts spin a horizontal two-disk type-wheel at 10 to 30 revolutions per second, through a long coil spring that cushions stops and gives extra push on restart. The lower disk carries pivoted type bars in two rows and has downward lugs; the upper disk sits loosely on the shaft, tied to the lower by a spring, and its openings hold the top ends of the type bars. Pressing a key lifts a stop pin that the lug of the matching type hits, stopping the lower disk; the upper disk carries on, forcing the type down, and a pivoted lever helps push the top of the right type bar through the aperture to print on the paper below. The key also trips one of two escapements (narrow- and wide-letter sets) driven by a weighted cord that draw the paper carriage along by an endless belt by one letter space. A knee lever shifts a board so the inner row of type (capitals) comes over the window; a treadle returns the carriage and turns the paper roll for the line space.
