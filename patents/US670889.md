## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Full-keyboard typewriters are tall because their key banks are stepped one above another, and the last impression is often hidden; the inventor wants a compact portable machine with all writing visible.

**Solution:** A portable type-wheel machine with a divided-key keyboard in which grouped keys travel together, and a reciprocating slide that carries the wheel, turns it by a pinion until a rack hits the pressed key, and then advances to print.

**How it works:** The type wheel (three rows: small letters, capitals, figures) sits on a spindle on a rectangular slide plate that moves toward the platen at right angles to it. Pressing a key (two banks, each split into groups of three, where pressing a lower finger piece carries the shorter keys of its group down with it) pulls pivoted beams and links that push the slide forward and at the same time swing a segment gear. The segment gear turns a pinion and the wheel until a stop rack on the slide touches the pressed key's vertical part. The rack then locks the pinion, holding the wheel against lost motion, and the slide continues forward until the type strikes the paper. A feed pawl on the slide engages a feed rack on the platen carriage and spaces it one letter per stroke. A separate pair of levers lifts the wheel on its spindle to select the case row. Two spring-armed ink rollers touch the wheel and springs return everything.
