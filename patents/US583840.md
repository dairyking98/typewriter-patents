## Summary

**Kind:** type-element machine · **Subject (patent classes):** Typewriters or selective printing mechanisms characterised by the mounting, arrangement or disposition of the types or dies with types or dies mounted on carriers rotatable for selection the plane of the type or die face being parallel to the axis of rotation, e.g. with type on the periphery of cylindrical carriers

**In the patent’s own words:** My invention relates to certain new and useful improvements in type-writing machines of that class in which the type and other characters are displayed upon a revoluble wheel which is caused to rotate and move longitudinally through the action of levers constituting the keyboard.

**How it works (reading):** Five improvements to the Blickensderfer type-wheel machine: sector hooks that lock one side so the wheel aligns accurately before it descends, a feed that stores a little power per keystroke instead of a wound spring, a one-hand carriage release, an adjustable pivoted ink roller, and a slotted spring-plate lock for the wheel.

## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** On the Blickensderfer wheel machine of US472692 the wheel could be thrown past its character by lost motion, the carriage spring had to be wound for a whole line and strained the dogs, the carriage was awkward to free, the ink roller was hard to adjust, and the wheel was not easily removable.

**Solution:** Five improvements to the Blickensderfer type-wheel machine: sector hooks that lock one side so the wheel aligns accurately before it descends, a feed that stores a little power per keystroke instead of a wound spring, a one-hand carriage release, an adjustable pivoted ink roller, and a slotted spring-plate lock for the wheel.

**How it works:** Key levers depress bails that move sectors on each side of the machine, rotating the type wheel through segmental racks to the chosen character. A hook on each sector engages the rack extension on the opposite side, so the first part of a stroke takes up lost motion, and the wheel cannot be thrown ahead until the second bail has engaged. Further depression meshes an intermediate sector with a rack on a pawl that locks the wheel's ratchet, and only then does the wheel support carry the wheel down against the paper. A spacing lever or the moving wheel spoke draws a feed plate forward against a coil spring; on release the spring returns it and a pivoted feed dog steps the paper carriage one rack notch while a detent locks it. Pressing a wire bail on the carriage pushes the plate over and frees the dogs from the rack for hand travel. A pivoted ink-roller frame on a slotted plate can be raised or lowered by a binding screw, and the wheel hub, slit on one side, is held on a grooved spindle by a thin bifurcated spring locking plate that turns to release.
