## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A typebar must be guided accurately onto the platen just before it prints, but the typebar's centering member can bind in the guide recess and fail to release, so the type does not return cleanly.

**Solution:** Overlapping L-shaped leaf springs lie across the centering recess so the type's centering tip pushes them aside and they kick it back toward rest.

**How it works:** A plate fixed to the typebar-supporting frame has a neck with a hole (the centering recess), and a window lets the operator see the scale that positions the flanges. Two L-shaped leaf springs are screwed to the plate with their free ends overlapping across the hole. As the typebar swings in, its centering member passes through the hole and spreads the spring ends, loading them. After printing the stored tension throws the type away from the centering member and starts its return. The springs are weak enough not to stop full contact with the platen.
