## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A simple, cheap printing device is wanted that holds types firmly in rows and can serve both as a stamp-like printer and as a one-character-at-a-time typewriter.

**Solution:** Insert types into square holes in elastic rubber bands stretched on a removable frame, supported by sliding bars between holes, so pressing a type's back prints it accurately.

**How it works:** Strongly elastic rubber bands have square-tube holes into which types are inserted and held firmly. The bands, hooked by end holes onto pegs of a square frame, are stretched tight; sliding support bars pass under the bands between the type holes. The frame sits on a printing box above a sliding printing plate that a pawl on a push rod advances one rack tooth at a time. For printing, the frame is placed on a drawer, ink is rolled over the backs of the types, the frame is returned to the box, paper laid on the plate and the roller rolled again. For typewriter use, each type's back is pressed individually and the push rod pushed after each to advance the paper. The band's elasticity presses only the pressed type down vertically.
