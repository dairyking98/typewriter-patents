## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** The ball-bearing carriage support of an earlier Shimer application had grooved rails side by side, and held its rigidity by spring pressure.

**Solution:** Set the grooved rails one above the other (edgewise) so the machine's own weight gives the ball bearings their rigidity.

**How it works:** The paper-roll frame, which forms the carriage, is pivoted to a reciprocating traverse rail so it can be lifted to inspect writing. The traverse rail has grooves in its upper and lower edges. One ball runs between it and a fixed grooved upper guide rail, and two balls near the ends run between it and a lower grooved guide rail, held in place by pins. The lower rail rests on two vertical rods bearing on lugs of side bases that carry the machine on cushioned feet, so the weight of the machine loads the bearings. The lower rail is secured by set-screws in elongated holes for adjustment.

**Parts:** traverse rail; upper guide rail; lower guide rail; ball; vertical rod; side base; cushioned foot; paper-roll frame; pin

**Numbers:** one ball in upper rail, two balls in lower rail
