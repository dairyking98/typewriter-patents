## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Carriages and beds of light, foldable visible typebar typewriters needed many parts and a cheap, strong way to run on ball bearings.

**Solution:** Form the carriage and its bed from single pieces of pressed sheet metal whose pressed ribs together make ball-bearing raceways, with an integral finger on the carriage helping to work the line-space lever.

**How it works:** The bed is one sheet-metal piece with upturned flanges and pressed lengthwise ribs that form two guideways. Three balls, joined by a wire so they move in unison, lie in the guideways. The carriage base, also sheet metal with flanges and integral end walls, rests on the balls and has its own pressed ribs opposite those of the bed. A bracket and a screw on the bed flanges keep the carriage from lifting. One end wall carries arms holding a rod that pivots the line-space pawl lever, and an integral upward finger gives a fixed grip against which the lever is squeezed.

**Features:** [FRM:frame-construction](../features/frm-frame-construction.md), [FRM:few-parts](../features/frm-few-parts.md), [FRM:compact-portable](../features/frm-compact-portable.md), [PAP:line-space](../features/pap-line-space.md), [FRM:writing-visible](../features/frm-writing-visible.md), [ELEM:typebar](../features/elem-typebar.md)

**Parts:** sheet-metal carriage bed; sheet-metal carriage; pressed rib; ball; ball-joining wire; bracket; carriage end wall; finger portion; line-space lever; pivot rod; platen

**Numbers:** three balls (two in one guideway, one in the other)

**Improves on:**

- multi-part carriage frames: carriage and bed are each one pressed sheet-metal piece, cheaper and lighter
