## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Automatic ribbon reversal needed a reliable trigger at the end of the ribbon and a way to switch feed from one spool to the other.

**Solution:** A spring finger on each spool is held in by the wound ribbon and springs out when the ribbon runs off, setting a connection so the carriage escapement arm itself throws a shifter that swaps which spool's feed pawl is engaged.

**How it works:** Each ribbon spool has a ratchet and a pawl, with the pawls on swinging arms that are driven by the escapement dog arm as the carriage steps. A shifter plate carries both pawl arms; sliding it one way engages one spool's pawl and frees the other so that spool runs free. When the ribbon is almost fully unwound, a spring finger on the spool, no longer held in by the ribbon, swings out and strikes a lever, which brings a spring-pressed plunger into the path of the escapement arm and unlocks the shifter lever. The escapement arm then pushes the plunger and swings the bell-crank lever to slide the shifter, putting the empty spool's pawl into action and releasing the other. A retaining spring with V-bends keeps the shifter in position, and in a variant a hump on the ribbon end replaces the finger.
