## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** A platen shift needs a simple, space-saving lock holding the paper carriage in its lower position, suited to portable typewriters.

**Solution:** A rearward extension of one shift key lever acts as a stop against a screw on the downward arm of the shift-frame lever, and play in the drive plus lever ratio lets it clear before the lifting begins.

**How it works:** The carriage rides on a lifting frame guided by parallel articulated levers, with a counterbalance spring. Both shift keys sit on a common shaft; the left key lever is one stamped piece with a rearward extension ending in a bent end that rests against an adjustable stop screw on the upper lever's arm, locking the frame down. When a shift key is pressed, the shaft turns and the bent end travels far more than the link point, so it clears the stop screw before the frame moves, helped by slight play in the link hook. A spring-returned lock button with a recess engages a frame pin to hold the shift position and is freed by pressing a shift key.
