## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Taking down speech such as lectures in shorthand quickly, without writing out full characters.

**Solution:** Record only the vowels, by stamping with fingertip-worn vowel stamps on ruled paper whose columns are labelled with consonants.

**How it works:** A sloped-lid box carries ruled paper wound between two hand-cranked rollers, exposed through two slots in the lid. Vertical lines carry consonants (printed, or marked by coloured threads wound on small turning markers). The operator wears bell-shaped vowel stamps, with raised vowel signs, on fingertips and presses them into the column of the consonant; carbon paper transfers the mark to the thin sheet beneath (or needle points emboss it directly). One word is written per row and the paper is wound on by the handle.

## Description (English translation of the Japanese text, opening)

> Specification. Shorthand device. This invention relates to a shorthand device in which vowel stamps, worn on the fingertips and pressed down, work together with ruled paper on which the consonants are printed, and with carbon (transfer) paper and the like. Its object is, in taking down any speech, such as lectures and addresses, to spell out the words by marking only the vowels on the paper. The attached drawings show a mechanism that can achieve this object. Fig. 1 is a perspective view of the whole device, with a part cut open to show the inside; Fig. 2 is a detail of a consonant marker; Fig. 3 is a detail of the vowel stamps. The same mark denotes the same part in all the figures. The base of the device is a box (イ). It may be square, round or of any suitable shape; its top edge slopes down towards the front, so that the lid (ロ) forms an inclined face. Near the upper and lower edges of the lid two parallel transverse slots (ヌ)(ル) are cut a suitable distance apart.
