## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Ordinary two-hand keyboards make fingers travel over many keys, and two-group keyboards where each key of one group combines with each key of another yield only the product of the key counts (25 or 36) where about 90 are needed.

**Solution:** Make the switching keys of one group into several keys (rows of two or three) on a shared key lever, each giving a different switching action, so five finger positions give 90 or more characters without moving the hands.

**How it works:** The left-hand group has six rows of three keys, each row on a key lever; two keys of a row ride loosely on it, the third is fixed. Pressing any key of a row tilts its lever, which pushes a shift rod sideways against a spring, and which key in the row was pressed sets a different drum switching. The shift rods cross crossbars carrying the keystroke keys of the other (right-hand) group, which are struck to trigger the combined effect. The fingers stay in place and the keys in a row are reached by pushing forward or back.

**Features:** [SEL:keyboard-few-keys](../features/sel-keyboard-few-keys.md), [SEL:dummy-keyboard](../features/sel-dummy-keyboard.md)

**Parts:** key; key lever; shift rod; crossbar; spring; keystroke key; switching drum

**Numbers:** 5 or 6 keys per group give 25 or 36 characters; about 90 characters needed; 6 rows of 3 keys in the switch group; more than 90 effects with 5 or 6 stroke keys

**Improves on:**

- two-group keyboards with product of key counts: each switch key is split into several keys, multiplying the combinations
