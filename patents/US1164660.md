## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Shifting the platen or type support by a plain key is jarring, noisy, and can rebound.

**Solution:** A rocker-armed key linkage and cam slot give a variable-leverage shift that starts slowly, accelerates, then retards and locks without rebound.

**How it works:** Shift keys are levers whose rocker ends bear on rocker arms fixed to a rock shaft. A rock arm on the shaft with a roller runs in a cam slot of the shift frame, swinging the frame (carrying the platen carriage) vertically. Leverage is high at first and falls as the key goes down. A counterbalance spring supports the frame weight. Either shift key operates it.

**Features:** [SHIFT:platen](../features/shift-platen.md), [FRM:few-parts](../features/frm-few-parts.md), [ELEM:typebar](../features/elem-typebar.md)

**Parts:** shift key; rocker arm; rock shaft; rock arm; cam slot; shift frame; counterbalance spring

**Improves on:**

- ordinary shift mechanisms: variable motion with positive hold against rebound and fewer parts
