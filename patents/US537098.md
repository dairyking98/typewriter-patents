## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A single full-space carriage feed per keystroke gives a large jolt, noise, momentum and wear, and can blur the print on fast writing.

**Solution:** Split each letter-space into two half-steps using a duplex rack with staggered teeth and a double-sided rocking dog, one half-step on key depression and one on release.

**How it works:** A slotted rack bar fixed to the carriage has two rows of ratchet teeth, one row on each side of the slot, with the teeth of one row offset midway between those of the other. A twin-sided dog is pivoted in a holder that rocks on two conical-pointed adjusting screws in a bracket on the type ring. Pressing a key lifts the universal bar and rocker arm, swinging the dog so one side leaves the rear row and the other enters the forward row, and the spring-driven carriage advances half a space until a tooth hits the dog. Releasing the key lets a spring return the dog and the carriage takes the second half-step. Variants cut the teeth on the outer edges of a solid bar with a forked dog, use one-third and two-thirds splits, or make one row of teeth shorter so the carriage has come to rest before the type reaches the platen.
