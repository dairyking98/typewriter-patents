## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Stock-ticker style synchronous printers print only on a paper tape, and their type wheels can miss pulses through inertia; the inventor wants a page printer for telegraph message blanks.

**Solution:** A start-stop synchronous telegraph page printer with a continuously motor-driven, magnet-stepped two-row type wheel above a horizontal paper carriage, struck from below by a solenoid hammer.

**How it works:** A motor drives the type-wheel shaft through a friction clutch, and a polarised-magnet escapement anchor on the shaft's escape wheel lets it step in time with line current reversals from a remote sunflower transmitter. A spiral-thread stop arm synchronises it by halting the wheel at a blank. When the transmitter holds on a key's segment, a separator relay fires a solenoid whose plunger hammer drives the paper (above a rubber strip) up against the type face; a commutator on the shaft routes the pulse instead to a shift magnet, latch magnet, carriage-return magnet or line-feed magnet. The wheel has two rows and slides along its keyed shaft, moved by a yoke and held by a magnet latch, to change case. The carriage is spaced by an ordinary typewriter escapement and friction-driven rack gear, with beveled gears reversed for return; paper is fed rearward by a ratchet and pusher, and a spring between shaft and wheel with keyway play prevents missed steps.
