## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** The inventor wants a compact, efficient, inexpensive typewriter with a type-wheel that is positioned to bring the right type into the printing plane, avoiding the many typebars and long swings of other designs.

**Solution:** A fan-shaped type-wheel of springy type-bearing tongues swings sideways between stepped gages to pick the tongue, slides vertically to pick the row and case, and a hammer strikes the selected tongue's back against a ribbon and platen.

**How it works:** The type-wheel is a set of radial type-bearing tongues held in segmental cross-heads on a spindle. Each key lever sets a vertical gage-bar against a fixed stepped gage, so that when a driving lever turns the wheel sideways, the stepped gage stops it with the chosen tongue in the printing plane. A key shoulder also rocks a tilting trip that raises or lowers the wheel by one row interval (two rows per case); two case-key levers raise the wheel more to reach the capitals field or the figures field, and can be locked. Just before the swing is checked, the driving lever stud trips a rock-shaft that throws a hammer against the back of the tongue, printing through a ribbon onto a roller platen. A feed spring and rack carriage is released by a pawl pair: the stop pawl is tripped as the hammer strikes, and the carriage steps one tooth when the key is released while the wheel returns to center.
