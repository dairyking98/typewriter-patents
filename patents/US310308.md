## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Typewriters are complicated and costly; Petit wants a very simple machine of few parts.

**Solution:** A flexible rubber-like type plate on a sliding carriage is shifted by a hand arm and pin over a chart plate of apertures to place the chosen type over a hole, and one pressing of the pin prints and lets the carriage advance a space.

**How it works:** The carriage slides on a rod and is pulled rightward by a spring. A flexible type plate rests on its bottom plate and is attached to a slotted plate carrying an arm with a spring-loaded pin. A fixed apertured plate shows the letters laid out like the type, so moving the pin over a letter slides the type plate until the matching type is above the hole in the carriage bottom. Pressing the pin swings a hinged plate that presses down a pair of levers and a cross-bar with a finger, which pushes that type through the hole onto the paper, with an ink pad at the rear. The cross-bar also carries two teeth: while it is down one tooth locks in a rack, and as it rises the second, spring-drawn tooth takes the rack and lets the carriage move one pitch. To return, the rack is rocked up by a handle and the carriage pushed back.
