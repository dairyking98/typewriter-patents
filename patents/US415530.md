## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** A hammer-struck double-case typebar machine needs a way to place lower or upper case type at the roller, strike it, and space the carriage, with simple adjustment of bar travel.

**Solution:** Typebars carry upper and lower case type and are stopped at one of two heights by a plate that upper-case keys pull down, while a hammer struck by tilting rock-bars gives the impression and a key-operated escapement frame spaces the carriage.

**How it works:** Pressing a key throws the typebar forward through a rod; a stop plate behind the bars halts it with the lower-case type at the roller. Upper-case keys pull the plate down, so the bar travels farther and the upper-case type reaches the roller, and they also turn a rock-bar so the key levers may travel further before hitting it. The hammer, driven through a lever frame, then strikes the bar. Each key or the space bar also moves an escapement frame that operates a fixed and a loose dog on the carriage rack. A separate key lowers both dogs out of the rack for free carriage movement, and a notched adjustable ring sets the rest position of alternate bars. A movable ink pad is raised against the type by its own key, and a rock-shaft with a handle returns the carriage.
