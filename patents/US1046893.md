## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** A ribbon vibrator for a two-stripe ribbon must reach different heights (upper stripe, lower stripe, or none for mimeograph stencil) and must work the same in either platen-shift case, without much added mechanism.

**Solution:** Two jointed levers share a constant total key-stroke motion, and a movable stop pinched between them decides how that motion is split, so the vibrator rises to a chosen height or not at all.

**How it works:** A link from the universal bar drives a floating lever pivoted on a second lever that is itself pivoted to the frame. A spring holds the second lever up, so at first the whole stroke swings it down. If a stop is placed between the levers, the second lever is arrested part way, its pivot becomes fixed, and the rest of the stroke lifts the floating lever, which carries the ribbon vibrator, until a pin on the vibrator stem hits the stop and is pinched. Moving a slide bar with a milled knob brings a lower stop, an upper stop, or none into play, giving the upper stripe, lower stripe, or no ribbon motion for stencil work. The stops ride with the shifting platen frame so the action is the same in both cases. A second form for the Monarch, where the type segment shifts, uses the same principle with three stepped stops.
