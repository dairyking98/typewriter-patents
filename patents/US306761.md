## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Earlier typewriters are complicated, slow to learn and costly; Lambert wants a simple, cheap, wide-range machine with visible writing.

**Solution:** One disc-shaped multiple key on a ball joint carries all characters and, pressed at a character, swings a matching type ring on its underside to a common stop and then prints.

**How it works:** A disc tablet carrying the characters is mounted on a ball at the end of a pivoted spring-balanced arm and kept from rotating in its plane. A downward arm below carries a printing ring whose characters lie on the same meridians as those on the key. Pressing the key at a character tips it like a hemisphere about the ball centre, swinging that character on the ring to the fixed stop point. Further pressing swings the arm against its spring and drives the character down onto the paper beneath, inked by a pad or ribbon. A paper cylinder and carriage spaces the sheet, with a device for variable spacing by character width.

**Features:** [Press the pointer to print](../features/sel-pointer-press-prints.md), [Type ring](../features/elem-ring.md), [Type sphere](../features/elem-sphere.md), [Interchangeable element](../features/elem-interchangeable.md), [Element struck to the platen](../features/imp-element-to-platen.md), [Hand presses the type through](../features/imp-press-through.md), [Ink pad](../features/ink-pad.md), [Ink ribbon](../features/ink-ribbon.md), [Proportional spacing](../features/esc-variable-spacing.md), [Roller platen](../features/pap-platen-roller.md), [Visible writing](../features/frm-writing-visible.md), [Few parts, low cost](../features/frm-few-parts.md)

**Parts:** multiple key disc; ball and socket; vibrating arm; printing ring; slide pin and rider; ink pad; paper cylinder; carriage; spring or counterweight
