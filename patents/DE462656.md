## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** On typewriters with multi-colour ribbons the colour zones are barely taller than the type, so any error in the ribbon lift puts the neighbouring colour under the type.

**Solution:** A ribbon-fork rocker arm mounted on bearings at both ends, with two drive arms of different length and adjustable stops, lets a sliding driver select each colour zone's lift precisely.

**How it works:** Each key stroke swings the universal rail and a drive lever, which turns a driver on an axis. A knob slides the driver sideways so a slot in it engages either a pin on the long arm or a pin on the short arm of the rocker arm, giving a small or large ribbon lift (black or red zone). A middle knob position engages neither pin, so the ribbon does not move (for wax stencils). An adjustable stop fixes the driver's end position and a second one fixes the rocker arm's rest position; twisting the two widely spaced arms of the rocker arm tunes each zone separately. The ribbon fork follows the carriage when it is raised for capitals.
