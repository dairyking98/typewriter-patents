## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** A typebar machine needs a way to shift between lower and upper case without moving the carriage or platen, using few parts.

**Solution:** Each typebar carries a pivoted type head with two faces that swings out by centrifugal force to print the upper-case face, unless a trigger locks it in the lower-case position.

**How it works:** A type head is pivoted on the typebar with more weight ahead of its pivot, so gravity returns it to the lower-case position. A trigger on the typebar normally locks the head there. When the case key is pressed, a movable trip is raised into the path of the trigger's trip-arm, so the trigger is knocked clear as the bar swings up. Centrifugal force then flips the head and the upper-case face prints. A fixed wire-semiring buffer on the carriage resets every trigger after each stroke, regardless of the movable trip's position.

**Features:** [ELEM:typebar](../features/elem-typebar.md), [SHIFT:rotary](../features/shift-rotary.md), [SHIFT:lock](../features/shift-lock.md), [IMP:element-to-platen](../features/imp-element-to-platen.md), [SEL:keyboard-full](../features/sel-keyboard-full.md)

**Parts:** typebar; pivoted type head; locking trigger; trip-arm; brake spring; movable trip; fixed buffer; case key; case-key lever
