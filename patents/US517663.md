## Summary

**Kind:** type-element machine · **Subject (patent classes):** Carriers stationary for impression, e.g. with the types or dies not moving relative to the carriers with the types or dies moving relative to the carriers or mounted on flexible carriers

**In the patent’s own words:** This invention relates to a typewriting ma chine of the class wherein is employed a rotating type wheel having type characters upon its lower side and which by its rotation is brought to the printing position-at which pointa printing lever or hammer is depressed impinging the type upon the paper.

**How it works (reading):** A rubber type wheel on a central shaft is rotated by a spring driven rack and pinion until a selector arm hits the depressed key lever, then a print lever hammers the selected type onto the paper.

## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Small cheap typewriters had a single print lever and were slow; the inventor wants a cheap machine with a key per character and rapid accurate printing.

**Solution:** A rubber type wheel on a central shaft is rotated by a spring driven rack and pinion until a selector arm hits the depressed key lever, then a print lever hammers the selected type onto the paper.

**How it works:** Key levers lie on a slotted disk and, when pressed, push an annulus down. Depressing a key tensions a spring that moves a rack, turning a pinion and the central shaft that carries the type wheel and a selector arm. The arm stops against the depressed key lever, so the matching letter lies over the perforation in a flat ring ink pad. The annulus also forces a bell crank print lever down, striking the wheel from above and pushing the type through the pad onto the paper. The paper roller sits in a carriage that advances per stroke and can be thrown out to show the writing. Upper and lower case, a spacing lever and a line lever are provided.
