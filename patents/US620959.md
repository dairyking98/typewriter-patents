## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Seeks speed, accuracy, uniform touch for all keys, manifolding, and simplicity, durability and cheapness in a machine with all types moved together.

**Solution:** A thin flexible slit type-plate with radial rows of type is oscillated sideways by driver levers whose travel depends on the key struck, and a hammer prints the positioned type.

**How it works:** Types sit in radial rows on the perimeter of a thin steel or brass plate, slit into radial slats about three-quarters of an inch long and set about an eighth of an inch from the platen. The plate is on a hub of a type-shaft with stop and driver arms and oscillates in a vertical plane. Two transverse driver levers cross the key levers; the nearer a key is to their fulcra the greater the driver-lever motion and hence the type-plate swing. Thrust plates ride in guide slots to engage the driver arms, with deliberate lost motion so keys can be struck before full return. Stop arms fix the plate position and a hammer then drives the slat, which can flex slightly, against the paper.
