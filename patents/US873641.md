## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Underwood's platen-shifting carriage needs a shift motion that is balanced, held firmly in both printing positions, and lets the carriage be removed or swapped for another width.

**Solution:** A split carriage whose platen section rides on a counterbalanced, knife-edge shift frame lifted by eccentric pins that reach dead center in each printing position.

**How it works:** The typebar action is conventional, with key levers driving typebars and a universal bar that releases an escapement wheel and pinion against the feed rack. The carriage has a front platen section and a rear escapement section, both pivoting on the same rear rod. The front section rolls on a shift rail that hangs loosely on a shift frame, so the rail stays vertical when the frame tilts. A spring with adjustable notches counterbalances the frame's weight. Depressing a shift key turns a toothed sector and pinion, which turns a shaft with eccentric pins that raise lifting bars and the frame. The pins reach dead center in the upper and lower positions, which locks the frame. A latch holds the carriage up for shift lock, and a second shift key releases it. The carriage rod has recesses and pins in slotted supports, secured by a latch, so a carriage of different width can be fitted.
