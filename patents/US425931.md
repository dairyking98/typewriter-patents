## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Typewriters hid the work, needed a ribbon, and required one key per character.

**Solution:** A visible-writing typebar machine whose typelevers carry a pivoting two-type head that rotates by rack and pinion to strike either type at a common print point, with types inked by swinging back against a felt pad and a spring-driven shaft-and-pinion escapement for the flat paper carriage.

**How it works:** Each upright typelever carries a type head with two prongs, each holding a type (capital and lower case). Pressing a key straight down swings the typelever forward, and a rack on a turning lever rotates the head by a pinion to bring the lower-case type to the common print center; drawing the key backward as it is pressed lifts a second elbow lever that lengthens the rack travel so the capital prong prints. When the key rises, the types swing back against a circular felt ink pad behind the lever row, which replaces the ribbon. A cross bar under the keys trips an escapement pawl so a perforated escapement wheel on a spring-wound driving shaft advances one tooth, and a sliding pinion on that shaft moves the rack on the flat paper carriage one space. A crank with a pin withdrawn from the wheel winds the spring and returns the carriage, and a spring clamp strip holds the paper.
