**Kind:** index machine · **Subject (patent classes):** Typewriters or selective printing mechanisms characterised by the mounting, arrangement or disposition of the types or dies with types or dies mounted on carriers rotatable for selection; Flexible thin piece, plate, or bar slides from a rotary typeface box

**In the English translation of the patent’s own words:** This invention relates to a Japanese-script typewriter in which a type storage box and a type index plate are set side by side along the arc of a quarter circle, a pivot arm is fitted to their lower end to form a sickle-shaped swinging piece, many such pieces are pivoted side by side on a main shaft, and each index plate covers the top of the neighbouring storage box.

## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Earlier Japanese typewriters stored all types in one heavy box, making them awkward and imprecise to operate.

**Solution:** Many light sickle-shaped swinging pieces, each a quarter-circle type box with its index plate, pivoted side by side on one shaft, each holding few types.

**How it works:** Each swinging piece carries a pressed arc-shaped storage box with type cells and an index plate that covers the neighbouring box, with a pivot arm at the lower end. Pieces are pivoted on a main shaft with spring-hooked arms so they swing easily. The operator swings a piece by its handle to bring the wanted type to the machine centre line, and slides the printing lever there. Pressing the lever works bent and curved levers that drive the push-out rod, pushing the type out against the paper cylinder to print; a stop plate keeps types from falling out.
