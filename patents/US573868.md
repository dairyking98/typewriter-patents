## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Fisher's book-page typebar machine needed better draw-rod tension adjustment, a low-friction carriage feed, an easily adjusted line-stop, a follower plate to flatten warped leaves and a letter-writing attachment.

**Solution:** Refinements to his track-mounted typebar machine: draw-rod tension nuts, a sheet-flattening follower plate, adjustable line stops and a platen frame attachment for loose letters.

**How it works:** The machine moves on tracks resting on the book page. Because the leaf may warp and bulge from the platen under it, a follower plate rests on the leaf and is pushed along by the frame. Draw-rods from key levers to typebars have tension devices, and the type-head shifting arm and ring clips are improved. The carriage feeds with pawls under reduced friction, with a line-stop that is quickly adjusted. A letter-writing attachment carries its own platen with line spacing and holding pawl.
