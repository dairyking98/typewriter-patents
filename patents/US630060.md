## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typewriters were noisy from type impact, escapement concussion, carriage-shift stops, carriage return stop, and key-lever stops.

**Solution:** A typebar machine made silent by air-cushioning (dash-pot and piston) the shifting carriage, return stop, escapement tooth and line-space rack, plus rolling-cam key rests and a fixed block platen.

**How it works:** Dash-pots with pistons and check valves are fitted to the ends of the double rocker-arm that shifts the carriage between three impression positions, so stops are reached through a compressed air cushion; a movable stop and valve collar select the cushion point for each position. A piston and dash-pot also stop the carriage on its return. The escapement uses a rocking block with a pivoted laterally-oscillating tooth and a second tooth, moved by a rocking cam tied to a piston, so the rack is caught without clack. Each key lever rests on a spring-held rocking cam that arrests it silently. The platen is a fixed block with one work surface over which paper is fed by small feed rolls, with a line-space lever, rack, pinion and ratchet turning the feed roll; a drop rack is tripped for carriage release.

**Parts:** typebar; key lever; rocking cam; universal bar; escapement tooth; escapement rack; dash-pot; piston; valve; double rocker-arm; movable stop; block platen; feed roll; line-space lever; ink ribbon; ribbon spool; adjusting screw

**Numbers:** three impression positions; three characters per type head

**Improves on:**

- Kidder's older machine: keeps its movable stop and shift but adds air cushions and a new fixed platen
- Feed-roll as platen: fixed block platen avoids uneven wear and permits smaller feed rolls
