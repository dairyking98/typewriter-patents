**Kind:** type-element machine · **Subject (patent classes):** Carriers moving for impression

**In the patent’s own words:** The object of this invention is to provide a machine for printing or impressing characters, the characters being selected by means 1o of key-actuated devices and the work of impressing the characters effected by mechanism which operates the type or impression devices automatically after the same have been selected by means of the keys.

**How it works (reading):** A continuously revolving horizontal wheel carries pivoted type arms around its rim; a key-controlled tappet sets a stationary detent in the path of one type's trip arm, which makes that type roll against a fixed platen with its face momentarily at rest.

## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** The inventor wants a typewriter in which a keyed selection is followed by automatic, forceful, clean impression, with the writing in plain view of the operator and enough force for many manifold copies.

**Solution:** A continuously revolving horizontal wheel carries pivoted type arms around its rim; a key-controlled tappet sets a stationary detent in the path of one type's trip arm, which makes that type roll against a fixed platen with its face momentarily at rest.

**How it works:** The type wheel is turned continuously by a belt. Each pivoted type has an impression arm with a character and a trip arm, and springs return it. Pressing a finger key drops a plunger into the path of a tappet that revolves with the wheel; the tappet swings inward, and through bevel gears, a rock shaft, a sliding rod and a lever it raises a detent just as the wanted type approaches. The detent stops the trip arm, so the type turns about its own pivot, opposite to the wheel's motion, like a gear on a rack, and its face presses the paper against the platen with no lateral slip. Cams on the wheel then reset the detent and tappet. A spring on the detent also turns a pinion one tooth to advance the rack-bar paper carriage one letter space. A locking plate lets several keys be struck in one revolution and print in the correct order. The platen is adjustable by a screw for paper thickness or copy count, and ink comes from a roller touching the passing type faces.
