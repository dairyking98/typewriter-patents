## Summary

**Kind:** a part or mechanism (any kind of machine) · **Subject (patent classes):** Means for centralising short lines characterised by denominational arrangements

**In Google’s translation of the patent’s own words:** The invention relates to a table writing device for writing business machines, in which the individual stops are pivoted in and out of the effective position, and is preferably suitable for the use of the smallest characters.

**How it works (reading):** Stops for adjacent character pitches carry mutually offset stop parts that are pivoted into and out of the effective position, so stops can sit at every column even for small pitches.

## Reading (short summary from the opening, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Table stops spaced at the usual 2.5 to 3 mm pitch cannot be made strong enough for narrow characters of about 1.5 mm, and a wider stop pitch loses positions.

**Solution:** Stops for adjacent character pitches carry mutually offset stop parts that are pivoted into and out of the effective position, so stops can sit at every column even for small pitches.

**Features:**

- **Spacing and escapement:** [Tabulator](../features/esc-tabulator.md), [Margin stops](../features/esc-margin-stop.md)

**Parts:** pivoting table stops; offset stop parts; guides between adjacent stops

**Numbers:** usual pitch 2.5 to 3 mm; narrow character width about 1.5 mm

> Description Translated from German Table writing device for business typing machines The The invention relates to a table writing device for writing business machines, in which the individual stops are pivoted in and out of the effective position, and is preferably suitable for the use of the smallest characters. In known table writing devices, spacings corresponding to the usual character pitches of 2.5 to 3 mm are provided for the positioning stops. These spacings generally allow a construction of the standing stops and of the guides provided between the adjacent stop parts of the adjacent adjusting stops, which hold the stops against impacts in the direction of movement of the carriage, which satisfies the strength requirements. Often, however, especially when using characters with a smaller width (e.g. 1.5 mm) or with greater loads, the spacing between the stop parts and guides of adjacent standing stops caused by the character division is not sufficient to give these parts the required strength.
