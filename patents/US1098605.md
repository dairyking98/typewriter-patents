## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Single-element typewriters need simpler, faster operating mechanisms with fewer parts.

**Solution:** Mount a semi-cylindrical type element on an axle with a helically twisted flat section so that pressing any key both rotates the element to its character and presses it onto the paper.

**How it works:** A semi-cylindrical member carries a multiplicity of types on its curved periphery and sits on the end of an axle. Mid-axle the axle is flattened and twisted into a helical surface, and its rear end is journaled in a ball or socket allowing universal movement. Each key has a stem that, when depressed, contacts the helical surface at a point specific to that key and turns the axle, bringing the matching character into position. The same downward stroke tips the axle toward the paper until the type touches it. A spring at the front lifts the axle back after printing, and key springs and pins return and limit the keys. The carriage frame slides along tracks on the base.

**Features:** [SEL:keyboard-full](../features/sel-keyboard-full.md), [SEL:differential-drive](../features/sel-differential-drive.md), [ELEM:cylinder-multirow](../features/elem-cylinder-multirow.md), [IMP:element-to-platen](../features/imp-element-to-platen.md), [IMP:press-through](../features/imp-press-through.md), [FRM:few-parts](../features/frm-few-parts.md)

**Parts:** semi-cylindrical type member; axle; twisted flattened section; ball journal; key stem; key; key spring; stop pin; return spring; carriage frame; track; base

**Improves on:**

- earlier single-element typewriters: fewer parts and quicker operation by combining selection and impression in one key stroke
