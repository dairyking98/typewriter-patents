## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Type-wheel typewriters were complicated and had many parts; the inventor wants a compact, simple, durable, low-cost and rapid wheel machine with fewer parts.

**Solution:** Every key lever slides a toothed setting bar by a key-specific amount to rotate a horizontal-faced type wheel via rack and pinion, then the same key stroke locks the wheel, steps the carriage by pawl and ratchet and fires a hammer from behind the paper.

**How it works:** Key levers (fulcrums set at three distances so touch is uniform) each carry an inclined strike block of a different width that enters a matching notch in a transverse toothed bar. The bar slides sideways by that distance and, through a rack and pinion, turns a vertical shaft carrying the type wheel to the right letter. After about two-thirds of the stroke the strike block clears the tooth and the rest of the stroke depresses a spring-supported rocking frame. The frame drives a toggle-link pawl that turns a ratchet and gear one tooth to move the carriage one space by a rack, swings a knife-edged detent into a crown wheel (34 spaces, 28 slotted) on the shaft to lock the wheel, and then hooks a hammer frame so that the hammer strikes the paper against an inked ribbon and the type. Two outer keys lift the wheel on its shaft by different amounts to bring the capital or figure row into the hammer line. On release a spring and inclined faces return the bar and wheel to centre. Also described are a space bar working the rocking frame alone, a two-spool ribbon with ratchets and a reversing knob, a bell tripped by a strike bar on the carriage, roller platen with line-space ratchet, and a recurved hammer support that pushes in for transport.
