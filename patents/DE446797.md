## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Line ratchet clutches in typewriter platens either can be operated from only one side, need two interacting springs that malfunction, or spring back immediately so the disengaged state needs continuous pressure.

**Solution:** A shift rod running through the hollow platen axle and projecting on both sides moves a cone that spreads rocker clutch levers, and the levers' own tension springs hold the cone in either position.

**How it works:** The line ratchet wheel turns freely inside the platen cylinder and has internal teeth. Three double-armed rocker levers in slots of a hub sleeve are pulled by tension springs so their front ends engage those teeth, coupling platen to ratchet wheel. Pushing the shift rod in moves a cone that spreads the levers' rear ends, withdrawing the front ends and freeing the platen for hand turning. A cylindrical land after the cone slope lets the levers hold the cone, so disengagement persists until the rod is pushed back from the other end, when the levers' sloped ends and springs return it.

**Features:** [Roller platen](../features/pap-platen-roller.md), [Line spacing](../features/pap-line-space.md), [Feed rollers and paper release](../features/pap-feed-rollers.md)

**Parts:** platen cylinder; line ratchet wheel; hub sleeve; rocker clutch lever; tension spring; hollow axle; shift rod; cone disc; cross pin; grip knob

**Numbers:** 3 clutch levers

**Improves on:**

- split shift rod line ratchet clutches: operable from both sides with one rod
- clutches with separate cone return spring: no second spring to balance; levers hold and return the cone
