## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** A typebar machine needing a simpler, more adjustable carriage drive, spacing, bell, margin stop, ribbon drive and a way to see where the print point falls on the paper.

**Solution:** A key-operated typebar machine whose carriage is pulled by a spring drum and metered one letter at a time by a reciprocating dog frame, with an adjustable bell and drum stop, and a main frame that swings up to expose the print point.

**How it works:** Each key has two vertical arms joined by a rod; the key swings its typebar in a circular hanger slot to the platen, while the rod depresses a pivoted spacing frame. The spacing frame moves a vertical sliding frame carrying two dogs that alternately catch the ratchet on the spring-driven drum, so the drum (geared to a rack that pushes the platen carrier) advances one tooth per key release. A further push on the centre finger lifts the frame and trips a lever that holds both dogs clear, letting the carriage run freely; the rear dog is flexible so the carriage can be pushed back by hand. A toothed wheel on the drum carries an adjustable trip that rings a bell near line end and doubles as a drum stop, and extra lugs fix other limits. A plunger at the platen end drives the line-space ratchet by a cam-slotted pawl, adjustable for one, two or more teeth. Two shift keys swing the vibratory key and typebar frame (or optionally the platen) through a cam and friction-wheel linkage, with a detent to hold the shifted position; a hinged part of the main frame swings up, with a pointer that marks the print point, and ribbon spools are advanced by pawls on a bar pressed down by every key.
