## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Earlier platen sideways-feed mechanisms for Japanese typewriters were complicated and easily broken, and reversing the feed direction was awkward.

**Solution:** A spring-returned crank worked by a lever carries a hand-reversible pawl that steps a gear one tooth per stroke, and a shaft links that gear to a pinion meshing the platen rack.

**How it works:** Pressing the sideways-feed working lever beside the print lever pushes down one end of a crank loose on a shaft, and the pawl at the other end slips back over the gear. When the lever rises, a spring pulls the crank back and the pawl turns the gear by one tooth. The gear is fixed to one end of a connecting shaft, whose other-end gear meshes with a rack on the platen, so the platen steps sideways one pitch per stroke. A knob on the pawl, sprung, switches it to the opposite side of the gear so each stroke turns the gear the other way and feeds the platen in the opposite direction.

## Description (English translation of the Japanese text, opening)

> Patent No. 152097 (Examined publication No. 2249 of 1942). Class 112, 2: Japanese-script printing machines. Filed 3 May 1941, published 20 May 1942, granted 17 August 1942. Patentee (inventor) Morishita Shōkichi, 1116, 1-chōme Kitazawa, Setagaya-ku, Tokyo; agent, patent attorney Kumagai Fukuichi. Platen sideways-feed device in a Japanese typewriter. Nature and object of the invention, in outline. The invention concerns a platen sideways-feed device in a Japanese typewriter, characterised in that at the tip of a crank, turned by a spring in step with the up-and-down movement of a working lever, an engaging pawl is fitted that can be changed over to left or right by hand. The pawl engages a gear fixed to one end of a connecting shaft, and a gear at the other end of the connecting shaft meshes with a rack fitted to the platen. Its object is, with a very simple mechanism, to make it easy to reverse the platen's left-right movement by changing the pawl over to left or right by hand, and to make the platen's sideways feed to left or right easy.
