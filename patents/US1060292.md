## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** When a tabulator releases the carriage from the escapement, the spring drum drives it across and it hits the column stop with a hard shock.

**Solution:** A drum filled with shot, geared to the carriage by a rack, is held still during tabulation so a blade on the pinion shaft must churn through the shot, retarding the carriage.

**How it works:** A second rack on the carriage always meshes with a pinion on a vertical shaft carrying a radial blade inside a shot-filled drum. In normal typing the drum turns freely with the shaft, so there is no drag. When a tabulator key is pressed, the feed rack is lifted from the feed pinion, freeing the carriage to the spring drum, and a stop is moved into the path of a pin on the drum, holding it still. The blade then has to move through the shot, which slows the carriage before the column stop meets the stop head. On key release the stop withdraws and the drum turns freely again.
