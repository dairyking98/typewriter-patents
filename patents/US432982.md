## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** The text does not state a problem; it refines the inventor's earlier index typewriter (US432981) with a three-row shiftable wheel, a toothed wheel to stop vibration, reversible ribbon winding and an adjustable anvil.

**Solution:** A pointer lever on a notched index plate winds a spring-returned, three-row type wheel by cord, a toothed wheel on the hub locks the position at each stroke, and depressing the trunnioned frame prints, spaces and winds the ribbon.

**How it works:** A rectangular frame pivoted on trunnions and held up by springs carries a spring-loaded shaft with a splined type wheel carrying three parallel rows of type on detachable strips. A bell-crank lever with a lug engages indentations on a segmental index plate; a cord from this lever to a sheave on the shaft rotates the wheel, and the shaft spring returns it. A side lever with a pin in an annular groove of the hub slides the wheel along the shaft to bring any of the three rows to the print point. A toothed wheel on the hub meshes with a fixed lug each time the frame is pressed down, which stops vibration and fixes the character exactly at the print point. Pressing the frame brings the wheel onto an adjustable anvil, and the same motion works a link and ratchet that advances the paper carriage one step, and a pawl that turns a ratchet to wind the ribbon between spools in either direction, selected by sliding a pinion shaft. A spring shield with a hole at the print point lies under the wheel, and a weight cord takes up rack lash and a bell rings at line end.
