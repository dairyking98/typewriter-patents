## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A key-released spring latch can be re-tripped or fouled if the operator still holds the key down when the latch is driven back, because the return is faster than the finger can lift.

**Solution:** Give the latch and the key lever two pairs of catches (two fixed, two yielding) that act in succession like an escapement, so the latch stays hooked back whether the key is held or released.

**How it works:** A spring-loaded reciprocating latch is carried in a revolving turret and is driven back by a starting arm. The latch has two oppositely placed fixed catches; the key lever carries two spring catches, one set slightly forward of the other. At rest the upper spring catch holds the latch back. Pressing the key lifts the lower spring catch against the lower fixed catch, then forces the upper catch off, releasing the latch forward. If the key is still down when the starting arm drives the latch back, the lower fixed catch engages the lower spring catch and holds it. As the key rises, the upper catch drops in front of the upper fixed catch just before the lower pair disengages, resetting the system. Variants pivot the catches, move them to the frame, or swing them crosswise.
