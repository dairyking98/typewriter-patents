## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Front-strike typebar actions had uneven leverage and key touch across the keyboard, side strain at the edges, a heavy start to the key stroke, and could not be assembled outside the machine.

**Solution:** Key levers of four lengths feed a horizontal link that forms a reverse toggle with the lever arm, driving upright sub-levers of progressively varied height that pull the typebars, giving uniform leverage and a gradual acceleration.

**How it works:** Key levers of the first order sit in four banks, pivoted at four different fore-and-aft points so their rear arms end in one plane while keeping equal lever ratios. Each rear arm is end-to-end with a horizontal link, forming a toggle at dead centre that breaks as the key is pressed, so resistance is light at first and the pull builds. The link turns an upright sub-lever whose upper end pulls the typebar by a forward link. Sub-lever pivots rise and their arms lengthen toward the sides, and they incline inward to avoid side strain. The key lever lifts a universal bar that rocks a shaft to operate an escapement lever with feed dogs and a ribbon vibrator. The typebar segment shifts on parallel links with a counterbalance spring for case change, and the whole key and sub-lever system can be assembled on a detachable plate outside the machine.
