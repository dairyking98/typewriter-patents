## Summary

**Kind:** typebar machine · **Subject (patent classes):** Typewriters or selective printing mechanisms characterised by the mounting, arrangement or disposition of the types or dies with types or dies carried on sliding bars or rods

**How it works (reading):** A pivoted lever normally engaged with the guide member tilts it positively, and is disengaged when the other key is pressed.

## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** In power-driven typebar machines with multiple types per bar, the guide member that tilts the bar for the other type group should be driven positively from the key levers in a simple way.

**Solution:** A pivoted lever normally engaged with the guide member tilts it positively, and is disengaged when the other key is pressed.

**How it works:** Each type bar with four types slides in a guide member pivoted on the frame. Two key levers (B and C) control the bar. Both move a lever through slotted links, and a pawl on a continuously rotating power shaft pulls the bar forward through a toggle link system. A push rod normally rests on a pin of a lever linked to the guide member via a spring; key B withdraws the rod so the bar prints without tilting, while key C leaves it in contact so the guide is tilted and the lower type group prints. The spring allows further drive movement after the guide reaches its limit. The platen is moved to a second position to choose between the other pair of types.
