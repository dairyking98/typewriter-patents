## Summary

**Kind:** type-element machine · **Subject (patent classes):** Types or dies fixed on wheel, drum, cylinder, or like carriers

**In the patent’s own words:** This invention relates to that class of typewriters in which there are keys to be depressed successively by the action of a finger, and these keys move frames to turn the type-Wheel a greater or less distance to bring the proper type in line for impression upon the paper, and by a continuation of the move ment of the key the type is impressed upon the paper and the feed takes place between one impressiop and the next.

**How it works (reading):** Each key has a slanted cam slot that slides a rack frame by a set distance to turn a type wheel to its letter, and an incline that then feeds the paper and fires an impression lever pressing the one aligned type slug forward onto the paper.

## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Not stated explicitly; the patent describes a keyboard machine in which one key stroke positions the type wheel, prints and spaces, with the types carried as separate slugs on a wheel.

**Solution:** Each key has a slanted cam slot that slides a rack frame by a set distance to turn a type wheel to its letter, and an incline that then feeds the paper and fires an impression lever pressing the one aligned type slug forward onto the paper.

**How it works:** Banks of vertically sliding keys each carry a cam slot, slanted left or right by an amount particular to that key, and an incline on one edge. Depressing a key makes its slot push a cross-wire of a sliding frame sideways by a key-specific distance; a rack on that frame turns a pinion on the type-wheel arbor to the right angle. Almost at once the key's incline moves a second sliding frame; its cam lever lifts a two-ended latch that works like a watch escapement and releases the spring-driven paper roll one rack tooth. Near the end of the stroke a finger on the second frame hits a lever whose tapered end pushes forward the single type slug in line, held in a radial mortise with V-grooves and a spring, against the paper on the roll. A detent holds the positioning frame still during the impression, and a ring on the wheel back stops the slugs going too far back. A hand lever slides the rack to turn the wheel half a revolution to bring in a second type set, and felt pads inking the faces as the wheel turns.
