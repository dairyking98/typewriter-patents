## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A pocket typewriter must be flat, but earlier multi-cylinder machines with all cylinders turned together used intricate gearing and bulky aligning discs.

**Solution:** Mount three single-row type wheels (lower case, capitals, figures) side by side on a sliding carrier, geared together to one rack and pointer, and slide the wanted wheel in front of the platen.

**How it works:** Three type wheels each carry a gear and all mesh with one common gear on a shaft. That shaft also carries a larger gear driven by a handled rack, whose pointer moves over a scale of letters and figures, so all wheels rotate together to the chosen character. A lever with a toothed sector turns a second gear to slide the carrier sideways, putting the lower case, capital or figure wheel in front of the paper roller. A push button moves the carrier plate to print; as it does so a tooth falls into a gap of a flat comb on the rack to align the character exactly. The ribbon is an endless belt over resilient rollers, advanced by a ratchet linked to the push button.
