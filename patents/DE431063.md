## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** Electric type-cylinder typewriters were slow, noisy, worn and sometimes printed a shadow of neighbouring types.

**Solution:** Six rows of types on a cylinder of about half the former diameter, with rotation limited by key-driven slides that stop an arm on the cylinder axis, and electromagnet-driven row shifting and impression.

**How it works:** Each type key pushes a radial member that advances a radial slide; the slide stands in the path of a stop arm on the rotatable type cylinder axis so the cylinder turns only until the chosen type faces the paper roller. Types lie in six rows (two lower case, two capitals, two figures), 15 per row with 30 keys. The cylinder axis is lifted along its length by an electromagnet, or by two shift keys through a toggle bracket and tie rods, to select the row. An electromagnet armature plate performs the impression.

**Numbers:** six type rows; 15 types per row; 30 type keys; cylinder diameter reduced to about half
