## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A typewriter using a segmental type-disk needs a simple way to bring the chosen character to the print point, print it and space, with few parts.

**Solution:** Each key lever raises a stop pin and, through a bail and slotted rock-arm riding a curved incline, turns a two-row type segment on a spindle until its tail hits the raised pin; a spring-driven pivoted platen, normally locked, is released by the same key to strike the segment.

**How it works:** A type segment with two rows of characters sits on a sleeve over a spindle in a standard. Depressing a key lever raises its own gravity stop pin and lifts a bail that pushes down a slotted rock-arm; this rides along a curved inclined arm on the spindle and turns the spindle and segment by an amount fixed by the key, until an arm at the segment's rear touches the raised stop pin, which fixes the print point. A separate shift key lifts a rod that raises the segment axially to present the lower row. Each key press also releases a locking pawl through a lever and pin, letting a spring throw an L-shaped pivoted platen forward against the type; on release a pin returns and relocks it. A feed bail under the keys rocks a pivoted pawl so that when the key is released the carriage advances one rack tooth; the ribbon spools are driven by a cord from the moving carriage so no reversing is needed.
