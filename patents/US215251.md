## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typewriters of the time spaced every letter equally and had no sure way to hold a type wheel exactly in position while printing; capitals and small letters also needed different spacing.

**Solution:** A spring-driven type wheel is stopped at the chosen letter by a key-selected stop arm and locked by a locking wheel, with the wheel shifted vertically for capitals and the carriage spaced by key-lever notches according to letter width.

**How it works:** A mainspring turns the type wheel through gearing continuously until a depressed type key pushes a rod whose arm stops the wheel's pinned arm at that letter's position. The same key movement lifts a locking wheel to release the arm, and when the key rises the locking wheel drops back and locks the arm and type wheel in a notch. A printing hammer behind the paper is cocked by the key motion and springs against the paper when the key is released, pressing it against the wheel. The key-lever bar also drives pawls on a rack-and-pinion carriage, with notches cut in the lever bottoms so narrow letters (I, J) move the carriage less than wide ones (L, M). A shift lever raises the type wheel vertically to bring capitals into use and switches to a smaller ratchet wheel for wider spacing. A carriage lever lifts the carriage out of the rack, returns it, rewinds the wheel spring, drops it to advance the line, and rings a bell near the end of the line.
