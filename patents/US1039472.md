## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** A light key touch must still give the typebar a firm printing blow, and the type should leave the platen quickly for a clear impression.

**Solution:** A bell-crank, link and cam arrangement gives the typebar an easy initial push and then accelerates it through a lost-motion connection, with a stop that checks the bar just before the platen so its own spring flicks the type off.

**How it works:** The key lever's ear presses on a bell-crank near its pivot, so an easy touch starts the bell-crank moving. The bell-crank pulls a curved link and a camming member rearward; the cam surface pushes a cam projection far out on the typebar to start it. Further key travel takes up a slot-and-pin lost motion nearer the typebar pivot, which accelerates the bar. A stop projection on the typebar meets a ridge on the segment just before the type reaches the platen, so the bar's spring carries the type to the platen and rebounds at once. The stop height can be ground to adjust type position at impression.
