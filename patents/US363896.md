## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Existing typewriters are complicated, costly, and give poor alignment of the printed characters.

**Solution:** A hand-held type wheel on a pivoted lever, mounted on a sliding standard on a feed rack, prints onto a platen that can be shifted to reach either set of type.

**How it works:** The frame has two end stanchions joined by a ratchet feed rack, and the roller platen sits in slotted bearings. A standard slides along the rack and carries an operating lever with a type wheel and hand grasp, held up by a coil spring. A shoe-shaped pawl on the lever engages the rack teeth, spacing the standard as it prints and a release lever frees it. The platen frame can be moved between two positions, locked by pins in notches, to bring it under either of two sets of type on the wheel. Spring-metal index fingers and a bar hold the paper.
