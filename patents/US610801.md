## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Marginal stops on a typewriter carriage are hard to adjust, give no way to finish a word past the margin, and letters pile up at the line end.

**Solution:** A banded margin stop with teeth that locks to a toothed carriage rod by a slight tilt, has a second shoulder to let the carriage move a few more spaces, and trips a buffer that blocks the typebars at the end of the line.

**How it works:** The carriage-supporting rod has a longitudinal groove with a toothed edge. A band-shaped stop with inner teeth slides freely along the rod when its teeth line up with the groove and locks when tilted so they engage the rod teeth; a counterweight returns it to the locked position. A projection on the stop meets a yoke on the carriage to halt it; tilting the stop moves this projection aside and allows a few extra spaces to finish a word. At the line end a rock-shaft arm on the carriage rides a cam on the left stop, turning the shaft and swinging a buffer bail into the path of the typebars so letters cannot pile on one spot.
