## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** A typewriter carriage escapement normally gives one fixed letter-space per stroke, so keys that print two or three characters, or wider characters, cannot advance the carriage by different amounts.

**Solution:** Two separately controlled escapement wheels are tied to the carriage drive shaft through a bevel-gear epicyclic train, so one escapement gives a single space, the other a double space, and both together a triple space.

**How it works:** A spring drum pulls the carriage to the left, and a rack on the carriage meshes with a gear and pinion on a vertical shaft. On that shaft a bevel-gear differential has its carrier fixed to the shaft and two loose bevel gears, each fastened to an escapement wheel. Each wheel is held by a fixed and a loose dog on a rocking arm, driven by its own universal bar of the Smith Premier type grid. Keys that print one character trip only the first bar, keys with two characters trip the second, and three-character keys trip both, so the shaft turns by the sum of the two escapement steps. A clutch in the shaft lets the carriage be moved freely one way, and a carriage release breaks the connection for both directions.
