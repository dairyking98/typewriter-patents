## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Printing a long line of repeated characters such as dots or stipples on a typebar machine takes one stroke per character, and the ribbon is tedious to wind and unwind.

**Solution:** A stipple character wheel is pressed through the ribbon against the platen while the released carriage runs across, so it prints a whole line (or part) in one pass, with the ribbon driven from the carriage rack.

**How it works:** A key lowers the spacing bar levers and, through a pitman, cranks and links, swings a character wheel in towards the platen. At the same time a toe raises a ribbon guide so the ribbon lies between the wheel and the platen. The key also sets the tabulating bar so the spring-driven carriage is released against a stop. As the carriage runs, the platen drags against the wheel through the ribbon and turns it, printing a row of stipples. A rack bar on the carriage drives a gear and a spring-loaded clutch to the ribbon spool ratchet so the ribbon moves in step with the platen; a lever engages or disengages this gear, and releasing the key returns everything.
