## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Ordinary two-case typewriters shift the platen between two positions; the inventor wants a three-case machine with an extra position for miscellaneous characters, with fewer parts, less weight and lower cost than a machine with more keys.

**Solution:** Two separate shift keys move the platen carrier transversely different distances, one twice the other, with fixed end stops and a third retractable stop that positively locates the middle position.

**How it works:** The platen sits on a carrier that slides transversely on the traveling carriage. One shift key works an angle lever through a connecting rod to push the carrier to the far extreme; a second key works a lever with half the travel, bringing the carrier to a middle position. Two fixed stops limit the extremes, while a third stop slides vertically into the carrier's path when the half-shift key is pressed, so the middle position is a hard abutment rather than depending on lever travel. A coil spring returns the carrier to the forward, normal position when the keys are released. Each typebar carries three faces: lower case, upper case and a miscellaneous character.
