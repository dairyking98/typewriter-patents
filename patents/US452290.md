## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typewriters of the day needed several separate actions to space, ink and print, and were costly to make.

**Solution:** A one-key machine: a single key turns a type wheel on a spindle and, when pressed, pushes the wheel forward against the paper while automatically feeding and inking.

**How it works:** The type wheel sits free on a spindle fixed to a slide that moves at right angles to the carriage. The key is pivoted under the wheel and the spindle passes through a slot in it, so swinging the key turns the wheel to the character; pressing it down acts through a collar on a forked lever and drives the slide forward against a return spring. A knife-edge stationary pin enters grooves cut in the wheel's lower rim to force exact alignment and lock the wheel during the impression. A flat pin on the slide enters a deep rack on the carriage to correct overfeed, and a feed pawl on a lever advances the carriage. The type is a rubber strip hooked into a groove in the wheel rim and swapped by unhooking its end clamps.
