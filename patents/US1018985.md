## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Typebar type can arrive misaligned at the printing point and print crooked; earlier centering pins need extra drilling, weaken the bar, and sit far from the writing line.

**Solution:** Notch the end of the type block itself to form an integral flat-faced finger that enters a fixed U-shaped centering guide at the platen, with large flat faces gripping the block's sides.

**How it works:** The type is a rectangular block with character faces along one side and an unused extension below the last character. One die-press notch cut in the extension leaves a finger with upper, lower and inner flat faces (optionally bevelled) flush with the block's sides. A fixed U-shaped member at the printing point has a square opening for the finger and a web that enters the notch, with two flat inner side faces. As the type swings in, the finger and web enter and the opposing flat faces grip the block from the notch edge to the block's lower edge, so the type is aligned and held from flexing at the instant of printing. Slight clearances are left, the main centering being by the lower notch wall against the upper wall of the opening.
