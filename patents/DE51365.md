## Summary

**Kind:** typebar machine · **Subject (patent classes):** Typewriters or selective printing mechanisms characterised by the mounting, arrangement or disposition of the types or dies with types or dies carried on sliding bars or rods

**How it works (reading):** Many type levers arranged in a circle swing each type to a common fixed print point at the centre when its key is pressed, with self-inking types.

## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Wants a typewriter taking little space that works fast, with types that ink themselves after every print and stay in register despite wear.

**Solution:** Many type levers arranged in a circle swing each type to a common fixed print point at the centre when its key is pressed, with self-inking types.

**How it works:** Keys in parallel rows (about eight per row) sit on shafts in sleeves and turn key levers below the bed plate. Each lever's rear end connects by a vertical connecting rod and joints to a type carrier on a ring pin in a circular head plate. All connecting rods lie in a circle around a central type guide, and cross-hinges with radial ring bearings reconcile the rows of keys with the circle. Pressing a key swings the type carrier, a rod with arms hinged to a central column, so its type reaches the fixed print point at the centre. The paper sledge shifts after each print by a letter-escapement device, with a line-feed mechanism, a signal (bell) and a spring drive. The remaining text, including the self-inking device, is cut off.
