## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** Platen-cylinder typewriters (Caligraph, Remington) hold the cylinder only through the pawl-and-ratchet, so the operator cannot hold it at an arbitrary rotation, e.g. to type fractions or superscripts between lines.

**Solution:** A clip-on attachment on the carriage end-bars locks the printing cylinder at any rotation via a spring-loaded slide rod, and operating it also lifts the machine's actuating and locking pawls off the ratchet.

**How it works:** Two inverted T-shaped frames are bolted to the carriage end-bars and carry a slide rod with a button, pushed by a leaf spring toward the locked position. A bar on the rod carries a locking member that engages a penetrable (felt) disk fixed to the cylinder end, so the cylinder cannot turn. Moving the rod the other way frees the disk and, by a lever linkage, holds the actuating and locking pawls out of the ratchet wheel so the cylinder can be rotated freely. A modified form reverses the lever positions for a particular Remington.
