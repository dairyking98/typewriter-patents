## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Typewriters on the market are complicated; a simple strong machine is wanted that does all they do.

**Solution:** A single-key index typewriter: a lever dropped into one tooth of a saw-toothed index plate turns a spring-returned type wheel through a rack and pinion, and pressing on prints and spaces.

**How it works:** The single lever carries a rack that turns a pinion; the pinion drives the type wheel through a three-tooth ratchet and pawl, so lever travel sets the wheel angle. The lever's hinged end is swung sideways into a tooth of either of two rows of saw teeth on the index plate, each tooth standing for two characters; the slide on the lever lets it go deeper to reach the second. Pressing further moves the index plate, rocking a lever and bell crank whose cam piece cocks and releases a spring striker under the type wheel, which hits the paper against the ribbon. The same plate movement frees a double-dog escapement so the spring-drum carriage advances one tooth; pressing lightly spaces without printing. Pushing the lever into a notch below the lowest tooth turns the wheel past a step of a stepped cam held by a pawl, shifting the wheel by a third of its circumference between three sets (capitals, small letters, figures); pushing the lever up releases the pawl and the wheel springs back, with a pointer showing the set.

## Description (English translation of the Japanese text, opening)

> New typewriter. Object. This invention relates to improvements in typewriters. Its object is a typewriter of simple and strong construction that can do everything the complicated typewriters now sold on the market can do. Summary. The typewriter belongs to the kind that uses a rotary type wheel and a single operating key working together with an index plate suited to choosing the character to be printed. It has a multiplying gear (複合聯動機) connecting the rotary type wheel and the single key. The index plate is stepped like saw teeth to work with the key, and is controlled by a spring so that the key can move it to make the impression. The multiplying gear reduces the travel the key needs to select the different characters. To select a character the key is engaged with the corresponding saw tooth, and the pressure then put on the key moves the index plate against its spring and so makes the impression. If the index plate is set upright, any particular tooth is easy to choose. The spacing mechanism may be built to be worked, when needed, by means other than this action, but the movement of the key can also be linked to it.
