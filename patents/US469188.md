## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Ordinary paper carriages cannot space letters of different widths exactly, and need separate action to return to the next line.

**Solution:** Wrap the paper on a cylinder that a wound spring turns through an escapement, so each key releases exactly the rotation needed for its sign width, and the cylinder shifts along its axis by one line pitch at line end.

**How it works:** The paper is wrapped on a cylinder held by a leather cover and hooks, mounted on a square spindle that turns it. A spring barrel, wound by a hand crank on a screwed shaft, drives a gear train to the spindle. An escape wheel in the train is held by a pallet carried on a friction arm; each key lifts the pallet by an adjustable-screw amount, so the train runs only as far as that key's width needs, and a brake pad holds the wheel while the key is down. Ratchets are made nearly stepless by series of sloped pawls (or six escapements worked by a cam ring) that subdivide the tooth angle. A second gear pair drives a carriage along the fixed screw as the spindle turns; a pair of snail cams of half the screw pitch lets the cylinder lag, then spring forward at line end by one line pitch while its rotation takes the margin past the print point. A hand rack-and-pinion turns the cylinder independently.

**Numbers:** cam-ring cycle repeats every quadrant of 6 parts; six pallets act in a 90 degree turn; escape-wheel motion one-ninth of cam-ring motion; snail pitch half the screw pitch

**Improves on:**

- ordinary carriage escapements with ratchet and pawl: spring-driven escapement gives indefinitely fine stopping places for variable-width letters
