## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A typewriter of great simplicity and sure operation, where the type wheel is turned by hand to a letter and printed by pressing it down on the paper.

**Solution:** A hand-turned type wheel with a letter dial on its face behind a pointer hangs on a pendulum lever above a small carriage that runs on a toothed rod; pressing the button locks the wheel's toothing, inks the type through a cut-out ink pad and prints, and the carriage steps one tooth on release.

**How it works:** The sheet is clamped on a base plate marked with ruled lines for registration. The type wheel is loose on a pin of a forked pendulum lever and is turned by a knob until the wanted letter, marked on the wheel's front face offset to match the type on the rim, stands under a pointer. Pressing the button first brings a fixed projection on the carriage into a toothed ring on the wheel's underside (one tooth per letter), so the wheel cannot turn, while the selected type passes through the cut-out of a spring-mounted ink pad and its neighbours are inked against the pad. Further pressure moves the carriage down to print. The carriage pivots about a toothed rod; a lever with a ratchet tooth holds it between teeth, and on pressing a spacing pawl drops to the next tooth gap, then on release the spring lifts the carriage and the pawl pushes it one tooth along. A stop adjustable on a rod trips a bell near the line end, and lifting an arm frees the carriage. A simplified second form puts the carriage on a rod that vibrates with it, and the wheel is a cylinder with rubber type strips pressed against its inner wall through openings by a leaf spring.
