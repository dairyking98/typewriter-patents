## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Fitting many typebars into a front-strike machine without thin weak bars, and getting two-case typebars to print cleanly with a short shift and without cranking (twisting) blur.

**Solution:** Thick straight typebars of equal length are pivoted on a stepped segment, each carrying two types with backward-inclined faces, and the platen frame shifts on two centres so the printing line moves only the distance between the two types.

**How it works:** Bell-crank key levers of equal arm length move straight typebars mounted on stepped bearings of a segment so the bar ends sit clear of each other at rest. Each bar head carries an upper and lower case type whose faces incline rearwardly and outwardly from a point in front of them; the lower case type lies in the bar's plane to avoid cranking. A curved universal bar meets the lever arms at points that move equally, giving uniform operation of the dog-rocker and escapement wheel on a spring-drum carriage. A shift key rocks the platen frame about two centres (link pivots and platen axis) so the print face moves only the type-to-type distance while the far side moves more; a catch on the shift key gives shift lock, and adjustable screw stops set the shift positions and take up wear. A spring detent on the line-space ratchet holds the platen to its frame during shift.
