## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** Line-spacing selectors on typewriters are complicated, hard to reach and heavy, especially for portables.

**Solution:** A cam lever with detents sets a stop for the line-shift lever, and the toggle-shaped pawl is held firmly in the platen ratchet during the stroke.

**How it works:** The line-shift lever turns on a bolt in a bearing block on the carriage side wall and drives an intermediate lever linked to an angled toggle-lever pawl. A cam lever with detents, pivoted on a second intermediate lever, enters the travel of the shift lever by an amount that depends on its set position, limiting the pawl path and so the line spacing. During the stroke the pawl is pressed against the platen ratchet by toggle action so a hard stroke cannot throw the platen further. A coil spring returns all parts.
