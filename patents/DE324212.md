## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** On machines with letters of different widths the carriage step must vary per character; earlier jumping teeth or small racks were complicated and needed the key shift to travel several teeth.

**Solution:** A loose movable switching tooth is coupled to a tooth-like control member that the ratchet wheel itself carries along, so the ratchet regulates the step length and the key shift only has to travel one tooth.

**How it works:** A carriage rack drives a gear on a shaft carrying the ratchet wheel; spring pulls the carriage. A switch lock body below the ratchet is swung by the universal bar on every key stroke. On it sit a fixed tooth, a loose switching tooth, and a control member that is also a tooth. On the stroke the control member advances toward the ratchet while the loose tooth is moved out of the ratchet teeth; the ratchet then carries the control member and in turn the loose tooth until the wanted number of teeth has passed, and the loose tooth is returned to rest. Adjustable stop rods, moved by group bridges that serve all characters of equal width, set how far the control member may travel, for steps of one to four teeth.

**Improves on:**
