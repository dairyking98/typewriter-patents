## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** A single circle of keys over the head, as in the inventor's earlier machines, is unlike usual practice and hard for typists to learn.

**Solution:** The keys are moved to banks in front of the head, with key levers linked to the type hammer sleeves and acting on a space-feed spindle with its flange reversed above the casing.

**How it works:** Type bars with pinions on their outer ends are turned by a ring gear to change case, as in the earlier applications. Key levers pivoted on the main casing pass under the flange of a central spindle, and a link and lever connect each to the sleeve of its type bar. Two space-key levers act on the same flange so either hand can space. A spring returns the spindle, which has a slot for a lever that engages the carriage mechanism when the head is down and disengages when it is tilted back. A lever on the ring gear projects to the front for case change by either hand, and an outer casing covers about three quarters of the head.
