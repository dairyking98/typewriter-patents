## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Existing typewriters cannot print on a page of a bound book, and selection is made harder when capitals and small letters are on separate banks.

**Solution:** A stationary wedge-shaped platen supports the leaf of a book while a travelling carriage carries an index with capitals and small letters side by side and types on spring arms struck by trip-hammers.

**How it works:** The wedge platen is thin at the left so it slips between book leaves, with an elastic sheet over it and clamps that hold the paper. A bar with grooves carries brackets and two parallel rods that form the raceway of the type carriage. Pins on one rod give the step feed after each impression. Types are mounted on elastic spring arms arranged on an arc, each independently operated, and a trip-hammer descends on a type to strike it against the paper.

**Features:** [Flat bed or book typewriter](../features/pap-flat-bed.md), [Book typewriter](../features/use-book-typewriter.md), [Separate type slugs or plungers](../features/elem-individual-slugs.md), [Element struck to the platen](../features/imp-element-to-platen.md), [Moving print head](../features/esc-moving-print-head.md), [Pawl-and-rack escapement](../features/esc-pawl-rack.md), [Pointer over a chart](../features/sel-pointer-chart.md), [Visible writing](../features/frm-writing-visible.md)

**Parts:** wedge platen; rubber sheet; clamp; grooved bar; bracket; carriage rod; feed pins; type spring arm; trip-hammer; index
