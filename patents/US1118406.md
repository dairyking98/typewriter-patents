## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** The carriage must be freed from the escapement for tabulating or release, and pushed back or back-spaced, without disturbing the escape wheel and without the wear of a single-tooth pawl.

**Solution:** A loose escape wheel is coupled to the rack pinion spindle by a ratchet and a U-shaped multi-tooth pivoted pawl that a sliding plate can lift out of engagement in any rotational position.

**How it works:** A spindle carries a pinion meshing with the carriage rack and a back-spacing pinion; an escape wheel with a crown ratchet turns loosely on it. A U-shaped pawl pivoted across the spindle has a segment of teeth that engage the ratchet, held by a spring, so under spring drive the escape wheel follows the carriage and the dogs meter it letter by letter. Pushing the carriage back lets the pawl teeth ride over the ratchet, leaving the escape wheel still. A key-operated pawl on the back-spacing pinion steps the carriage back one tooth. A plate surrounding the spindle, depressed by tabulator keys or the carriage release rod, presses the free arms of the U to lift the teeth out of the ratchet, so the carriage runs free. A spring detent stops the escape wheel being dragged backward.
