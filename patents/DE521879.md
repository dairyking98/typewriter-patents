## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Known sliding-type-bar machines have types on only one side of each bar, so many bars and many individual control members are needed, and the hammer deflection is small.

**Solution:** Double-sided type bars carried in a frame that moves along and across the platen are struck by one hammer whose upward or downward stroke picks the upper or lower type.

**How it works:** Type bars, with characters on top and bottom, rotate about a common axis in a frame and are held at rest by springs. The frame is moved lengthwise along the platen to bring one bar into the hammer's plane, and crosswise to bring a particular type of that bar into position. The hammer swings through more than 90 degrees; a bar in its path is carried along on the upward stroke when the frame displacement is within the type spacing of the bar (printing the top, small characters). If the frame is displaced further than the sum of the type spacings (the shift position), the bar is taken only on the downward stroke and prints the underside, capital characters. The hammer flywheel arc radius exceeds the bar length, and a raised portion on each bar takes the hammer blow so the hammer does not touch the types.
