## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** A spherical alining pin engaging a perforation gives accurate alignment only at one instant, so alignment varies with paper thickness.

**Solution:** Use a curved cylindrical pin on each type-bar entering a double-flaring (hourglass) perforation in a stationary member, so the full pin diameter is held constant over a range of travel.

**How it works:** In the Secor front-strike machine, type-bars pivot on an arc and swing back to the platen. Each carries a cylindrical pin curved on an arc about its own pivot. A hardened bushing in the frisket guide, at the centre of the arc of pivots, has a perforation that flares at both ends and is narrowest at the middle, equal to the pin diameter. Once the pin reaches full diameter in the narrow part the engagement stays exact while the bar continues travelling, so the type is aligned for any paper thickness and equally for outer and inner bars.

**Features:** [Alignment lock at the print point](../features/elem-alignment-lock.md), [Typebars](../features/elem-typebar.md), [Adjustment and wear take-up](../features/frm-adjustment-wear.md), [Element struck to the platen](../features/imp-element-to-platen.md)

**Parts:** type-bar; curved cylindrical alining pin; hardened bushing; double flaring perforation; frisket guide; key-bar; bell crank lever; return spring

**Numbers:** pin diameter equals smallest perforation diameter

**Improves on:**

- [US786056](../patents/US786056.md): the acorn or spherical pin gave exact alignment only at one point of travel; the cylindrical pin in a double-flaring hole holds alignment through a range
