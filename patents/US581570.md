## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Shorthand-speed recording of speech in ordinary letters, legible to anyone without a code, which a conventional one-letter-per-stroke typewriter cannot keep up with.

**Solution:** Three swinging type frames carrying consonants, vowels and terminal consonants are tilted by shift keys to bring chosen longitudinal rows to the print line, so a whole word prints across a paper strip in one chord of simultaneous key presses.

**How it works:** Sixteen flat-ended key bars lie side by side, each under one transverse row of type on three type frames mounted on a shaft above them. Shift keys, struck together with the printing keys, swing a frame up against a stop, bringing a chosen longitudinal row of its characters over the bars; a second shift key moves a swinging stop aside so a fourth row can be reached, and a spring returns the frame when released. A struck bar pushes the paper strip and ribbon up against its letter, so letters from all three frames print side by side to spell a word with unneeded letters left out. The same upward motion of the bars lifts a spring-loaded feeding frame that carries the strip forward past a friction paper-grip, which blocks backward travel so fresh paper appears when the keys drop. The ribbon winds on either reel by an elastic belt from the paper-roll spindle, and is reversed by moving the belt to the other pulley.

**Improves on:**
