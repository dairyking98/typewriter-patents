## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Typewriters were too slow for shorthand reporting because the space key was struck separately after each word.

**Solution:** A second, weighted escapement lets the carriage feed two teeth at once when an extra key is pressed together with a letter.

**How it works:** A key lever, optionally coupled to the space key, acts through a wire on a weighted anvil pivoted on the upper arm of a spring-acted elbow lever that works the carriage rack bar. The anvil has a lug engaging the stop of a swinging weighted escapement lever. Normally the carriage advances one tooth per key. When the escapement key is pressed the lug releases the stop so that the lever swings further and the rack feeds two teeth. Thus the space follows the last letter of a word in one stroke.

**Features:** [ESC:space-key](../features/esc-space-key.md), [ESC:pawl-rack](../features/esc-pawl-rack.md), [ESC:variable-spacing](../features/esc-variable-spacing.md), [USE:stenotype](../features/use-stenotype.md)

**Parts:** escapement key; elbow lever; anvil; escapement lever; rack bar; stop pin; spring; weight

**Numbers:** one or two teeth

**Improves on:**

- common typewriters: one stroke can add the word space as well as the letter
