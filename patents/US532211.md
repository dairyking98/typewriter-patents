## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** On machines where key depression gives variable travel to a type-wheel or segment, the ribbon guide must still reach the same position at each impression while keeping the line visible between strokes.

**Solution:** A spring-held ribbon guide normally rests down to expose the line, and a cam-incline movement lifts it to a fixed position for each stroke regardless of how far the key action travels.

**How it works:** The ribbon guide rests on the front feed roll under the pull of spring arms fixed to the frame, so the ribbon is down and the line shows. Each key-lever action drives a movable member (a vibratory lever) against an incline in a cam plate, or an incline on the guide itself, lifting the guide to the print point and holding it there for the blow. The cam is shaped to hold a determinate height however far the variable action goes. Rotary reciprocating type segments, as in the Hammond, swing in front of the ribbon and a vibrating hammer-platen at the rear strikes ribbon, paper and a flexible pad strip against the type.
