## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** On keyboard-driven type wheel toys, a hard stroke can throw the switching bridge past its stop so the type wheel overshoots and prints a wrong letter; known holding devices are too complex for cheap machines.

**Solution:** Each key lever gets a nose that enters its slot in the switching bridge and couples the bridge to the key lever as it reaches its stop, so no extra holder is needed.

**How it works:** Key levers in four rows pivot on axis 4; a switching bridge with stepped slots pivots on eccentric axis 5. A pressed key lever travels until it meets the lower slot edge and then carries the bridge with it, the slot gradation setting the swing angle. Lever 6, pressure bar 7 and toothed segment 9 turn gear 13 on the type wheel axis 12, so the wheel turns to the character for that key. Nose 18 on each key lever enters the bridge slot and couples the two, so the bridge stops exactly where the key lever hits the stop plate and cannot overshoot.
