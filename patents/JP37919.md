## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** The feed mechanism of the original Shimada-type machine (JP34924) that rotates and traverses the paper cylinder was complicated and unreliable.

**Solution:** A hook hanging from an upper lever on the cylinder frame engages a lower key-driven lever, and a spring-loaded pin on the hook drives a ratchet and gear on a rack so one key stroke traverses the paper cylinder one character.

**How it works:** The paper-cylinder frame rides on rollers along a rail. A pivoting lever on that frame has a hook that engages a plate on a lower lever on the base; pressing the printing key turns the lower lever, pulls the hook down and lifts the upper lever with its feed pawl. On release a coil spring returns the upper lever and the pawl turns the cylinder's ratchet wheel one tooth (one character). For row printing the pawl is set out of engagement and a spring-loaded pin on the hook is set (by a small lever in a long or short groove of a sleeve) to engage a second ratchet wheel whose gear meshes with a fixed rack, so each stroke moves the cylinder sideways one character. For column printing the pin is disengaged and the pawl engaged instead. A separate lever feeds without printing; an optional detent and line-end bell are mentioned.
