## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** Hand-turning a type wheel to marks on an index is slow and imprecise, with only about two degrees per character.

**Solution:** A spring on the type wheel plus a pen tied to the wheel by a cord, stopping against frets in rows on the index.

**How it works:** A semicircular type segment on a centre post is pulled by a spring to rest at the left. The operator draws the pen on its cord to the right and touches the plate just right of the wanted character's fret; the spring pulls the pen leftward until it hits the fret, which stops the wheel with that character at the printing point. The frets are posts or ridges about one-sixteenth inch high in rows like a keyboard, with a wide space before each. Printing and the pin-and-hole lock follow the World typewriter and are not improved.

**Features:**

- **Selection:** [Pointer over a chart](../features/sel-pointer-chart.md), [Pointer into a hole or notch](../features/sel-pointer-hole.md), [Arm swung over an arc](../features/sel-arc-arm.md), [Selection locked for printing](../features/sel-selector-locking.md)
- **Type element:** [Type sector or segment](../features/elem-sector-segment.md), [Type wheel (types on the rim)](../features/elem-wheel-rim.md)

**Parts:** index plate; fret; type wheel segment; spoke; centre post; pen; cord; spring; printing point

**Numbers:** about 70 divisions on semicircle, little over 2 degrees each; fret height about 1/16 in.; link attached about 1/16 in. from pen point

**Improves on:**

- World typewriter: frets give a tactile stop instead of marks
