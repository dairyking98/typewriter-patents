## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A Japanese-script typewriter platen could feed only one fixed spacing; the maker wanted the feed step to be changed easily and freely.

**Solution:** A push-pull knob rod shifts a lifting plate that moves the swinging feed pawl relative to the fixed feed pawl, changing the rack step between two values.

**How it works:** A knob rod rocks a cam plate whose slanting slot slides a lifting plate up or down. The lifting plate drives an inverted-L plate whose hook moves a pin on a rocking cam arm, and this raises or lowers a spring-loaded swinging pawl arm on the pawl lever, so the gap between the swinging pawl and the fixed pawl changes. Striking the main printing lever rocks the pawl lever through an arm and link, and the two pawls take the platen rack alternately, advancing it by that gap. A separate auxiliary striking lever feeds without printing, so a wide space is made by a blank stroke plus a printing stroke.
