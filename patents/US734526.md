## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Electric typewriters of the time needed much mechanism and effort at the key; the inventor wants to cut the operator's effort and greatly simplify the mechanism.

**Solution:** A motor spins a type wheel continuously, together with a trailer arm that sweeps a ring of contact segments, and the key the operator touches fires a print magnet at the instant its character is at the paper.

**How it works:** A motor turns the type wheel and, through gearing, a trailer arm in unison at about 500 rpm. The trailer arm brushes a circular commutator whose insulated segments are wired one to each key and match the characters on the wheel. When a key is pressed, a cut-off magnet first latches a switch lever. The key's second contact then energizes its segment, and when the brush crosses that segment the current reaches the print magnet. The magnet pulls the whole swinging wheel frame down against a spring, so the inked type strikes the paper while the wheel keeps rotating. A second magnet then resets the switch lever so the character prints only once. Releasing the magnet lets a lever advance the spacing rack one tooth, and a space bar does the same. A shift key energizes a magnet that slides the two-row wheel axially along its shaft for the second case. Variants use a pinned cylinder and spring contacts instead of the commutator, or two wheels printing on two paper rolls at once.
