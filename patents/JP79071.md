## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Printing the very large Japanese character set quickly and with even force; a hand-struck key gives uneven blows and is slow.

**Solution:** A motor-driven wheel or belt of types carries a type-lever for each row, and electrically released keys let the wheel's turning lift the chosen lever, so the motor, not the finger, supplies the printing blow.

**How it works:** A wide wheel carrying many types round its rim, with hold-down bars at each end face, is keyed on a shaft kept turning by an electric motor. Pivoted round a companion wheel are type-levers, one per row of types along the wheel's axis; each lever tip enters a notch in a type. Auxiliary keys move the wheel along the shaft through swinging arms and a slider, so the wanted type in a row lies at the lever tip. Each printing key closes two circuits (its row and its column) and energises two electromagnets whose armatures push out two guide plates. The levers' fan-shaped bases have teeth cut in different places: one plate engages a whole group, the second picks out the single lever in the group, and it is lifted with its type. The turning wheel then carries the lever into a slit in a V-shaped guide in front of the platen, which makes it strike, and it returns to its place.

## Description (English translation of the Japanese text, opening)

> Specification. Japanese-script typewriter. Nature and object of the invention. This invention relates to a Japanese-script typewriter in which a great many types are held, by means of a type-holding device, round a wheel or an endless belt; a type-lever is fitted for each row of types running parallel to the axis of the wheel or belt, and turns continually on the same axis together with the wheel or belt; the many printing keys on the machine are each connected electrically to the striking-release device belonging to them, so that striking any key works its release device, and the wanted type is pulled up by a type-lever and printed against the platen. Its object is to provide a Japanese-script typewriter that prints the very great number of Japanese characters quickly and with even force: merely touching a key lightly sets off the release device of the wanted type-lever, and the printing is done by the lifting force given to the type-lever by the turning of the wheel or belt that holds the types.
