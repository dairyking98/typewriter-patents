## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Typewriters were complicated, offered one type style, and spaced all letters equally regardless of width.

**Solution:** A stylus on a gimbal moves a convex-sided type block under a hammer to match the letter touched on a perforated letter plate, and the depth the plate is pressed (set by hole diameter) selects the carriage step for proportional spacing.

**How it works:** A four-sided type block with curved faces hangs by pins in a gimbal frame so it can tilt freely. A conical stylus joined to the gimbal by a ball joint is moved over a perforated stylus plate; over each hole a letter on a spring-lifted letter plate is visible, and the type block tilts so the matching letter is under the hammer. Pressing the stylus through a hole depresses the letter plate, which pulls down a rod that lifts the hammer until a hook slips off and the hammer drops, driving the paper and an endless ribbon against the type. Wide letters have larger holes so the stylus goes deeper, and the extra depression lets a lever pick a later offset on a stepped bar so the weight-driven carriage rack travels farther before a dog catches it. The block can be turned to a different face (e.g. italic) between letters, and up to three ribbon colours are carried on adjustable rollers.
