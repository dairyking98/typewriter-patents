## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A bookwork typewriter must print on either exposed page of an open book without readjusting the book supports each time or needing exact book placement.

**Solution:** A double flat platen with a slot between the leaves, hinged on a sliding rod so it swings up to expose the book and shifts lengthwise to line the slot up with the binding crease.

**How it works:** A table base has a longitudinal slot with adjustable-height support strips that carry the machine's track rails. A platen spanning the strips has two leaves, coextensive with the two exposed book pages, separated by a transverse slot. Book leaves are turned up, pushed edgewise through the slot, and folded out flat onto the platen, where the tracks and machine weight hold them. The platen is hinged at the rear edge on eyes sliding on an extended hinge rod, so it swings up to turn leaves and slides along the writing line to align the slot with the binding. A single-leaf variant is also shown, and the strips are raised by worm-driven segment gears and racks claimed in a companion application.
