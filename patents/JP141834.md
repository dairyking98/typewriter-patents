## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** In a Japanese typewriter with a bidirectional gear-driven platen feed, releasing the platen from the feed gear used to mean swinging or drawing back a heavy whole feed device.

**Solution:** Only the small feed gear is moved up and down: a change-over handle picks one of two counter-rotating driving gears for left or right feed, and a separate key lever lifts it clear of the platen rack while a detent blade is withdrawn.

**How it works:** A rack on the platen front meshes with a feed gear sitting between two meshing driving gears turning in opposite senses. A change-over handle, through a link rod, fork lever and lifting sleeve, lifts the feed gear to mesh with one driving gear (platen moves right) or lowers it to mesh with the other (platen moves left), a spring under the shaft seat plate returning it. A separate key lever swings a link whose cross pin lifts the shaft's seat plate further against that spring, taking the feed gear out of the rack and both driving gears so the platen moves freely. A toothed blade in the frame meshes with the lifted gear to stop it spinning. The same key also swings a branch arm and pin that tip a detent blade out of the rack, so the platen is fully free.
