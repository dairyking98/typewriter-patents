## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** The Hall typewriter moves its printing mechanism over a stationary paper clip, with expensive parts that are hard to make interchangeable; carbon copies were also hard because of soft rubber type and little leverage.

**Solution:** The Hall printing frame is held stationary lengthwise while the paper carriage steps along, with pointer, flexible type form and indicator unchanged.

**How it works:** The printing parts sit on a plate with a central adjustable impression stud; an indicator on the plate has spaces with the letters and figures. A flexible type form of rubber, or hardened characters on rubber, hangs from loosely connected levers beneath the plate so that it moves in any direction, moved by a pointer. Moving the pointer inks the type on a pad below with a central opening, and placing the pointer in the indicator recess for a letter puts that letter under the impression point. Pressing the pointer forces the letter through the opening against the paper. The plate is pivoted in a frame with no longitudinal motion, and a paper carriage with rack bar, operating pawl and holding pawl steps one tooth per impression, with a means to vary the step.
