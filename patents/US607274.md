## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A cheap typewriter is wanted; when each key positions, prints and spaces at once the parts must be made very accurately, which costs money.

**Solution:** Split the work: the character keys only position a light hard-rubber type wheel or segment (the key stem stops a swinging arm), while separate finger plates tilt the platen carriage to print and feed one space.

**How it works:** Two key levers, one each side, push down on opposite faces of a double cam on a sleeve that carries a stem engaging the type segment, so the segment turns either way. A stop arm on that cam swings until it hits the stem of whichever key has been pressed (the stem drops below a fixed plate into its path), which fixes the angle for that character. A second lever hinged on each positioning lever, with a finger plate next to the keys, tilts a support carrying the platen carriage towards the segment so the paper takes the impression; because it is pivoted on the positioning lever it also presses the stop arm firmly against the key stem during printing. The segment sits on a vertically sliding non-rotating shaft lifted by two levers (one stepped) to bring one of several rows to the print line. An ink roller on a sprung arm hinged to the tilting support rides up over the type as the platen comes forward and returns over the face. The tilt also trips a pawl on a spring-fed lever so the platen carriage steps one tooth along its rack, and a separate spacing lever tilts less to space without printing.
