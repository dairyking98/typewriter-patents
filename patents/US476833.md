## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Wear between the escapement dog and pawl and the rack teeth creates lost motion in carriage spacing.

**Solution:** Pivot the dog on an eccentric pin that a worm-like set screw turns, so the dog-to-pawl gap can be adjusted finely.

**How it works:** A key-operated rod turns a crank and the pawl spindle, so the pawl drops into the rack teeth while the dog lifts clear, and the reverse on release; the dog drops below the tooth line before the pawl leaves it. The dog pivots on a pin whose inner end is smaller and eccentric to the outer end. That inner end has a toothed pinion cut on it, meshed by the threaded end of a stationary screw held by a pin in an annular groove. Turning the screw rotates the pin and moves the dog's engaging end nearer to or farther from the pawl, taking up wear. An alternative has the outer end smaller and eccentric.
