## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** In a type-wheel machine (Blickensderfer-like) that rotates the wheel to the character, locks it with a dog, then swings it to the platen, the sudden stop makes the wheel rebound and print a neighbouring letter. Earlier spring-loaded rebound catches fix this but add resistance to the key.

**Solution:** A passive rebound catch, held up by a removable support and left unsupported (held only by its own inertia) at the instant the wheel locks, so the snail overtakes it and it adds almost no resistance to the printing stroke.

**How it works:** Two key-driven sectors on either side of a pinion rotate the type-wheel, each about half a revolution in opposite directions, until the wheel's snail (cycloidal rack) reaches the chosen tooth. A snail-stop driven by a bail from the same key then drops into that tooth and locks the wheel; further key travel moves the sectors and wheel radially toward the platen. A rebound catch hangs opposite the snail, supported by fingers on the sectors. When the radial stroke starts the fingers drop away so the catch is momentarily unsupported; the snail overtakes it and is held against rebound with no spring tension to overcome. On the return stroke the sector arms or fingers lift the catch back to rest.
