## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** Turning the platen by pawl and ratchet to feed, discharge and line up paper such as letter-heads is slow and imprecise.

**Solution:** A rack sliding in a guideway turns a pinion on the platen shaft, with adjustable stop collars and notches so one push sets exact line positions or ejects the paper.

**How it works:** A pinion sits on the platen shaft and a rack runs through a stationary guideway on the carriage end. A pressure spring keeps the rack against one side so a fixed stop engages notches on the rack. Stop collars fixed by thumb screws set the travel; pushing the rack in rotates the platen to bring the first address line to the type line, and a small pull and push moves to the second. Pulling the rack the other way turns the platen backward and discharges the paper. It can be built in or attached to the carriage.

**Parts:** pinion; rack; guideway; pressure spring; fixed stop; notch; stop collar; thumb button; platen shaft

- pawl-and-ratchet platen turning: one rack stroke gives continuous or full rotation
