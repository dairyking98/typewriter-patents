## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** The line-space lever nose rests loosely against the line-ratchet carrier (to let the platen shift between character groups), which gives play and a rattling noise in the paper carriage.

**Solution:** A weak spring, in the form of a thin resilient shield hinged on the lever's nose, presses the lever lightly against its stop in rest position and cushions the start of the line-space stroke.

**How it works:** The line-space lever pivots on the paper carriage and has a nose that drives a slider carrying the line-space pawl, which engages the line index wheel on the platen shaft. A strong spring pulls the pawl carrier against its stop. Between the lever nose and the carrier a thin shield is pivoted at its middle on the nose, with a sheet-metal tongue punched out of it that twists it so its tip yields against the carrier. This takes up lost motion in rest, without hindering the cross shift between character groups, and softly absorbs shock at the start of line spacing.
