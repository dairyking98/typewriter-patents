## Summary

**Kind:** a part or mechanism (any kind of machine) · **Subject (patent classes):** Typewriters or selective printing or marking mechanisms characterised by the purpose for which they are constructed

**In the patent’s own words:** The object of my invention is to construct a type-writer in which a large number of characters or letters may be printed without the necessity of multiplying keys.

**How it works (reading):** Tubular type-arms whose spindle heads carry several types on different faces are rotated by a common circular rack, so one key per arm can print several characters.

## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typewriters need many keys for many characters; ribbon reels need manual reversing; fingers slip on keys.

**Solution:** Tubular type-arms whose spindle heads carry several types on different faces are rotated by a common circular rack, so one key per arm can print several characters.

**How it works:** Right-angle arms on a plate carry rollers supporting a circular rack with inwardly curved teeth. Each type-arm is a tube pivoted in a triangular block, holding a wire spindle with a multi-faced type head at one end and a segmental pinion at the other, engaged with the rack. A locking and releasing mechanism and a double-acting key with levers turn the rack either way, which rotates all heads together to change case; pressing a key then swings its arm to print. Also described are two ink-ribbon reels on a shaft with a clutch alternating them for automatic reversal, a spring-drum carriage drive, and a paper roll that lifts.
