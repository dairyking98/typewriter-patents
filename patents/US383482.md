## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Typewriters like the Hammond had no convenient way to move the carriage back one space at a time, needed to rewrite erased letters or insert forgotten punctuation.

**Solution:** A friction strap normally clear of the spring barrel is tightened by a key lever so it grips the barrel and rotates it backward by one ratchet tooth.

**How it works:** The Hammond carriage rack drives a pinion on a free sleeve, which drives the escapement shaft through a spring pawl and ratchet, so the carriage can be pushed right without turning the escapement wheel. A grooved ring on the spring barrel carries a wire friction strap lying close to but not touching the groove, one end hinged to an arm on a shaft, the other end fixed through a short spring. Pressing a spring-returned button rotates the shaft, tightening the strap on the ring and pulling the barrel backward, winding the mainspring until the pawl drops into the next ratchet tooth. A stop under the button limits the throw to one space, and releasing the button lets the strap spring clear. Variants use a flat strap, a cam-shaped pulley with both strap ends movable, or a separate drum for machines without an accessible barrel.

**Features:** [ESC:backspace](../features/esc-backspace.md), [ESC:spring-drum](../features/esc-spring-drum.md), [ESC:pawl-rack](../features/esc-pawl-rack.md), [ESC:escape-wheel](../features/esc-escape-wheel.md), [USE:accessory](../features/use-accessory.md)

**Parts:** spring barrel; grooved ring; friction strap wire; strap spring; shaft; short lever; button key; return spring; carriage rack; pinion sleeve; ratchet wheel; spring pawl; escapement wheel

**Numbers:** one space per key depression

**Improves on:**
