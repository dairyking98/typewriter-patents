## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** On tray-type Japanese typewriters with many closely packed types, a plain pointer on a character board is hard to read, moves continuously and does not show when the printing device is exactly over a type, so operators confuse characters and work slowly.

**Solution:** A translucent indicator board showing the type layout is lit from behind, one character at a time, by lamps whose position follows the two relative motions of types and printing device and whose circuit closes only when the two are exactly in line.

**How it works:** The characters are drawn on a light-transmitting board in the same order as the types in the magazine. Behind it a lamp carrier, with one lamp per character of a column, is moved sideways by a rack and pinion tied to the side-to-side motion of the printing device. A brush running on a bar of insulated conductor segments follows the fore-and-aft motion and closes the circuit of one lamp, while a second brush on a conductor with insulating gaps completes the circuit only when the device is centred on a row and column. Two motors with a reversing controller move the paper-cylinder frame over a fixed type magazine. The board can be enlarged and set wherever it is easiest to see.

## Description (English translation of the Japanese text, opening)

> Specification. Indicator board for a typewriter. Nature and object of the invention. This invention relates to an indicator board for a typewriter, characterised by a transparent or translucent indicator board on which the arrangement of the types stored in the typewriter is drawn; electric lamps behind it able to light the characters on the board one at a time; and, in the lamp wiring, switches that make contact when the centre line of the typing mechanism coincides with the centre line of each row, and of each column, of the types in the type magazine. Its object is an indicating device that, each time a type and the printing mechanism coincide, lights up one character by switching on a lamp, so that it stands out sharply from the other characters. This makes the wanted character easy to recognise, avoids confusing it with others, and raises the speed of printing. Brief description of the drawings. The drawings show one embodiment.
