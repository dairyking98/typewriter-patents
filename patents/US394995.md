## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Earlier striking type-wheel machines needed the operator to supply the print force, aligned the wheel imprecisely, and needed hand shifting of the ribbon reversal; the inventor wants the wheel to strike automatically with adjustable timing and position.

**Solution:** Key levers turn a three-row type wheel through toothed segments and spiral check-pin shafts, and the same stroke trips a rocking rack-bar so the mainspring-driven paper carriage pulls the wheel against the platen and escapes one tooth.

**How it works:** Each key lever moves a driver lever whose toothed segment turns one of two check-pin shafts, which mesh by a segmental bevel with a miter wheel on the type-wheel shaft, turning the wheel left or right to the character; spiral-set pins on the check shafts and regulator screws on the key levers stop it exactly. Key motion also lifts a transverse lever that releases a hinged rack-bar on the spring-driven carriage from a stop and engages it with a bell-crank joined to the type-wheel lever, so the carriage's pull moves the wheel forward to strike the platen while the carriage advances one tooth. Return springs of opposite hand hold the wheel at rest and take up gear backlash. Shift bars lift the wheel on its shaft to present the upper or lower row, with a splined or pinned hub allowing the wheel to slide yet turn. The space key moves the rack-bar but a blocking lever stops the wheel from reaching the paper. The ribbon is lifted out of view after each strike, and a screw-threaded spool shaft with a travelling nut trips an automatic ribbon reversal.
