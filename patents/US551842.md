## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** In typebar machines with two types per bar, shifting the whole heavy paper carriage between lower and upper case takes effort and requires a heavy, complex carriage.

**Solution:** Keep the paper carriage fixed and shift only the light platen, mounted in a platen carrier that swings about a pivot eccentric to the platen axis, so the printing line moves from the lower-case to the upper-case type position.

**How it works:** The platen axle is carried in two plates pivoted on uprights of a non-shifting main carriage, with the pivot axis eccentric to the platen's own axis. Typebars with two types each are set radially about that pivot axis. Pressing the shift key raises a connecting rod that turns bellcranks and swings a shift rail. The rail tips a pivoted yoke block, which pulls a link down and rocks the plates, so the platen moves back by the centre-to-centre distance of the two types. Adjustable screw stops at each end limit the swing, and a spring returns the parts when the key is released. A spring-loaded feed roller hung from the plates stays against the platen.
