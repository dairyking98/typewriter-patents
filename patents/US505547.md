## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** In Gardner's type-cylinder machine the stop finger moved on an arc, forcing keys onto concentric arcs, the cylinder did not reliably return to zero, and backlash left it unsteady at the moment of impression.

**Solution:** Two geared oscillating bars (a driving bar and a stop bar) with inclined wings, rocked by any key in a linear keyboard, rotate the cylinder to the chosen character, lock it there against the key's stop, and a cam with spring rollers returns it to absolute zero.

**How it works:** The type cylinder is turned through a long pinion by a quadrant on a driving bar. Pressing a key first depresses a light rail, releasing the cam plate whose two shoulders normally hold the cylinder at zero, then the key's shoulder pushes its wing on the driving bar, rotating the bar by an angle set by the wing's inclined tip. The driving bar is geared by quadrants and the pinion to a parallel stop bar with alternating wings; the key's stop meets its matching stop wing and halts both bars, locking the cylinder exactly in the printing position with backlash taken up. The key motion also works the letter-spacing dogs and releases the impression hammer. On release springs return the rail rollers against the cam shoulders so the cylinder returns to zero from either direction.
