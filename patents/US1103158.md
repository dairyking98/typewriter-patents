## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** On a three-character typebar machine the platen must be held firmly in a central position and also shifted up or down for the other two cases, quietly and with a lock. Earlier shifts did not lock the platen at centre and made noise when stopping.

**Solution:** Two shift keys act on a floating lever; each key's detent locks the platen centred until pressed, and one key's pivot serves as a fixed fulcrum while the other is depressed to move the platen either side of centre.

**How it works:** The platen frame slides vertically in the carriage and carries a channel bar. A roller block on a rear lever rides in that channel so the carriage can travel. Two shift levers are joined by a floating lever to the rear lever. In rest position each shift lever sits against a stop, held by a detent on a fixed shoulder, which locks the platen centred. Pressing a shift key rides its detent off the shoulder; the other lever stays locked and acts as a fixed fulcrum, so the floating lever swings and raises or lowers the platen. Toggle links (or a roller in a U-shaped guide) stop the travel without impact, and one spring restores everything. A lock key beside the shift key catches the lever under a shoulder to hold the shifted position.
