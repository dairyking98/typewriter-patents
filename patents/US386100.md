## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A typebar pivot must be free enough to fall back by gravity and respond to a light touch, yet hold the type in exact alignment, and its wear must be adjustable without removing the hanger or disturbing alignment.

**Solution:** A U-shaped springy hanger with hardened conical bearings holds the typebar's conical journal, and a single transverse countersunk screw between the forks draws them together to set and later take up the bearing play.

**How it works:** The typebar carries a short cross journal with conical ends near one end, where a connecting rod from the key lever attaches. The hanger is a U-shaped metal strip whose forks normally spread slightly apart, with hardened bushings pressed into holes in the forks and each bored with a conical bearing. A transverse screw between the forks, with a conical head in one fork and a thread in the other, pulls the forks together until the bearings grip the journal ends, leaving the bar free to swing but not sideways. The screw is under tension so it does not work loose, and its square body takes a wrench. As the cones wear, tightening the screw takes up the play with the hanger still mounted. Hangers sit side by side because the screw does not protrude.
