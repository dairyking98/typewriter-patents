## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** When a tabulator frees the carriage from its step-by-step escapement, the spring-driven carriage runs at excessive speed and slams into the column stop.

**Solution:** A retarder is swung into mesh with the carriage drive gear when the tabulator operates; it turns a corkscrew wire in a bottle of granular graphite, which resists and so limits carriage speed.

**How it works:** A mainspring drum pulls the carriage through a gear meshing with a rack on the carriage rail, with a clutch normally coupling the gear train to the escapement. Pressing a tabulator key lifts a universal bar that rocks a shaft, which disengages the clutch and frees the carriage. The same shaft rocks an arm, through a spring, a bottle-shaped barrel carrying a bevel pinion on a shaft whose lower end is a corkscrew-shaped wire. The pinion meshes with the carriage gear so the carriage turns the wire through a mass of powdered graphite (or shot and graphite), whose resistance caps the carriage speed. A screw plug in the barrel bottom sets how deep the screw is buried and so the resistance, and an adjustable stop screw sets pinion mesh depth.

**Features:** [ESC:tabulator](../features/esc-tabulator.md), [ESC:spring-drum](../features/esc-spring-drum.md), [ESC:carriage-release](../features/esc-carriage-release.md), [ESC:pawl-rack](../features/esc-pawl-rack.md), [FRM:adjustment-wear](../features/frm-adjustment-wear.md), [PAP:platen-roller](../features/pap-platen-roller.md)

**Parts:** mainspring drum; driving gear; rack bar; clutch member; tabulator key; universal bar; rock shaft; retarder barrel; bevel pinion; corkscrew wire; graphite mass; adjusting plug; stop screw; column stop; denominational stop
