## Summary

**Kind:** type-element machine · **Subject (patent classes):** Typewriters or selective printing mechanisms characterised by the mounting, arrangement or disposition of the types or dies with separate or detached types or dies

**How it works (reading):** A typebar machine whose bars are hung from two unequal parallel arms so the type block travels on a near-vertical arc onto a horizontal ink pad and then down onto a platen that tilts forward or back to select one of three types on each block.

## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** A visible-writing typebar machine is wanted in which the types strike the paper vertically without smearing, the typebars are well inked, the carriage runs with little friction, and many characters are available without a conventional shift key.

**Solution:** A typebar machine whose bars are hung from two unequal parallel arms so the type block travels on a near-vertical arc onto a horizontal ink pad and then down onto a platen that tilts forward or back to select one of three types on each block.

**How it works:** Each typebar hangs from two arms of unequal length, the farther one longer, so a small key movement lifts the type block in a half-ellipse up over the platen and strikes it down nearly vertically; counter-pressure springs soften the motion and return springs bring it home. There are two groups of bars, one in front of and one behind the platen, each resting on a horizontal ink pad. Each block carries three types (lower case in the middle, capitals and signs at the ends), and a key lever swings the whole platen carriage on pivots forward or back to bring one of the three into the strike line, with a lockable key to hold the position; 28 types give 84 designations. The carriage runs on balls in V-grooved rails pulled by a spring drum, and a rack engages one of two pawls, the second stepping one or more teeth and the first catching the rack on release so that each key or the space bar advances one space; a bell, a line-feed handle with ratchet and adjustable step, and wire paper catchers complete the carriage.
