## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Front-strike visible-writing typebar machines swing their bars through about a quarter circle from a horizontal rest, giving a hard, disagreeable key action.

**Solution:** Hang short typebars from pivots below the platen in a near-vertical rest position and swing them through about a half circle, driven by links from three-armed key levers.

**How it works:** Short typebars, each carrying three types, hang from a curved pivot rod in a slotted segment below the point of impression and fan out radially. Each three-armed key lever has a key arm, an upward arm joined by a thin link to the typebar below its fulcrum (a third-class lever), and a rear arm lifting the universal bar for step-by-step carriage feed. Pressing the key pushes the link forward, swinging the bar forward over the keyboard and up and back to the platen, with an easy start and a toggle action at impact. Radial slots in the segment guide each bar, and the lower ends are bent into crooks so the types lie close side by side in a single arc.

**Features:** [ELEM:typebar](../features/elem-typebar.md), [SEL:keyboard-full](../features/sel-keyboard-full.md), [IMP:element-to-platen](../features/imp-element-to-platen.md), [FRM:writing-visible](../features/frm-writing-visible.md), [ESC:pawl-rack](../features/esc-pawl-rack.md)

**Parts:** typebar; pivot rod; slotted segment; three-armed key lever; link; universal bar; platen; rest; type crook

**Numbers:** at least 26 typebars; 3 types per bar; about 180 degree typebar swing; 3 key banks

**Improves on:**

- front-strike typewriters with horizontal typebars: typebars hang near vertical and swing a half circle for an easier key action and a stronger blow
