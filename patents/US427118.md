## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** On Hall-type plate index typewriters (US238387) the light printing stud and short hand-piece stroke need severe hand effort for manifold copies, causing fatigue and limiting the number of copies.

**Solution:** A pivoted strap lever on the carriage plate is swung down on the printing stud so the hand's effort drives the stud with multiplied force; an improved stud and a detachable fitting are included.

**How it works:** In the Hall machine a movable type-form is brought by an arm so the chosen letter lies under an opening in a plate, and a stud forces that letter through onto paper on the platen. Here a bar attached under the front edge of the plate carries brackets, in which a metallic strap is pivoted by rivets. Swinging the strap down meets the stud's upper end, and slots in its ends adjust the leverage. Plates fitted at the front edge enclose the upper plate and let the spacing key arm pass. The attachment bolts on and can be removed.
