## Summary

**Kind:** type-element machine · **Subject (patent classes):** Typewriters or selective printing mechanisms characterised by the mounting, arrangement or disposition of the types or dies with types or dies mounted on carriers rotatable for selection the plane of the type or die face being parallel to the axis of rotation, e.g. with type on the periphery of cylindrical carriers

**In the patent’s own words:** This invention relates to certain improvements in that class of type-writers in which the type are arranged on the periphery of a type-wheel having a tendency to revolve under the action of a constant source of power, but prevented from revolving by suitable mechanism, except when a type-key or its equivalent is depressed.

**How it works (reading):** A clockwork-driven type-wheel is held by a catch on a notched stop-wheel and released by any key, then stopped by key-lever stop plates meeting pins on disks, then locked and pressed on the platen by the same key stroke.

## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Myers wants a spring-driven type-wheel machine that is more efficient, easier to work and produces neater work, including letters spaced by their width.

**Solution:** A clockwork-driven type-wheel is held by a catch on a notched stop-wheel and released by any key, then stopped by key-lever stop plates meeting pins on disks, then locked and pressed on the platen by the same key stroke.

**How it works:** A coiled spring in a barrel constantly urges the type-wheel and a notched stop-wheel on the same shaft to turn. A catch holds the stop-wheel until a key depresses a rocking frame and lifts the catch, so the wheel spins. Each key lever carries a stop plate that meets one pin on one of three pinned disks on the shaft, so the wheel stops near the right character. Three disk sets, chosen by sliding the key frame sideways with a handle and locked by a pin, give capitals, small letters and figures. Further key pressure tilts the whole wheel frame about its pivots. A catch is forced into a wide-mouthed notch of the stop-wheel to align and lock it, and the wheel comes down onto the paper on the platen. The same motion draws a toothed spacing rack, and a stop-arm meeting a raised annulus on the stop-wheel arrests the rack by the width of the character, giving proportional spacing. An ink pad swings across the type as the frame falls. A pendulum or spring finger with a ratchet rewinds the driving spring as the machine oscillates.
