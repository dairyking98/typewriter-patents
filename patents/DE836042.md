## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Small travel typewriters are still tall; a simple non-folding machine about half normal height is wanted.

**Solution:** Lower the typebar bearing until the short arm just clears the base plate, put the key lever bearing close to and below it, and tilt the key planes at rest so they swing evenly about horizontal.

**How it works:** Two-armed typebars are driven by pull rods from intermediate levers whose bearing lies about in the plane of the key lever bearing, near the top key row. Driving pins sit far from the key lever bearing to keep the ratio moderate, so identical intermediate levers fit all keys. Key levers are inclined near their bearing and partly overlap under the intermediate lever bearing, which is shaped as a bridge. The machine keeps 44 to 45 typebars and a normal platen.

## Description (Google Patents OCR, opening)

> Description Translated from German Typewriter The aim of the invention is to create a typewriter that is as flat as possible, about half the height of the usual small typewriters, which cannot be folded up and is therefore simple and stable and, with the smallest dimensions, suitable as a real travel machine and still 44 to 45 type levers and still one of the normal small machines has the usual paper roll diameter of 32.5 mm. This is achieved in that the type lever bearing is arranged so deep that the arm of the type lever moved by the pull rod plays directly above the level of the base plate. That means, since the type lever is two-armed in a known manner, that its short arm, which is moved by the pull rod and turned away from the type, just has enough space to move freely. The key lever bearing is also as close as possible to the type lever bearing so that the key levers, while keeping all dimensions small, do not make too large an angular deflection that would bring the key plane too far from the horizontal when pressed down with a usual depth that is necessary for a not too high stroke force.
