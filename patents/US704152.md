## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Typebar pivots in visible typewriters work loose or wear, parts are hard to disconnect, and a large number of typebars must be packed closely on a segmental support.

**Solution:** A two-part threaded pivot with an adjustable bearing and a springy bifurcated link make typebars easy to remove and take up wear, and hangers are staggered and overlapped on alternate faces of the segment.

**How it works:** Each typebar is pivoted to a hanger by an internally threaded member and an externally threaded member that screw together through the forked part. The inner member is locked against turning relative to the part it moves with, so only its concentric bearing surface rubs and it cannot unscrew. Turning the outer member with a spanner closes or opens the fork to take up wear. A forked link between the key-lever's angle lever and the typebar can be sprung apart to disconnect. Hangers have a wide forked arm that straddles a lug on the segmental support and is held by a loose set screw, giving small lateral and length adjustment. The hangers are fitted alternately on opposite faces and overlap, so three fit where two would on one face while the pivots stay aligned on the support's centre line.
