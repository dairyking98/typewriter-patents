**In the patent’s own words:** My invention relates to type-writing machines, and particularly to that class in which the writing is visible, the printing being done upon the front upper quarter of the cylindrical impression-platen.

## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** A visible-writing, front-strike typebar machine needs a better key action, typebar mounting, shift, carriage and escapement than existing key-lever center-strike machines.

**Solution:** A complete visible typebar typewriter with a slotted-link double-stop typebar action, a quadrant of two-character typebars raised for capitals while the platen carriage drops, a rack-and-spring-wheel carriage and a single-tooth escapement.

**How it works:** Each key lever pushes a rod whose slotted end first levers the heel of the typebar, then slides so a pin stops the bar just before it hits, giving a sharp spring-back blow through the ribbon at the upper front of the platen. Bars sit in groups of three or four between guide wires on a quadrant arc centred on the printing point. A rock-shaft raises the quadrant (via a locking lever or a temporary shift key) and drops the carriage rail by half the interval between the two characters on a bar, so the platen rocks and the print point stays square. The carriage rides on rails, is pulled by a spring wheel with an adjustable tension rack, and is stepped one space by a pawl and a rockable rack engaging a stationary tooth. Line spacing is by a hand crank and dog on a platen ratchet set for single or double, and a release handle frees the carriage by hand.
