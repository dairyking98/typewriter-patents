## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Earlier automatic ribbon reversers pivoted the rocker arms on special head-screw axes joined by screwed rods, making manufacture and assembly dear and the device bulky.

**Solution:** Mount each swinging jaw lever on its own spool axle through a slot so it can both pivot and shift, and connect the two levers by a displaceable bracket with pin-and-slot joints so parts only need to be plugged together.

**How it works:** Two ribbon spool carriers on the rear bridge each hold a spool axle, guide pins and a bearing pin. A laterally sliding bracket has downward bolts in angled slots of the carriers and upward bolts in open slots of two jaw levers pivoted on the spool axles. Each jaw lever carries a pawl that engages a ratchet wheel whose driver pins sit in holes in the ribbon spool, and feeler arms on pawl brackets, sprung against the ribbon, track each spool. A drive rod through a driver bracket and lift bracket gives the pawls their stroke, which also releases the hammer. When the unwinding spool is nearly empty the feeler lets the bracket slide, so the other jaw lever and pawl take over the drive and the ribbon reverses.
