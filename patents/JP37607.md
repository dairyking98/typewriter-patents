## Summary

**Kind:** a part or mechanism (any kind of machine) · **Subject (patent classes):** Typewriters or selective printing mechanisms characterised by the mounting, arrangement or disposition of the types or dies

**Class note:** J-PlatPat files it as a Western-type typewriter (B41J1/00@P); it is a Japanese-script (kanji or kana) machine built on Western lines.

**In the English translation of the patent’s own words:** The object is a typewriter that can print many thousands of characters quickly.

**How it works (reading):** Many long many-sided rotary type carriers, one per row of keys, are turned and slid by key-driven bars so that any of thousands of characters is thrown onto the same point of the paper cylinder.

## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Western typebars carry only two or three characters, which cannot cover the thousands of Oriental characters.

**Solution:** Many long many-sided rotary type carriers, one per row of keys, are turned and slid by key-driven bars so that any of thousands of characters is thrown onto the same point of the paper cylinder.

**How it works:** Each carrier is a seven-sided bar with seven zones of four characters per face, 196 characters in all, mounted on a pair of parallel swinging levers above a cross-wise paper cylinder. Pressing a key pulls down an upper bar, which swings that carrier's levers and throws it against the cylinder to print. The same key also presses a middle bar, which pushes a long rod against a flange whose stud runs in a screw groove on the carrier shaft, turning the carrier to the right face. A lower bar pushes another rod into a groove at the carrier end to slide it lengthwise to the right zone and position. Springs return the carrier, flange and rods after the stroke.

## Description (English translation of the Japanese text, opening)

> Specification. Typewriter. Nature and object of the invention. This invention is a typewriter that prints a great many characters, such as Oriental characters. In the ordinary Western typewriter each typebar carries only two or three characters in a row; in this invention many type carriers are provided, each built to rotate, with many characters arranged round its surface. The carriers are lined up near the paper cylinder, each on its own support, and by pressing one of many separately provided finger keys, the appropriate carrier is turned through a suitable mechanism and the wanted character is thrown down and struck at one and the same point on the paper cylinder, and so printed.
