## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Finding a type on a Japanese typewriter is slow and existing machines are heavy to operate.

**Solution:** Two-step keying: a change key turns a hollow multi-compartment type cylinder to the required row via a cam shaft and sector, then an operating key slides the paper cylinder to the column, and its second press brings the type cylinder to the paper and ejects the one type.

**How it works:** The type cylinder is a hollow drum with types in a grid of compartments, driven by a pinion and sector so a change key (cam on a cam shaft) turns it to the row holding the wanted character. A first press of an operating key turns a second cam shaft that slides the paper cylinder by a rack and sector to the right column, and turns a core shaft so a helically arranged pusher tube faces the chosen type. A second press drops the key lug into the cam notch, which swings the type cylinder close to the paper. An off-centre ejector bar then rotates and pushes the pusher rod, driving the type out onto the paper against a coil spring that otherwise holds it in. Horizontal and vertical pawls engaging a sawtooth wheel and rack register the cylinder, and ribbon or Japanese travelling inker may be used.

> Specification. Japanese-script typewriter. Nature and object of the invention. A second press brings the type cylinder close to the paper cylinder and pushes the wanted type out, pressing it onto the paper on the paper cylinder.
