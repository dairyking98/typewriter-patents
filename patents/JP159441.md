## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** European letters have different face widths but the typewriter's platen feed is fixed, so gaps between letters are uneven.

**Solution:** Label each type with its half-width in platen-feed units and turn a feed dial by the sum of the previous and next type's half-widths before each strike.

**How it works:** Each European type's face half-width is measured as a multiple of the smallest platen step (0.1 mm) and shown as a 'holding number' on a label under its case in the character tray. A dial on the front plate is numbered 1 to 73 in the same units. Before striking, the operator sets the dial pointer to the sum of the holding numbers of the character just printed and the one about to be printed. The dial shaft drives the platen sideways by that amount (using the mechanism of JP155610), moving the print point from the centre of one face to the centre of the next. An extra gap can be added to each sum for looser spacing.
