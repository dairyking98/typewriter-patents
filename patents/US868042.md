## Reading (full text, OCR poor)

**Problem:** On Underwood-type front-strike typebar machines, the escapement, carriage return speed and ribbon feed need to be simplified and made quicker, with the ribbon driven from the escapement shaft.

**Solution:** Drive the escapement wheel, a loose carriage pinion and an oppositely acting ribbon-feed pinion from one shaft, with a stop pawl pivoted on the frame so the carriage spring returns the key bar through a long throw.

**How it works:** Key levers act on a universal bar carrying a sliding escapement bar and a release pawl; the stop pawl is pivoted on the frame and moved by the bar, so the carriage spring gives the bar a long, quick return. An escapement shaft carries the escapement wheel and a loose feed pinion engaged by a spring pawl, so hand movement of the carriage does not turn the shaft. A second loose pinion with an oppositely facing pawl drives the ribbon spools through bevel gears, and ribbon loops trip latches that reverse the feed. A shift frame is lifted by eccentric pins that pass dead centre at both positions, with two shift keys, one latching and one releasing. Tabulator and column stops, a margin-stop bell and line-lock lever, and a removable carriage rod complete the machine.
