## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** In a front-strike visible typewriter, type-bars could not be removed individually for repair or replacement without disturbing the others.

**Solution:** Each type-bar pivots on its own pintle in an open slot of a common curved bracket and is held there by its own screw-fixed retaining device, so any one bar can be removed independently.

**How it works:** Stamped sheet-metal key levers hang on hooked cross bars and are linked to type-bars pivoted in slots of a curved rear bracket. Each slot is open transversely and has an overhanging portion forming a seat for the bar's lateral pintle. An angular (L-shaped) retaining device slides vertically on the bracket, its slotted beveled upper end spans the bar and engages the pintle from below, and a tongue in the slot stops sideways movement. One screw per device holds it; loosening it lowers the device so that bar alone can be lifted out, and screw adjustment takes up wear. The platen carriage is mounted on a support that can be raised or folded over the keys.
