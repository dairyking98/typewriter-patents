## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** To produce justified, typeset-looking copy on a typewriter the carriage step must vary with character width and font, and the line must be balanced, without the operator doing calculations.

**Solution:** A carriage switching device with variable step that serves both different character widths and different fonts, combined with an automatic line-justification device and a carriage return that retraces by varying distances per stop.

**How it works:** The opening describes the two known justification methods: typing a line, measuring the shortfall and retyping it with the remainder distributed over letters or word spaces, or typing an unjustified line in the left half of a double-width sheet and retyping it justified on the right. It discusses prior devices that enlarge word spaces by doubling the space step or by lever settings. The invention is a fully automatic variable-step carriage drive that can be used without special skill. The mechanism itself falls beyond the portion shown.
