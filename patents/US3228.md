## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** A writing substitute for people who cannot use a pen (the blind, the nervous) and a way to make accurate public records, with proportional letter spacing so narrow letters do not leave big gaps.

**Solution:** A horizontal wheel carries one key per character with type soldered to its lower end; the wheel is whirled to bring a key over a hole and the key is pressed down onto paper on a movable platen carriage, which is advanced by an amount set by each key's steady-pin.

**How it works:** A brass wheel with two rims turns on an arbor and carries spring-returned keys through its rims, each with type on the bottom and a raised-letter head on top. The operator turns the wheel by hand to bring the wanted key over a hole in the center bar, then presses it down through the hole onto the paper. A pin on each key strikes a lever and dog that turn a ratchet and pinion, driving a rack that pushes the platen carriage forward; the pin length or width decides where the dog is thrown out of gear, so narrow letters advance the paper less and motion stops just before the type touches. Ink comes from a roller in an ink box via cushions on the wheel's lower rim, which pass over the roller and transfer ink to small deerskin rolls that ink the type as it passes. Word spaces are made by pushing a lever by hand, and a spring-point index with perforated rows sets line spacing.
