## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Ordinary typebar machines such as the Remington and Caligraph have to be driven electrically (remotely, for printing telegraph use) without changing the machine itself.

**Solution:** An electromagnet-driven selector arm, stopped by electrically raised pins, positions a plunger over one of forty rods linked to the key levers, and a printing magnet's hammer then drives it.

**How it works:** Forty headed rods, in four rows of ten, pass through a curved cross-bar and connect through elbow levers to the key levers of a typebar machine. A selector arm on a shaft is swung to either side by two opposed electromagnets through multiplying segment gears. Movable stop pins (four each side) are raised in pairs by pin magnets to arrest the arm at one of several stations, choosing the column. Other magnets slide a sleeve along the shaft to bring the plunger (hunter) in line with one of the four rows. A printing magnet then swings a hammer against the plunger, which pushes the chosen rod and depresses the key lever.
