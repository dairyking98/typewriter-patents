## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** In silent (pressure, non-impact) typewriters the type-actuating mechanism must be adjusted toward or from the platen and fixed firmly, and the key levers must stop without impact or noise.

**Solution:** A bracket adjustable by opposed screws carries the type actuators, and cushioned stops arrest the key levers without concussion.

**How it works:** A bracket with a milled shank enters an opening in the machine frame. Two screws act in opposite directions: either pushes the bracket from the frame to set its distance from the platen, and the other clamps it rigid, so it is held fixed after adjustment. Sliding type bars on the bed are pushed to and from the platen by key levers through links and connecting rods to the type-actuating devices. The key lever is held up against a cushion that arrests it silently, with several alternative stop arrangements shown in later figures.
