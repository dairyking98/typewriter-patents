## Summary

**Kind:** type-element machine · **Subject (patent classes):** Typewriters or selective printing mechanisms characterised by the mounting, arrangement or disposition of the types or dies with types or dies mounted on carriers rotatable for selection the plane of the type or die face being perpendicular to the axis of rotation

**In the patent’s own words:** The object of my improvements is to simplify and reduce the number of parts of a type-writer and provide a practical machine that can be put on the market 'at comparatively small cost.

**How it works (reading):** A keyboard machine with a light flexible sheet-metal sector type-plate rotated by a slotted bar whose travel is set by where each key lever meets an inclined chafing surface, locked when the key enters the slot, and then struck by a single common striker via a hammer-plate.

## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Typewriters have too many parts and cost too much; rotating type cages have momentum that prevents stopping them instantly at the striker; a long strong carriage spring is costly.

**Solution:** A keyboard machine with a light flexible sheet-metal sector type-plate rotated by a slotted bar whose travel is set by where each key lever meets an inclined chafing surface, locked when the key enters the slot, and then struck by a single common striker via a hammer-plate.

**How it works:** A semicircular flexible type-plate with radial slots carries the characters on a vertical axle; two toothed lever arms (one per bank of keys) turn it through pins on a disk. Pressing a key lever first tilts a slotted bar so a connecting rod engages the lever arm, then slides the bar rearward along the key's inclined flange; the distance travelled (e.g. one-fiftieth of an inch per letter) sets the rotation. When the key end enters the slot the bar is locked and the type-plate stops; the key continues upward and flips a hammer-plate that drives a single vertical striker up against the paper through the ribbon. The carriage is spaced by a small spring, a fixed pinion and a ratchet pawl worked by space levers. A shift key tilts the carriage and striker frame forward on pivoted posts to use a second row of characters on the plate.

**Parts:** type-plate sector; vertical axle; disk with pins; toothed lever arm; slotted bar; inclined chafing flange; connecting rod; key lever; hammer-plate; striker; posts; space lever; ratchet; pinion; rack plate; roller carriage; shift lever; ribbon

**Numbers:** key-end spacing about 1/50 in. per character step; 26 letters need slightly over 1/2 in. of bar travel (26/50 in.), half when split over two sides

**Improves on:**

- rotating cages of vertical typebars: light flexible type-plate lowers momentum and is locked while struck
- conventional long strong carriage spring: uses only a spring to move one space at a time
