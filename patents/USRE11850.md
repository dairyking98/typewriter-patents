## Reading (opening only, OCR poor)

**Problem:** The original Fisher book typewriter needed an easier-to-assemble type-bar ring, a way to move the ribbon to show the line, a twist-free carriage, fewer parts and a simpler case shift and book holder.

**Solution:** A reissued Fisher machine with type-bars in adjustable clips on a ring, tracks that rest on the page and a hard-rubber platen inserted between book leaves.

**How it works:** A carriage travels transversely on tracks resting on the sheet or page, with a hard-rubber platen slipped between the leaves of a book to take the impression. One track is loose to suit the page width and the other is fixed to the platen at its ends by clips. Type-bars sit in upper and lower clips on a supporting ring, offset so lower-clip bars strike between the upper ones. Locking screws in radial and longitudinal slots adjust each clip for alignment. Draw rods link key levers to the type-bars, and pivoted type heads on the bar ends give upper and lower case, reducing the number of bars and keys. The front and rear carriage guides are brought closer to stop twisting.

**Parts:** type-bar ring; type-bar clip; locking screw; type-bar; draw rod; key lever; type head; platen plate; track; carriage; ribbon guide

**Improves on:**

- US569627 (original Fisher patent, reissued): easier type-bar mounting and removal, ribbon shift, fewer parts, better case shift and book holder
