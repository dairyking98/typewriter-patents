## Reading (opening only, OCR poor)

**Problem:** On Remington-type machines the operator must press the carriage shift key for a capital and then release it separately before the next lower-case letter.

**Solution:** A locking shift key holds the carriage shifted and is released automatically by the stroke of the very next letter key, so the carriage resets right after the capital is struck.

**How it works:** A shift lever connects to the carriage through a bell-crank and link, with its throw limited by a slot and an adjustable stop screw. The lever is pressed by a finger piece with two pads that tilts a piece on its pivot, engaging one of two recesses in a slotted plate to lock the lever down. Striking any letter key then operates a release that throws off the lock, so the carriage returns to lower-case position as soon as the capital is printed.
