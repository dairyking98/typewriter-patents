## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A typewriter in which one rotary type wheel prints across a line, with accurate positioning of each character, a pressing of the paper to the type, and proper spacing between characters.

**Solution:** A crank arm with a spring-loaded pin is turned over a graduated face plate (geared to the type wheel) and the pin dropped in a flaring hole beside the letter, which corrects the wheel position and also presses a platform that lifts the paper against the type; a separate lever then screws the type wheel sideways one space.

**How it works:** The type wheel slides on a splined shaft, geared to a crank shaft that swings a crank arm over a face plate carrying the same characters in the same order, each with a flaring hole. The operator turns the arm to the letter, then presses the vertically yielding pin into the hole; the taper centres the arm and so corrects the wheel's angle. The pin also depresses a platform linked to a frame carrying the paper rollers and cross bar, so the paper is lifted against the inked type (platen-to-element). A spacing lever turns a ratchet spur wheel through a pawl, which drives screw worms whose travelling nuts push the type wheel along its shaft one letter space; a stop pawl holds it. At line end a crank and eccentric release the stop pawl and a wound convolute spring returns the wheel. A separate pawl and ratchet turn the paper rollers for line feed, and an ink roller on an arm travels with the wheel.
