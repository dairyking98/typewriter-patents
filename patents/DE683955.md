## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** A typewriter whose type carrier moves in two perpendicular directions still needs many keys; earlier schemes using geometric-progression key strokes cut the key count but were hard to remember and needed several switchings.

**Solution:** Six keys, three for each axis and weighted 1, 2, 4, with their movements adding when pressed together, select any of 126 types on a type plate or cylinder following a Braille dot pattern, then a hammer prints.

**How it works:** Three keys on each side of the middle drive movement of a type carrier in one direction, the other group at a right angle. Under the keys, multi-part slides carry wedge-tipped frames: wedge widths follow the geometric series 1:2:4, so pressing keys together adds their travel, to a maximum of 7 units, moving the carrier by whole type distances. The slide parts act through a tongue and double-armed lever on the frame carrying the type carrier. The carrier, in front of the platen, holds the types loosely so that a hammer strikes the chosen type against the paper. A wide key returns the carriage, and the slide and levers return under weight and springs.
