## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Keyless type cylinder machines need much hand movement to set and print, and the index chart gets too small to read if the stroke is small.

**Solution:** One handle moves a swinging frame and rack to rotate and slide the cylinder, with a magnifying linkage that enlarges the pointer travel over a larger index chart.

**How it works:** The cylinder axis, with the cylinder overhanging the housing end, runs in a rectangular frame that swings about a horizontal pin axis guided by parallel brackets. A gear on the axis meshes a rack carrying the handle and a pointer that indicates the type on a checkerboard index plate. With the handle raised the operator sets the pointer, and pressing down prints. A knife in a comb and another knife in a star wheel give the exact setting as the frame descends. A felt roller inks the type. A pantograph-like linkage or sleeve with two racks enlarges pointer travel, and a left-hand button advances the paper.
