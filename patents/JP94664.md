## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Sugimoto-style tray typewriters needed a compact layout and accurate printing with right-reading characters, using ordinary market-bought types.

**Solution:** A pointer over a right-reading index board lines up a push-up rod that lifts one loose type into a sliding case; a crank rod then carries the case along a tubular guide bar, inking it, and at the end of travel the same push turns the bar to press the type on the paper cylinder.

**How it works:** The type tray sits directly under the paper cylinder, beside an index board laid out like the types. The operator points at a character; the push-up rod beneath the matching type is raised (by hand link or electromagnet) and pushes the type into a case at one end of a tubular guide bar, where it stops against an ink roller mounted on the bar's shaft. A crank rod then slides the case along the bar's track groove, rubbing the type over the roller to ink it. At the end of the stroke a roller on the crank rod's elbow presses the bar so it rotates about its shaft and prints the type on the cylinder. Releasing the link returns the case and drops the type back into its tray position. A gear variant adds a second ink roller and a hook that blocks the case if it is empty.

## Description (English translation of the Japanese text, opening)

> Specification. Crank-type typewriter. Summary of the nature and purpose of the invention. This invention consists of a case to receive a type pushed up from below the type tray; a guide bar along which the case slides; a crank rod that pushes the case along and further makes the guide bar print on the paper cylinder; and an ink-rubbing roller that limits how far the type is pushed up and also applies ink. In printing, a type at the bottom of the paper cylinder is pushed by the push-up mechanism into the case; the pushed-in type is stopped by the ink roller set directly above the case and settles in its set place in the case. The crank rod pushes it to the other end of the guide bar, and when the case reaches the end, the pushing force of the crank rod is turned automatically into the printing movement by the folding (toggle) action of the rod. Its purposes are to save space by housing the type tray directly under the paper cylinder; to print very accurately, with characters looked up in right-reading form, by a comparatively simple mechanism, raising efficiency markedly; and to allow types sold on the general market to be used as they are.
