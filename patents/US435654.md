## Summary

**Kind:** type-element machine · **Subject (patent classes):** Typewriters or selective printing mechanisms characterised by the mounting, arrangement or disposition of the types or dies with types or dies on spherical, truncated-spherical, or like surfaces

**In the patent’s own words:** This invention relates to the class of typewriting machines in which a printing-plunger has a convex printing-face, and is set to bring the different types into their printing position by key'levers operated by a key-board.

**How it works (reading):** A single gimbal-mounted printing plunger with a convex type-plate is tilted to the chosen character by a key-operated push-pin, then pressed down onto a short platen while the whole print head travels along the line.

## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typewriters needed a simpler way to bring many characters to the print point than a typebar per character, and a machine whose print is visible, whose platen is small and which spaces according to character width.

**Solution:** A single gimbal-mounted printing plunger with a convex type-plate is tilted to the chosen character by a key-operated push-pin, then pressed down onto a short platen while the whole print head travels along the line.

**How it works:** A carriage rides on a track with a short sliding platen bar tied to it so platen and carriage move together. A spring-raised oscillating arm hinged on the carriage carries the printing plunger on a universal joint; its lower end is a convex type-plate bearing several fields of types in parallel rows. A bank of spring-held push-pins sits in two perforated plates converging on the gimbal center, each pin under a key lever on a keyboard carried on the arm. Pressing a key pushes its pin down; as the arm is depressed a brace and rod throw the plunger top rearward until a guide meets the pin, which enters an axial channel, releases a latch and holds the plunger at the angle that brings the chosen type to the platen, and the plunger then prints. The keyboard shifts lengthwise to bring a different row of buttons over the pins, selecting another field of types. On arm rise a clutch-and-pinion feed on a fixed rack advances the carriage, its extent limited by a notched spacing lever so narrow letters space less (variable spacing). The ink ribbon on tensioned spools is swung off the platen after each stroke by a hook on the arm, and a ratchet roller feeds paper line by line.
