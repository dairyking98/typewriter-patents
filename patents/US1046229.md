## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** In silent typewriters printing by pressure rather than impact, any give in the platen support ruins print quality and reduces carbon copies; the platen also needs fine adjustment toward or away from the printing plane, e.g. for manifolding.

**Solution:** Mount the platen head on a rigid truss-shaped cross bar and adjust its distance to the printing plane with a sliding wedge.

**How it works:** A truss-shaped channel bar spans the frame uprights behind the platen and carries a head holding the platen. The head has an inclined face pressed by a spring-loaded screw against an oppositely inclined sliding wedge. A rod, adjusting screw and milled nut journaled in the frame slide the wedge along the truss, pushing the head and platen toward or letting it recede from the printing plane. A friction piece, leaf spring in notches, or a spring detent released by a push pin holds the wedge so printing pressure cannot back it off. An optional scale shows the platen position.
