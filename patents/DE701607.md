## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Printing telegraph receivers that set the type carrier by summing code movements through cords or a band needed the selector on the moving part or a fixed type carrier with a moving platen, so the moving masses were large and settings slow.

**Solution:** A light type wheel on a travelling carriage in front of a fixed platen is turned by a closed band whose length is changed by five laterally movable rollers, each adding a step count of 1, 2, 4, 8 or 16, so the wheel goes directly from one character to the next.

**How it works:** The type wheel body runs on a pin on the carriage and a thin spring-steel band wound around its drum is led over five selector rollers on the fixed frame to a spring-loaded reverse drum. Five magnets store the code pulses with remanent magnetism, and a control rail with selector arms pushes the chosen rollers aside, lengthening the band by the sum of steps. A conical centering pin locks the wheel in a hole of its flange while the band changes, then lets go, and the torsion spring and the stronger drum spring turn the wheel to the new position. The pin locks it again just before the print hammer, driven by a cam-cocked spring, strikes the rear of the type, whose long Z-ended extension hangs in slots of a tilting guide disc. The disc tilts so the type strikes the paper like a typebar, and the ribbon is a narrow vertical one. The paper roller stays fixed while the light carriage travels along rails.
