## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A kana printing machine that is simple to handle, can mix in kanji, and prints the same text on many sheets at once.

**Solution:** A turning disc of kana types is rocked on a hinged carrier to press a character onto one of many gear-linked printing rollers, sliding along a rail across the roller axis.

**How it works:** A flexible lower disc carries kana types in its underside edge and has one kanji hole; an upper disc with holes over each type lies on it, turning on an axle in a round carrier. The operator sets a plunger tip into the hole of the wanted kana, turns the disc to the print point and presses; disc and carrier tilt about a hinge on a support block and press the type onto the roller. The block slides on a rail along the roller axis, held by a pawl in notches under the rail. Many rollers hang concentrically from one shaft between two support plates, each geared to a shaft gear so all turn together, so each roller receives the same character. Kanji are printed from a separate type stick passed through the kanji holes.

## Description (English translation of the Japanese text, opening)

> Printing machine. Nature and object of the invention. A printing machine characterised as follows. A turning disc is made by laying an upper disc of nearly the same diameter on a flexible lower disc which has a single kanji-insertion hole at its edge and kana types set into the edge of its underside; the upper disc has holes, including a kanji-insertion hole, over each of the types and the kanji hole of the lower disc. This turning disc is fitted to turn freely on an axle standing up from the centre of a round disc carrier. A rail laid across the top rear of the two side plates of the case passes through the hole in the support block, so that the disc can move along the rail in the direction of the roller's axis.
