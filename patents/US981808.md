## Reading (opening only, OCR poor)

**Problem:** With a constant rotation of the winding spool per carriage step, the ribbon speed past the type field rises as the spool fills, from about 100 to 47 impressions per inch of ribbon, more than doubling the ribbon travel.

**Solution:** Vary the speed and rotation of the winding spool progressively with the amount of ribbon wound, so the ribbon moves a uniform length per step.

**How it works:** The carriage is pulled by a spring drum whose pawl engages a ratchet wheel linked to a bevel gear meshing with a companion bevel gear. That gear sits loosely on a shaft that can turn and slide longitudinally, held in mesh by a fixed fork engaging a groove in its collar. A sleeve with arms engaging a flange on the gear sleeve transfers rotation to the shaft. Longitudinal shifting of the shaft changes the effective drive to the spool as the winding builds up. Three modified forms are shown.

**Features:** [INK:ribbon-feed](../features/ink-ribbon-feed.md), [ESC:spring-drum](../features/esc-spring-drum.md), [ESC:pawl-rack](../features/esc-pawl-rack.md), [INK:ribbon-reverse](../features/ink-ribbon-reverse.md)

**Parts:** ribbon; ribbon spool; spring drum; pawl; ratchet wheel; bevel gear; shaft; fork; sleeve; ribbon vibrator

**Numbers:** about 100 impressions per inch at start; about 47 impressions per inch at full spool

**Improves on:**

- constant-rotation ribbon winding on Monarch and similar machines: winding rotation varies with spool diameter for uniform ribbon travel
