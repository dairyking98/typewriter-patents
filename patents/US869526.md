## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Front-strike typebar actions needed an easy touch at the start of the key stroke with fast typebar motion at the end, and typebar pivots that do not gum up with dirt and oil.

**Solution:** A key-lever link bears on a bell-crank arm at a contact point that moves toward the fulcrum as the stroke proceeds, giving progressively decreasing leverage, and the typebar hanger and heel have a narrow concentric bearing band with registering holes that expel dirt.

**How it works:** A key-lever pivoted at the rear carries a looped vertical link whose top bears on the long horizontal arm of a bell-crank sub-lever. The bell crank's other arm drives the typebar through a horizontal link. At first the link presses far from the bell-crank fulcrum for maximum leverage and light touch. As the bell crank turns, the contact slides toward the fulcrum, so about three-fourths of the key travel moves the typebar 45 degrees (half its stroke) and the last fourth finishes it. A rib in a recess and a roller on a pin keep the link and bell crank engaged. The typebar heel sits between two stamped cheek plates joined by a spacing and binding screw. Inner faces have annular recesses leaving a narrow bearing band at large radius, and concentric holes in plates and heel register as the bar moves, pushing out dirt and lint.
