## Summary

**Kind:** type-element machine · **Subject (patent classes):** Typewriters or selective printing mechanisms characterised by the mounting, arrangement or disposition of the types or dies with types or dies mounted on carriers rotatable for selection the plane of the type or die face being perpendicular to the axis of rotation

**How it works (reading):** A thin sheet-metal type disc with elastic rubber-typed tongues is turned by a lever swung over a notched letter table, and pressing the lever into a notch prints through a stamp and releases the carriage by one tooth.

## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** A simple, inexpensive index typewriter with automatic spacing and both upper and lower case is wanted.

**Solution:** A thin sheet-metal type disc with elastic rubber-typed tongues is turned by a lever swung over a notched letter table, and pressing the lever into a notch prints through a stamp and releases the carriage by one tooth.

**How it works:** The type disc has radial cuts forming tongues, each with a vulcanized rubber type, on a vertical pivot with a spring that holds it off the ribbon. A lever M turns a toothed segment geared to the disc pivot so the disc follows the lever; the lever's handle is moved over a letter table of two staggered rows of V-notched toothed rails, outer row for capitals and inner row for small letters. Pushing the handle into a notch depresses a disc, turning a crank rod that pulls a bent rod and toggle lever so a spring-loaded stamp presses the selected type through a slot in the inking disc and ribbon onto the paper. The same rod swings an arm that tips the escapement lever, so the spring-driven carriage advances one tooth with fixed and resilient blocks. Space levers advance the carriage without printing, and a handle disengages the lock so the carriage can be pushed back, winding the spring.
