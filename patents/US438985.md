## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typebar machines with a ribbon covered the freshly printed line, and having the keys vibrate the ribbon made them heavy to touch. Typebar alignment per character was hard to adjust.

**Solution:** A spring-driven escapement, released as each key rises, spaces the traveling platen and also feeds and vibrates the ribbon guide, so the ribbon is dropped to the platen only at the print point and the keys stay light.

**How it works:** Pressing a typebar key depresses a variable spacing bar whose arm releases a detent pawl onto an escapement ratchet disk, while another pawl is thrown off, so the disk is held during the stroke. The same motion swings a lever that pushes an upright piece down, tilting the ribbon-guide forks to bring the ribbon to the platen as the type descends. When the key returns, the detent releases and a coiled spring in a drum turns the disk, driving a worm wheel or a pinion that advances the platen carriage a distance set by the width of the character typed. This same rotation lifts the upright piece, whose pawl turns the ribbon-spool ratchet and raises the ribbon to expose the print. A sliding rod swaps the two spool pawls to reverse the ribbon by hand, and a stepped stop piece with set-screws sets each type's printing position on the bar.
