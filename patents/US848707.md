## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** On a front-strike visible Underwood with a two-colour ribbon, the operator had to strike a key to find out which colour band was in use, and could not switch the ribbon to a stencil (no-ribbon) position easily.

**Solution:** A stepped member on the ribbon carrier gives the ribbon-vibrating lever a long, short or idle stroke, selected by a red key, a black key and a middle position of one two-arm key lever.

**How it works:** At each type stroke the typebar pushes a universal bar frame, which rocks a lever whose arm lifts the ribbon carrier up to cover the print point and lets it drop. A plate pivoted on the back of the carrier has two steps with an opening between; the black (upper) step gives a short lift for the upper black band, the lower step gives a full lift for the lower red band, and in the middle position the lever plays idly in the opening so the ribbon does not move and types strike bare for mimeograph stencils. A fixed shifter bar with a vertical fork engaging a pin on the plate moves it between the three positions without hindering the carrier's rise and fall; three notches and a spring detent hold it. A rock-shaft with a two-arm lever carries a red key and a black key in front of the keyboard so a glance shows the colour in use. The lever and carrier also lift with the platen shift rail for capitals, with a cam-operated stop preventing overthrow in lower case.
