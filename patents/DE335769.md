## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** A strong spring-engaged friction clutch between platen and line-space wheel needs a lock to hold it disengaged; known locks needed a separate operating member besides the control rod, or ball detents too weak for the spring, or complicated switching mechanisms.

**Solution:** A pawl in the platen axis latches a control rod in the disengaged position, so a single push button moving the rod one way or the other alone disengages or engages the clutch.

**How it works:** A cone slides on the axis inside the platen sleeve and is pushed by a spring so that a lever spreads an expanding ring and couples the platen to the line-space wheel. A control rod in a bore of the axis, with a nose through a slot, carries the cone away from the lever when a push button on a tube slide in the handwheel hub is pressed, uncoupling the platen. Meanwhile a spring-loaded pawl swings up and holds the rod against pushing back, so the release is permanent. Pulling the button back brings bent tabs on the slide against an inclined surface of the pawl, which swings it down and frees the rod, and the cone spring re-couples the clutch. Parts are simple and need no great accuracy.
