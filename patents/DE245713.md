## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** Moving the ribbon on a type-wheel typewriter with a linkage from the wheel's drive adds friction and a heavy key action.

**Solution:** Let the free top end of the type wheel axis, as it moves to the platen, lift a tongue on a pawl-carrying rod, so the ribbon advances without any articulated connection.

**How it works:** A rod pivots under the machine cover plate, with bent arms carrying pawls that engage toothed rings on the two ribbon spool discs. A tongue arm in the middle of the rod lies above the top of the type wheel axis. When a key lever is struck, the axis moves toward the platen and lifts the tongue against a spring, rotating the rod and advancing the pawls one tooth. The rod slides lengthwise in a slot so that only one pawl engages, choosing the ribbon direction. The tongue is wide enough to be hit in every rod position.
