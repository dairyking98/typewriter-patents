## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Inventor wants a simplified typewriter in which the paper itself is aligned with stationary type, avoiding a moving type mechanism.

**Solution:** Paper rides on a sliding frame beneath a stationary type-plate; a stylus on that frame is guided through notches in a stationary letter-plate, and its other end pushes the paper up against the chosen type.

**How it works:** A base carries a stationary letter-plate at the front (a notch beside each letter) and a stationary type-plate at the rear with types on its underside. A paper frame slides on ways beneath and parallel to the plates and carries a rubber-covered feed roller and a stylus pivoted on a rod. The operator slides the frame/stylus until the stylus point is over the wanted letter, then presses the handle so the point enters the notch and the flat rear end rises and presses the paper (through an ink tape) against the type. A spring returns the stylus. A two-toothed pawl on the stylus engages a ratchet rack on the frame so that the rise of the stylus feeds the frame one letter space; the second tooth locks the stylus to the rack during positioning, and a deep press frees it to slide back for a new line.
