## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Typewriters cannot print on surfaces that will not go into the machine, such as maps, charts, advertising signs and books.

**Solution:** A small hand-held printing head with a knob-turned type wheel runs along a rail frame, and pressing the wheel down prints and, through a bell crank and pawl on a rack, steps the head one space.

**How it works:** A casing travels along a grooved guide frame laid on the work. A spring-raised frame on the casing carries a type wheel with a knob; the operator turns the wheel until the index letter is opposite a pointer, and lugs on the wheel slide in a slot to prevent turning during the downstroke. A celluloid plate with a single-type opening lets only the chosen type reach the paper, and spring-held ink rollers on the frame ink the types. Pressing the wheel down prints and pushes a bell crank that carries a pawl over the next tooth of a rack on the guide frame. When the springs lift the wheel, the pawl pushes the head one step forward. A catch with a beveled shoulder holds the pawl so accidental pushing cannot advance the head, and depressing the wheel and lifting the pawl over the stop frees the head to slide back for a new line.
