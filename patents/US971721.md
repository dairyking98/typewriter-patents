## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Front-strike typebar machines needed flat typebars with large, steady, adjustable ball bearings for alignment, a case shift that does not disturb the key leverage, and protection of the bearings from paper rubbings falling from the platen.

**Solution:** Flat typebars pivot on adjustable split-eye ball bearings on a curved segment that shifts vertically on parallel links, with a sloping top plate shielding the bearings.

**How it works:** Flat, goose-necked typebars carrying two-type blocks swing up and rearward, each on a row of balls in a radial race of a curved segment bar. The heel of each bar is a split eye whose two halves, joined by screws reachable from the front, press together a pair of conoidal rings that form the ball race, so wear is taken up by tightening the screws. Key-operated rock shafts drive bell cranks and bowed resilient links to each bar, with greatest leverage at the start of the stroke. The whole segment is carried on parallel links so it moves straight up and down for case shift, held in either position by an over-center spring and hand lever, with adjustable stops fixing both positions and a shift key. A fixed sloping top plate lies between the platen and the bearings to catch rubbings.
