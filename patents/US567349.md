## Summary

**Kind:** type-element machine · **Subject (patent classes):** Typewriters or selective printing mechanisms characterised by the mounting, arrangement or disposition of the types or dies with separate or detached types or dies

**In the patent’s own words:** My invention relates to that class of typewriters or printing-machines whereby one or more letters or characters, constituting part of a word or even a whole word or other representation of an idea, may be printed at the same time with a single manipulation of the hand to afford rapidity in writing;

**How it works (reading):** Radially arranged horizontal typebars in four banks, any number of which can be picked by their finger-pieces and then thrown together at the platen by one foot-treadle carriage.

## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Typesetting by typewriter is slow one letter at a time; the inventor wants to print several letters or a whole word in one stroke, pressing the characters into a metal sheet that becomes a stereotype matrix.

**Solution:** Radially arranged horizontal typebars in four banks, any number of which can be picked by their finger-pieces and then thrown together at the platen by one foot-treadle carriage.

**How it works:** Typebars sit in a removable frame in four banks converging on one print point, each bar as thick as its character is wide. Pressing a bar's finger-piece drops a stud into engagement with a shoulder on a reciprocating carriage underneath. The foot treadle drives that carriage forward and the picked bars fly on by momentum to strike the rubber-covered platen. On the return stroke bars on the carriage engage a lug on each bar and put it back, where a spring-held notch locks it. A funnel guide with one movable wall is pushed aside by the bar; its spring return then ratchets the platen carriage a whole or half space, using two pawls of unequal length. Lifting the platen allows upper and lower case on one bar.
