## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** On type-wheel typewriters with lower and upper case rows, the change between rows was not firmly guided at the stop, giving imprecise or jumping print.

**Solution:** A guide plate carrying the swivelling type wheel has an S-shaped slot with two end paths parallel to the platen; a bolt moved by the lower or upper case key sits in one end path or the other, shifting the wheel by exactly one row pitch while locking its position to the platen.

**How it works:** The type wheel (two rows, lower and capital) turns on an axle in a guide plate that slides in a tiltable bracket. The wheel is turned to the wanted letter, then the lower-case or capital key is pressed. The key tensions a bracket spring and releases a nose, so the spring tips the bracket and wheel onto the platen. A bolt on a slide engages the S-slot of the guide plate. For capitals, the capital key moves the slide so the bolt rides the S-curve and lifts the plate by exactly the row distance, then holds in the other end path. A sprung detent lever with a nose enters the notches of the wheel to fix its angle at the moment of impression. If the wheel is set between notches, the nose rests on the web and a linking piece stops the key from going fully down, so a misspaced letter cannot be printed.
