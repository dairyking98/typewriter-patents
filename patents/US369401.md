## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Existing typewriters are complicated, with exposed mechanism that collects dust, and cannot easily change type style or language.

**Solution:** A cased index typewriter in which a lever swung over a slotted character disc positions a wheel carrying a swappable rubber type-band, and depressing the lever pushes the chosen type out onto the platen and steps the spring-driven carriage one space.

**How it works:** The operator lifts lever L, swings it around the index disk K to the wanted character and presses it down into a slot in the disk flange, which locks the type wheel. A push bolt under the lever rocks an angle lever whose presser bar enters a slot behind the chosen character on the elastic type-band and pushes that type out through a guide slot onto the paper on the rubber platen roll. On release a spring lifts the lever, and the rising lever arm trips a two-tooth pawl lever (one pivoted dog, one rigid dog) acting on a rack bar so the spring-driven carriage advances exactly one letter space. Inking rolls on either side of the wheel ink the type. A blank space on the disk and band lets the lever be pressed without printing to make word spaces. The top is hinged and the bottom detachable for access, and the rubber band is swapped for other styles or languages.
