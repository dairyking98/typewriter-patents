## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** In a front-strike typewriter whose carriage and platen fold forward over the keyboard, the escapement must be split so it still works after folding, must trip late in the typebar stroke to avoid piling letters, and must release for free carriage travel.

**Solution:** A rack-and-pinion escape wheel with a fixed dog on one lever and a loose dog on a second lever pivoted to it, tripped by a universal bar that the key levers reach only in the last quarter of the typebar stroke, and both dogs can be held clear by a sliding release bar.

**How it works:** The carriage rack drives a pinion and escape (ratchet) wheel under spring-drum pull. Key levers press a universal bar late in their stroke, rocking a frame whose tappet moves the fixed-dog lever so the fixed dog engages the wheel while the loose dog, carried on the second lever, is withdrawn. On the key's return the levers move back, the loose dog re-enters the path of the teeth and the carriage advances one tooth until the loose dog meets an adjustable stop. The two levers are spring-held together but can move independently. A sliding release bar first rocks a bell crank that locks the fixed-dog lever out of engagement, then moves the loose-dog lever, so the carriage runs freely in either direction for tabulating. The escapement frame is attached to the foldable carriage bed, with the tappet and rocking frame staying with the key-levers when folded.
