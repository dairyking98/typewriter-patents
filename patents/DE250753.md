## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** On shift typewriters the shift key must be held until the shifted character has printed; existing automatic holding devices are complicated, costly and cannot be retrofitted.

**Solution:** A simple spring-loaded latch holds the carriage in the shifted position and is tripped by the universal rail just before the typebar reaches the platen, relying on the inertia of the moving carriage.

**How it works:** A latch member held by a flat spring catches the shifted carriage. When any typebar is struck, the universal rail moves and trips the latch just before the type hits the platen. The inertia of the carriage lets it travel only a tiny distance in that short time, so the print is still at the right line height. A deflecting nose on the latch prevents it from re-catching on the way down from a second shift position (figures) so multi-shift machines can use it. On machines where the typebasket tilts, a pull hook from the universal rail trips a trunnion latch.
