## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** A simple, cheap typewriter built as a hand device with a pointer.

**Solution:** A sliding carriage carries a hinged key-board lever with a type wheel turned by a cord pulled by a pointer against a notched index; pressing the lever prints and a pawl feeder steps the carriage.

**How it works:** A base has a ratchet bar and a printing bed shaped like a half-cylinder over which the paper lies under wires. A carriage slides on the base and carries a hinged key-board lever that is normally raised by a spiral spring. A standard at its free end carries a type-wheel turning on an axis, with a pin at the line of each radius. A cord in the wheel groove, pulled by a pointer or pencil, turns the wheel clockwise, and a spring in the back of the wheel unwinds it on release. The pointer is set in a V-shaped notch of an index along the front for each character. A heavy ink wheel with a felt band rubs the type wheel by friction. Pressing the key-board lever prints; a feeder with swinging pawl engages the ratchet bar and advances the carriage, with a trip released on return. A register and indicator show progress, and a signal tips upright at a set position near the line end.
