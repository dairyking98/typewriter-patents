## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Cash registers and calculating machines should not print leading zeros above the highest digit of the entered amount; earlier zero-suppression devices need complicated toothing, scanning or spring-driven type carrier movements.

**Solution:** Switching disks rigidly joined to the type wheels by hollow shafts are controlled by overlap pawls so that the type carriers in front of the highest digit are moved one unit from zero to the empty position, driven by pawls on the drive shaft rather than springs.

**How it works:** Adjusting segments set the type carriers through hollow shafts according to the entered value. Each segment tooth initially engages a toothed switching disk fixed to the type wheel's hollow shaft. Overlap pawls controlling the disks can be taken along in one direction when a digit 1 to 9 is set, but for digit 0 the disk is free to turn independently of the setting means in the other direction. Pawls moved by the drive shaft enter cutouts in the disks when the overlap pawls release them, and so move the carrier one unit to the empty position. The same disks also move pawls that lock the type carriers during the printing process.
