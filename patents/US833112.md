## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typebar pivots and hangers were costly to make and align, needed adjustment at assembly, wore out, and limited how many bars fit in the segment.

**Solution:** A thin flat sheet-metal hanger with a hardened round bearing hole and a guiding fin sits in a slot of the segment, and a folded U-section typebar straddles it on a hardened sleeve riveted to the bar, so alignment is built in.

**How it works:** Each hanger is a flat plate with arms that fit closely in slots of the curved segment and are held by a screw; the slots can be cut very close together so many bars fit in a short arc. The hanger has a hardened circular aperture and a guiding fin extending to both sides of it. The typebar is folded sheet metal, U-shaped in section, with its rear wall cut away to form a fork that receives the hanger and fin, the side walls separated by just more than the hanger thickness. A hardened sleeve (washer) fits the hanger aperture, its ends abut the inner faces of the side walls, and a rivet through the sleeve fixes it to the bar so the bar rocks on the sleeve. The type plate with the characters is fitted and soldered to the bent upper end of the bar.
