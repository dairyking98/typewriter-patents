## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** When the platen is shifted for upper-case type, the paper at the extreme bottom edge slips out of contact with the platen roller, so a line cannot be typed close to the end of the sheet.

**Solution:** A paper-holding plate with a slot for the type, linked to the platen shift frame, swings in a smaller arc than the platen so it keeps pressing the paper against the platen when shifted.

**How it works:** The platen sits in a swinging frame that is raised by a crank lever and rod from the shift key. A bar attached to the frame carries a plate with a vertical head that presses against the paper near the print point, with a central slot through which the type strikes. Because the lever fulcrum is forward of the frame pivot, the plate moves in a lesser arc than the platen and stays in contact at the paper end after the shift. The head also carries an auxiliary scale with a pointer, offset from the normal scale so the operator can still read the position under the plate.
