## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Type wheel machines with a vertical-plane drive lever are tall and need many intermediate links; a lower, simpler machine is wanted.

**Solution:** Carry the type wheel on a drive lever that swings in the horizontal plane about a vertical pin, so the same thumb-driven swing presses the wheel on the platen, spaces the carriage one tooth and passes the ink roller over the type.

**How it works:** The drive lever, pushed by the writer's thumb, carries the type wheel on its own pin. A rack on the lever, driven by a spring-loaded arm whose ball tip snaps into hemispherical recesses in a drawing board (chart), turns the wheel through the teeth on its edge to the chosen character. As the lever swings, a double-armed pawl on the lever advances the toothed rail of the roller carriage one tooth against a fixed stop; further swing is allowed by a slotted spring-loaded pivot so the wheel can press the roller without spacing again. At the end of the swing the wheel's toothing enters a fixed locking tooth to secure the setting. An ink roller on a hooked arm is swung by the same lever around the wheel's circumference, also touching an ink supply roller. A pushbutton raises the wheel (three type zones) with a stop giving two heights.
