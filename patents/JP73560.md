## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Japanese typewriters need many characters in a simple, small machine with easy character selection.

**Solution:** An endless chain of pivoting type bars hung above the frame is cranked round; a selecting rod on the print handle picks the character by its index letter, and striking the handle turns the type face to the paper, brings the ribbon and a rear hammer.

**How it works:** A chain type plate of many small type bars (characters on one side face, index characters on the top face) hangs on sprockets and is turned by a hand crank through gears. The operator moves a carriage with a selecting rod whose hole is placed over the index character of the wanted bar, which sits at the printing place with its handle in the notch of a type-turning lever. Striking the print handle lifts a cross bar; a lever and strut turn the type bar so its face points at the paper; swing levers push the ribbon frame so the ribbon meets the type; a pawl locks the chain ratchet; and the paper frame swings the paper close to the ribbon. A stop lever locks the carriage in a serrated bar, and finally a hammer bent in a U shape presses the paper from behind onto the type. Letter spacing is adapted from the ordinary European typewriter.
