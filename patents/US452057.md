## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** On the Remington typewriter the paper-cylinder shift key is awkward to reach; the inventor wants a convenient shift key on either side of the keyboard.

**Solution:** A key lever pivoted on a rod across the machine, connected by a rod to the existing shift bell-crank, with matching left and right keys and a pencil holder formed on the lever.

**How it works:** A key lever on a bearing rod at the left of the keyboard is joined by a swivelled connecting rod to the bell-crank that shifts the paper cylinder. Pressing it turns the bell-crank and moves the cylinder back; the usual springs restore it. The pivot end of the lever is extended laterally along the bearing rod to a second, matching key on the right, so either key shifts the cylinder, and the part between them is shaped as a pencil holder that can also be used as a key.

**Features:** [SHIFT:platen](../features/shift-platen.md), [USE:accessory](../features/use-accessory.md), [ELEM:typebar](../features/elem-typebar.md)

**Parts:** key lever; bearing rod; connecting rod; swivel; bell-crank lever; paper cylinder; pencil holder

**Numbers:** lever end extended an inch or two on the bearing

**Improves on:**

- Remington typewriter shift key: adds a second, easier-to-reach shift key on the opposite side
