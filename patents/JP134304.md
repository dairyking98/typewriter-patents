## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Earlier electromagnetic Japanese printers turned a type wheel by one main electromagnet, which the inventor finds heavy and slow in operation; he wants a light, quick Japanese-script printer.

**Solution:** Keep the Japanese types fixed in a partial cylinder of loose plunger-types and move the platen (sliding and rotating) over the chosen type, with a key-per-character keyboard whose row and column contacts set the platen stops electromagnetically.

**How it works:** Pressing one character key closes a circuit through its row and column terminals, which energises a relay and pushes out one left-right positioning piece and one rotary positioning piece. Two magnets then slide the platen base left and rotate it until stop rods hit the extended pieces, putting the printing point over the wanted type. When both stops are reached, contacts close the circuit of a striker magnet, which pushes the type up from below against the platen; an ink pad on a linked frame swings aside after inking it. Releasing magnets free the stops and a feed magnet steps the platen one character down the column. At the column end a contact moves the platen one line to the right and releases the ratchet so the platen rotation returns by spring.
