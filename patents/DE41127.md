## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** A cheap pointer-set index typewriter is wanted in which the carrier is positioned by hand and the printing and spacing happen in one pressing action.

**Solution:** A carriage-like index plate slides along a toothed rod; a pointer arm swings a pivoted rubber type segment to the chosen character, and one pressure lever presses that type onto the paper while a pawl advances the whole print head one tooth.

**How it works:** The index plate B slides on a toothed feed rod parallel to the paper roller and carries a chart of characters. A rubber segment bearing the types in reverse order pivots at the plate centre, and its arm ends in a pointer which the writer sets over the wanted character on the chart. Pressing the bracket f lowers the pressure lever e; its pin presses the chosen type through flat springs against the ink pads and down onto the paper, and a pawl on the lever steps the plate one tooth to the right, reaching the end of its travel before the impression so that printing happens with everything standing still. A metal centring segment with countersunk holes receives a pin on the lever and snaps the type segment into exact alignment. Pressing only the thumb plate lowers the lever part-way, so the print head spaces without printing. Releasing the pawl lets the writer pull the head back to the left; a bell on the segment strikes a hammer near line end, and a ratchet on the roller handwheel sets the line spacing.
