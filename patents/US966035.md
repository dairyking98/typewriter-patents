## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Back-spacing on a Remington-type machine must move the carriage back exactly one space and must not lock or break if the back-space key and a character key are pressed together.

**Solution:** A key-operated swinging dog engages a toothed wheel on the escapement shaft and rotates it one tooth back, with a hinged dog and spring so the escapement only temporarily disables it instead of jamming.

**How it works:** A rack on the carriage drives a pinion on a shaft that carries the escapement wheels, which a pawl on a rock shaft releases one tooth per key stroke while a spring pulls the carriage forward. A toothed wheel beside the escapement wheels is fixed on the same shaft. Depressing the back-space key lever pushes a link that first engages the teeth of a swinging dog with the toothed wheel, then swings dog and bracket bodily around the shaft, turning the wheel and pinion and moving the carriage back one space. On release the dog falls back by gravity. A transverse hinge in the dog and a leaf spring let it yield if a character key is struck at the same time.

**Features:** [ESC:backspace](../features/esc-backspace.md), [ESC:pawl-rack](../features/esc-pawl-rack.md), [ESC:escape-wheel](../features/esc-escape-wheel.md), [ESC:spring-drum](../features/esc-spring-drum.md), [IMP:print-lever](../features/imp-print-lever.md)

**Parts:** carriage rack; pinion; shaft; escapement wheel; escapement pawl; rock shaft; toothed back-space wheel; swinging dog; bracket; leaf spring; key lever; link

**Improves on:**

- Remington typebar machine back-spacing: a safety hinge lets the back-space and escapement operate together without locking or damage
