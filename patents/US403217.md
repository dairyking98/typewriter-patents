## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** The latch that starts the type wheel moves so fast (its out and back strokes take under a fiftieth of a second) that a key-driven trip arm may not clear the returning latch pawl, so the trip must be sudden.

**Solution:** A key-driven carrier with a pivoted snap pawl and a detent spring that makes the carrier complete its stroke instantly, unhooking a pawl on the latch.

**How it works:** A spring-driven latch in a revolving turret is hooked back on the frame by a pivoted latch pawl; a starting arm drives the latch back after each firing. A key pushes a sliding carrier down through an intermediate spring until the carrier's detent arm forces the detent spring aside, after which the compressed spring snaps the carrier down quickly. A pivoted snap pawl on the carrier strikes the arm of the latch pawl, unhooking it so the latch flies forward. If the latch returns while the key is still down, the snap pawl stands beneath the latch-pawl arm and simply turns aside on its pivot as the carrier rises. The patent says it applies as a wheel-starting device to typewriters like Dodson US299751, whose type wheels have independent orbital and axial rotary motions.
