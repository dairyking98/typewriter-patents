## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Typebars differ slightly in length from pivot to type, so the types do not line up at the printing point, and correcting one bar normally means removing it from the common pivot wire.

**Solution:** Give each typebar a pivot end made of two concentric eyes joined by a deformable neck, and bend the bar permanently to lengthen or shorten it while it is locked to the segment with a tool.

**How it works:** The sheet-metal typebar has a C-shaped slot around its pivot hole, leaving a bearing eye (threaded on the common pivot wire) joined to the outer body by a thin, compressed neck. To adjust a bar, it is swung to the platen so a notch in the bar lines up with an arcuate slot in the segment, and a bar-like locking tool is inserted to lock the pivot end rigidly to the segment. The adjuster then pushes the type end away from or toward the platen, permanently distorting the neck and changing the effective length. Only the one bar is touched and the pivot wire stays in place.
