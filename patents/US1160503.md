## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Stencil cards for addressing machines are typed directly by the bare type with no ribbon, so the impressions cannot be read; the ribbon must ink the back without spoiling the stencil cut.

**Solution:** A hollow segmental card-writing platen carries a card holder that rocks with the platen shaft, with a ribbon run over the platen behind the card and spooled back and forth by gearing driven from the platen's line-space movement.

**How it works:** A segmental platen with a celluloid writing face is fixed on the platen shaft, and a U-shaped card holder with spring clips is raised and lowered by arms on the shaft to line-space the card. A ribbon runs from an outer spool down over the platen face, between card and platen, to a second spool mounted inside the hollow platen. A pawl on the card-holder arms advances a gear train when the card holder moves up; a shift lever swings pinions to drive one spool or the other, so the ribbon winds back and forth. Stops on the shaft by the ratchet limit the small rocking arc and locate the top line of the card.
