## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** On a multi-row type-wheel that is raised to select a row and then thrown to the platen, the raised wheel can shift vertically during the strike and misalign the impression; the key leverage and inking also needed simplifying.

**Solution:** A spring-hung locking bar that vibrates with the type-wheel shaft engages a collar on the shaft in one of several recesses, holding the chosen row height during the impression stroke.

**How it works:** Separate shift keys press a lever whose inner end lifts the free-standing type-wheel shaft by different amounts to bring row one, two or three to the print line. As the key stroke swings the shaft laterally to the platen, a collar on the shaft enters one of three recesses in a locking bar hung from the cross-shaft, which locks the shaft against vertical movement and moves aside on its spring with the shaft. Key-lever tumblers with a long straight leverage that changes to a short cam leverage start the heavy wheel easily, and each tumbler's face distance sets which tooth a pair of opposed pawls catches on the stopping ratchet, with a steadying ratchet pawl entering the gap under the chosen type. An ink roller on a swinging arm lies in the wheel's path and is knocked out of the way as the type strikes. Sliding underscore or ruling wheels in boxes spring back from the platen. The wheel itself is hard rubber, moulded or blown with a knurled metal bushing in the hub and perforated top and bottom to save weight.
