## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** A platen-shift typewriter needs a way to hold the platen up for a run of capitals and also to capitalise just one character (such as the first letter of a sentence) without the operator releasing the shift key.

**Solution:** A spring catch locks the shift key down for continuous capitals, and a second catch locks it only until the next keystroke, when a release block tripped by the universal bar frees it so the platen drops back to lower case.

**How it works:** Shift key levers move the platen holder on arms carried by a rock shaft on the carriage, via a roller bearing on a rod parallel to the carriage travel, so shifting works at any carriage position. For a permanent shift, a spring-loaded catch engages a pin on the shift lever and holds it down. For a single capital, a catch piece pressed by a spring catches a pin on the shift lever when it is depressed. A spring-yieldable release block on the catch piece is pushed back by a pin on an arm of the universal bar rock shaft. When a key is struck, the pin drops and the block springs forward; on the universal bar's return, the pin rides up the block's cam face and swings the catch free, so the platen returns by gravity.
