## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** On the Oliver three-case typewriter, the platen-shift frame (paper carriage on a shifting frame) was heavy and slow to return to the lower-case position, and its escapement linkage was upset by the frame's movement.

**Solution:** Hang the shift frame from suspension links so it rises slightly as it moves away from centre, letting its weight help return it, and use a curved slot in the escapement yoke to cancel the rise.

**How it works:** Typebars each carry three types, in two groups either side of the centre line; two shift levers rock a transverse shaft that moves the shift frame (carrying the paper carriage and platen) forward or back from a central position to bring one of three types to the print point. The forward end of the frame hangs from horizontal pivoted links from the type-bar standards, vertical at centre, so moving off centre lifts the frame slightly; the rear end slides on a central rod. Because gravity then helps return the frame, lighter springs can be used and the shift keys start with little resistance. The carriage escapement (escape wheel, pawl, oscillating lever) is carried on the shift frame and driven from the universal bar through a yoke whose slot is curved to match the link radius, so the rise does not disturb the escapement.
