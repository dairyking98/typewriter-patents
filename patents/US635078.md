## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** The Remington carriage escapement needed a better construction by which the spring-driven carriage advances one tooth per letter and for word spaces.

**Solution:** A crown-tooth disk geared to the carriage rack is held by a latch-like retaining dog that is crowded out on key release, with a let-off dog limiting the advance.

**How it works:** The carriage rack drives a pinion on a spindle that carries a hub with ratchet teeth and pawl coupling it to a disk with crown teeth, so the disk turns with the carriage in the feed direction and is free the other way. A retaining dog pivoted so its shoulder moves nearly radially to the disk is thrown into the teeth when a key or the space bar moves the universal bar and its lever. Meanwhile a supplemental let-off dog on the retaining dog is carried clear of its tooth. When the key is released, the disk's pull crowds the retaining dog out like a latch and the let-off dog catches the next tooth and its stop, giving a single tooth of advance. An adjustable spring and stop set the lever.

**Features:**

- **Spacing and escapement:** [Escape-wheel escapement](../features/esc-escape-wheel.md), [Pawl-and-rack escapement](../features/esc-pawl-rack.md), [Space key or bar](../features/esc-space-key.md)

**Parts:** carriage rack; pinion; spindle; ratchet hub; pawl; crown-tooth disk; retaining dog; let-off dog; lever; universal bar; spring; stop

**Improves on:**

- Remington escapement: crown-tooth disk with radial retaining dog replaces the straight rack dogs
