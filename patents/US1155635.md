## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Platen shifting on a three-type-per-bar machine must be silent, positive and accurate, with the platen reliably held at the middle position and located exactly in both shifted positions.

**Solution:** Two independent shift levers each drive a cam member on a shift-shaft yoke through a roll in a slotted cam with dwells, so one lever shifts the platen up or down while the other stays still, and both rolls lock the shaft in the centre.

**How it works:** A rock shaft with an offset yoke carries two slotted cam members; arms and links from the shaft lift or lower the platen rail. Each key-operated shift lever has a roll that sits in the cam slot's dwell on the shaft axis, and a second roll that rests on the cam's outer surface to lock it in the centre position. Depressing a lever drives its roll along the slot, turning the cam and shaft one way; the other lever's cam turns it the other way. Dwells at the slot ends hold the platen exactly in the upper or lower position despite slight lever movement. Shift lever travel ends on silent stops made of a bent-back spring-metal strip with a felt face, and a spring (adjustable collar) centres the carriage.
