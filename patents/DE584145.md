## Summary

**Kind:** type-element machine · **Subject (patent classes):** Typewriters or selective printing mechanisms characterised by the mounting, arrangement or disposition of the types or dies with types or dies mounted on carriers rotatable for selection the plane of the type or die face being parallel to the axis of rotation, e.g. with type on the periphery of cylindrical carriers

**How it works (reading):** Make the shift from each starting position always the same one-row step up, down or none, with a shift of three row pitches between the two starting positions.

## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A six-row type cylinder with only a single shift needs the writing keys to move the cylinder by very unequal amounts (up to four row pitches), which makes a hard-working, uneven action on machines without power drive.

**Solution:** Make the shift from each starting position always the same one-row step up, down or none, with a shift of three row pitches between the two starting positions.

**How it works:** In rest position the second row from the top is at print height. The shift key raises the whole cylinder carrier by exactly three row pitches, bringing the fifth row to print height. Key groups then swing an arm left or right, which slides a cam plate sideways. A pin on the cylinder-axis carrier rides in the plate's slot, which is straight in the middle and at its ends, so the axis rises or falls exactly one row pitch, or stays put. The horizontal cam motion is converted to vertical, and the side thrust is taken by a ball-guided prismatic rail so the axis feels almost no sliding friction. A variant puts the start positions between rows so each key moves the cylinder only half a row pitch.

**Numbers:** six type rows; shift = 3 row pitches; key shift = 1 row pitch (or 1/2 pitch in variant)

**Improves on:**

- eight-row cylinder with single shift: key shifts varied up to four row pitches; here always the same one-row amount
- six-row cylinder with double shift: needed two shift stages; here single shift suffices
