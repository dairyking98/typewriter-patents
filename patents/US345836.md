## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** The inventor wants a typewriter where the printing is visible as typed, with few moving parts, and where one set of keys and arms prints several character sets (small letters, capitals, figures).

**Solution:** A fan of laterally hinged type-arms on a common shaft strike face down onto the paper in full view, with three types per arm selected by shifting the arm shaft frame, and inked by a roller instead of a ribbon.

**How it works:** Key bars on a shaft below the platen are linked to type-arms on a common horizontal shaft; each arm has a lateral hinge and a progressively larger sideways bend, so depressing a key swings the arm down and fixed directing bars guide all types to one impression point. Each arm bears three types (small letters, capitals, figures) in line along its length; a shift lever moves the pivoted frame carrying the arm shaft to one of three notched positions so the chosen type strikes the printing point. A short ink roller in a pivoted frame stands in the type path; the descending type inks itself on it, pushes it aside, prints, and the rising arm knocks the roller back. A paper carriage slides sideways, stepped by a pawl and ratchet with rack and pinion each time a key or the space bar is pressed, with a reversed-tooth stop ratchet limiting travel; fully depressing the space key frees the carriage. Paper is gripped by the platen and small rollers and rolled up in a cylindrical receiver; a bell rings near line end.
