## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Taking notes of speech at speaking speed with a kana-printing machine.

**Solution:** A spring-driven type wheel spins freely when any key is pressed and is stopped at the chosen character by a projection on its shaft meeting a projection under that key, after which a crank lowers the shaft to print while the paper table advances one space.

**How it works:** Pressing a key drops a key frame, which releases two stops and lets the mainspring, through a speed-up gear train, spin the brass type wheel and its shaft. The shaft carries a spiral of square projections, one per character, and when one meets the projection under the depressed key the wheel is arrested at that character; the wheel is inked on the way round by a cylindrical ink pad. Meanwhile a second spring-driven gear moves the paper table one character left via a rack and gear, and a crank-pin wheel lowers one end of the type-wheel shaft so the character prints onto a soft platen roll. At line end the operator pushes the paper table in, a gear meeting a bent wire turns the take-up roll to feed one line. Both springs must be wound by hand regularly.

## Description (English translation of the Japanese text, opening)

> Specification. Shorthand machine. This invention is a method of taking down speech in shorthand. When a key (字符, "character token") is pressed with a fingertip, the machine is set going and prints the character on paper at will; it is meant for taking notes of what one hears. In the attached drawings Fig. 1 is a perspective view of the whole machine; Fig. 2 is a view from above of the construction inside the box (keys removed); Fig. 3 is a view of the same from the side; Fig. 4 is the right part of a vertical section of Fig. 1 on the dotted line (一—二); Fig. 5 is the left part of the same; Fig. 6 is a view of the paper table from above. In these figures the same mark denotes the same part. The machine falls broadly into three parts: first, the keys set in a row; second, the rotating type wheel; third, the travelling paper table. The box (ゐ?) is of wood, made to suit the size of the machine. The top lid (て) and the left-side lid (あ) are both hinged. The top lid is opened to press the keys, and the left lid is opened to put the paper table in and take it out. The type wheel (ほ) is made of brass in the form of a toothed wheel.
