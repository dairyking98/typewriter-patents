## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** General improvement of the type-wheel typewriter: a compact keyboard machine with a type wheel that prints, spaces and feeds without a separate print lever, and handles common letter groups.

**Solution:** Keys of graduated length and shoulder position press a plate that turns the type wheel through two opposed racks to the chosen type, the same key stroke then releases catches so the wheel frame drops and prints, and the downstroke winds an escapement that spaces the carriage.

**How it works:** The type wheel (three rows, slid on its shaft by two graduated shift keys with a return spring) has a spur-gear hub engaged by two vertical rack bars moving in opposite directions. Each key has its shoulder at a different distance from its end, so pressing it carries a presser plate on the first rack down by a different amount, turning the wheel to that key's type; the second rack's plate then meets the key ends and stops the rotation. Continued pressing moves a link plate sideways that spreads the swinging catches holding the wheel frame, so the whole frame carries the wheel down against the paper roller, which has springy journals, and prints; springs restore everything. The frame moving down also trips a rocker-and-pawl escapement that lets a spring-driven pinion advance the carriage rack one letter. A spacer key presses the frame without rotating the wheel so a blank prints and the carriage spaces; a special key and ratchet space two or three letters when type with 2 or 3 letters (it, at, the, and) are used. A plain version inks directly from the type; a ribbon version needs no other change.
