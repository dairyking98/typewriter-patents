## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** In key-driven type-wheel machines the wheel is positioned by the key travel, so extra key pressure after the wheel arrives strains the mechanism unless costly compensating parts are added.

**Solution:** Put a spring as a link between the key and the type-wheel so the key can keep going down after the wheel has stopped at the chosen character, and use that surplus travel to lock the wheel, fire the hammer and release the escapement.

**How it works:** Each type button presses a broad lever that turns a bevel gear on a vertical shaft; the bevel gear is joined through a coil spring to a large gear segment, which drives a small gear carrying the type-wheel and a lock-wheel. Arms fixed to the segment swing round until one of them hits the shank of the depressed button, stopping the wheel with that character at the print point. Further downward travel of the button only winds the spring, while the broad lever strikes a second lever that engages the lock-wheel and works the hammer and carriage escapement. Release lets button and wheel springs restore everything. Variants put the spring on the button shank or replace the bevels with a spiral-grooved sleeve.
