## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Printing telegraph messages by hand on a paper web without moving the paper, in a simpler and more compact machine.

**Solution:** A continuously turning type-wheel is stopped by a key-selected pin with the chosen letter at the print position and is traversed along its shaft across a stationary paper strip.

**How it works:** The type-wheel slides on a sleeve over a shaft driven by a pulley through friction, with guide rods that turn it. Spiral-arranged pins on the shaft are caught by a stop pin moved into their path by the depressed key through a cam, halting the wheel at the right letter. A bar under the keys works a forked pawl that steps a rack bar, and with it the type-wheel and the inking roller, one space per stroke; a separate space lever steps without printing. A notched rack with a spring pawl holds position and a weight returns it to line start when a wedge lifts the pawl at line end. A padded bar beneath the wheel makes the impression and further devices feed the paper to the next line.
