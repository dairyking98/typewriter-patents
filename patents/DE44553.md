## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Improvements in the construction of a typebar typewriter: the way case characters are changed on the type rods, carriage motion, ribbon feed and line-end bell.

**Solution:** A ring of 26 type rods, each rotatable on its axis with three faces carrying capital, small letter and figure, pressed by push rods; rotating the rods changes the character set.

**How it works:** The machine head, tilted forward, has a lower ring with 26 bearings, each holding a sleeve that swings in a vertical plane. A type rod is inserted in each sleeve and can turn on its axis; its top is three-sided with capital, small letter and number or symbol in relief. A push rod with a button, on a wire spring, articulated to a tab on the sleeve, swings the rod down to a center printing point. A mechanism acting on conical wheels on the rods turns them to bring another face up. Beneath, a base plate with a lower carriage on disks and an upper carriage holding the paper cylinder advance letter by letter; also ribbon feed, bell, counter and a pointer for finding defective places.
