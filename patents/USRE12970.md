## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** On a typebar machine with a shifting platen carriage, the shift frame must move smoothly, be held firmly in both printing positions against vibration, and the carriage should be easy to remove.

**Solution:** Shift keys turn eccentric pins through a toothed sector and pinion so the platen shift frame is lifted with a slow start and stop and rests on dead centre in both the upper and lower positions, with a latch for shift lock and a spring to balance the carriage weight.

**How it works:** The carriage is split into a front portion carrying the platen and a rear portion carrying the feed rack, both swinging on the same rear rod; the front rollers ride on a rail in a shift frame hung on knife edges. A spring, adjustable by notches and a screw, pushes the shift frame up to balance the carriage weight. Two shift keys act on a rock shaft with a toothed sector that turns a pinion and shaft carrying eccentric pins; these pins move lifting bars connected to the shift frame, and at the end of each movement they sit on dead centre, locking the frame against accidental movement or rebound. One shift key engages a latch to hold the carriage up (shift lock) and the other releases it. The carriage rod can be slid lengthwise to free its pins from slotted supports and locked by a latch, so a carriage of another width can be fitted. Escapement is a wheel on a shaft with pinion engaging the feed rack, released through the universal bar.
