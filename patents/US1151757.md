## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Keyboard typewriters give uneven impressions because the blow depends on how hard and fast the operator strikes the keys.

**Solution:** Keys only position a rotary annular type rack (by notch depth) and then close an electric circuit, so a solenoid-driven hammer gives every impression the same force.

**How it works:** Each key lever carries an inclined notch of different depth; on first depression a finger on a shaft rides into the notch to its bottom, turning the shaft a limited, key-specific amount. That shaft turns, through a link, rack and pinion, a vertical shaft carrying an annular rack with spring type bars, bringing the chosen type to the platen. Pressing the key further pivots it on a secondary fulcrum, which trips a switch plate and closes a circuit through an adjustable resistance to a solenoid. The solenoid core jerks a spring arm and shaft, which drives a gear-segment and pinion hammer that strikes the type against the platen, then breaks the switch and resets. A centring arm enters a slot in the rack frame to hold it exactly, and a gap in the rack keeps the writing visible.
