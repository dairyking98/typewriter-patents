## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** The platen should be turned one or two lines by the same handle that returns the carriage, yet remain fully disengaged from the line-feed mechanism otherwise, so it can be swung up or removed.

**Solution:** A revolver concentric with the platen, slid by the carriage-return lever and turned by a spiral slot cam, carries a spring pawl that engages the platen's end ratchet only during the stroke.

**How it works:** The platen frame hangs on eccentric trunnions in open bearings so the platen can be swung up to view the work or lifted out. A ratchet wheel on the platen has its teeth on its circumference and ends, with a spring detent holding the line. The revolver is a cylindrical body journaled in the carriage nearly concentric with the platen, with an arm carrying a sliding spring pawl, and a stud that runs in a spiral slot cam. Continuing the carriage-return lever's stroke past the carriage stop slides the revolver, which both brings the pawl into the end teeth and, via the cam, rotates it to turn the platen forward. A pivoted stop gauge with two shoulders limits the lever to one or two lines per stroke, and a spring returns the revolver, leaving the pawl clear of the platen.
