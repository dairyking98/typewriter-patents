## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** On a stylus-operated type-carrier machine, getting both capitals and small letters from one set of marked holes needed a handy way to turn the type holder independently of the stylus arm; also manifold copies needed adjustable paper clip pressure.

**Solution:** A rack bar and gear turn the common type carrier by a fixed amount independently of the stylus arm, so the same stylus holes print capitals or small letters, and the paper clips on the roller have adjustable pressure.

**How it works:** A stationary perforated indicator plate has holes in a circle, and a horizontal rotating type carrier below it holds the types in a circle, with capitals and small letters as a double series. A stylus on a radial arm of the arbor is inserted in a hole, turning the arm and carrier and pressing the matching type against the paper on the paper roller. A gear on the arbor meshes with a sliding rack bar that shifts the carrier by one half-series so either case comes under the same hole. The clips that bind the paper to the roller are arranged so their pressure can be varied, for manifolding.
