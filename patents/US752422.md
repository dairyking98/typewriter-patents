## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** The inventor wants a cheap, durable, quick-to-repair typewriter that works well for skilled and unskilled typists and is faster than costly high-class machines.

**Solution:** A full-keyboard machine in which each key moves an adjustable pivoted deflector that shoves a reciprocating bar by a set amount, oscillating a type-wheel segment to that character, with a hammer printing from behind the paper.

**How it works:** Each finger-key lever is linked by a rod to its own pivoted deflector on a removable plate, and each deflector has a tangential finger that engages a pin on a reciprocating bar. Some deflectors push the bar one way and others the other way, by different distances, so a rod from the bar oscillates the type-wheel (or wheel segment) on its vertical shaft to the chosen character. A notch on the deflector locks onto the pin to hold the wheel rigid at impact. The universal frame pushes a bell-crank and rod to swing a hammer against the paper and ribbon behind it, and a spring-loaded wedge centres the bar again after each stroke. Case change is by key-operated levers that lift the notched wheel shaft one or more steps, bringing a different row of the wheel to the hammer, and the wheel falls back by gravity. A step-by-step rack and pawl, driven from the universal frame or a separate space key, advances a sliding paper frame, and the ribbon moves between two spools by a ratchet and double pawl that can be reversed by hand.
