## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** The earlier Shimada kanji machine (JP33067) was awkward to handle: the operator had to read characters reversed or guess the type position, and could not see the print.

**Solution:** Pointed-top types in spring-tensioned bands sit under a character board arranged in mirror image to the types, with a pointer on the moving frame, so the pointer always names the type that the plunger will print.

**How it works:** Types with pyramid tops are hooked or corded into bands held taut by springs in a moving frame that slides forward-back and left-right on rails. A character board on top is laid out reversed top-to-bottom and left-to-right relative to the types, and a pointer fixed to the frame shows the character under the plunger. Pressing the print button swings a lever that drives the plunger down; the type enters the conical hollow in its underside (the pointed top self-centres a slightly misplaced type), rubs over the ink roller below, and prints on the paper cylinder. A fixed needle through the plunger pushes the type out of the hollow as the plunger rises. A second button advances the cylinder via a slide bar, ratchets and a spiral spring, and a third swings a lever that presses down part of the band so the print can be seen through the gap.
