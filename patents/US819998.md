## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typewriters needed finger force for every impression and carriage step; the inventor wants a power-driven machine with a light key touch and high speed at low cost compared with other power machines.

**Solution:** A continuously rotating type wheel is stopped at the chosen character by a key-raised stop pin catching a stop arm, and the same friction-clutch slip drives a cam that swings the wheel to the platen and steps the carriage.

**How it works:** A motor turns a vertical driving shaft; a driven shaft, coupled by a spring-tongue friction clutch, carries the type wheel via a universal joint and spins at about 500 rpm. A ring of vertical stop pins, one per key lever, surrounds the shaft; pressing a key raises its pin into the path of a radial stop arm on the driven shaft, which halts the wheel with the character at the print point while the driving shaft keeps turning. The continued rotation of the clutch head's spiral cam lifts a cam slide, which pushes a rod and a bow spring that swing the wheel frame to the platen for an elastic blow, and the same rod rocks the escapement pawl lever to step the carriage. A spring bolt locks the slide up until the key is released so one press gives one impression, with a knob to withdraw it for underscoring. Two shift keys slide the three-row wheel axially on its shaft. The space bar raises a stop pin but swings the actuating arm clear of the wheel frame, so only the escapement acts.

**Numbers:** about 500 revolutions per minute; three rows of characters on the wheel; 37 claims
