## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** On small-keyboard typewriters where type is arranged in groups, shifting the carriage between several positions is noisy because the moving parts slam against their stops.

**Solution:** Shift the carriage to two or three accurately set positions, with each position stop formed by an air-cushion dashpot so the carriage lands silently.

**How it works:** A vertically reciprocating carriage is connected through a block and a double rocker arm to two adjustable pistons, each working in a loosely fitting dashpot. Pressing the cap shift lever moves the carriage until its piston bottoms in the dashpot, the trapped air cushioning the stop. The second (figure) shift lever drives the same linkage but also slides a cam out from under the movable dashpot, which swings lower and lets the carriage travel to a third position, cushioned by the same air effect. Springs return the carriage to its initial position against a third cushioned dashpot, and a pivoted shift-key lock can hold either shift lever down. The piston rods are screw-adjustable with lock nuts to set each position.

**Features:** [SHIFT:platen](../features/shift-platen.md), [SHIFT:triple](../features/shift-triple.md), [SHIFT:lock](../features/shift-lock.md), [ELEM:typebar](../features/elem-typebar.md), [FRM:adjustment-wear](../features/frm-adjustment-wear.md)

**Parts:** carriage; channel bar; double rocker arm; piston; dashpot; adjustable piston rod; sliding cam; cam roller; cap shift lever; figure shift lever; shift key lock; return spring; bell crank lever

**Numbers:** three carriage positions; two shift levers
