## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** The cylinder feed of a typewriter moves only in steps of the feed gear's pitch, so spacings other than a whole number of pitches cannot be printed, and a slightly wrong position cannot be corrected.

**Solution:** A finger-operated knob wheel with a small gear meshing the cylinder's large gear can turn the cylinder by any small amount, while one press of a finger-piece both brakes the knob shaft and frees the feed ratchet's pawl.

**How it works:** A small gear on a knob-wheel shaft meshes with the large gear on the printing-cylinder shaft. The normal vertical feed uses another small gear and ratchet on one shaft, caught by a pawl on a swinging arm. Pressing a finger-piece turns a carrier whose roller lifts a spring arm so it presses on the knob shaft as a friction brake. The same motion pushes a bracket that rocks a rod, sliding a slide whose bent end presses a pin on the pawl arm, lifting the pawl out of the ratchet so the large gear is free. The operator then turns the knob wheel slowly to move the cylinder any small distance; releasing the finger-piece lets a spring restore the pawl.

## Description (English translation of the Japanese text, opening)

> Specification. Fine adjusting device for the printing spacing of a typewriter. Nature and object of the invention. This invention relates to a fine adjusting device for the printing spacing of a typewriter. The shaft of a knob wheel carries a small gear that meshes with a large gear fixed on the shaft of the printing cylinder. When needed, a brake is applied to that knob-wheel shaft, and at the same time the holding engagement of the vertical-feed mechanism, which works at a set pitch of the large gear, is made to run freely. Its object is to let the printing spacing be adjusted and moved at will by small distances less than the set pitch, so that printing becomes possible at spacings other than the set pitch.
