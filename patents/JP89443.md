## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Collectors' receipts for money are open to misunderstanding, disputes and dishonesty; a device is wanted that prints the amount and words on a strip and shows them to the payer.

**Solution:** A boxed recorder with a turntable of radial swinging type arms and an outside index disc: turn the disc to a character, press a centre spindle to print it on a paper strip, and the release stroke feeds the strip one step.

**How it works:** Swinging arms (6), each with a type (7) at its tip, are pivoted round an umbrella-hub turntable (3) and held up by springs (11); an index disc (5) fixed to the turntable carries the matching characters in the same order and is turned by hand until the wanted character is at an arrow on the top plate. A centre spindle (18), pushed down by a knob (14) against a return spring (20), carries an arm (15) that hooks the selected arm's hanger (8) and knocks that arm down, so its type inks on a cup-shaped ink roller (43) as it passes and prints on a paper strip (23) lying on a rubber-faced stamp table (29). When the spindle rises, a feed pawl (16) on a second arm (17) turns a ratchet wheel (35) one tooth, and gears (36-38, 34) turn a roll (33) to advance the strip a set distance; the printed text appears under a window (32) for the payer to check. A blank position on the disc (arm and type removed) feeds the paper without printing, giving a space. A hand crank winds the strip onto a take-up spool.

## Description (English translation of the Japanese text, opening)

> Specification. Money-collection recorder. Nature and object of the invention. Many swinging arms, each carrying at its tip a type needed for recording a collection, are pivoted radially round an umbrella-frame (傘轆轤, the hub of an umbrella) turntable, and through a link and a spring their tips are always held up. A disc, fixed concentric with the turntable and marked round its edge with index characters in the same order as the types, can be turned outside the box. When a centre spindle, which passes loosely through the turntable and can rise and fall freely but does not turn, is pressed down, an arm on its lower end strikes down one of the swinging arms at a fixed position and prints one character on a ribbon-shaped paper strip spread directly beneath. When the spindle rises, a feed pawl on its lower end turns a ratchet wheel, and through the linked gears and a roll the paper is fed on by a set distance. The object is to print the required characters, show them in a window in the box for the payer to check, and so prevent misunderstandings and disputes over collections, and dishonesty by the collector.
