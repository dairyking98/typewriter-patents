## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Improve the inventor's earlier type-wheel typewriters: simpler, more accurate and more compact, with less wheel travel per letter.

**Solution:** A type wheel on a vertical shaft is turned left or right from a zero point by a double-faced cam pushed by key-driven posts, then arrested by a stop lever while a tripped hammer presses the paper against the type.

**How it works:** Key levers lift one of two half-length frames, each with a post whose inward finger strikes an inclined face of a double-faced cam fixed on the type-wheel shaft; the height of the lift, set by how much of the key lever is cut away, fixes how far the wheel turns, and left or right frames turn it in opposite directions from a centre zero. Letters are placed on the rim by frequency either side of zero (E, S, I, O, R nearest) to shorten travel. Every key also raises a rear frame that lifts a stop lever to catch one of two series of notches on the wheel's lower edge and trips a spring-driven hammer to strike the paper against the wheel. A spearhead spring lever returns the cam and wheel to zero. A feed pawl moves between two offset combs on the paper carriage, giving half a letter space on the way up and half on the return, and a spring drum with a cord pulls the carriage forward.
