## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Type-cylinder shafts that are square only at their ends need two exacting milling operations whose faces must match and be square to each other; the short bearings then wear under the stroke.

**Solution:** Use a drawn square bar for the whole shaft, guided in square-holed bushings at both ends of a rotating sleeve.

**How it works:** The type cylinder sits on a bushing with a square hole that is clamped on top of a square shaft. The shaft slides lengthwise in a sleeve that has square holes at top and bottom, and the sleeve is rotatable but axially fixed on a plate. A toothed sector engages gear teeth on the sleeve and turns sleeve, shaft and cylinder together. A lever and pusher under a pan at the shaft's lower end lift the shaft to choose the type row. The plate pivots about a pin and a rod swings the whole cylinder against the paper roller to strike. The long square guidance keeps wear small despite the stroke shocks.
