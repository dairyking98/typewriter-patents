## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** Backspace devices had many parts and long links, requiring large key force against the carriage spring.

**Solution:** Connect the backspace pawl directly to the key's lifting rod so it engages a ratchet tooth first and is then lifted by a driver to rotate the ratchet one tooth.

**How it works:** The pawl, with a notch and shoulder, is mounted by a screw sliding in a slot of a fixed plate. Pressing the key raises the rod, and a spring first swings the pawl into a ratchet tooth, the shoulder preventing too deep engagement. A driver on the rod then lifts the pawl, turning the ratchet one tooth to move the carriage back one space. A spring returns all.

**Features:** [ESC:backspace](../features/esc-backspace.md), [ESC:pawl-rack](../features/esc-pawl-rack.md), [FRM:few-parts](../features/frm-few-parts.md)

**Parts:** backspace pawl; lifting rod; driver; guide plate with slot; ratchet wheel; tension spring

**Improves on:**

- earlier backspace devices with multiple links: direct pawl on lifting rod, less force
