## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Hard-rubber type segments give imperfect letters, and earlier metal type wheels or arms were too heavy, so their inertia in starting and stopping was a problem.

**Solution:** A type segment whose face is electro-deposited copper or bronze, stiffened by a rim and V-grooves, carried on a seamless hollow openwork sheet-metal frame soldered to the hub.

**How it works:** The type face is a curved segment of hard copper or bronze electro-deposited on a matrix, stiffened by a backward rim around its edge and deep V-grooves between the lines of type (three lines of types). A conical blank is spun or struck from one piece of sheet metal, then die-formed into a hollow frame with diverging sides and a segmental edge, and trimmed with openings to lighten it. Its outer edge fits inside the segment rim and its inner edge inside the hub flanges, and both are soldered. The hub has a tubular axis. A rubber or leather strip at the segment end cushions its stop against a fixed stop. One or two segments may be used per machine.
