## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typebar machines need a quieter escapement, accurate lateral and longitudinal guiding of each type-bar at the print point, a way to shift that guide for upper case on a single-case machine, and a better carriage mount.

**Solution:** A cam-actuated two-dog escapement, a single common notched guide that locks each type-bar at the instant of impact, and a ball-bearing carriage rockable by raising a track.

**How it works:** Key depression moves a universal bar against a cam-bar that draws a rod and rocks the escapement lever, shifting the carriage from one dog to the other so it moves half a space, and on release it completes the other half. The cam shifts the fulcrum for quick re-engagement and an easing down that removes the sharp click. A curved guide bar has a notch that each swinging type-bar enters, while the notch bottom enters a recess in the type-bar so it is locked sideways and lengthwise during the blow. A ribbon guide carrier shifts for upper case, and the carriage rolls on ball-bearing rollers.
