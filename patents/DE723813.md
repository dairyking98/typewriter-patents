## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Guide jaws of magnetic type-setting jigs (used to hold types while soldering them onto type levers) are made of magnetisable steel, or of V2a steel that is hard to work and attacked by soldering acid.

**Solution:** Make the guide jaws of soapstone (ceramic), which is easily machined, non-magnetic and indifferent to solder and flux.

**How it works:** An iron core with a coil sits in the lower part of a jig; its flat top fits a rectangular opening in the upper part. Soapstone guide jaws, screwed into recesses of the upper part, guide the type laterally while magnetic force pulls it down onto the type lever. A directional comb on the core engages a recess in the type for alignment.

The guide jaws of the type setting devices of this type passed ; mostly made of ordinary steel. This material has the disadvantage that it is magnetizable and is not indifferent to solder and flux. It was therefore posed to this Defects. to eliminate, the guide jaws made of V 2a steel. V 2a steel is difficult to be processed and is also against the hydrochloric acid, which mainly forms the soldering water not completely invulnerable, so that it is in the case of soldering devices with guide jaws from this material also requires special care in order to avoid that Soldering fluid reaches the guide jaws. In order to eliminate these disadvantages, are according to the invention Guide jaws made of soapstone, which are easy to machine and non-magnetizable and is completely indifferent to solder and flux.
