## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Shifting the carriage or platen of a front-strike Royal machine needs a light, even key touch, a latch holding the lower-case position, and a shift lock, all in little space and without long key levers.

**Solution:** A forward-pulling draw-bar linkage acting as a reverse (breaking) toggle first withdraws a latch and then lifts the carriage, with a spring counterbalance and a friction lock for the shifted position.

**How it works:** The carriage rides on parallel links at each side with vertical links at their ends, and a latch normally locks it in the lower position. A shift key at either side of the keyboard rocks a front shaft carrying an arm. Depressing the arm first draws a link forward to pull the latch off, then draws a second hooked link that turns a rock shaft and lifts the carriage. The links act as a reverse toggle, so resistance is smallest while the carriage inertia is being overcome and the carriage accelerates as the pivot passes the line. A spring balances the carriage weight. An eccentric lug on the arm can be caught by a pivoted lever to lock the shift, and pressing the right-hand shift key lets a light spring release it. Adjustable stop screws set the lower and upper positions.

**Features:** [SHIFT:platen](../features/shift-platen.md), [SHIFT:lock](../features/shift-lock.md), [ELEM:typebar](../features/elem-typebar.md), [FRM:writing-visible](../features/frm-writing-visible.md), [FRM:adjustment-wear](../features/frm-adjustment-wear.md)

**Parts:** shift key; rock shaft; latch; reverse toggle link; draw link; counterbalance spring; carriage support link; adjustable stop screw; locking lever; eccentric lug

**Improves on:**

- ordinary long shift key levers: a forward draw bar and toggle give a lighter, more uniform touch in less space and allow a key on each side
