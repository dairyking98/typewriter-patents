## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Typebar pivots in a front-strike machine need accurate, adjustable alignment, and fixed guides at the print point obscure the line of writing.

**Solution:** Mount each typebar between two adjustable ball bearings, and carry the print-point guide and ribbon guides on a block that swings in only at the instant of impression, driven by the universal bar.

**How it works:** Typebars sit in a vertical plate, each held between a pair of balls resting in recesses in the plate and in the bar sides. Pivoted blades behind the balls, pressed by adjusting screws through a cover plate, tighten or loosen the bearings, giving a spring effect; releasing the screws lets a bar be withdrawn. When a typebar's heel strikes the semicircular universal bar, it moves back and through a rock shaft, links and a member pulls a pivoted guide block to the rear. Guide balls between two guide posts on the block then align the type as it reaches the platen, and the ribbon guide fingers move with it. A spring returns the parts so the printed line is visible again.
