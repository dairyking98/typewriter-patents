## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** The carriage of an electric typewriter must be returned and line-spaced after each line.

**Solution:** An electromechanical carriage reverse mechanism: the same impulse that energizes the type-lever magnets drives the letter spacing, and carriage recoil shifts the rack bar and turns the paper roll one line.

**How it works:** Type-levers in a ring are pulled by chains from armatures of electromagnets, each closed by a radial key lever. A letter-spacing motor acts on a rack bar of the traveling carriage. For reversal, a shifting mechanism moves the rack bar so the carriage returns; the recoil of the carriage actuates a pawl lever that rotates the paper roll one line space at the start of the next line.

**Features:** [IMP:electric](../features/imp-electric.md), [ESC:carriage-return](../features/esc-carriage-return.md), [ESC:pawl-rack](../features/esc-pawl-rack.md), [PAP:line-space](../features/pap-line-space.md), [ELEM:typebar](../features/elem-typebar.md), [SEL:keyboard-full](../features/sel-keyboard-full.md)

**Parts:** circular base; type lever; electromagnet; armature; key lever; letter-spacing motor; rack bar; pawl lever; paper roll; ratchet

**Improves on:**

- [US369954](../patents/US369954.md): letter spacing driven by the same impulse as the type magnets, not a local source
