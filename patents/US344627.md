## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Existing typewriters need two hands and the writing is hidden while typing; the inventor wants the type-selecting and spacing devices concentrated in one plane round a common centre.

**Solution:** A rotating turret holds a ring of radial spring-returned type plungers, turned by a cord-driven pulley to a ring dial, with a push-down striker to print and a push for letter and word spacing.

**How it works:** A driving pulley on a hollow spindle turns the type-turret by a cord wrapped round the turret's central tube, the pulley-to-turret diameter ratio being about 4 to 6. The turret holds a full alphabet, numerals and signs in one plane, each type a hollow radial body slid in the turret with a withdrawing spring so only its face projects at rest. A ring dial lettered with the characters is set to the wanted type and the type is pushed out to print. Types lift out singly, so a damaged type or the whole font (Roman, italic, Greek, music) can be exchanged. A duplex two-tier turret with intermediate striker adds a second row, and an inking and spacing mechanism sits in the same centre.
