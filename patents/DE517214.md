## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Invoicing and booking machines combine a calculating machine with a type-cylinder typewriter, but fitting the cylinder, letter board and setting pointer beside the adding machine's printing unit was awkward.

**Solution:** A parallelogram lever linkage, a swinging slider and a rack carry the setting pointer's motion to a vertical type cylinder placed right beside the adding machine's printing unit.

**How it works:** The operator's left hand moves a setting pointer over a letter board left of the ten-key adding machine. A ball-jointed arm drives a parallelogram of levers that moves a slider sideways, and through a push rod with a rack turns a gear on the vertical type cylinder shaft so the chosen type faces the print point. Moving the pointer forward also swings lever arms on a cross shaft, raising or lowering the cylinder to the right row. The right hand then depresses a write key, which through a lever train and link pushes the cylinder shaft fitting against the platen; side spurs in guide combs fix the height and a locking wheel on a rail fixes the radial position. The same print stroke trips a carriage switch lock (loose and fixed tooth on a ratchet wheel) so the paper carriage steps one tooth, while a separate key spaces without printing. A set of riders and pawls switches carriage stepping between the typewriter and the calculating machine's column work.
