## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** A key-driven typewriter needs many characters from few keys and a compact, light, cheap construction, with the type held rigidly at the print point and the writing visible.

**Solution:** Two rows of keys turn a single type-wheel shaft in opposite directions from a home position (full stop), a lock stops it at the chosen type, and a hammer lifts a small platen to press the paper against the wheel, while three axially shiftable type-wheels give lower case, capitals and figures.

**How it works:** Each key tilts an inclined cross-bar by an amount depending on its position; the bar works a lever and an oscillating toothed segment that meshes with a mutilated pinion on the type-wheel shaft, so one key row turns the shaft one way and the other row the other way. The same key also pushes a bell-crank and radial locking lever into the path of stop arms on the shaft, arresting it with the chosen type over the platen. A return spring brings the shaft home and locks it at the full stop. A hand lever with a shifting bail slides three type-wheels along splined sleeves so that the wanted wheel is keyed to the shaft; the inking wheels lift while the wheels shift. The key motion releases a spring-plate hammer that hits a small vertically sliding platen, lifting the paper against the type; the same lever steps an escapement wheel that advances a spring-driven paper carriage, and the space key does so with a part dip that does not strike.
