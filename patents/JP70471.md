## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Machines holding a great many types (Japanese script) are hard to work lightly, quickly and accurately by finger pressure alone, and motor drives are less nimble.

**Solution:** One main electromagnet with two movable iron cores moves a cylindrical type wheel along its axis and turns it to the chosen type, then the spare force of the core drives a spring hammer to print.

**How it works:** A flat keyboard laid out like the wheel's rows and columns closes one travel-stop switch and one rotation-stop switch per key via crossed wires. Stop magnets set a catch for the axial travel and a stop bar for the rotation, a change-over magnet picks left or right rotation, and relays then energise the main magnet. Its travel core slides the cylindrical type wheel along its axis until a cross-shaped catch hits the set stop; the rotating core pulls a stud through a spiral helix tube that turns the wheel through a planetary gear train until a selector bar is stopped by the set stop bar. The core's remaining stroke drives a rack that cocks and releases a spring hammer, striking the type through paper and ribbon from behind. An automatic breaker cuts the main circuit at the strike so keyboard contacts do not spark, and a return magnet resets the cores.

## Description (English translation of the Japanese text, opening)
