## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A Japanese typewriter should print vertical and horizontal writing and several typefaces without changing machines, and let the operator find characters quickly.

**Solution:** Each type body carries both vertical and horizontal character forms on one side, with an index character on its top; pressing a handle pushes the chosen type up on a leg into a type-holding plate where a hammer behind the paper strikes it, and vertical or horizontal writing is chosen merely by raising or lowering the platen.

**How it works:** Type bodies stand in a type case, each with a supporting leg whose arrow-nock tip passes through a hole in the case. A support frame moves left-right on balls and forward-back on a moving frame to bring an actuating rod under the wanted type. Pressing a handle turns a shaft and arm; rods and levers lift the actuating rod, which pushes the type up until hooks on the type-holding plate enter holes in the type body and studs steady it. A guard plate shields the index character from ink. The same arm motion pulls a rod that turns a pinion on a rack and swings a hammer behind the platen, striking the holding plate and impressing the character. Platen height, borrowed from European typewriters' shift, selects vertical or horizontal forms.
