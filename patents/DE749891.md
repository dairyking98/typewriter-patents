## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Type-wheel typewriters with electromagnetic printing paid attention only to setting the wheel, not to controlling the magnet that swings the wheel to the platen, so writing speed stayed low.

**Solution:** A motor-driven spiked roller is clutched to the type wheel until a struck key's stop halts it, and the resulting clutch lag closes a contact that makes a relay swing the wheel against the platen, after which both springs return to rest.

**How it works:** Each key lever has a stop that enters the path of its own spike on a spiral-arranged spiked roller. Striking the key closes a contact so an electromagnet engages a clutch between the high-speed motor and the roller. The roller and type wheel (through gears) turn until the key stop halts them, while the clutch keeps turning and closes a contact. This energises a relay that pivots the type wheel carrier against the platen. At the end of the swing the clutch magnet circuit is broken, and springs return the roller and wheel to home while the key lever is released. A shift key raises the wheel axially between two rims for capitals, and the relay also steps the carriage. Optional adding, subtracting and multiplication units couple to the spiked roller.
