## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Removable type capsules for calculating machines let types, springs or guide parts fall out when the capsule is taken off, and the type rows could not be aligned to print in a straight line.

**Solution:** A milled-from-solid removable type capsule holds the types captive with two springs acting on a common return rail plus a guide rod, and is clamped through a slotted hole so it can be shifted up or down to align the type rows.

**How it works:** Individual types slide in rectangular transverse recesses of the capsule, each with a shoulder against which a return rail lies across all types. Two springs inside the capsule press on the rail with one leg and are anchored in bores with the other; their bent ends reach between neighbouring types and also locate the rail lengthwise. When a type is pushed to the stop its shoulder pushes the rail back, compressing one spring (or both, for a middle type), and the springs return the type to rest. A guide rod in a longitudinal recess of the front wall, held by a bent end in a bore, also guides the types and stops anything falling out. The capsule hooks onto a cranked tongue at its lower end and is held by a screw through an elongated hole at the top, so it can be moved up or down for line straightness.
