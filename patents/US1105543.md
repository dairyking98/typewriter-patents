## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** A front-strike typebar action needs a light starting touch yet must deliver a heavy impression for manifolding, and the key levers should be removable from the front.

**Solution:** A key lever works the type bar through an intermediate lever and link that gives the bar its initial throw, while a toothed disk meshing with gear teeth on the bar's heel adds a powerful finishing stroke.

**How it works:** Each key lever pivots on a rear rod and slides on a pin in the forked front arm of a two-armed intermediate lever, held back by a tension spring with an adjusting screw. Depressing the key swings the intermediate lever's vertical arm forward, moving a link in a straight line (guided by a pin-and-slot connection to a toothed disk) whose tip strikes a shoulder on the type bar and throws it upward. As the bar moves, gear teeth on its heel mesh with the toothed disk, which rotates anticlockwise and, if the key stroke is followed through, gives extra pressure on the platen. A quick stroke can drive the bar to the platen by the first impulse alone. Release lets the spring and rebound return everything; a ribbon guide slide is lifted by a universal bar beneath the key levers.
