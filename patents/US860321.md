## Reading (opening only, OCR poor)

**Problem:** Typewriters need the operator to return the carriage or touch the platen to start a new line; earlier horizontal platens were returned to a common printing point.

**Solution:** Use a vertical platen that rotates about a vertical axis, always in the same direction, stepping under a spring motor with each key stroke and rising to start each new line.

**How it works:** The paper is clamped around a vertical platen carried on a rotatable vertical bar. A toothed wheel with about as many teeth as printing units in the longest line (say seventy) is driven by a spring-motor gear train held back by an escapement. Every typebar or space bar stroke vibrates a dog arm and lets the wheels advance one tooth, rotating the platen one letter space. At the end of the line the platen moves up to present the paper for the next line, so no carriage return is needed. A knob and clutch allow independent platen rotation, and a ribbon-vibrating device works from the key frame.
