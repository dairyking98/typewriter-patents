## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** On pointer-and-index plate machines the hand that moves the index lever cannot also print; the other hand must work the print key, leaving no hand free for spacing.

**Solution:** Put a push-button on the end of the index lever that closes a battery circuit to an electromagnet which works the print lever and hammer.

**How it works:** An index lever travels over a character index and, through a segmental gear and a second gear, oscillates a flexible-type plate or wheel to bring the chosen type opposite a hammer. A forked hammer lever, kept normally retracted by a spring, is thrown forward when the print-key lever is pressed: the key lever raises a second lever that rides up an incline on a plate attached to the hammer arm, so the hammer strikes the back of the type and prints, then drops off the incline end and returns clear of the slots. In the improvement an electromagnet's armature lies over the print-key lever. A push-button on the handle of the index lever, wired along the lever to the magnet and a battery, energises it, so the same hand selects and prints. A blank index position (type omitted) would feed the paper without printing, replacing the space lever.
