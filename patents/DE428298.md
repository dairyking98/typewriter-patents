**How it works (reading):** Redraw the letters so all have the same width and similar ink area: block letters, with narrow letters given wide serifs or overstrokes and m, w and M shortened in the middle.

## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typewriter fonts have varied letter widths but fixed carriage advance, causing uneven gaps, wasted space and clogged wide letters.

**Solution:** Redraw the letters so all have the same width and similar ink area: block letters, with narrow letters given wide serifs or overstrokes and m, w and M shortened in the middle.

**How it works:** Wide letters are drawn as block capitals; i, l, t, f get horizontal over- and understrokes widening them to the width of n, o, h, p. Letters with more than two verticals have their middle parts shortened. All letters then share one width, less than the usual advance. Type width is stated as about 1.4 to 1.8 mm against 2.2 to 2.5 mm before. Narrower type also shrinks a typebar segment and speeds typing, and the lighter wide letters foul less.
