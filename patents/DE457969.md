## Summary

**Kind:** a part or mechanism (any kind of machine) · **Subject (patent classes):** Locks for subsidiary keys, e.g. for shift keys

**How it works (reading):** The shift lock is released not by character keys but by a harder than normal stroke of the space key, so the platen can be locked in shift and still space freely.

## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** On machines where the shift lock was triggered by the first character stroke, the shift key had to be held down for the entire writing, while the space key worked in either position.

**Solution:** The shift lock is released not by character keys but by a harder than normal stroke of the space key, so the platen can be locked in shift and still space freely.

**How it works:** The shift key lever turns a double lever that spaces the carriage through a lever on the space switching arm and lifts the platen frame through a link. A torsion-spring pawl snaps into a detent on a stop part of the platen lift lever when it is raised, locking the shift. The space key lever drives a levers train that includes a pin in a slot of the pawl. On a normal stroke the pin moves freely in the slot, so spacing works in both positions. On a harder stroke the pin reaches the slot end and lifts the pawl, releasing the lock.
