## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** A disc-type typewriter needs a simple key-driven way to turn the type disc to the right character, lock it during printing, change tiers, and ink the type, in the line of the inventor's earlier US357090.

**Solution:** Character keys move vertical levers at different distances from the fulcrum of a pair of lever-segments, so the stroke turns a pinion on the type disc by a character-dependent angle, then locks the disc while the paper roll oscillates against the type.

**How it works:** A type disc (or segment) with three tiers of type on its periphery is keyed to a pinion and a locking disc on a fixed vertical shaft. Each character key moves a vertical lever against a horizontal lever carrying a gear segment; the nearer the vertical lever is to the fulcrum, the shorter the motion, so each key turns the pinion, and so the disc, a different amount. Keys on the right or left side shift the matching segment into mesh and release the other. The same key stroke tilts the paper-carrying roll toward the type, and a lever carries an ink roller up across the type face then out of the way before the impression, while a locking lever enters a slot in the locking disc to hold the disc during printing. Two levers slide the type disc down its shaft to bring the middle or upper tier to the print point. An escape pawl driven by any key frame or the spacing lever advances the spring-driven carriage one letter, and a push-button disengages the carriage pinion for return.
