## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A very simple, inexpensive hand-held type-wheel writer that gives good alignment and even impressions.

**Solution:** A small carriage on a guide rod carries a thumb-turned type wheel on a centrally pivoted arm; pressing the wheel down locks a notch on a pin, prints through a slot in a spring foot, and the same rocking motion advances the carriage one tooth.

**How it works:** A carriage slides on a guide rod fixed at the edge of a base plate and has a curved arm carrying a spindle with a thumb button at one end and a type wheel at the other. The wheel has a flange with notches, each opposite one type, and the characters repeated on the flange inside as a guide; the wheel is turned by the button until the wanted letter and notch lie over a vertical guide pin on a spring-metal foot. Pressing down on the button locks the notch on the pin, pushes the type through a printing slot in the foot (which also shields the paper from neighbouring types) onto the paper on the felt-covered platen, and the spring foot lifts the carriage again. A gravity pawl on a rear heel piece of the centrally pivoted carriage pushes along a toothed rack on the paper holder one space each time the button is pressed and rides over the next tooth on release. A spring-held ink roller rubs gently on the type faces, and a spring clip holds the paper.
