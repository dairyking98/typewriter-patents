## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** A friction-driven continuously rotating carrier of dies needs a way to bring any chosen die to the working point precisely and hold it there, including repeating the same die without any wasted movement.

**Solution:** A die wheel is friction-driven but normally held by a ratchet and pawl; pressing a key frees the wheel, a selector driven by the key is thrust into the path of a stop finger for that die and halts the wheel there, and the pawl then re-engages to hold it while a spring hammer prints.

**How it works:** The die carrier (16 characters in the example) turns on a shaft driven through an adjustable friction clutch, so it always tends to rotate, but a pawl on a ratchet on the shaft normally holds it still. Each die has a radial stop finger on a hub of the carrier, and each key lever has a cam that thrusts a selector into the path of that finger; depressing a key first withdraws the pawl, so the wheel runs on until the finger meets the selected selector and stops with its die opposite the platen. Further depression compresses a spring between the hammer arm and its actuating arm until a catch trips and the hammer flies forward, driving platen, paper and ribbon against the die. As the key rises the pawl re-engages the ratchet before the selector releases, so the wheel stays put; if the same character is to repeat, the beveled selector is pushed under the finger and the wheel hardly moves. The key release also oscillates a fixed-and-loose-dog escapement on the carriage rack, advancing the spring-driven paper carriage one tooth.
