## Reading (full text, OCR poor)

**Problem:** In a dog-and-rack letter-space escapement the loose dog's single spring had to be both heavy and finely adjusted, causing friction, wear and noise.

**Solution:** Give the loose (pivoted) dog two independent, light springs, one acting on the dog directly and one acting through a separate positioning lever, so each does its own job and each is easy to adjust.

**How it works:** A dog carrier is pivoted on the carriage bed and swung sideways at each key stroke or space-bar stroke by a tappet on the universal-bar rocking rod; a spring, anchored at one of several teeth to vary tension, returns it. The carrier bears a fixed dog and a loose dog that engage a rack on the carriage base plate to release one letter space at a time. The loose dog can swing either way on its pivot, so it rides over the rack teeth on carriage return. A separate lever pivoted on the carrier positions the loose dog relative to the fixed dog (trimming the lever end alters the dog timing), while a lighter spring on the dog and a stronger one on the lever act in opposite directions and independently; a stop tooth limits the dog. The machine shown is a three-case typebar machine with a folding carriage.
