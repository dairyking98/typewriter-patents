## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** In a motor-driven type-wheel machine the driving element moves a constant distance, but the type carrier must travel a variable distance and be stopped and held exactly for each character.

**Solution:** A clutch-like toggle lock couples the type-carrier arm to a constantly turning shaft; a key-selected stop pin trips the toggle at the right angle, and a dog then drops into a fixed rack to lock the carrier in place for printing.

**How it works:** A cam driven by a constant-travel lever turns a shaft, which carries a collar locked to a plate sliding on the carrier arm through a straight-line toggle of beveled projections, a pin and a pivoted tappet. The arm and its type wheel therefore swing with the shaft to one side of a rest position. Each key lifts a stop pin (or the key lever itself) into the path of one of the tappet arms. When the tappet arm hits the raised pin the carrier stops, the tappet rocks and breaks the toggle, and the shaft's continued rotation pushes the collar against the plate wall and drives a dog into a tooth of a fixed rack, locking the carrier at the printing position. On return the beveled collar pulls the plate back, re-aligns the toggle and re-centres the tappet with a spring-pressed dog. Modifications put the lock at the lower end of the shaft or replace the collar with a forked lever and double-inclined slot.
