## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** In an automatic ribbon reverse, a toggle-spring shift rail can stall at its dead center with both spool pawls disengaged, for example when both rail ends are pushed in by hand; the earlier fix of a spring piston on a pointed tooth added friction to every reversal.

**Solution:** Keep the friction-free toggle spring and add an auxiliary device on parts that move with each keystroke so that, if the rail rests at neutral, a following keystroke pushes it out of neutral.

**How it works:** Each key lever strikes a universal rod and square shaft whose arms pull links to an angle lever, one per ribbon spool. Each lever carries a feed pawl on the spool's ratchet wheel and a holding pawl on the same pivot, so the feed pawl steps the spool with each keystroke while the holding pawl stops it running back. A flat curved shift rail pinned to both angle levers can slide lengthwise. Its end pins lift the pawls of the spool that is finished, and put the other spool's pawls in. When one spool runs empty a control part in it drops and holds the arm, which pushes the shift rail over and the toggle spring completes the reversal. The new auxiliary device uses parts moved by each keystroke to knock the rail out of neutral.
