## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** In a calculating or accounting machine printing unit with sliding type blocks, the spring or long lever that returns struck blocks must span the whole row of blocks and fit between narrow carriers, so it is weak and only usable with about ten blocks.

**Solution:** Give each type carrier a short hook-shaped sprung lever, pivoted behind the carrier, whose end passes between adjacent carriers near the hammer stop and pushes struck blocks back with bevelled surfaces acting on side cams.

**How it works:** Each type carrier is raised to the wanted character and carries type blocks that slide across it. The print hammer drives the block in the writing position against the platen through the ink ribbon. A double-armed lever on a fixed axle, held by a spring, has its long arm between two blocks and a widened end with two inclined surfaces. The struck block pushes the lever against its spring on the stroke. Afterwards the inclined surfaces act on the side cam of the block and return it, and any neighbour jarred out of place, to rest before it reaches the ribbon.
