## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** A key-rotated, axially shiftable type wheel must be light so key force overcomes its inertia, yet strong enough at hub and rim to survive the jarring of shifting and, when the wheel itself is the hammer, the blow against the platen.

**Solution:** Mould the wheel as one hollow hard-rubber piece with hub, rim and closed top and bottom solid webs, so rim and hub brace each other while the wheel stays light.

**How it works:** The wheel is a hollow shell of hard rubber: an axial hub or sleeve, a thin periphery carrying one or more rows of characters, and solid disc webs closing top and bottom between hub and rim. It is cast in wedge-shaped dies arranged around a base plate with the characters in intaglio; plastic compound and a little water are put in, the mould is clamped and heated so steam forces the rubber into every crevice. The wheel sits loose on a shaft, is turned by key levers, and is lifted on the shaft by a sleeve arm engaging an annular groove in the hub to bring a chosen row to the print point. Some rows carry whole words, whose type faces lie on flat chords of the rim so the word strikes the platen evenly end to end. A hole in the lower web lets the guide pin in and lets gases escape from the mould.
