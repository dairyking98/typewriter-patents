## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Keyboard typewriters need two hands and a mass of typebars, rods and levers, so the operator cannot follow notes with one hand while the other types.

**Solution:** A one-handed, keyboardless machine in which a multirow type cylinder on a swinging arm is slid and rotated by a hand grip to bring the wanted character to the platen, and pressing the arm down prints.

**How it works:** A swinging type arm carries a sleeve with an 81-character type cylinder (nine circumferential rows of nine), the three front rows capitals, the middle rows small letters, the rear rows numerals and punctuation. The operator, who has memorised the layout, holds a bracket handle to twist the sleeve left or right (a spring and stop collars return it) and an eyelet handle to slide the cylinder lengthwise through a rack and toothed wheel (a spring returns it). Depressing the arm presses the cylinder onto an inked ribbon and the paper on the platen; on the arm's return a pawl advances the carriage ratchet by one tooth, with a higher lift giving two or more spaces. A mainspring returns the carriage, a lever trips it at line end, and a carriage stop turns the platen for the line feed; the ribbon spools are fed by a double pawl, and the ribbon shifts sideways at each case change.
