## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** Released carriages in column work need raising and releasing with controlled stops at chosen scale positions.

**Solution:** A hand lever on the carriage that both lifts the feed rack out of the spacing dog and swings a dog to catch preset pins in a perforated stop rod.

**How it works:** On a Remington carriage a lever pivots on a bracket and has a rear arm with a stop lug and a bar reaching over a rock-shaft finger key. Pressing the lever handle rocks the bar, which turns the rock-shaft and lowers the hinged frame arm, lifting the rack from the spacing dog. The carriage spring then runs the carriage until the lug meets a pin set in a numbered perforated rod on the top plate, with holes spaced to the machine scale at one per five figures. A pointer shows the position, and pins are removable. Normally the lug passes the pins freely.

**Features:** [Tabulator](../features/esc-tabulator.md), [Carriage release](../features/esc-carriage-release.md), [Pawl-and-rack escapement](../features/esc-pawl-rack.md), [Mainspring-drawn carriage](../features/esc-spring-drum.md), [Attachment or accessory](../features/use-accessory.md)

**Parts:** carriage; hinged rack frame; feed rack; spacing dog; lever; bracket; rock-shaft; stop lug; stop rod; stop pin; pointer; scale

**Numbers:** one hole per five scale figures
