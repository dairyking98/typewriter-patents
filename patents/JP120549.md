## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Reversing the turning shaft of a differential-driven Japanese machine needs a hard key pressure to release the brake and tighten the belt.

**Solution:** The brake band and belt tightener are worked by a worm drive reversed by cone friction wheels on the motor shaft, so a light key press suffices.

**How it works:** A differential has its starting gear and its intermediate wheel each belted to a double pulley on the motor shaft; the starting belt is tight, the intermediate-wheel belt slack. A brake band acts on the intermediate wheel. Pressing a key shifts a pair of cone wheels so one touches a cone friction wheel on the motor shaft, turning a worm and worm wheel one way; a link slackens the brake and tightens the belt, so the output shaft turns forward and carries the printing point to the type. Releasing the key lets a spring bring the other cone wheel into contact, reversing the worm, braking the intermediate wheel and sending the output back.
