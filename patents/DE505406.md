## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** On small machines the line ratchet is small, so the pawl's vertical travel is large and a loose pivot would wobble and spoil exact line spacing.

**Solution:** Hinge the pawl on a pull tab that moves horizontally, itself spring-hinged to the short arm of the angled lever, so lateral play is negligible while radial movement happens at the joint.

**How it works:** An adjustable comb disc beside the ratchet wheel decides, by a nose, how early the pawl engages the ratchet and so the step size; a pivot lever with a spring detent holds the comb disc. A spring-loaded angle lever with a handle carries the pawl movable in three directions through a bent bearing piece and pull tab. A bent nose guides the pawl in the groove between the ratchet and platen face so the machine does not get wider. Right-angle bends in the levers bring the handle toward the machine centre.

Line switch devices are already known in which a Toothed ratchet wheel is adjusted or advanced by a line shift lever and its variable switching step by one on the axis of rotation of the platen seated, adjustable comb disc regulated wind. The rotation is done by means of a pawl, which by a nose depending on the position of the comb disc earlier or later comes into engagement with the teeth of the ratchet wheel. So the latch finds out in addition to the movement in the direction of the horizontal also one depending on the curvature of the Ratchet wheel larger or smaller movement in the direction of the vertical. With the movement in the horizontal the movement in the vertical inevitably takes place, since yes the pawl engages the ratchet wheel and the movement of the teeth to the apex of the ratchet. The pawl is moved by means of an angle lever, which is connected to the pawl by a pivot. The earlier arrangements were larger machines.
