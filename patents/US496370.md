## Summary

**Kind:** a part or mechanism (any kind of machine) · **Subject (patent classes):** Bearings with rolling contact, for exclusively rotary movement with bearing balls essentially of the same size in one or more circular rows for both radial and axial load with a single row of balls with angular contact

**In the patent’s own words:** Its object is to provide means for adjusting the hangers to the cone bearings of the type shaft with the greatest nicety for the purpose of giving the type a perfectly accurate stroke and yet permit it to be operated easily not only when the machine is new but when the hanger bearings become worn by long use;

**How it works (reading):** Flexible sheet-brass hangers whose ears are sprung together by a screw pushing the middle of the hanger upward onto a cone-ended axle, plus a telescoping hammer helve and a slide-in pitman hook.

## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** Hammer-type typebar hangers on a ring need to be adjusted accurately to their cone bearings, and kept tight as the bearings wear, with the type aligned and the pitman quickly detachable.

**Solution:** Flexible sheet-brass hangers whose ears are sprung together by a screw pushing the middle of the hanger upward onto a cone-ended axle, plus a telescoping hammer helve and a slide-in pitman hook.

**How it works:** Each type hammer has a shank fixed on an axle with conical ends that sit in conical bearings in the ears of a folded sheet-brass hanger. Two screws fix the hanger to the ring plate; the plate is countersunk under the screw over the axle so the hanger rests only at its sides. Tightening that screw bows the middle of the hanger upward, springing the ears toward each other and taking up wear in the cone bearings. The other screw has some play for lateral alignment, and the type's cylindrical shank can be turned and its telescoping helve set with a screw to align it. A pitman links each hammer to a key lever through a sliding hook held by a spring catch, so it can be unhooked by hand without tools.
