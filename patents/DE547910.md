## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** In power-driven typewriters with multi-type sliding bars, the adjustment for impression pressure and the adjustment for height against the platen are coupled in one mechanism and cannot be set separately.

**Solution:** Support each type bar's frame member at two widely separated points so that one allows adjustment in the strike direction (impression) and the other allows vertical adjustment, independently.

**How it works:** Each type bar slides in a guide on an L-shaped frame member whose rear part passes through a block and is set vertically by upper and lower screws. The front end passes through a cutout with a convex surface held by a plate with a convex underside, allowing the vertical adjustment. Loosening a screw lets the frame member be moved forward or back relative to the platen. The type bar is driven by a link and lever system. Two keys per bar (B and C) cause a continuously running power drive to pull rods which swing angle levers, bringing either the upper or lower type of a group to print, and springs return the parts.
