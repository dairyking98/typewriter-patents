## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Key-lever typewriters with selecting and printing means are complex and not positive in positioning.

**Solution:** One key stroke turns a ratchet-driven type-wheel until the key meets a graduated stop, then continues to print: positive positioning followed by a positive impression.

**How it works:** Key levers, a type-operating bail and a selecting and printing bail share one pivot bar. Pressing a key depresses the operating bail, whose ratchet teeth engage a gear on the type-wheel shaft and turn the wheel. The key stops against a step surface on the selecting bail at a height unique to that key, fixing the rotation. Further depression moves the whole wheel against the platen to print. Springs return the parts, and a modification uses a cord and segment instead of the bail.

**Features:** [ELEM:wheel-rim](../features/elem-wheel-rim.md), [SEL:keyboard-full](../features/sel-keyboard-full.md), [SEL:differential-drive](../features/sel-differential-drive.md), [IMP:element-to-platen](../features/imp-element-to-platen.md), [IMP:print-lever](../features/imp-print-lever.md), [FRM:few-parts](../features/frm-few-parts.md), [FRM:compact-portable](../features/frm-compact-portable.md)

**Parts:** key lever; type-operating bail; selecting and printing bail; stop surface step; ratchet segment; gear; type-wheel; platen; return spring; carriage
