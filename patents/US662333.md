## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** Printing in cipher on a typewriter needs a simple, inexpensive device that allows rapid change of code.

**Solution:** A duplicate keyboard plate that sits over the typewriter keys and can be lifted and re-seated on posts in several offset positions, so each key presses a neighbouring key.

**How it works:** A base plate is hooked to the front extension of a Caligraph typewriter. A plate above carries duplicate keys whose stems pass through guide holes and end in rubber cushions resting on the real keys. Two posts on the base plate fit in groups of seven holes in the plate, one central for normal printing and six for offset positions spaced by the key row distance. Lifting the plate and re-seating it shifts the key mapping, and the correspondent is told the hole numbers to decode. The character labels are disks with studs in the plate, transposable, and the rear key row has a removable section.

**Features:** [USE:accessory](../features/use-accessory.md), [SEL:chart-interchangeable](../features/sel-chart-interchangeable.md), [SEL:keyboard-full](../features/sel-keyboard-full.md), [FRM:few-parts](../features/frm-few-parts.md)

**Parts:** base plate; hook; key plate; duplicate key; rubber cushion; post; positioning hole; character disk

**Numbers:** seven holes per post: one normal, six cipher positions
