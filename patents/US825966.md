## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** The ring-shaped Hammond type shuttle needed a way to be held in place on its anvil yet lifted off and replaced quickly when changing type sets.

**Solution:** Two spring-held retainer bars with overhanging fingers clamp over the shuttle's top flange and are squeezed inward to release it for vertical removal.

**How it works:** The continuous ring shuttle has a flange around its top edge that rests on the upper edge of a ring-shaped anvil on a centre post, optionally on balls. Two retainer bars, mounted on the anvil cross-arms by slotted screws, carry fingers that reach over the inner edge of the flange. Pressing the upturned finger ends together draws the fingers clear and the shuttle lifts straight off; the shuttle arm's upturned end just slips out of a notch in the flange. To refit, the shuttle is dropped on and a finger pressed between the curved ends spreads the bars until the fingers snap over the flange. A separate rod with an arm engaging a kerf in the anvil holds the anvil on the post and swings aside to free it. The ring carries several sets of characters, including a Braille set of pits, with a shoulder around each hammer point that limits its entry so the paper is embossed rather than punctured.

**Parts:** ring shuttle; flange; anvil; centre post; ball bearing; hammer; retainer bar; retainer finger; shuttle arm; notch; anvil retaining rod; Braille hammer point; shoulder

**Numbers:** three sets of characters a, b, c on the shuttle

**Improves on:**

- ordinary Hammond typewriter shuttle mounting: shuttle is retained and released by squeezing two sprung bars, with removal by plain vertical lift
