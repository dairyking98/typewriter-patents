## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** The inventor wants a simple, inexpensive typewriter with a movable type carrier that gives perfect letter alignment and spacing.

**Solution:** A grid of keys positions a movable type-form in two perpendicular directions through two rocking finger-bars with distance-proportional strokes, and an electromagnet hammer prints only when the key has reached its stop and closed a circuit.

**How it works:** Each key sits at the crossing of fingers on two rocking bars at right angles; depression rocks one bar to move the type-carrier vertically and the other to slide it horizontally, by an amount proportional to the key's distance from the bar axes. Pivoted stops under the keys block the finger cross-rods at exact travel, so each type reaches an exactly predetermined position. When the cross-rod meets its stop a battery circuit closes through an electromagnet whose jointed lever drives a hammer, forcing the type in front of it against the impression cylinder through a carbon or inking ribbon; the types are retracted by small springs. Breaking a second normally closed circuit at the start of key travel releases a magnet-controlled escapement so the spring-driven carriage steps half a rack tooth, and again on re-closing. Ribbon spools are wound from the carriage rack through selectable gears for reversal, and returning the carriage trips a lever that advances the cylinder a line, adjustable in amount.
