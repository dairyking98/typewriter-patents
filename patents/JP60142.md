## Summary

**Kind:** a part or mechanism (any kind of machine) · **Subject (patent classes):** Typewriters or selective printing mechanisms characterised by the mounting, arrangement or disposition of the types or dies

**Class note:** J-PlatPat files it as a Western-type typewriter (B41J1/00@P); it is a Japanese-script (kanji or kana) machine built on Western lines.

**In the English translation of the patent’s own words:** This invention relates to a Japanese-script typewriter in which a number of type cylinders, each engraved with a number of types equal to the product of the number of row keys and the number of character keys, are mounted in a type-cylinder frame, each on its own shaft so as to turn with it and slide along it.

**How it works (reading):** Several multi-row type cylinders, only one of which is coupled to the mechanism at a time, are slid by a row key and turned by a character key, each driven by a weight or spring band and stopped by pins and notched stop cylinder, then hit by a hammer from behind the paper.

## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Japanese typewriters need thousands of characters yet must be usable by touch with a small Western-style keyboard and without great motor force.

**Solution:** Several multi-row type cylinders, only one of which is coupled to the mechanism at a time, are slid by a row key and turned by a character key, each driven by a weight or spring band and stopped by pins and notched stop cylinder, then hit by a hammer from behind the paper.

**How it works:** Each type cylinder carries rows times columns of characters and turns and slides on its own shaft in a movable frame; moving the frame couples one cylinder to the slide fork and to the drive gear. Pressing a row key releases a brake so a weight pushes the slide and cylinder axially until a pawl hits that key's stop pin, bringing the chosen row to the print point. Pressing a character key releases a second brake so a weight turns the cylinder and stop cylinder until the key's pawl drops into its notch, which halts the cylinder at the chosen type. The same drop pulls a lever that swings a hammer, striking the type from behind the paper with a force proportional to the key pressure. Releasing the keys lets a cord pull the cylinder back and brakes stop all motion, while a star-wheel escapement spaces the paper rollers one step per character. A chart on each cylinder shows key combinations.
