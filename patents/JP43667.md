## Summary

**Kind:** index machine · **Subject (patent classes):** Typewriters or selective printing mechanisms characterised by the mounting, arrangement or disposition of the types or dies

**Class note:** J-PlatPat files it as a Western-type typewriter (B41J1/00@P); it is a Japanese-script (kanji or kana) machine built on Western lines.

**In the English translation of the patent’s own words:** This invention relates to a printing device, and its mechanism, which prints any character quickly on one and the same sheet, using by a suitable mechanism both types mounted on typebars and types held in a store of special construction.

**How it works (reading):** Put the frequent characters on typebars with four-level carriage shift, and keep the rare characters as loose types in a rotating and sliding cylindrical store, chosen by an index on the drum and a pointer and printed by a type-catching bar that grips the type, prints, and returns it.

## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A Japanese-script typewriter needs far more characters than a keyboard can carry, yet the commonest characters must be typed fast.

**Solution:** Put the frequent characters on typebars with four-level carriage shift, and keep the rare characters as loose types in a rotating and sliding cylindrical store, chosen by an index on the drum and a pointer and printed by a type-catching bar that grips the type, prints, and returns it.

**How it works:** Typebars on an arc segment are driven by key levers through bell-crank linkages that speed up late in the stroke; each stroke trips a common trigger that rocks an anchor-shaped trigger and steps the rack carriage half a tooth on the blow and half on release, and also lifts the ribbon holder. The platen carriage is raised to one of four levels by shift keys with a stepped stop plate, so each bar prints four types. For rare characters, flat type cases stand radially in two slotted flanges on a hollow shaft that is turned and slid on rails by a knob until the wanted character on the drum's outside index is at a pointer; releasing the knob pushes that case outward. A store key then raises a type pusher and the type-catching bar, whose square tube receives the type from below and locks it with a half-cut pin in a groove; the bar swings and prints like a typebar, then the type drops back into its cell. A locking key detents the drum before the store key can be struck.

## Description (English translation of the Japanese text, opening)

> Specification. Matsumoto-type high-speed Japanese-script printing machine. Nature and object of the invention. This invention relates to a printing device, and its mechanism, which prints any character quickly on one and the same sheet, using by a suitable mechanism both types mounted on typebars and types held in a store of special construction. Its object is to mount the characters used most often in ordinary writing on typebars and print them at very high speed, while the other characters are kept in a standing type store and can be printed quickly on the same sheet when needed, so that a whole document is printed completely and at very high speed.
