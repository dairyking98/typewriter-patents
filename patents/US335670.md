## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Fit a full font, or several fonts of different size, compactly in a rotating or oscillating disk, and space the carriage in proportion to letter width.

**Solution:** A horizontal type-disk carries spring-lifted type-pins; a hinged impression-key that engages a type-pin swings the disk to a fixed stop pin and then presses the type down, while an adjustable pin on the key moves a pawl lever to space the carriage by the letter's width.

**How it works:** A disk made of upper and lower perforated plates sits horizontally over the paper bed on a central pin, holding rows of spring-raised type-pins in batteries. The inner end of the impression-key fits loosely over the disk axis; the operator puts its recessed pins over the chosen type-pin and swings the disk around until the key meets a fixed stop pin, which brings the type to the printing center. Depressing the key then presses the type onto the paper, with ink rollers at each end of the carriage inking the type as the disk passes. A strap spring in the hub returns the disk to rest. An adjustable pin on the key (or alternatively type-pins of different lengths) hits a pawl lever on a rack before the impression, moving the carriage one, two or more notches by letter width. A bifurcated lever shifts the disk axis in an elongated hub slot to bring a second, large-type row to the print line, and a power-driven option uses stop keys to catch arms on the disk.
