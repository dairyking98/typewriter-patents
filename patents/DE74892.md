## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** Spring-driven carriages space either on the key's downstroke (reverse circuit) or after the impression (natural circuit), and experts and beginners each prefer a different one.

**Solution:** A single pawl and rack mechanism that can be set by one lever to give either reverse or natural letter switching.

**How it works:** On a Remington machine the carriage slides on a guide rail with a rack frame carrying rack 5. Two pawls 6 and 7 sit on a pawl holder that a key lever turns one way through tie rods and a spring turns back. Pawl 7 has a fork-shaped lever with three claws; raising the lever lets claw 26 push pawl 7 into its left position so it works as the natural-switching pawl while pawl 6 is limited by claw 25 and a stop. Pressing the lever down brings claw 24 into play so that pawl 6 acts as the lock and pawl 7 drives the rack on the downstroke for reverse switching. A second form uses one pawl and two racks hung on a slider in slots and drawn by springs, each rack with adjustable stops.

**Features:** [ESC:pawl-rack](../features/esc-pawl-rack.md), [ESC:spring-drum](../features/esc-spring-drum.md), [ESC:print-and-space](../features/esc-print-and-space.md), [ELEM:typebar](../features/elem-typebar.md)

**Parts:** carriage; guide rail; rack frame; rack; pawl; pawl holder; shaft; tie rod; spring; fork-shaped lever; claw; stop; slider

**Improves on:**

- Remington typewriter spacing: switchable between spacing on the downstroke or after the impression
