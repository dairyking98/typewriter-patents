## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Type-wheel machines needed to be cheap, simple, portable and fast, with few parts and little adjustment, and with equal key resistance and stroke.

**Solution:** Each key of a normal keyboard turns the type wheel via a differential universal lever and rack-and-pinion to roughly the right angle, while a key-mounted stop arrests a sliding shuttle geared to the wheel shaft at the exact angle, then the swinging frame tilts the wheel against the platen.

**How it works:** The two-row type wheel sits on a shaft in a swinging frame. Keys are split into a left and a right series; pressing a key displaces a universal differential lever by an amount that depends on the key, which moves a double-arc rack that meshes with a pinion on the wheel shaft and turns the wheel up to half a revolution either way, deliberately a little too far. A gear on the shaft also meshes with a rack on a transverse sliding shuttle; the depressed key raises a stop into the shuttle's path so the shuttle (and so the wheel) halts at the exact character position. The stops are arranged in three groups in different planes (a vernier arrangement) so the shuttle travels only one third of the key-bank width. Continued driving force holds the shuttle against its stop so the wheel does not rebound. The swinging frame then carries the wheel bodily forward to the front of the platen to print, and on key release the wheel retracts and rotates back to a locked normal position. The paper carriage has a spacing mechanism, line lock, line-space, margin and tab stops, and a disc-shaped ribbon (the OCR text breaks off before the printing mechanism and these details are described).

**Numbers:** 84 characters in two rows of 42; 42 keys in two series of 21; wheel turns at most half a revolution either way; shuttle travel one third of key-bank width (3 stop groups)

**Improves on:**
