## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** On Sugimoto-type Japanese typewriters the sideways line-feed lever had to be disengaged from the rack to place the paper cylinder at an arbitrary point, which was awkward when starting mid-sheet, leaving part-line gaps or inserting Western words.

**Solution:** Couple the line-feed lever's saw-tooth wheel to the rack gear through a releasable friction clutch, so the cylinder can be moved freely by a lifter without disengaging the lever, plus a hand-turned fine-adjustment gear on the same rack.

**How it works:** The paper cylinder rides on a sliding frame that moves lengthwise and crosswise over a tray of upright types. A gear (5) meshing with the sideways-feed rack (4) is fixed on a vertical shaft (6) whose lower half is a slit tube with a conical mouth and ring flange (8). A conical engaging piece (9), pulled up by a lifter (13) under a coil spring (16), spreads the tube so the flange grips the bore of a sleeve (10) by friction, and the saw-tooth wheel (11) fixed to the sleeve then turns with the gear; the operating lever (17) stepping that wheel feeds the cylinder a line at a time. Pressing the lifter down withdraws the cone, so the wheel runs free of the gear and the cylinder can be slid sideways anywhere. A hand-turned fine-adjustment gear (19) on an arm of the frame also meshes with the rack, and a stepped projection (20) on the lever with a tiltable stepped stop (21) gives two- or three-line feeds.

## Description (English translation of the Japanese text, opening)

> Specification. Sideways-feed device in a Japanese-script typewriter. Nature and object of the invention. This invention is an improvement to the device that moves the paper cylinder sideways to change the printing line, in a Japanese typewriter (邦文印書機) in which the paper cylinder is mounted so as to move freely lengthwise and crosswise relative to a type tray holding many types, and a printing mechanism is fitted to it. It concerns a construction in which a saw-tooth wheel is fixed to a sleeve that is always in friction engagement with the vertical shaft carrying the gear that meshes with the sideways-feed rack; the operating lever engages the saw-tooth wheel; the friction engagement between shaft and sleeve can be released so that the saw-tooth wheel runs free of the gear; and a fine-adjustment gear is added on the rack. Its object, by a very simple construction, is that when printing is to begin at any point on the sheet, or a blank space is to be left between printed lines, this can be done without regard to the sideways feed, greatly saving the typist's trouble and raising the printing rate.
