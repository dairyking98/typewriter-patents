## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** On the Oliver typewriter the whole shift-frame swings fore and aft on suspension links, and both ends must be locked and released together so the platen stays square.

**Solution:** Shift levers on the left and matching locking levers on the right, joined by two transverse rock shafts, lock or release both ends of the shift-frame from either of two shift keys.

**How it works:** The paper carriage rides on a shift-frame hung from pivoted suspension links, with a rear guide stem limited by stop nuts. Two spring-lifted shift levers at the left carry stops that hold the frame's left end in its central position; depressing one lifts its stop away and leaves the other stop to catch the frame on return. Cranks and links connect the shift levers to two rock shafts that run across the machine. At the right side the same shafts drive two similar locking levers acting on a stop arm of the frame, so both ends release and lock together. A rod and link from the rock shaft moves the frame, and cushioning pieces limit lever travel. Rear brackets on the base plate carry the rock shaft bearings.
