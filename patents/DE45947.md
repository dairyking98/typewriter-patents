## Summary

**Kind:** a part or mechanism (any kind of machine) · **Subject (patent classes):** Typewriters or selective printing or marking mechanisms characterised by the purpose for which they are constructed for printing in Braille or with keyboards specially adapted for use by blind or disabled persons

**How it works (reading):** Six independent pin levers raised by keys emboss the dots of each Braille cell.

## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Braille was written by assembling individual types, which is awkward.

**Solution:** Six independent pin levers raised by keys emboss the dots of each Braille cell.

**How it works:** Six levers each carry a pressure pin laid out like a dice-six cell. In the simple machine, six keys lift levers so their pins press the paper into a die plate with cavities, forming dots; the paper is moved by hand or a mechanism one letter width. In the larger machine each character has its own key whose pins push specific cell levers, so one key forms the right pattern of dots. The paper rides in a frame moved for letters and lines.

**Parts:** key; lever; pressure pin; die plate; paper frame; cross-piece; pin on key lever

**Numbers:** six levers per cell
