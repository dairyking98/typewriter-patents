## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typebar key mechanisms need a heavy keystroke because the key lever must drive the bar through its whole travel; the inventor wants a very light, fast touch on a front-stroke visible machine.

**Solution:** Let the key lever flick a free actuator that throws the typebar by momentum, so the typebar flies on by itself while the key and actuator are already returning.

**How it works:** Each key lever is spring-held up and linked by a vertical link to a sub-lever actuator pivoted on the same pivot as its typebar in a segment plate. The actuator is normally disengaged from the typebar, so a key press first moves the actuator and link by momentum before the actuator strikes a lug on the typebar and throws it. The typebar flies to the platen under its own momentum while the actuator and key return. On its way back the bar's shoulder pushes a spring-loaded universal bar, whose spring then returns the bar. A spring-pressed frame holds the links up.
