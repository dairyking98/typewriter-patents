## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A simple hand-operated typewriter is wanted in which the writer picks a character by finger and the same movement prints and spaces, avoiding a separate key per character and complex spacing gear.

**Solution:** A tilting index-lever carried on a sliding frame holds a sliding type-rack; a finger key with an indicator slides the rack to the chosen character, drops into a notch of a tooth-rack to lock it, and pressing further prints the type onto a convex elastic block while a pawl steps the frame one tooth along a rack.

**How it works:** A sliding frame rides on a horizontal shaft above a base table and carries a tablet (index-lever) pivoted on screw pivots and held up by a spring. The tablet bears a character index and a groove in which a type-rack slides, with types spaced to match the index. A finger key with an indicator moves the rack until the indicator sits over the wanted character, then pressing the key drops the indicator into the matching recess of a tooth-rack on the lever, locking the position, and continued pressure lowers the lever so the type meets the paper on a convex elastic printing block, inked through a ribbon. As the lever goes down a pawl engages a rack on the table and advances the frame by one or more teeth, and on release everything returns. Word spaces come from pressing with the rack positioned so no type touches the paper, or from a separate space pawl-lever; at line end the frame is returned and the first depression of the lever turns ribbed feed rollers via a ratchet and pawl lever to feed a line. Variants replace the rack with a pivoted segmental type rack, a wheel segment and type-wheel worked by a row of indicating keys, or keep the lever still and move the paper on a sliding frame.
