## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Turkish, Arabic and Hindostan scripts have around 110 forms per font (four forms per letter), making a one-key-per-character keyboard impractical.

**Solution:** Break the letters into common parts (basic shapes, dots, final swashes) on separate keys struck together so that combined impressions form the letters, cutting 110 types to a few dozen.

**How it works:** Letters are grouped by common basic shapes; dots and ornaments are placed on 6 dot keys that do not advance the carriage and are struck before or with the letter key, like accent keys. Final swing types replace the final forms 1 and 2 of many letters by extending a basic type. This reduces 88 letters to 6 + 32 = 38 types, and 24 final forms to 2 + 12 = 14 types, for about 48 characters in all, plus numerals and punctuation reached by the shift. The space key is replaced by the shift key position, with two space keys on both sides of the keyboard. The carriage moves left to right instead of right to left, with four special back-space keys. Otherwise the machine remains as in current typebar systems.
