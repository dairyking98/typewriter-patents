## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Earlier type-cylinder machines were slow and could not manifold like typebar machines because the key-to-cylinder linkage was complex and the positioning motions wasted power.

**Solution:** A keyboard machine in which a rocking carrier swings a type cylinder to the platen while key-lever cam slopes drive a light positioning frame that rotates the cylinder by rack and pinion, with key shift levers sliding it axially.

**How it works:** Each key lever presses a rocking printing frame down so the cylinder carrier swings toward the platen. Near the end of the key stroke an inclined face on the key lever hits one of two bars on a light positioning frame and swings it by an amount set by the slope. A connecting rod turns a toothed sector, which turns a pinion on a spindle that rotates the cylinder up to 180 degrees either way. The six-row cylinder is moved axially by a slide with two shift levers (half and full steps) plus a parallelogram linkage that lowers it two rows for unnotched keys, and a star wheel with a dog pushed in just before impact fixes the angle. Springs aimed along a radius of the swing return the frames, with a spring-lock stop to kill overshoot.

**Numbers:** six rows of types on the cylinder; rotation up to 180 degrees either way; shift lever 11 moves half the distance of lever 12; unnotched keys lower cylinder by two rows

**Improves on:**

- earlier type-cylinder machines: simpler key linkage, positioning done late in the key stroke, less power wasted, and the cylinder can print several copies
