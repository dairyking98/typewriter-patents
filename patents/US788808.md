## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** Front-strike machines need platen and carriage scales that let the operator place the paper and read the carriage position accurately without the type bars hitting the scale.

**Solution:** One bent spring-mounted scale plate sits in front of the platen below the line of writing, its upper part graduated as the platen scale and its lower part as the carriage scale read against a pointer.

**How it works:** Two forward spring arms, screwed to lugs on the Monarch carriage through slotted holes, run under the platen and carry the scale plate. The spring arms press the straight upper edge against the platen or paper and let it follow paper thickness. The upper part is graduated as the platen scale; the plate is bent back and then down and forward, and the lower part, graduated to match, is read against a pointer stamped from the ribbon-slot plate. The scale lies behind the type bar and in front of the platen. For a shift machine its top edge sits at least two line-space units below the line of writing; the operator registers the desired line with that edge then spaces up.

**Features:** [Line and point indicator](../features/pap-line-indicator.md), [Paper table and guides](../features/pap-paper-guide.md), [Line spacing](../features/pap-line-space.md), [Adjustment and wear take-up](../features/frm-adjustment-wear.md)

**Parts:** scale plate; spring arm; slot; lug; platen; pointer; ribbon guide; type bar

**Numbers:** scale edge at least two single line-space units below the line of writing

**Improves on:**

- earlier platen and carriage scales: one corrugated spring-held plate carries both scales
