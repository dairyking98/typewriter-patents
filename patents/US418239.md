## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typewriters with a full keyboard of many key-levers are complicated, large, heavy and costly.

**Solution:** Replace the keyboard with one right and one left impact lever, with a set of stop-rods touched by the fingers to hold a polygonal shell of type strips so the chosen character is at the printing point.

**How it works:** A polygonal shell carrying strips of raised letters (metal, hard rubber, or cast integral) turns loosely on a horizontal shaft between standards. A hanger on a guide-rod and slotted arms linked to a lever and shaft carry the shell and the hammer mechanism. A long group of stop-rods, with upturned ends passing through a perforated hand-plate, is touched by the fingers so that its inner end enters the path of the hanger and arrests the shell at the right letter. The single downward stroke of the right or left lever then impacts that character.

**Features:**

- **Selection:** [Slider along a straight index](../features/sel-linear-slider.md), [Selection locked for printing](../features/sel-selector-locking.md)
- **Type element:** [Multi-row type cylinder](../features/elem-cylinder-multirow.md), [Flat type plate](../features/elem-plate-flat.md), [Metal type](../features/elem-metal-type.md), [Rubber type](../features/elem-rubber-type.md)
- **Impression:** [Separate print key or lever](../features/imp-print-lever.md)
- **Frame and body:** [Few parts, low cost](../features/frm-few-parts.md)

**Parts:** polygonal shell; type strip; standard; hanger; guide-rod; stop-rod; hand-plate; impact lever; shaft; spring

**Improves on:**

- typebar machines with a separate key-lever per character: two levers and stop-rods replace the whole keyboard
