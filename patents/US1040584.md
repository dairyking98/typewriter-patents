## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Power-assisted typewriters stop working when the auxiliary power fails, while manual machines lack the power-operated touch.

**Solution:** A convertible key action in which electric auxiliary power or the force of the key stroke itself impels the typebars, selected by a shifting arm or finger.

**How it works:** Key levers act through long links on typebars pivoted to the top plate. In power mode an electrically operated mechanism, via arms and push fingers, impels the typebars to the printing point on key depression. A converter moves the arms so that in manual mode the key force drives the bars directly. The return of bars and keys is independent of each other, and the long links reduce torsional strain on the bars.

**Features:**

- **Selection:** [Full keyboard, a key per character](../features/sel-keyboard-full.md)
- **Typebars:** [Typebars](../features/elem-typebar.md), [Typebar action](../features/bar-type-action.md), [Key touch and even impression](../features/bar-touch.md)
- **Impression:** [Electric drive](../features/imp-electric.md)

**Parts:** key lever; typebar; arm; push finger; link; electric mechanism; platen

**Improves on:**

- Cahill electric typewriter: still usable when power fails
- Remington typewriter: adds power assistance
