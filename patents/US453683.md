## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typewriters of the day had many parts, were costly, noisy, heavy and hard to learn; the inventor wants far fewer parts, quieter action, lighter weight and easy learning.

**Solution:** A multi-row type wheel on a rocking frame is turned by a seesaw finger-board whose depressible pins stop it against a stop-plate at the chosen character, row keys slide the wheel along its shaft, and the same downward stroke prints and spaces.

**How it works:** A rocking frame carries the type-wheel shaft, a toothed segment on an oscillating arm, and a finger-board pivoted directly above the wheel shaft. Pressing a lettered pin in the board and tilting the board swings the segment, which turns a pinion on the wheel shaft until the pin meets a stop-plate and halts the wheel at that character. A row of keys, one per row of type, turns a crank that slides the wheel shaft through a ball-and-socket link to bring the chosen row to the print point. Pushing the frame down locks the pinion with a toothed lever against a post pin, swings an ink roller across the type and onto an ink pad, then strikes the paper roll. The same downward motion lifts a carriage pawl off its rack so a spring advances the paper carriage one tooth. Springs return the arm, keys and frame.
