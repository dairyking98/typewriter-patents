## Summary

**Kind:** type-element machine · **Subject (patent classes):** Typewriters or selective printing mechanisms characterised by the mounting, arrangement or disposition of the types or dies with types or dies mounted on carriers rotatable for selection the plane of the type or die face being parallel to the axis of rotation, e.g. with type on the periphery of cylindrical carriers

**In the patent’s own words:** The improvements relate to that class of machines in which the letters are contained on a shuttle in the form of a portion of a cylindrical surface, which shuttle is rotated about its axis by a driving-arm, so as to bring the required letter to the point of impression.

**How it works (reading):** Several shuttles share one circular anvil, each with a fin gap that a spring driving-arm snaps into, so the operator swaps the working shuttle by pushing the shuttles around or by raising the anvil to a second groove.

## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** On Hammond-type shuttle machines, writing a second language, italics or symbols meant removing the working shuttle and fitting another, so the sets could not be mixed in one line.

**Solution:** Several shuttles share one circular anvil, each with a fin gap that a spring driving-arm snaps into, so the operator swaps the working shuttle by pushing the shuttles around or by raising the anvil to a second groove.

**How it works:** A full circle of metal serves as the anvil, and the shuttles sit side by side around its circumference, each with a thin fin running in a groove and a boss with a square hole open toward the centre. The driving-arm engages that hole to turn the working shuttle. To change shuttle, the operator springs the flexible arm out of the gap and pushes the shuttles around until another is opposite the arm. The fins curve outward from the gaps so an approaching shuttle wedges the arm back and the arm snaps in by itself. The anvil is split horizontally so shuttles can be inserted. In the modification, shuttles lie in two grooves one above the other on a double-height anvil that a cam raises on its shaft; the existing shift keys still raise the shaft for caps and figures, and four fixed horns hold the idle shuttle centred.

**Numbers:** three lines of type per shuttle (small, capitals, figures); two shuttles in two grooves in the modification

**Improves on:**

- Hammond type-writer: shuttles stay in the machine and are swapped in a second without removing them, the open-sided square gap disengages from the driving-arm by springing the arm rather than lifting the shuttle
- GB3268 of 1895: the author's earlier circular shuttle arrangement with new details
