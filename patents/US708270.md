## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** In a bar-lock typewriter, typebar hangers need a long pivot bearing yet the largest possible number of bars must fit in a limited area; machined typebars also need much hand work.

**Solution:** Stagger the hangers in stepped rows on a grooved arc plate so each can have a single long pivot with the bar midway, and stamp the typebar from a single piece of sheet metal.

**How it works:** Hangers are blocks with a flange that enters a groove on the concave face of an arc plate, each held by a screw passing through an aperture in the plate. They are stepped in four-step series so each bar lies beside its neighbour without contact, allowing short bars. Each hanger has pivoting ears and a threaded socket adjusting a single pivot, with the bar fastened midway between the bearings. The typebar is stamped from sheet with an extension carrying a ligament eye and a pivot hole, and at the other end slit and pressed out to form a socket for a two-character type slug used with a shifting cradle.
