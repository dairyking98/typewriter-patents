## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** Wheel typewriters need an impression hammer that strikes with a controlled, hand-independent blow and is simple to cock and reset.

**Solution:** A spring-driven sliding hammer in a tube is cocked by the key-operated lever, released by a trigger tripped by an adjustable screw on that same lever, and reset on the lever's return.

**How it works:** The hammer and its spiral spring sit mostly enclosed in a horizontal slotted tube on the frame. The bifurcated upper end of the operating lever, which swings from the key mechanism, first compresses the spring against projections on an adjustable rod through the hammer. Then an adjustable set screw on the lever strikes the trigger and lifts its catch off a cross piece on the hammer's pendent lug, so the spring throws the hammer forward against the paper and type. On the return stroke a cross pin on the lever catches the lug and draws the hammer back to the cocked position. A nut on the rod threaded from the back sets the spring tension and thus the blow force.
