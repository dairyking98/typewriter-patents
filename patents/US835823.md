## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Carriage feed on motor-driven (electric) typewriters needed to be positive and not depend on a spring-driven carriage with a holding dog that had to be released before the carriage could be moved by hand.

**Solution:** A feed pawl riding on a friction-held sliding plate is moved up and forward, then down and back, by a key-controlled element, so the pawl itself pushes the carriage one tooth per stroke and the carriage is otherwise freely movable.

**How it works:** The carriage runs on rollers in guides and carries two rack bars. A feed pawl sits in a diagonal guide on a slide that is held to a fixed plate by friction greater than the friction between pawl and slide. When the key-driven element (an electromagnet armature in the example) moves, a lever and bell-crank first slide the pawl diagonally into engagement with the rack, then drag pawl and slide together one tooth, feeding the carriage; in the return stroke the pawl drops out of the tooth and slides back to the next tooth. A spring on a rod stores the feeding force in the first form. A spring-held roll on the fixed frame engages the second rack to check carriage momentum. In the preferred form the pawl is normally out of the rack, so the carriage is free to move either way without a release. Feeding happens before the type wheel strikes. The platen has spring-mounted friction rolls so papers of different widths can be used.
