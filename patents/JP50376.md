## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A Japanese typewriter should write either vertically or horizontally (right-to-left or left-to-right), with easy change between the two feeds.

**Solution:** A single handle-turned change-over decides whether pressing the print lever advances the paper cylinder around (vertical feed) or along (horizontal feed) its axis, and which way the horizontal pawl pushes.

**How it works:** The paper cylinder rides on a carriage over a loose-type magazine. Vertical feed swings arms with a pawl rod that steps a ratchet on the cylinder; horizontal feed turns a gear that meshes with a rack under the cylinder frame, sliding it along its axis. A handle-turned pawl spindle and cam slide a bent lever into or out of a hole in the press lever: forward, the press lever swings the lever and the pawl steps the horizontal gear on each print; rearward, the press lever misses the lever and instead a sliding pushing piece lifts the vertical-feed arm plate. The pawl has an upright working face and a sloping face, and turning the spindle 180 degrees reverses the horizontal feed direction. Separate vertical and horizontal space keys space without printing, and cams adjust the spacing stroke, with sloped stops to prevent bounce.

## Description (English translation of the Japanese text, opening)

> Specification. Vertical and horizontal spacing device for a typewriter. Nature and object of the invention. This invention relates to a vertical and horizontal spacing device for a typewriter, built as follows. A shaft is placed beside the press lever that works the printing mechanism, and a gear is fixed to it. A pawl spindle, carrying a pawl that engages the teeth of this gear, is mounted in a support frame fitted on the shaft so that it can turn. On the same shaft is fitted a lever that turns together with the support frame and can also slide along the shaft. This lever is put through a hole made in the press lever and through the hole of a pushing piece fitted so that it can slide along one side of the press lever. The hole in the press lever is made wide, so that the lever can move from a front position to a rear position; in the front position the lever touches the step at the top of the hole and is pressed down with the press lever, and in the rear position that part of the hole is made long, so that the lever is not acted on even when the press lever is pressed down.
