## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Printing many kinds of characters such as kanji quickly and clearly, saving hand writing and copying, in a machine that also handles character spacing, line spacing, writing visibility and ribbon wear.

**Solution:** A key-per-character machine in which each key releases a vertical type rod with a removable type head that is pushed down through a pyramid funnel onto paper wrapped on a rubber cylinder, which is turned by a pawl and toothed ring for character spacing and shifted sideways one line space per revolution.

**How it works:** Many type rods hang radially around a funnel, each held up by a hook spring resting on a strip. Pressing a key with a long and a short leg frees the rod's hook and presses a bar chain (bar, spring bar, driving bar) that drives the rod down so its type head prints through the ribbon on the paper on the cylinder. Halfway through the stroke the same bar moves levers that turn a pawl wheel, which steps a toothed ring on the cylinder by one tooth, giving the character space by turning the cylinder; other levers feed the ribbon and a wavy-grooved drum swings the ribbon sideways to spread wear. When the cylinder has made one revolution a projection on the ring trips a detent, and a wound elastic band moves the cylinder one line space. A handle raises the cylinder to show the text, a second toothed ring and key step the spacing back, and a rack rod with a handle moves the line back.

## Description (English translation of the Japanese text, opening)

> Printing machine. [Summary.] This invention comprises: type rods with type heads that can be changed at will; a set of devices that press a rod down to print at a fixed place on the paper cylinder; a device that adjusts the spacing of the characters by turning the paper cylinder automatically each time a character is printed; a device that moves the cylinder one line space each time it has made one revolution; a device by which these character and line spacings can be changed at will; a device that raises the paper cylinder so that the characters just printed can be seen; and a device that, to save ribbon, feeds the ribbon out from its spool while swinging it in a wave and then automatically feeds it back. The object of the invention is to print quickly and clearly characters of very many kinds, such as kanji, saving the labour of writing and copying by hand, for use in commerce in general and elsewhere. The attached drawings show a construction that attains the object.
