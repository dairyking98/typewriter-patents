## Summary

**Kind:** type-element machine · **Subject (patent classes):** Typewriters or selective printing mechanisms characterised by the mounting, arrangement or disposition of the types or dies with types or dies arranged in stationary or sliding cases or frames or upon flexible strips, plates, bars or rods; Japanese-language typewriter

**In the English translation of the patent’s own words:** This invention relates to a Japanese-script printing machine in which the machine body is mounted, free to slide, on a rail shaft laid across the frame, and rollers on the back of the body are supported on a cross shaft laid across the frame, so that the body can be tipped forward about the rail shaft.

**How it works (reading):** Hang the whole machine body on a sliding rail shaft so it tips forward on pulling the handle lever, which both exposes the printed area and lets the printing lever reach into the tilted type store to grip a type.

## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** In existing Japanese tray machines the type is pushed up from below and gripped by the printing lever, which limits the depth of the type store, and the printed area is hard to see.

**Solution:** Hang the whole machine body on a sliding rail shaft so it tips forward on pulling the handle lever, which both exposes the printed area and lets the printing lever reach into the tilted type store to grip a type.

**How it works:** The body hangs from pulleys on a rail shaft and slides sideways; rollers at its back run on a fixed cross bar and a stop limits the forward tip. Pulling the handle lever tips the body forward and drops the printing lever's curved tip into the gap at the wanted type in the store. Further pressing turns a bell crank that pulls a link pivoted above the printing lever pivot, swinging the printing lever while pushing a secondary lever pivoted below it, so the secondary lever's projection enters the groove on the type's back and clamps it. The gripped type touches a sprung ink roller on the handle lever and then strikes the paper cylinder. Releasing the handle returns the body under its own weight and frees the type.
