## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** On a flat-platen writing machine the writing mechanism moves only in fixed line-spacing steps, so it cannot print between lines, and work replaced on the platen cannot be realigned exactly.

**Solution:** Fine longitudinal shifting of the line-spacing racks, self-retaining, moves the whole writing mechanism a small amount apart from the step-by-step feed.

**How it works:** The writing mechanism hangs by rollers from guides between standards that travel lengthwise on two track rails, each with a line-spacing rack. The standards run on grooved rollers on a rounded rib atop each rail, and work rollers bear on the underside of an outward flange, so the standards bear on both upper and lower track surfaces for even travel. An adjusting device at the forward end of the track frame shifts the line-spacing racks finely and holds them in the shifted position, enabling inter-line writing and re-registering of repeated work. A line finder slides under the base plate, worked by a pivoted lever, to show the line position. The step-by-step feed uses rigid and loose dog plates.
