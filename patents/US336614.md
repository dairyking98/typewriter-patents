## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Finger-keys on typebar machines were fixed to key levers by screw-threaded or pinned straps that let the key turn so the letters tilted, and the printed characters in the key tops were uncovered and easily defaced.

**Solution:** A springy sheet-metal link pivoted in a strap on the key lever clasps the split lower end of the key stem by simple push-in, and the key top carries a concave glass cover over the printed character disk.

**How it works:** A metal strap with a tongue bent over the top of the key lever holds a rivet on which a springy rectangular link pivots. The link has a thin split partition so its upper ends spring together as a clasp. The finger-key stem, slit lengthwise at the bottom, is pushed astride the partition through the guide-bar perforation, and dovetailed bevels keep it from coming loose or turning. The key itself is a metal disk on the stem, a printed leather or cloth disk, and a concave glass cover held by a thin cylinder spun over the edges.

**Features:**

- **Selection:** [Full keyboard, a key per character](../features/sel-keyboard-full.md)

**Parts:** key lever; strap; tongue; connecting link; split partition; finger-key stem; key disk; printed index disk; concave glass cover; retaining cylinder; guide bar

**Numbers:** six parallel guide bars

**Improves on:**

- screw-threaded and pinned finger-key stems: push-in spring clasp that cannot turn and needs no tool in tight space
