## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Existing machines have hard-hitting keys that tire the typist, hide the line being written, and use a ribbon; spacing is also uniform regardless of letter width.

**Solution:** A visible-writing typebar machine with rubber types inked from a resting pad, three characters per bar selected by shifting the paper carriage, and a nested-frame escapement that gives each letter a width-dependent space.

**How it works:** About 32 spring-loaded keys act through toggle-like linkages on typebars whose rubber types rest on an ink pad and jump forward to the paper on a rubber-covered roller when a key is pressed. Each bar carries three types (capital, small, figure) in a row; three levers on the left slide the paper carriage forward or back so one of the three types meets the print point, with a spring holding the chosen case. A spring drum pulls the carriage along, checked by a rack, gear and ratchet wheel held by a two-armed escapement; three nested frames under the keys are depressed by the keys and release the ratchet by two, three or four teeth depending on the letter width, with a hand-pressed frame for a full word space. A hand lever returns the carriage and advances the line.
