## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typewriters of the time hide the paper or force the carriage to be lifted to read what has been written; the inventor wants a horizontal, always visible sheet and a simpler machine.

**Solution:** A Crandall-style type cylinder with several rows is rotated to the pressed key's letter by a key-driven gear train, then the whole cylinder swings down onto the ribbon and the horizontal paper on the roller.

**How it works:** Each key lever has a curved slot that rides on a pin of an arm on a long oscillating shaft; depressing the key turns this shaft, which drives a segment-and-gear train that rotates the type cylinder to the key's angle. The cylinder stays where the last key left it, and the slots are shaped so any later key still brings its type to the print position. As the key lever rises it lifts a cross bar, which pulls the cylinder's carrier cylinder and shaft down against the ribbon and paper, and a ratchet toothed bar holds the cylinder rotationally during the strike. Case or row changes slide the cylinder axially via a plate, two levers and a ratchet-wheel sleeve, with the third and fourth rows being capitals. A space key lifts a pawl off a ratchet wheel and when released lets it drop to the next tooth, moving the rack-driven carriage one letter pitch, and a line-space lever also lifts the rack out of mesh.
