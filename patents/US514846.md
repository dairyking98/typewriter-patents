## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Typebar typewriters are long and tiring to operate because keys pivoted between their ends have to be worked with wrist movement; figures are also tedious to align in columns.

**Solution:** Bell-crank key levers whose finger portions point inward toward the typebars, a shifting frame carrying two staggered rows of typebars, and a detachable notating scale for decimal columns.

**How it works:** Bell-crank key levers hang on a transverse rod and are joined by wires to short arms on the typebars; the finger ends point inward so the keys move on an arc matching elbow movement, and the machine is shorter. Wire attachment points vary with lever length to equalise key touch. The typebars are pivoted in two staggered rows on U-shaped hangers on a V-section curved end piece of a shifting frame, so the lower row bars are longer to reach the same print point. Depressing a side lever lifts the inner end of the shifting frame, bringing the upper-case letters of each bar to the print point instead of the lower-case. A universal frame behind the key levers moves a vibrating frame with feed dogs against the feed rack on every key. Scales on the type-bar guide with platen-mounted pointers show the print position, and a clip-on scale with decimal notches lets figure columns be aligned by units, tens and hundreds.
