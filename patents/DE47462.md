## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A simple keyboard typewriter is wanted in which one key stroke selects, locks and prints a character from a type disc.

**Solution:** A horizontal type disc with types on its underside is turned by an amount fixed by key lever length and slot depth, locked by a pin dropping into a hole, and then pressed down onto the paper roller by the swinging machine frame.

**How it works:** Keys in three rows sit on levers of different lengths on a common shaft; each lever stops at the bottom of a slot of its own depth, so the shaft turns by an angle unique to the key. Bevel gear segments carry that rotation to a vertical shaft and a toothed sector that turns the type disc, which carries three groups of types (capitals, small letters, figures) near its rim. Continued key pressure swings the hinged frame down; a linkage raises a locking pin into the hole opposite the chosen type, locking the wheel and tilting its printing side, while a counter-pressure roller stops the light disc bending. During the first part of this drop a wedge on the base plate pushes a rack and the paper roller carriage forward one letter space; at the bottom the roller presses the paper against the type. An absorbent ink roller on a counterweighted lever blackens the types as they pass, a hand wheel with a spring latch picks the type group, and the frame bar gives word space. A variant puts the types on an arc-shaped sector with the three groups on faces of a rotatable prism.
