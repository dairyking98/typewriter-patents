## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Worrall wants a typewriter in which the type frame, the paper bed movement and the impression piston are all driven by a continuously rotating shaft or other power.

**Solution:** A treadle- or power-driven shaft reciprocates a frame of type-pins past a piston, while depressed stop-pins halt the frame at the chosen character and the piston drives the type-pin onto the paper.

**How it works:** A treadle turns a stepped band pulley and a cone-pulley shaft, whose crank and cross-head push the type-carrying frame forward at each revolution; springs draw it back until stopped by a pin that has been depressed. Type-holding frames carry batteries of type pins in several fonts or styles and can be slid sideways to select a font. An eccentric on the shaft works the piston that presses the aligned type-pin onto the paper, with a conical recess to centre it. A disc, ratchet and pawl, and interchangeable cord drums move the paper carriage.
