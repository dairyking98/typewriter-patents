## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Wheel typewriters of the day position the type carrier badly, especially at speed or after wear, because the carrier overthrows or is shifted bodily before it is properly located, and the lock does not hold it during printing.

**Solution:** A keyboard machine in which the type wheel is positioned by a three-armed lever pulled against a stopping link so that opposing pins on opposite sides of the wheel axis hold it exactly, with the key, wheel frame and positioning gear all locked together while the wheel swings to the paper.

**How it works:** Pressing a key moves one of three bails (two half-width bails and one full-width) through adjusting screws set for that key, and the bail moves sliding rods through levers. A rod pushes a plate against the arms of a three-armed lever, rocking it off dead centre and turning the type wheel, and then carries it bodily so the wheel rotates further. The full-width bail then draws a link whose slot end meets a pin on the opposite end of the wheel's cross-piece, so the wheel is stopped and held by two pins on opposite sides of its axis. The wheel sits on a swinging bar and the continued key motion moves it bodily back against the platen for the impression, while a locking bar under the key arms keeps the other keys from moving and locks the key, frame and gear together. A carriage spring on a pulley and cord (tension set by a thumb ratchet) advances the paper carriage a tooth on the return via a fast-and-loose pawl, a space key works the same bell-crank with lost motion, and two shift keys slide the wheel vertically for three rows of type. A ribbon between two spools is fed by a pawl bar moved by the wheel frame, which can be reversed by swapping the pawl.
