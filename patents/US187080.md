## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Earlier writing machines need the operator to find and set the type by hand; the inventor wants a wheel whose rotation is governed by keys.

**Solution:** A clockwork-driven type-wheel spins continuously and is stopped at the chosen character by a key tooth engaging a spiral toothed rib on a drum.

**How it works:** A drum with a spiral toothed rib and a spur wheel is turned by clockwork, carrying the type-wheel (two disks, letters and figures) on a feathered shaft. A pawl on a hinged frame normally holds the spur wheel; pressing a key lifts the frame and pawl so the wheel turns, while the key's tooth meets the spiral rib and stops it at that character. The type-wheel shaft is spring-raised so the type clear the paper between blows, and a gravitating frame strikes it down to print. A hand lever slides the wheel along its shaft to bring the letter or figure disk over the bed. A two-bar sheet-holder is advanced by a pawl and rack and returned by a cord and spring reel, with a cam feed-wheel that opens the bars to advance the sheet.
