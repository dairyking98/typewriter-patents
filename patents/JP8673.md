## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** The Hammond shuttle needed to be light yet strong and carry many characters, change quickly between character sets, and let the arm follow fast key strokes on alternating sides; a blind-writing version also needed lighter blows that do not punch through paper.

**Solution:** A ring-shaped hard-rubber shuttle with an inner metal flange running between a two-part anvil, notched for several character sets and released from its driving arm by a slide, driven by swinging speed dogs, with a small seven-character shuttle for Braille where two keys build each character.

**How it works:** A hard-rubber ring shuttle, cast onto a pierced metal flange, rides on the anvil post between a fixed lower and a removable upper anvil part, so it can be lifted off and changed. A shuttle-arm finger engages one of several notches in the flange, one per character set; pulling a slide draws the finger out so the shuttle can be turned to another set without lifting it. Key drivers swing speed dogs about the centre post to push a stud on the shuttle arm, so one dog can start while the other is still returning. In the Braille version a seven-character shuttle and a hammer with three (or two columns of) pits emboss a character in two successive key strokes, with ordinary spacing leaving a small gap within and a large gap between characters.
