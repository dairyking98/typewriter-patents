**In the English translation of the patent’s own words:** This invention relates to a typewriter characterised in that a character is written directly on a plane formed by the tops of many keys arranged in a writing board, and the character is printed in reduced size on a print cylinder by the tips of typebars.

## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Typewriters need a set of types and the operator must hunt for the right key; the inventor wants a machine that prints any size or style of writing freely and quickly with no types.

**Solution:** A writing board made of many close-packed spring keys is written on directly; the keys under the pen strokes drive typebar tips that print the shape as a reduced cluster of dots on a print cylinder.

**How it works:** Many spring-loaded keys are packed so their tops form one plane, and each has its own typebar whose tip ends at the bottom of the board. Writing a character on the key tops presses only the keys under the strokes, so only those tips strike the paper on the cylinder, printing a reduced dot pattern. The board slides left and right on rails over the cylinder. The cylinder is spring-urged by a ratchet wheel and held by a pawl on a space lever; pressing the space key releases one step and, through a gear, moves an ink brush from an ink well across the tip ends.

## Description (English translation of the Japanese text, opening)

> Specification. Typewriter. Nature and object of the invention. This invention relates to a typewriter characterised in that a character is written directly on a plane formed by the tops of many keys arranged in a writing board, and the character is printed in reduced size on a print cylinder by the tips of typebars. Its object is a simple, new typewriter that needs no types and prints freely and quickly whatever the size or style of the writing. Brief description of the drawings. Fig. 1 is a front view of the typewriter, Fig. 2 a plan. Detailed description. Many keys (3), each with a spring (2), are packed closely in a writing board (1), so that their tops form one plane. Each key (3) has its own typebar (4), whose tip (5) projects at the bottom of the board, where all the tips are gathered together.
