## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** Lever-to-rod connections in typewriters are hard to assemble, misalign, and cost much.

**Solution:** A rod end with a ball bearing drops into an undercut transverse socket in the key lever and is held by a locking sleeve that also stops rotation.

**How it works:** A key lever has a transverse socket through its upper edge, a round bearing part with a narrower mouth. The connecting-rod end carries a ball of larger diameter than the mouth on a reduced neck, allowing limited oscillation. A tubular locking sleeve slides over the projecting faces of the ball and the lever, interlocking them. A flat on the rod end and the sleeve prevent relative rotation.

**Features:**

- **Selection:** [Full keyboard, a key per character](../features/sel-keyboard-full.md)
- **Frame and body:** [Few parts, low cost](../features/frm-few-parts.md), [Adjustment and wear take-up](../features/frm-adjustment-wear.md)

**Parts:** key lever; holding socket; connecting rod; rod end; ball bearing; locking sleeve; flat face
