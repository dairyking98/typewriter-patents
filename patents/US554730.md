## Summary

**Kind:** type-element machine · **Subject (patent classes):** Types or dies fixed on wheel, drum, cylinder, or like carriers with one or more carriers travelling across copy material in letter-space direction

**In the patent’s own words:** This invention relates to writing-machines, and has special reference to the class known as wheel-machines, wherein the type or characters to be printed are located upon a wheel or cylinder capable of being turned upon its axis by the operation of keys to bring a letter into position to be printed.

**How it works (reading):** A bank of key levers stops two opposed swinging plates against each other so the type cylinder jumps straight from its last position to the next letter by the shortest path, and a spring-released print and ink action plus per-key variable feed give proportional spacing.

## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Wheel machines turn the type wheel from a fixed home position or all the way round for each letter, wasting travel, momentum and time. They also space every letter the same distance, which forces distorted type.

**Solution:** A bank of key levers stops two opposed swinging plates against each other so the type cylinder jumps straight from its last position to the next letter by the shortest path, and a spring-released print and ink action plus per-key variable feed give proportional spacing.

**How it works:** Each key lever is a free bell-crank that, when depressed, pushes one of two swinging frames until the lever is jammed between two notched plates; the frames are linked to the two arms of a gear sector, which turns a pinion and the vertical shaft carrying the cylindrical type shell. Where the lever meets the plates sets the net sector throw, so the cylinder moves forward or back only the distance between the last and next letter and is then locked by the lever for the whole stroke. A second frame, moved by the full key stroke, lifts an ink pad against the type face, trips a pawl, and a spring then snaps the paper carriage's rock shaft so the paper strikes the type. A third frame draws a spacing pawl across a rack on the carriage; a notched spacing plate gives m and w a longer feed and i, l, t a shorter one. Two keys lift the cylinder on its shaft to bring the capital row or figures row to the print line, with a lock bar, and also lengthen the feed.
