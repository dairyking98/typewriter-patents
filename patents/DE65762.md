## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** A key-driven type-wheel machine needs a simple way to turn the wheel to the right letter from a rest position in either direction while the same stroke prints, with the wheel never turning more than half a revolution.

**Solution:** Two opposed toothed drive arms, one turning the type wheel shaft left and the other right, are swung by the key levers, and a stop on each key lever halts the wheel shaft at that letter before a spring-driven print rail strikes the paper against the type.

**How it works:** Each key lever presses on one of two drive arms with curved toothed segments; the left group of keys swings one arm, the right group the other. The swung arm meshes with a gear on the upright type wheel shaft and turns it until a stop arm on the shaft meets a stop on the key lever, which fixes the letter at the print point; keys nearer the centre line give smaller deflection. On release a spring returns the arm until the shaft's stop arm rests between the two arm stops in the home position. The type wheel carries several rows of type and slides along the shaft, being lifted to different heights by a rod with slots of different depth, driven by the keys for capitals and figures. The key stroke also cocks a print rail which a spring then throws forward to strike the paper against the type, and a pawl on a lever steps the carriage by rack on each stroke. Ribbon reels are advanced by pawls on a shifting rod. A variant adds a locking piece riding a notched disc to hold the wheel in home position.
