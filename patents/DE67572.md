## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** In a typebar machine with a circle of typebars striking the centre, the loosely supported paper carriage shifts slightly against the line direction during writing, so lines are no longer straight.

**Solution:** Guide the carriage frame on a rod through a pivoted rack frame with eccentric-adjustable rollers, and add a scale, pointer, margin stop and end-of-line bell.

**How it works:** The carriage frame hinges on a guide rod, and a rack-supporting frame hung on the same rod carries arms with rollers that slide on it. An eccentric lets the rollers be pressed more firmly against the rod when worn. A fixed rack and a spring-loaded oscillating rack engage a spacing pawl, so each stroke advances one tooth. Lugs on the frame rest on screws or on a second rod to relieve the pawl of the frame's weight. The frame can be tipped up to disengage the escapement so the carriage runs freely. A front rod carries rollers on a track with a 1 to 120 scale, a pointer showing the last letter, an adjustable stop for the left margin, and a stop pin that trips a hammer and bell at the line end.
