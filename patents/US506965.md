## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Connecting rods and key levers of typebar machines were costly to make and the rod slot wore and elongated with use until the key lever could slip lengthwise out of place.

**Solution:** Form the connecting rod from a blanked and folded U-section sheet-metal piece with a three-sided slot whose bent-back lip gives a broad wear surface, and fit a lugged first-order key lever into it.

**How it works:** A first-order key lever rocks in a slotted fulcrum post riveted to the bed plate; lugs near each end abut the rod and the finger-key stirrup, so the lever cannot slide endwise. Its rear end passes through an eye at the lower end of a vertical connecting rod, which is pivoted at the top to a lever on the type ring that drives the jointed type carrier. The rod is stamped flat with pivot holes, a clearance slot and the eye, then folded lengthwise into a U. The eye is cut on three sides and the severed metal bent back as a lip, giving a smooth, broad bearing for the lever's upper edge.
