## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** On a shift-type Underwood machine the platen could not be held in the upper (capital) position without the operator keeping the shift key down, and the shifted carriage could jar or tremble.

**Solution:** A hand-set locking lever catches a projection on the shift key to lock it down, and a spring-loaded steadying lever braces the carriage lifting frame firmly in either position.

**How it works:** Depressing a shift key rotates a bell-crank on each side, raising rollers that lift the front carriage section to the upper case. A pin on one shift key rides in an L-shaped slot of a locking lever, which falls forward and locks the key down until pulled back by hand; a tongue stops the lever being thrown back except when the key is up. Separately, a spring-loaded lever with an oblique slot is swung by a pin on the bell-crank; one member supports the lifting frame in its lower position and the other supports a pin on the frame in its upper position. The platen section is thus held firmly in either position, without hindering shifting.
