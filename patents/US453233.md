## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Shorthand machines needed to print phonographic characters very fast; one finger per symbol limits speed.

**Solution:** Three sets of six keys with finger-plates grouped so one finger can press one, two or three adjoining keys at once, each key's marker printing a stroke that combines into composite characters.

**How it works:** Each of three key sets (left, middle, right) has a central finger-plate, four surrounding plates and an outer encircling plate, so a finger on the abutting edges presses two or three keys together. Each key is a pivoted lever whose arm carries a type face (a marker) that prints a straight or curved stroke on a paper ribbon moving right to left under an inking ribbon. Strokes always print in the same relative position so any combination forms a composite mark, giving 63 symbols per set. Depressing a key also works a spacing lever with hook and ratchet that feeds the paper ribbon. A sideways-shifting spacer bar engages one of two ribbon-spool pawls alternately to reverse the ink ribbon. The marker guide is hinged in two parts so it can be opened for adjustment.
