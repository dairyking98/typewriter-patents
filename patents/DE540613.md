## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** In the line-spacing clutch of the main patent (DE508160), the radial pressure of the coupling bolts distorts the thin line-spacing wheel out of round across the small clearance, so the handle needs a large stroke before the clutch bites.

**Solution:** A slotted clamping ring between the line-spacing wheel and the platen cover is spread by the flats on the coupling bolt's end web, so the wheel keeps its round shape and the clutch engages with little rotation.

**How it works:** Coupling bolts lie in the platen cover along a diameter or chord. Turning a bolt about its axis makes the narrow edges of its end web step out of the cover's cylindrical surface and press on the inside of the line-spacing wheel, coupling the platen to the wheel. In this addition the web enters the slot of a slotted ring set between wheel and cover. The ring and bolt end are ground cylindrical together. When the bolt turns, the angled flats of the web spread the ring apart and press it firmly against the wheel, so the wheel cannot go out of round. The ring may be as wide as the wheel or narrower and seated in a groove in the cover; if the bolt is flattened at both ends the ring is made in two halves.
