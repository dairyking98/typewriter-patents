## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** A type-wheel machine should set the wheel to a letter and print it in one key stroke, and also let the carriage be stopped at fixed points of the line so that columns of figures such as invoices can be typed under each other.

**Solution:** Keys in two groups drive the wheel left or right through two toothed arcs that rotate the wheel and swing it to the platen together, with a heart-shaped ratchet wheel and pliers-like double pawl stopping the rotation at the letter, and tabulating lock keys stopping the carriage.

**How it works:** The type levers are split into two groups with the space lever between; a struck lever depresses its group's frame, which swings one of two toothed arcs, and the arc rolls a gear on the type wheel axle, turning the wheel and at the same time swinging its axis down toward the paper cylinder. The levers have stepped stop faces of different length that strike a third frame, whose arm sets a pawl into a heart-shaped ratchet wheel (one tooth per lever, spaced further apart outward) to stop rotation at that letter. A second counter-pawl grips from the opposite side like pliers so the pawl is not overstressed. As the wheel lands on the paper cylinder a ratchet wheel tooth also meets a fixed tooth to fix the exact position. Three type rows are chosen by keys that lift the wheel axle by different amounts, secured by a notched locking piece. An inking roller on a spring arm is pushed aside by the swinging wheel. A rocking pawl pair steps the carriage by one tooth per stroke and locks it, with a lighter space lever lifting the pawls for word spaces, and a bell, left-margin locking lever and six column-lock key levers with a settable stop rail complete the carriage.
