## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** On typewriters with variable (letter-width) carriage spacing, the carriage cannot easily be set back to an exact point on the line without loosening the paper on the platen, which is risky with multiple carbon copies.

**Solution:** Replace the carriage rack with a screwed spindle that the escapement catches engage, so the carriage can also be moved finely by turning the spindle by hand.

**How it works:** A threaded spindle is mounted rotatably but not slidably in the paper carriage. Two catches shaped to the thread, one on a key-driven rocking lever and one on a slide limited by slots, engage the thread and act like a conventional escapement on a rack as keys are struck. To move the carriage without typing, a small handwheel on the spindle end is turned, the escapement tooth acting as a stationary nut. A toothed wheel on the spindle end with a spring-pressed roller limits the motion to steps matching the letter-width unit gradation. A worm-wheel variant is also shown.

**Features:** [ESC:pawl-rack](../features/esc-pawl-rack.md), [ESC:variable-spacing](../features/esc-variable-spacing.md), [ESC:carriage-release](../features/esc-carriage-release.md)

**Parts:** screwed spindle; paper carriage; escapement catch; rocking lever; slotted slide; handwheel; toothed wheel; spring-pressed roller; worm-wheel

- rack-and-escapement carriage: fine hand repositioning without disturbing the paper
