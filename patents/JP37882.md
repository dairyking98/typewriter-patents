## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Existing machines for large character sets are complicated; a simple, easily worked typewriter is wanted, especially for Japanese telegram forms.

**Solution:** Move a type plate under a fixed index board with one handle; a conical point seating in a perforated plate corrects the position, and pressing the same handle strikes the paper onto the chosen character.

**How it works:** A type plate engraved with many characters slides on a cross-slide carriage, joined to a perforated plate and to a rod that comes out past the machine frame and over the index board, bearing a pointer and a frame-shaped handle lever. The paper frame above it carries paper and carbon, and moves lengthwise and crosswise for line and character spacing. Moving the handle until the pointer is on a character and pressing it presses an offset (crank-like) shaft, turning the printing lever, which strikes the paper onto the engraved face. During the stroke, a projection pushes a lever whose conical point enters a hole of the perforated plate and settles the type plate exactly before the print lever lands. Releasing the handle feeds one character space through a toothed frame, a sector plate and two projections; a handle pawl feeds the line. Pushing the printing-lever shaft sideways against its spring prints voiced marks at the shoulder of the previous character.

## Description (English translation of the Japanese text, opening)

> Specification. Typewriter. Nature and object of the invention. Under an index board, this invention provides a paper-holding frame that moves lengthwise and crosswise to give the character spacing and line spacing; under that, a type plate that can move lengthwise and crosswise, with a perforated plate joined to it. A rod fixed to them is carried over the side of the machine frame and extended above the index board; a frame-shaped lever is pivoted to it and carries a handle. On top of the machine frame a shaft, part of which is offset sideways like a crankshaft, is mounted parallel to the line of print, and the printing lever is fixed to it; the shaft is pressed from its end by a spring, so that it can slide a little against that spring. The part of the handle's lever that lies at right angles to the line of characters rests on the offset part of the shaft. So wherever the handle is moved over the index board, pressing it down turns the shaft a little and the printing lever strikes the paper over the type plate. During this motion a lever carrying a conical point is moved, and the point enters a round hole of the perforated plate and stops the type plate in the right position; and…
