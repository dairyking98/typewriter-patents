## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A type-wheel that is positioned by a key must be locked exactly at the chosen letter and then struck with perfect alignment, with as few working parts as possible; earlier arrangements needed more parts and gave less accurate alignment.

**Solution:** The type-wheel shaft carries a double-toothed eccentric ratchet that moves with the wheel, and a pawl whose travel is graduated inversely to the wheel's travel locks the wheel at the chosen character, then the same key stroke throws the locked wheel against the paper like a hammer.

**How it works:** A shaft carries a type-wheel (one of three stacked wheels that can be shifted onto the shaft spline by one of two shift keys) and a ratchet whose teeth lie on two opposite sides of a neutral point. Each character key presses on stepped inclined flanges of one of two side frames; the frame's rack turns the shaft up to a half turn either way, farther for letters far from the zero point. At the same time the key presses the stepped flange of a central pawl frame, which has the opposite incline, so the pawl is already close to the ratchet when the wheel travel is long and moves farther when the wheel travel is short. When the wheel has turned to its place the pawl drops on the matching ratchet tooth and locks it. Continued key depression then pushes the pawl frame and the shaft frame forward, throwing the locked wheel against the paper or pad as a hammer, and also feeding the carriage by an arm on the tilting frame; a spring returns everything on release.
