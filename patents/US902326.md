## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Adjustable work-performing members such as type dies must move freely during adjustment yet be exactly positioned and rigidly held while striking, taking up the looseness needed for free shifting.

**Solution:** After approximate adjustment along guide surfaces, a separate device gives each selected member its final precise position and holds it rigidly during the work stroke, after which it returns to normal.

**How it works:** Members are mounted on a carrier and shifted by adjusting means until approximately in position. They are built to move freely along guide faces. When a member is about to work, a precisionizing device engages it, takes up the slack and locks it. After the stroke the member is returned to its normal inoperative position. The drawing shows a type-die carrier in a casing with a main operating shaft (a power-driven typographic machine).
