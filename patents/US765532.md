## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typewriters need lateral motion of types or paper as each character is struck, and the operator must attend to starting new lines.

**Solution:** A type cylinder with one longitudinal row of characters per letter-space and a hammer cylinder, so printing travels left to right and lines follow automatically without lateral motion of type or paper.

**How it works:** A hollow light-metal type cylinder carries 32 longitudinal rows parallel to its axis, each row with different characters and as many characters as there are letter spaces in the line; 30 rows carry characters and 2 are blank (one blank at the printing level, one for spacing with a projecting arm). Cords, pulleys and springs from a few keys, used singly and in combinations, rotate the cylinder axle by the amount a drawn-out line allows, with a spring returning it. A hammer cylinder on a rocking frame faces the type cylinder with the paper between them and strikes the selected character. The paper is fed so lines slant or lie straight depending on feed angle relative to the hammer cylinder axis.
