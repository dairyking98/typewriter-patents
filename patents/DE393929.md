## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** In pointer typewriters the pointer and character plate sit far from the type wheel and are linked by linkage, so setting is imprecise and parts bend at impact, and the pointer must move in all directions.

**Solution:** Put the pointer and character plate in the machine's symmetry plane directly above the type wheel axis, and have the pointer drive the wheel through a toothed segment and gear.

**How it works:** The type wheel sits in a swinging frame above the paper roller, with its axis in a vertical plane. A plate above carries the characters in three arc-shaped rows. A handled pointer is fixed to a toothed segment that meshes with a gear on the type wheel shaft; moving the pointer sideways turns the wheel so the chosen type faces the roller. Moving the pointer to another row swings the wheel frame and toothed segment bearing together on common pins, so the wheel follows. Hitting a key swings the frame down so the wheel prints, and a spring returns it. A spring-loaded pawl with as many notches as there are type rows holds the selected row while the frame is down. A separate key makes spaces.
