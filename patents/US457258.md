## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Typebar machines are costly and frictional because key levers and typebars are joined through intermediate parts, the shift moves the heavy platen, the ribbon obstructs the written line, and carriage feed only happens after the typebar has returned.

**Solution:** A cheap typebar machine in which key levers interlock directly with the typebars, the case shift lifts the whole typebar frame while the platen stays still, a pivoted guide frame carries and lifts the ribbon, and a two-part jointed dog on two racks feeds the carriage half a space on the stroke and half on the return.

**How it works:** The inner end of each key lever has a curved socket that catches a curved arm on its typebar, so pressing the key swings the bar nearly a quarter turn to the paper. Division plates separate the bars and levers. Pressing the front of a lifting frame raises the typebar frame so capitals align with the print point, with set screws regulating the lift. Every key and the spacing lever depress an L-shaped feed lever whose head carries a jointed two-part dog working alternately in two racks on a stamped one-piece carriage plate, moving it one half space on the way down and one half space on the way up, while a spring drum pulls the carriage. The feed lever also raises the pivoted ribbon guide frame before the bar strikes, then drops it so the line stays visible, and its dogs wind the ribbon onto spools, reversed by a sliding bar.
