## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Starting a word with a capital meant spacing, then holding the shift key with one hand while typing the capital with the other. This was slow, and the impression could land while the platen was still moving, returning or vibrating.

**Solution:** A deeper dip of the space key also shifts the platen, which then locks automatically and is released by the next printing or space stroke, so one key does both space and shift.

**How it works:** A projection on each space key lever overhangs the adjacent shift key lever. A shallow dip of the space key spaces the carriage only. A deeper dip pushes the shift key lever down, raising the platen to upper case, and one escapement dog simply travels further without releasing. At the end of the shift stroke a spring-drawn latch on the shift lever snaps under a fixed locking member, holding the platen still. A controlling member on the latch rests on the universal bar. The next printing or space key stroke pushes the universal bar down, which trips the latch and lets the platen drop back after one capital. A hand-moved spring plate disables this automatic lock, and a separate permanent shift key with its own latch holds upper case indefinitely.
