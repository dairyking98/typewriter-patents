## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** Carriage propulsion on a typebar machine needs a spring drive that does not run down noticeably over the full carriage travel and is simple to apply.

**Solution:** A long spiral spring inside a tube around a shaft, acting by torsion, pulls the carriage through a cord and snail cam, with a ratchet and double pawl to wind it.

**How it works:** A shaft runs the length of the machine above the key levers, surrounded by a tube with a spiral spring in the annular space, one end fixed to the tube and the other to the shaft. The tube end carries a ratchet wheel with a crank stud for winding and a double-toothed pawl as detent. At the other end of the shaft a snail or involute cam takes a cord that passes over a sheave to the carriage, the cam compensating for the falling spring force. The carriage escapement and the ribbon-roll movement at right angles to carriage travel are only mentioned in the opening.

**Features:**

- **Inking:** [Ink ribbon](../features/ink-ribbon.md)
- **Spacing and escapement:** [Mainspring-drawn carriage](../features/esc-spring-drum.md), [Pawl-and-rack escapement](../features/esc-pawl-rack.md)

**Parts:** shaft; tube; spiral spring; ratchet wheel; double pawl; snail cam; cord; sheave; carriage; platen

**Improves on:**

- involute spring carriage drives: a long torsion spring barely diminishes its pull over a long run, so the compensator is less needed
