## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Spring-driven platen impression accelerates the platen so it hits the type at top speed, giving noisy, blurry prints.

**Solution:** Drive the platen forward and back by a toggle (knee) joint powered by a motor, so platen speed is nearly zero at the moment of impression while force is greatest.

**How it works:** The platen and its end walls form a part of the carriage movable transversely, resting just behind the print point. A knee joint at each end of a rail, driven by cranks from a motor shaft via helical gears and a sliding coupling gear, is straightened to push the platen against the standby type and bent to withdraw it. The rail spans the carriage so impression is even at any carriage position. Carriage spacing is by the usual ratchet and rack from the typebar drive.
