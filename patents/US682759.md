## Summary

**Kind:** type-element machine · **Subject (patent classes):** Typewriters or selective printing mechanisms characterised by the mounting, arrangement or disposition of the types or dies with types or dies mounted on carriers rotatable for selection the plane of the type or die face being parallel to the axis of rotation, e.g. with type on the periphery of cylindrical carriers

**In the patent’s own words:** The objects of this invention are to provide a construction adapted to print an indefinite number of characters without enlarging the diameter of the segment or segments, to properly ink that character and only that character which is about to be printed, and to produce the accurate alinement of the characters printed.

**How it works (reading):** Stack several independent single-row type segments on a common axis, lift the chosen one into the impression line, turn only that one to the character, ink only that character with a roller and clamp the segment rigid as the hammer strikes.

## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Type-segment machines of the Hammond class cannot hold many characters without a larger segment, ink the whole type surface (gumming unused type), and print letters with imperfect alignment, so they do not look like letter-press work.

**Solution:** Stack several independent single-row type segments on a common axis, lift the chosen one into the impression line, turn only that one to the character, ink only that character with a roller and clamp the segment rigid as the hammer strikes.

**How it works:** Three skeleton type segments, each with one row of type on its curved rim and a blank gap at the middle, ride on a rotatable sleeve that finger-key mechanism turns either way. The shift keys lift a slotted bar whose four arms hold the segments at spacing, raising the wanted segment into the horizontal impression line. A tongue on the sleeve fits a vertical groove in the hub of whichever segment is in the line, so only that one turns; stop arms and a clamping lever stop the others from rotating. A swinging arm with ink rollers is tied to the hammer and rolls up the vertical impression line, so it touches only the character at the printing point, then rolls on an inking platen to recharge. As the hammer is released, a spring-linked bar pulls a clamping lever down onto a flange on the segment against a fixed finger, locking the segment at the exact position while the hammer forces the paper onto the type.
