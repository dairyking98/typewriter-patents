## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typists need a column-stop (tabulating) device that releases the carriage from the letter-spacing escapement and lets the spring-driven carriage run to preset points for typing columns of figures.

**Solution:** Adjustable stops on the carriage frame meet a key-operated stop member that moves into their path, arresting the freed carriage at the chosen column.

**How it works:** Adjustable stops are mounted on a longitudinal member of the paper carriage. A key-actuated stop lever swings a stop member into the path of these stops. Pressing the key also releases the carriage from the escapement so the actuating spring runs it until a stop meets the stop member. It is shown on an Oliver typebar machine with a shift frame, whose carriage rack is the front frame bar and an escape wheel on the pinion shaft.

**Features:** [Tabulator](../features/esc-tabulator.md), [Pawl-and-rack escapement](../features/esc-pawl-rack.md), [Carriage release](../features/esc-carriage-release.md), [Margin stops](../features/esc-margin-stop.md), [Mainspring-drawn carriage](../features/esc-spring-drum.md), [Typebars](../features/elem-typebar.md), [Shift by moving the platen](../features/shift-platen.md)

**Parts:** adjustable column stop; stop member; key lever; carriage; shift frame; rack bar; pinion; escape wheel; actuating spring

**Improves on:**

- Oliver typewriter column stop: adjustable carriage-mounted stops with a key-actuated stop member
