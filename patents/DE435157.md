## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Power-driven typebar machines are slowed by the drive rail staying coupled to the type lever through the whole stroke, and type levers can rebound at high speed.

**Solution:** The drive rail is suddenly stopped and retracted before the type reaches the platen, so the type lever finishes its stroke on its own momentum while the rail is ready for the next lever.

**How it works:** Key levers trip push levers hinged to the type levers; a spring-loaded drive rail, coupled to a continuously turning power shaft, throws the type lever forward. A rigid retraction linkage pulls the rail back suddenly before impact so the lever flies free and the rail can serve the next key. The push-lever ends are hooked so they catch the rail edge at the end of the return stroke and stop rebound. An adjusting screw acting on a resilient strip on the drive rail sets the moment of release.
