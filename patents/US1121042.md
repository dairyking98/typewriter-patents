## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** A front-strike machine with a ribbon vibrator needs a simple way to present either of two colour fields of the ribbon to the printing point, or to switch the ribbon off for cutting stencils.

**Solution:** A hand-set finger lever mounted on the key-driven rocking bar engages the ribbon vibrator's operating lever at different distances from its pivot, giving a long throw, a short throw, or none.

**How it works:** A bell-crank ribbon vibrator pivoted on a bracket in front of the platen holds the ribbon in a C-shaped loop below the printing point; a spring returns it. Each key stroke rocks a bar, and a finger lever pivoted on that bar carries two engaging parts at different distances from its pivot. Set to one side, the far part strikes a lug on a horizontal lever that moves the vibrator, lifting the ribbon a long way to show one field (red); set to the other side, the near part gives a shorter throw to show another field (black). In the centre position a gap lines up with the lug, so the vibrator stays idle and the typebars strike bare for stencils. A spring-metal corrugated bracket detents the lever in its three positions, with a colour strip as an indicator. Variants use a slide with a slot and a stepped lever so intermediate throws can use the whole ribbon width.
