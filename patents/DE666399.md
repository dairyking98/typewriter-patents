## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Rigid links between the type cylinder shaft and the shift bracket jam if the upper and lower machine parts are not assembled in exactly the same plane, and the height-correcting piece strikes noisily.

**Solution:** Connect the cylinder shaft to the shift bracket by a rod with ball joints at both ends so it can tilt sideways, and make the conical height-correcting piece spring-mounted.

**How it works:** The type cylinder shaft slides axially in a sleeve to change case or row, and the sleeve swings about a horizontal axis to throw the cylinder against the paper roller. A rod with ball-and-socket ends on hardened steel discs links the shaft foot to the shift bracket, so it can lean sideways without binding; a threaded stud at one end sets the line height on the platen and is clamped. A disc with a conical edge on the shaft foot drops into a toothed piece during the strike, correcting the cylinder's height exactly. That piece is pivoted on a spring so it yields slightly, quieting the impact while keeping the position, and its axis is threaded for height adjustment.
