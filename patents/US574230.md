## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Provide a fast, evenly printing typewriter with variable letter spacing, easy case shift and simple carriage feed.

**Solution:** Keep a disc of type heads constantly spinning past an ink roller and platen, and let each key flip up only its own type head to strike.

**How it works:** A disc on a ball-bearing hollow shaft is driven continuously by a belt from a spring or electric motor. Its rim carries loose vertical spindles, each with a grooved type head holding a lower-case character above an upper-case one. Each key lowers a vertical typebar that strikes a tilting lever under the disc, which lifts that spindle; its pinion then meets a fixed rack so the head rolls over, is inked by the roller, and prints on the platen, after which a cam forces the spindle down. A slide plate, shifted by the operator, lets the bar travel further so the lever lifts the head higher for the upper case. The platen carriage is pulled by a belt from a spring barrel; each key stroke moves a rack that turns a ratchet and pulley to advance the carriage, and notches of different depth under the keys give variable spacing. A clutch released by a return key lets the spring pull the carriage back, and a spiral slot on the platen shaft line-spaces automatically on return.
