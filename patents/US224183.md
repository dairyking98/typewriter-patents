## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Hammond's earlier machine moved the type segment with lifting frames and a serrated stop, which was complicated and less certain in positioning the segment exactly at the moment of the hammer blow.

**Solution:** Notched key levers act directly as cams on an oscillating rod to rotate the type segment, and pins raised by the same keys catch a stop arm on the segment shaft so it is held exactly in place for the hammer.

**How it works:** Each key lever has a notch whose incline sits at a different eccentricity, so pressing a key pushes a cross rod forward or back by an amount that depends on the key's distance from the centre. The rod swings a vibrating shaft whose arm and link rotate a lever carrying a toothed sector, which meshes with a gear on the vertical shaft of the type segment, turning the segment to the chosen letter. The same key lifts a spring-returned pin that stands in the path of a stop arm on the segment shaft, arresting the segment and holding it exactly. A vibrating bar over all the levers lifts a dog that cocks and releases a spring-driven hammer, which strikes the paper against the type and rebounds to rest. The same bar rocks the escapement shaft to step the carriage by a pawl in combs, a central key spaces without printing, and a ribbon passes between two spools that reverse automatically. A mainspring and fusee pull the carriage.
