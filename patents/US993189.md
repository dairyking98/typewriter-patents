## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Front-strike typebar machines had uneven, heavy key touch because linkages to typebars at the sides of the machine transmitted side thrust, and typebars rebounded and wore in their bearings.

**Solution:** Each finger key drives its typebar through a two-shaft crank-and-rod train whose second (counter)shaft sits parallel to the typebar's plane of swing, so thrust is uniform and in-plane.

**How it works:** Pressing a key turns a front-to-back shaft through a crank; a rod from a second crank turns a parallel countershaft. A crank on the countershaft, carrying a universal-jointed connecting rod, pushes (or, reversed, pulls) the typebar up about its pivot to strike the platen front. A spring-type crank can replace a rigid one to lighten the touch, and a tension spring returns the bar onto a sand-filled cushioned rest. Typebar pivots are conical, held between two plates drawn together by a right-and-left screw to take up wear, and the return-spring anchors slide in radial slots to adjust tension. The bars are arranged on a cone of about 45 degrees so they all strike one point.

**Features:** [SEL:keyboard-full](../features/sel-keyboard-full.md), [ELEM:typebar](../features/elem-typebar.md), [IMP:element-to-platen](../features/imp-element-to-platen.md), [FRM:adjustment-wear](../features/frm-adjustment-wear.md), [FRM:writing-visible](../features/frm-writing-visible.md)

**Parts:** finger key; key shaft; countershaft; connecting rod; typebar; return spring; cushioned typebar rest; conical pivot bearing plate; arc-shaped supporting plate

**Numbers:** typebar swing about 99 degrees; 42 keys; pivot axes on a cone of about 45 degrees
