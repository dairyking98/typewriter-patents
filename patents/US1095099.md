## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** On a key-wheel typewriter the paper must be fed and pressed against the type accurately wherever the carriage happens to be along the line.

**Solution:** Use a long platen bar on a rectangular pivoted frame, pushed toward the type wheel by a bent lever with a long ledge that every key lever can press on.

**How it works:** The platen is a rectangular frame pivoted by trunnions in the carriage sides, long enough to hold the paper feed rollers inside it. A rod across the frame rests in a groove on the upper arm of a bent elbow lever. The lower arm is a long ledge parallel to the carriage travel, and each key lever acts through an intermediate lever resting on the ledge. Pressing any key depresses the ledge, swings the bent lever and pushes the platen frame against the paper and the key wheel. The feed rollers stay stationary while the frame reciprocates, and a slotted paper guide plate lets the rollers grip the paper at several points.
