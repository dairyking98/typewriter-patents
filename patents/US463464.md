## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Wheel typewriters of the day used many intermediate levers between keys, type-wheel, hammer and lock, and made it hard to insert paper behind the platen; the inventor wants a simpler, compact, direct-acting machine.

**Solution:** Each key lever carries its own rack that drives a long common pinion directly attached to the type-wheel, a double-sided locking wheel with half as many teeth stops it, and the carriage escapement release also fires the hammer bail.

**How it works:** Key levers pivoted on both sides of a frame have toothed arms that mesh with one long pinion on the type-wheel shaft, turning the wheel up to half a turn either way; a wedge arm in a V centering device returns the wheel to centre. A rocking bail under the keys swings locking pins against either side of the teeth of a locking wheel on the shaft, so each tooth fixes two positions. Three shift keys push a rod by slotted cams to slide the shaft axially to one of three type rows. The same bail depression lowers a rack bar and frees a carriage feed pawl; the spring-drum carriage's rock shaft then advances one tooth and its arms flip the hammer bail up behind the paper to print. The space lever frees the pawl but a second arm blocks the hammer bail. Ink comes from a reservoir through valved ducts to two pads touching each side of the wheel, with a line-end bell that can be set along a scale and rubber-band paper guides under the platen.
