## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Earlier platen feeds varied the feed pawl's stroke to change the feed amount, which causes wrong feeds and unreliable working.

**Solution:** The feed pawl always moves the drive ratchet one tooth, and a sliding gear meshing with one of several aligned gears of different diameters changes the ratio to the platen.

**How it works:** A ratchet drive gear is advanced one tooth per stroke by the feed pawl. Its shaft carries four fixed gears of equal pitch and different diameters, each meshing with a second-group gear. The second-group gears sit on eccentric bosses of spacer discs so their teeth all line up on one straight line on the far side. A sliding gear keyed to an intermediate shaft is moved by a knob and rollers along this line to mesh with one of them, giving a different gear ratio and so a different platen feed per tooth. A small gear on that shaft drives the platen gear.
