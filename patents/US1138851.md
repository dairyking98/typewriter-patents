## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A cipher message must be printed so the same letter comes out as many different characters, with no short repeating cycle, and be readable only on a machine set the same way.

**Solution:** A hand-turned type wheel with a matching index is coupled to a stack of spring-return stop disks which, stopped one after another by an escapement ring, shift the printed character away from the pointed-at letter by a changing offset.

**How it works:** A wide-rimmed type wheel carries two rows of 40 characters in opposite order, one for enciphering, one for deciphering. The operator pulls the finger piece out, turns the wheel until a pointer sits on the plain letter on the index disk, then pushes it in to lock wheel and index together. Turning further rotates a shaft of ring-and-disk pairs until the lug of the ring in line with a stepped escapement stop ring hits an irregularly spaced lug, after which the disk inside stops against its own lug; this sets how far the wheel is offset. The whole frame is pressed down to print the wheel on a paper tape and the wheel is raised by a spring. On release a ratchet steps a screw shaft that moves the stop ring to the next ring, so each letter uses a different offset, and the screw reverses at either end. Aligning notches with a taper projection locate each type at the print point, and an ink pad inks the rim.
