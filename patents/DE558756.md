## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** On shorthand machines the ribbon feed is derived from a platen that is advanced by a complicated ratchet drive, and the ribbon can smear the paper if the paper sticks while the platen turns.

**Solution:** Advance the paper by teeth acting on the paper itself, which turns the platen by friction, and take the ribbon drive from the platen rotation through a planetary gear on a swivel arm.

**How it works:** Paper runs from a spool over a guide plate and under the platen, where a pawl presses it against the platen and prevents backward movement. Feed teeth on a switching arrangement, actuated by a return spring after the keys are released, grip the paper and move it; friction turns the platen. A gear on the platen axis drives a gearbox in a swivel arm. Its last wheel engages the gear of one of two ribbon spools, selected by a hand lever and a spring that holds the arm in either end position. Differential planetary wheels with slightly differing tooth counts reduce the speed. Because the ribbon moves only when the paper moves, the ribbon cannot be moved past stationary paper.
