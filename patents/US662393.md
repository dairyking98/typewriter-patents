## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** In front-strike machines the typebars rest nearly horizontal, so the whole bar weight must be lifted by the key and the touch feels heavy.

**Solution:** A spring hooked to the typebar pulls it up at rest and down at the print position, passing through dead centre in line with the fulcrum, so it counterbalances the bar's weight at rest.

**How it works:** The typebar pivots on a wire and is joined to the key lever by a ligament. A spring anchored to a fixed rod hooks into the typebar. At rest the spring tends to raise the bar, cancelling its weight; part-way through the stroke the spring lines up with the fulcrum and does nothing; at the print point it pulls the bar back down. Variants use two opposed springs stopped by a wire and acting on a pin in the ligament or key lever, or a long heavy key lever whose weight balances the typebar.

**Features:**

- **Selection:** [Full keyboard, a key per character](../features/sel-keyboard-full.md)
- **Type element:** [Typebars](../features/elem-typebar.md)
- **Impression:** [Element struck to the platen](../features/imp-element-to-platen.md), [Spring-powered blow](../features/imp-spring-energy.md)

**Parts:** type-lever; key-lever; ligament; spring; fulcrum rod; stop wire; pin; platen

**Improves on:**

- intermediate leverage mechanisms for front-strike machines: uses an over-centre spring counterbalance in place of variable-leverage linkages
