## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typebar machines of the day needed refinements in carriage running, line feed, case shifting and protection against over-striking at line end, while keeping the typebars light and cheap.

**Solution:** Light U-section type carriers on link-and-lever linkages strike a common point from a ring of inked pads, while the platen carriage rides on a single ball-bearing girder that rocks fore and aft to select one of three type lines on each three-character block.

**How it works:** Each type carrier is a bent flat-metal U-bar with a rod carrying a type block of three characters; it hangs from a pivoted link on the upper segmental frame and a longer lever on the base frame, so the block swings down almost vertically onto the platen. At rest the blocks sit face-down on a leather-faced felt ink pad in a curved pan, which re-inks them each time. Long and short key levers act on the carrier levers through push rods and tension springs. A rocking key tilts the girder rail that carries the paper carriage forward or back on levers, presenting the platen to the front or rear type of the block (three cases), with a positive lock that catches the carriage at the central position. The carriage runs on balls in a grooved rail, is pulled by a spring in a box, and is stepped by two short racks engaged alternately with a long rack by the universal space bar. One left-hand lever turns the platen a line, releases the escapement and throws the carriage back to the line start, and a key lock stops the keys near line end until released.
