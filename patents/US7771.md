## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Ordinary printing sets the whole form at once; Eddy wanted one set of types applied singly in succession to replace the pen.

**Solution:** A grid of spring-held type bars is moved in two directions until the wanted type lies over a hole in a fixed inking plate, then pressed through onto the paper.

**How it works:** Seventy-eight type faces are soldered to the ends of flat steel bars, in six rows of thirteen, passing through two horizontal plates and held up by spiral springs. The type-form slides laterally and longitudinally on slide-rods, the two motions combining diagonally, to bring a chosen type over the hole in an inking plate that always lies between type and paper. Pressure on the bar drives its type through the hole to the paper and the spring restores it. The paper is moved laterally between impressions and longitudinally between lines. The text is truncated after the opening.

**Features:** [Separate type slugs or plungers](../features/elem-individual-slugs.md), [Flat type plate](../features/elem-plate-flat.md), [Slider along a straight index](../features/sel-linear-slider.md), [Hand presses the type through](../features/imp-press-through.md), [Ink pad](../features/ink-pad.md)

**Parts:** type form; type bar; spiral spring; upper plate; lower plate; slide-rod; inking plate; paper carriage

**Numbers:** 78 types; 6 rows of 13; plates 1.75 in. apart; bars about 3.5 in. long
