## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Typebar machines are complicated and costly, their bent arms print crooked lines, and the writing is hidden from the operator.

**Solution:** A cheap index machine in which a sliding stick with a finger is set along a straight toothed index plate, turning a type wheel through a rack and pinion, and pressing down the whole pivoted wheel frame prints the character and ratchets the carriage one space.

**How it works:** A type wheel on an inclined shaft sits in a frame pivoted at the rear and held up by a spring above a bearing block on the paper carriage. A sliding bar with a finger on top and a rack underneath is moved along a straight toothed index plate marked with the characters, and the rack turns a pinion on the wheel shaft to bring the chosen letter over a slot in a plate beneath it. The operator presses the frame down so the finger drops between the index teeth, locating the wheel exactly, and the character prints on the paper on the carriage roller, with stops limiting the depth. The same downward motion tensions a spring pawl one tooth along the carriage rack, and when the frame rises the spring pulls the carriage one space. Lifting the frame slightly lifts the pawl clear so the carriage can be pushed back. Ribbon-wound inking wheels press on the type wheel.
