## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Two-case typebar machines needing a shift of the whole carriage or type head want a way to present upper or lower case with the fewest moving parts.

**Solution:** Each typebar carries a pivoted two-faced type head that centrifugal force flings to the upper-case face unless a per-bar trigger locks it, and a key-operated trip releases the triggers.

**How it works:** A two-faced type head is pivoted in the forked end of each typebar with its centre of gravity ahead of the pivot, so gravity returns it to the lower-case position as the bar rests. A pivoted trigger on the bar normally locks the head in lower case; swinging the bar quickly would otherwise throw the head to the upper-case face by centrifugal force. A case key lowers a spring-held semi-ring trip into the path of the triggers' trip-arms, so the arriving typebars push their triggers out of the way and the heads fly to upper case. A fixed wire buffer ring resets every trigger after each stroke, and a brake spring holds the trigger in either position.
