## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A type wheel turned by keys and then swung to the platen has momentum; a positive stop for far characters causes shock, rebound and wear.

**Solution:** A pinion on the wheel sits between two racks on swinging bails that are moved differentially by paired main and helper tumblers, so the wheel's rotation slows gradually to a stop just as both racks move together and carry the wheel to the platen.

**How it works:** The type wheel is loosely keyed by a long pin to a pinion that turns on a tube in a tilting frame; the wheel can turn about half a revolution each way. Two bails carry racks meshing on opposite sides of the pinion, so the pinion's angle depends on the difference between the bail positions. Each key drives a tumbler with two faces: the first pushes one bail quickly, the second later catches the other bail. A paired opposite tumbler acts as helper through an elliptic gear, moving the other bail slowly at first and faster as the key goes down, so the racks converge smoothly and the wheel stops without shock. When both racks move together the pinion stops turning and the whole tilting frame swings the wheel to the platen with increasing speed. Case change is by a shift wheel with a cam slot that raises the wheel shaft through the tube to bring the second or third row into line (about three-quarters of a turn for capitals, one and a half for figures). An underscore type is thrown in front of the wheel and struck by it, printing the line with the letter.
