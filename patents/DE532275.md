## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** In cam drives with a cylindrical rest track beside the cam, the sideways shift needed to put the follower on the rest track is either long or hard to do.

**Solution:** Make the rest track diameter equal to the sum of the largest and smallest cam radii and form the transitions as cone surfaces with apex on the shaft axis.

**How it works:** A cam body and a coaxial cylindrical rest track sit side by side on the shaft. The follower (roller) is shifted axially along inclined transition surfaces to move between cam and rest track, taking it out of action. Because the rest track diameter is large, the transition can be gentle for a given shift path. Making the transitions cone shells with apexes on the shaft centre means the roller is not displaced radially as the cam turns, so only the spring force, not acceleration forces, resists shifting. Fig. 4 shows a double cam with a rest track between.

**Parts:** cam; cylindrical rest track; roller; transition cone surface; shaft; spring

**Improves on:**

- conventional cam drive with rest track of diameter twice the smallest cam radius: about half the displacement path for the same slope
