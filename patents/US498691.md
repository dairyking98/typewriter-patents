## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** On the Hall typewriter, which prints with the carriage lowered and hides the work, an operator cannot easily correct a defective or omitted letter by realigning the paper with the type.

**Solution:** A V-shaped pointer hung on the type-carriage rests on the paper and, when the carriage is raised, swings down to show exactly where the next letter would print.

**How it works:** The V-shaped index is pivoted at the ends of its arms in brackets on the upper end of the pivoting type-carriage. A handle lets the operator raise it against the underside of the carriage or clamp it in a notch when idle. As the carriage is lifted about its pivot the index point moves down to the printing line, so the operator can align it with a misprint or gap by shifting the paper, then lower the carriage to print. A projection on the bracket lifts the handle and pointer out of the way when the carriage is raised fully for paper changes, and the pointer's centre of gravity decides whether it hangs on the paper or leans against the carriage.
