## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** On a Densmore-type typebar machine the carriage shift, margin stop, bell, key lock and ribbon reversing needed simpler, more reliable constructions.

**Solution:** A set of improvements: a fore-and-aft shifting carriage rail with an eccentric shift lock, a notched-rail margin stop that also locks the universal bar and trips the bell, and a rock-shaft that selects which ribbon spool is driven.

**How it works:** The carriage rails hang on pivoted end members so a shift key, through a link and angle lever, moves the whole carriage forward for capitals, and a handle lever with an eccentric boss holds it past a dead center for continuous capitals. The margin stop block clips onto a rail notched at letter-space pitch and is held by a pawl, so it arrests the carriage exactly at a letter space. As the stop's set-screw reaches a lever it locks the universal bar so no key or escapement can act, and a slotted bar on the stop trips a bell hammer a few strokes earlier. Each ribbon spool has a ratchet head engaged by a pivoted toothed segment on a lever, and one rock-shaft with a detent turns one pawl in and the other out to reverse the feed direction. A swivel block lets the shifting carriage keep its link to the escapement rack part without binding.

**Parts:** carriage; shiftable rail; shift key; angle lever; eccentric handle lever; marginal stop block; notched rail; universal bar; locking lever; bell; bell hammer; ribbon spool; ratchet segment; rock-shaft; detent spring; escapement shift bar; swivel block

**Numbers:** segment turns about a quarter circle; rail notch pitch equals the letter space

**Improves on:**

- US475057 (Heath, Densmore escapement pawl): retains the swinging escapement pawls and adds a lever to shift the escapement bar with the carriage frame raised
