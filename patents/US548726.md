## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** A Munson-type machine with a rotating, shifting type cylinder needed a durable, simple design that hits hard enough for manifolding (carbon copies), like the Remington typebar, while keeping the cylinder accurately positioned as it swings to the paper.

**Solution:** Mount vertical type cylinders in frames that swing horizontally on vertical pivots parallel to the cylinder axis, driven by rolling cams for a quick hard blow, with keys that cam the cylinder to rotate and lift it, and star-wheel guides that register it at the print point.

**How it works:** Two vertical type cylinders, each carrying half the characters in rows, sit at either side of the printing point on shafts in frames pivoted on vertical pivots; the right keys work the right cylinder and vice versa. Slotted key levers with inclined cams push a cross-bar either way; this rocks a shaft and, through a rod, crank, crown segment and lantern wheel, rotates the cylinder by an amount set by the cam slope. Keys with bevelled ends instead move a second bar that lifts the cylinder shaft, shifting row. Any key lifts a rod that, through rolling cams, swings the frame quickly toward the paper, gaining speed and momentum. Y-notched star-wheel on the shaft straddles a fixed pin and enters spring-and-rigid guide pairs, centering the type horizontally and vertically before impact, whatever the paper thickness on the platen. A key lifts the platen for upper-case (or to read), ribbon spools feed through a ratchet and reverse by lifting one spool shaft, and an ordinary escapement with a clutch-release bar spaces the carriage.
