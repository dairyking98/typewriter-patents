**Kind:** a part or mechanism (any kind of machine) · **Subject (patent classes):** Typewriters or selective printing or marking mechanisms characterised by the purpose for which they are constructed for stenographic writing

**In the patent’s own words:** My invention relates to certain new and useful improvements in type-writing machines, and more particularly to that class denominated stenographic machines, wherein the type-characters are adapted to imprint stenographic signs or symbols that indicate syllables or phonetic sounds or word symbols such as are usually employed in the art of stenography for rapidly recording speech or language;

**How it works (reading):** Two banks of paired keys, each pair struck by one finger to print one or two stenographic signs at once, with a key-driven carriage feed that steps one or two type widths according to which bank was struck.

## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Stenographic recording needs the maximum signs in the least time and effort; ordinary one-key-per-character machines are too slow.

**Solution:** Two banks of paired keys, each pair struck by one finger to print one or two stenographic signs at once, with a key-driven carriage feed that steps one or two type widths according to which bank was struck.

**How it works:** Twenty key levers in left and right banks each pull a typebar by a wire; adjacent keys form a circle and can be struck together because their types strike different points on the platen. Each bank prints in a different vertical plane so two characters of a word go down together. Every key depresses a universal bar (one arm twice as long under the left bank) whose pawls turn a notched spiral cylinder by one or two teeth on key release; a spring-pulley-driven carriage then advances until a dog on the carriage meets the next notch. At line end the carriage trips a clutch and a dog lifter, a stronger spring pulley returns it, and a beveled finger on the frame turns the platen to feed paper from a roll. The ribbon guide (hinged sections) projects over the platen during each stroke and retracts to expose the print, and ribbon spools reverse automatically through a cylinder-driven tappet wheel and snap levers.
