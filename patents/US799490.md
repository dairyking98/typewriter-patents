## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Typing columns of figures of different lengths so that units, tens and hundreds line up needs the carriage stopped at a different distance for each number.

**Solution:** A single key swung to a notch for the number's denomination and pressed releases the carriage and a stepped stop plate halts it against a preset stop pin on a carriage-driven disk.

**How it works:** A disk on a shaft turns with the carriage through a chain and grooved wheel, and carries adjustable stop pins, one per column. A stepped, spring-returned stop plate is moved by a key lever swung laterally into one of the notches marked units, tens, hundreds and so on. Pressing the key also pushes a sliding bar that turns a rock-shaft, which through a rod and crank arm shifts the escape-wheel pinion out of the carriage rack. The carriage runs under its mainspring until a stop pin meets the chosen step of the stop plate. A spring-loaded brake lever presses on the disk rim during the run to soften the stop shock. The key then springs back.

**Features:** [ESC:tabulator](../features/esc-tabulator.md), [ESC:carriage-release](../features/esc-carriage-release.md), [ESC:spring-drum](../features/esc-spring-drum.md), [ESC:escape-wheel](../features/esc-escape-wheel.md), [USE:accessory](../features/use-accessory.md), [FRM:adjustment-wear](../features/frm-adjustment-wear.md)

**Parts:** rotary stop disk; stop pin; stepped stop plate; key lever; notched segment; sliding bar; rock-shaft; crank arm; escape-wheel pinion; rack bar; brake lever; chain; grooved wheel

**Numbers:** eight notches on the segment (0, 1, 10, 100, 1,000...)
