## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** A type-writing or type-engraving machine that is strong in impression, simple in parts and easy for inexperienced users.

**Solution:** One or more type wheels are mounted on a vertically sliding bar; a centring pin drops into the wheel's periphery to locate the chosen character, and a lever presses the bar down to print and, through a pawl, space the carriage.

**How it works:** Type wheels turn on a spindle fixed to a bar that moves up and down in staples on a cross bracket and is lifted by a spiral spring. A centring bar with a reduced tip, hook or staple fits into recesses or pins on the wheel's rim, so the character opposite, at the bottom, is at the printing point. A lever pivoted at the rear of the bracket presses the centring bar, bar and wheel down so the type contacts the paper, and a spring returns it. The lever's rear end raises a pawl lever whose pawl advances a rack on the paper carriage one or more notches, set by a thumb screw. If spacing during the impression might smear the paper, the pawl lever is instead tied to the bar so the carriage moves as the wheel rises after printing.
