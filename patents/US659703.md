## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Keys that must be physically moved cost mechanical labour and make noise; the inventor wants a machine operated by a finger touch alone.

**Solution:** Fixed perforated keys connected to an air compressor: closing a key's hole with a finger raises a stop-pin, a compressed-air turbine spins the type-wheel until a stop lever hits that pin, and air pistons then strike the wheel against the platen and space the carriage.

**How it works:** Air from a compressor constantly leaks out through the perforated keys. Covering a key raises the air pressure in its pipe and lifts a small piston with a stop-pin, which also opens a bore that feeds a cylinder; that cylinder's piston releases a pawl holding the turbine. The turbine rotates the type-wheel until a stop lever on its axle hits the raised pin. A slide valve then admits air to a cylinder whose piston swings the wheel on a universal joint against the paper cylinder, with an inking roller pressed on the wheel. On the return stroke the lever is lifted over the pin so the wheel can continue to the next raised pin, allowing several letters in one rotation, and the same return movement releases an escapement that lets the carriage advance one tooth under air pressure. Separate air keys give extra spacing, carriage return, and platen shift to bring the upper type series into line.
