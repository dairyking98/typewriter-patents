## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** Typebar alignment normally needs a machinist to adjust; the inventor wants the operator to correct alignment directly.

**Solution:** Each typebar fulcrum pivot is moved radially by a screw-pin and nut, so turning the nut with a screwdriver shifts the type toward or away from the print point.

**How it works:** Typebars hang on links whose pivots sit in horizontal slots in the segmental frame. A rod on each pivot passes through the frame and carries a nut seated in the frame, held axially by a screw. Turning the nut draws the pivot in, or a coil or flat spring pushes it back out, shifting the typebar radially and correcting the alignment. Variants apply the same screw to directly pivoted upstrike and downstrike typebars.
