## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** Line-space slides with a spring pawl re-enter the ratchet noisily on return, only lock the platen at the end of the stroke so violent jerks can over-rotate it, and are hard to make and fit.

**Solution:** The pawl is guided by a slot so it engages the line-space wheel before the slide moves and leaves it only after the stroke ends, so the platen is always held and the return is quiet.

**How it works:** The line-space lever pushes a slide on a main bearing plate attached to the platen bearing piece. The pawl is pinned in a cam slot in the plate and also guided by a slot on a pin of the slide. As the slide first moves, the slot shape swings the pawl nose into the teeth of the line-space wheel before the wheel is driven, and the wheel is held by the pawl all through. After the stroke, when the lever is released, the pawl stays engaged briefly and is lifted from the teeth before its return, so it returns silently. An exchangeable adjusting lever with stop lugs, held by a spring in rests, sets the end position and hence the line-space range; the unit is mostly stamped parts, assembled on the plate and covered by a cap.
