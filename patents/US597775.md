## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Full keyboards force the operator to look for keys and use finger force to power the machine.

**Solution:** A small chord keyboard where combinations of a few keys, always under the fingers, set notched combination bars and release an independently powered striking action (solenoid).

**How it works:** A few banks of keys rock shafts that move longitudinal notched combination bars and a lock-bar. Letter-bars rest on the stubs between notches and drop only for the matching combination. A striking action driven from an independent power source, a solenoid on electric supply, then operates the key levers of the original typewriter. Fingers need only trigger it.

**Features:** [SEL:keyboard-few-keys](../features/sel-keyboard-few-keys.md), [SEL:dummy-keyboard](../features/sel-dummy-keyboard.md), [IMP:electric](../features/imp-electric.md), [IMP:print-lever](../features/imp-print-lever.md)

**Parts:** key bank; rock-shaft; notched combination bar; lock-bar; letter-bar; solenoid; striking bar; key-lever

**Numbers:** usual banks of 40 to 80 keys replaced by few keys

**Improves on:**

- full-keyboard typewriters: few keys, no finger force
