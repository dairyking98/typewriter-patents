## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typewriters whose shift key and space key must cooperate (shift locks the carriage at the first stop, the space key releases it) coupled the two key levers near their pivot, which needed a complicated, expensive key lever design.

**Solution:** Each key lever gets its own arm acting independently on a key switch drive lever, with the coupling between shift and space placed near the fingerboards.

**How it works:** Shift and space keys sit symmetrically on a common axis, with adjacent levers carrying arms that each lift a lever on the same axis, which acts on the carriage switch lock. Pressing the shift key depresses a switching lever linked through a switching shaft to the platen shift frame, and a spring hook latches over its nose to hold the shift. Each further shift press spaces the carriage one interval. Pressing the space key, via an inclined surface and roller on the hook bracket, unhooks the latch so the platen returns to capitals and spaces one interval. Space alone works normally.
