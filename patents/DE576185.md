## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Children's typewriters are either intricate or use type rulers or wheels with various drawbacks, and cannot easily write visibly, correct errors or make copies cheaply.

**Solution:** A button moves a type wheel sector with a type plate tape about a vertical axis to the letter, and pressing the button swings the whole sector against the platen and locks it with a pawl.

**How it works:** Button 16 rests on notches in box arch g and carries a pointer ring that indicates the letter on scale 17. Swinging the button turns sector 22, 25 (via driver 29 in a slot in the sector) about central axis 21, with the type plate tape 23a on sector sheet 23 guided by a fixed guide roller 22a so one type plate is pushed forward. Pressing the button down pivots the box arch and, through pull arms and a support bracket, swings the sector against platen 28; a pawl enters inside teeth 24 and locks the chosen letter, while the rest-position slot catch is released. Inking is by an ink roller that the control arm pushes aside, or by ribbon. Springs 30 and 31 return everything; shift levers 32 and 33 raise axis 21 for capitals or figures, and a space button moves the platen.
