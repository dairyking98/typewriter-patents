## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Existing typebar typewriters are costly and complicated; the inventor wants a cheaper simpler machine that still prints three characters per bar without blurring.

**Solution:** Typebars with a pivoted shoe carrying a three-faced head, and a spring-pulled paper carriage whose three fore-and-aft positions select which face strikes, with a simple knife-edge rack escapement.

**How it works:** Each key lever is linked to a typebar whose upper end carries a pivoted shoe with a head bearing three type faces. On the stroke a link turns the shoe so the faces rotate from the inking position to the printing position, and the shoe is then locked to the bar so the type hits and leaves the paper without turning, avoiding blur. The carriage rides on a frame that a hand lever and catch set in three positions (rear, middle, front), so a different one of the three faces lines up with the platen centre, giving capitals, small letters and figures. Any key or the space lever depresses an anchor that moves a plate carrying one fixed and one spring-held knife-edge dog across a double rack, letting the mainspring-driven carriage advance one tooth. Inking is by an arc-shaped pad faced with muslin or silk so the type never touches the felt and fibres do not clog it. A bell trip on an adjustable paper guide rings near line end.
