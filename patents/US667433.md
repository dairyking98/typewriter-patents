## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Type-wheel machines need a simple, direct, responsive way to turn the wheel to different angles from the type-keys, cheaply and with few parts.

**Solution:** A single pinion on the type-wheel shaft meshes with a short rack on each key lever, and an adjustable stop under each lever limits its travel, so every key turns the wheel by its own angle.

**How it works:** A pivoted keyboard carries a shaft with a type-wheel at one end and a pinion in the middle. Each key rod rides in a guide and straddles the free end of a pivoted key-lever, whose arched segment carries a rack of equal-length teeth over a plain non-operating face. Pressing a key swings its lever so the rack turns the pinion and wheel until the lever hits an adjustable set-screw stop, which fixes the angle for that character. Further pressure depresses the whole keyboard and the wheel toward the platen to print; the lever spring is set so that the wheel is positioned before the impression. Other levers are unaffected because the plain face rides at the pinion. Pressing a shift key slides the shaft lengthwise against a spring to bring a second character row into position. A swinging inked roll is pushed back by the shaft end as the wheel descends, and the same keyboard motion works the carriage spacing dog.
