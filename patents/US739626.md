## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Ordinary line spacing moves the platen only in whole ratchet steps, so partly printed blanks cannot be lined up exactly, and type always strikes the same lines of the platen and wears them.

**Solution:** The platen's line-space detent roller rides on a carrier that pivots about the platen axis, and an adjusting screw turns the carrier so that the detent drags the platen through a fraction of a line.

**How it works:** The platen carries a ratchet wheel with end teeth. A normal line-space pawl advances it one, two or three teeth as set by a stop piece. A spring yoke on a carrier, which turns about the platen's own axis, presses a detent roller into the ratchet teeth. A spring on the carrier holds it against the knurled end of an adjusting screw. In ordinary spacing the carrier stays still and the roller just bobs in and out of the teeth. Turning the screw rotates the carrier, and the roller, still seated in a tooth, carries the platen with it by a fraction of a line. This also shifts the lines of impact across the platen and spreads the wear.

**Features:** [PAP:platen-roller](../features/pap-platen-roller.md), [PAP:line-space](../features/pap-line-space.md), [FRM:adjustment-wear](../features/frm-adjustment-wear.md)

**Parts:** platen; ratchet wheel; platen head; line-spacing pawl; controlling stop piece; detent roller; yoke spring; detent carrier; adjusting screw; carrier spring; finger wheel

**Numbers:** single, double and triple line spacing (three stop positions)

**Improves on:**

- ordinary line-spacing mechanism: adds fractional steps below one ratchet tooth
