## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Type-wheel typewriters were complex and slow to operate; the inventor wants greater simplicity and ease with direct hand-lever control of the wheel.

**Solution:** A single hand lever, geared directly to a vertical type wheel and engaged with a notched lettered bar, rotates the wheel to the chosen character, presses it onto the paper and, by the same downstroke, feeds the paper table one tooth.

**How it works:** A vertical type wheel turns on a journal at the end of a lever arm fixed to a rock-shaft above the paper table. A jointed hand printing lever, fulcrumed on the lever arm, ends in a segment gear that meshes with a spur gear on the wheel hub; the lever is swung sideways into one of the notches on curved lettered bars, which sets how far the lever, and so the wheel, can rotate. Pressing the lever down rotates the wheel to the lettered position, depresses it onto the paper, and rocks the shaft, moving a reciprocating ratchet bar one tooth so a pawl carrier advances the table against a ribbon spring. At line end, stops raise the pawls and the spring returns the table automatically. A slide plate on the lever shortens its throw to print the lower-case letter between two capitals, and an elastic cord removes gear backlash and helps lift the lever.
