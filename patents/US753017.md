## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Antifriction balls or rollers supporting a typewriter carriage drift out of position relative to the carriage and frame, so the carriage can bind.

**Solution:** Seat the balls in a separator that is tied by cords around pulleys to the carriage and the frame, so it is forced to move at half the carriage travel.

**How it works:** Grooved fixed rails and a grooved carriage bar sandwich antifriction balls held loosely in openings of a separator. Two pulleys are pivoted on the separator. Each cord is fastened to the carriage bar, runs around a pulley and is fastened to a fixed stud on the frame, one cord pulling each way. Because of the pulley, the separator moves exactly half as far as the carriage, so the balls keep the same position relative to both tracks at every carriage position. Slack is set by a jam nut and taken up automatically by contractile springs in the cords. The carriage is pulled by a separate band to a spring drum.

**Features:** [ESC:spring-drum](../features/esc-spring-drum.md), [FRM:adjustment-wear](../features/frm-adjustment-wear.md)

**Parts:** fixed rail; carriage bar; antifriction ball; separator; pulley; cord; contractile spring; jam nut; spring drum band

**Numbers:** separator moves one half of the carriage travel
