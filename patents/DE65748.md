## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typewriters with a separate typebar per character are complex; the inventor wants one compact element carrying all characters, positioned by the keys and printed with a hand-independent blow.

**Solution:** A flexible type band with three rows of types is driven round a drum by a key-specific rotation and the whole band frame shifts axially for case, then a weighted trigger flicks a push rod up to print against a roller platen.

**How it works:** The type band runs like an endless belt over a drum and two rollers inside a type frame under a rubber-covered platen cylinder, with types in three rows (small letters, capitals, figures) that the frame slides sideways on pins to bring under the platen, set by two finger plates and a spring pawl. Every key lever, when pressed, depresses a common rail by an amount specific to that key (the rail's top is shaped differently under each lever), which turns a lever frame, a toothed arc and a gear on the drum shaft to the angle needed for that character; a hook on the key locks the frame at the bottom of the stroke. A brake shoe nips the drum just before the type arrives, and pins on the locking roller entering holes in the band with a notched wheel and locking piece register the type exactly. At the bottom of the stroke a held weighted trigger is released, flicks a pawl against the pressure lever and drives the push rod up, so the blow is quick and independent of finger speed. The key also steps the paper carriage one space via a feed pawl on a rack, and the carriage is clamped during the blow and lifted off the types at line end. The types are inked by an inking roller on a vibrating arm; a bell is included.
