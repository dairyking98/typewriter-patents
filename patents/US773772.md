## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A front-strike typewriter needs a light, resilient key touch from a simple linkage that rarely goes out of order.

**Solution:** Connect each key lever and its typebar through an intermediate lever by two spring-metal links that work in compression, giving a yielding touch while keeping a positive connection.

**How it works:** Typebars are pivoted on a semicircular rod at the rear of a vertical plate, with key levers pivoted on a horizontal rod directly beneath. An intermediate lever pivots at the rear and has an upright arm, linked to a lug on the typebar, and a rearward arm, linked to the key lever's rear extension. Pressing a key raises its rear end against a spring, which swings the intermediate lever and throws the typebar up to the platen through the links, both under compression. The link to the key lever stays nearly vertical for a straight pull, and the typebar link passes from slightly below to slightly above horizontal for uniform pressure. A shoulder on the raised typebar strikes a semicircular universal bar, and the typebars rest on a pad.
