## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** The loose let-off dog of a Remington-type escapement must sit in the right place for re-engaging the rack but also yield in either direction, such as when the carriage is moved back.

**Solution:** A spring-pressed plunger with bearing surfaces on both sides of the dog's axis centers the let-off dog yet lets it yield either way.

**How it works:** A rocker moved by any key lever or the space bar carries the retaining dog and the let-off dog transversely across the carriage rack, so when one leaves a tooth the other enters. The let-off dog then lets the rack advance one tooth until it meets a stop. The dog has two bearing surfaces, one each side of its pivot, and a spring-pressed plunger in a guide socket presses on both, centering it. A force on either side pushes the plunger back and the dog yields, so the rack ratchets past it when the carriage is returned without lifting the rack. The socket is adjustable on the rocker by a nut, which sets the dog's initial position.
