## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Earlier machines moved a type member to one side for half the characters and the other for the rest, which was slow because the parts had to be restored each time, could lock if two keys were struck together, and lost alignment as the actuating parts wore.

**Solution:** A single type plate is positioned in two axes by the key itself, using differential stem heights, so it never has to return to a home position, and a hammer behind the paper prints whichever character is at the print point.

**How it works:** Each key lever lifts a vertical stem whose head has two contact faces of key-specific length. The faces press on two bars of a rocking cradle on opposite sides of its axis, tilting the segmental type plate vertically to the row of the chosen character. Cam lugs on some stems rock a shaft that raises a bar whose shoulder pushes the plate carrier sideways against a spring, choosing the column. A Cap key bodily shifts the plate so its capital half takes the place of the lower-case half. Depressing the key also swings a bell crank that drives a hammer to push the paper and ribbon against the type, and its escapement teeth (one fixed, one yielding) release the spring-drawn carriage one tooth. Tabulating keys disengage the pinion from the carriage rack and stop it on pins in a numbered dial, and a pawl on the carriage spring drum line-spaces the paper.
