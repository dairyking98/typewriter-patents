## Summary

**Kind:** typebar machine · **Subject (patent classes):** Type-lever actuating mechanisms

**In the patent’s own words:** This invention relates particularly to correctly centering type-tipped plungers as they rise to print, and thereby securing regular and accurate alignment.

**How it works (reading):** Four small pivoted T-headed rocker-levers around the opening in a bridge centre each plunger tip as it rises, with the ink ribbon passing over them under tension springs.

## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Type-tipped plungers rise at different angles toward the print point and a fixed funnel does not centre them accurately enough for even alignment and sharp impressions.

**Solution:** Four small pivoted T-headed rocker-levers around the opening in a bridge centre each plunger tip as it rises, with the ink ribbon passing over them under tension springs.

**How it works:** A metal bridge on the upper shelf has a central circular opening with four cross-shaped slots. A piece below carries four rocker-levers pivoted on shoulders, their heads rising in the slots around the opening. As a type-tipped plunger rises, its tip pushes against the fingers, which centre it exactly before the type presses the ink ribbon against paper on the printing roller. Tension springs keep the ribbon tight over the fingers.
