## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Lowest-build typewriters need long typebar travel (up to 180 degrees), which requires much finger force and spoils touch, or they need raised levers that block the view.

**Solution:** Incline the segment so the typebars swing only about 90 degrees from rest to stop and reach almost to the machine's lowest point, with keys placed near the segment and driven through two-armed accelerating intermediate levers.

**How it works:** Two-armed intermediate levers change their arm ratio during key movement so the typebar accelerates as key resistance rises. The key levers lie close to and in front of the segment at about half the machine's height, shaped to the hand so the rows follow the fingers' natural motion. Intermediate levers sit at the height of the platen centre, driven by a nose on the key lever without a link piece.
