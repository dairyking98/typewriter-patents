## Summary

**Kind:** typebar machine · **Subject (patent classes):** Typewriters or selective printing mechanisms characterised by the mounting, arrangement or disposition of the types or dies with types or dies carried on sliding bars or rods on side surfaces thereof, e.g. fixed thereto the types or dies being movable relative to the bars or rods

**In the patent’s own words:** The objects of our invention are to sim-4 plify the construction of the devices whereby the platen is supported and moved 4trans-v versely to the printing-line and the devices whereby the platen is rotated, to improve-the construction of the guide which supports the platen-carriage, and to provide an efficient locking device whereby the key-levers are prevented from being operated when the platen has nearly reached the end of a line.

**How it works (reading):** A two-part platen carriage (primary longitudinal, secondary transverse) on a ball-bearing guide, with an adjustable-throw line-space pawl and a spring-raised locking plate under the key levers tripped by the carriage.

## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Jarvis typebar machine with double-type bars and a shifting platen needed a simpler, lighter platen support and shift, a better carriage guide, and a lock to stop the keys near line end.

**Solution:** A two-part platen carriage (primary longitudinal, secondary transverse) on a ball-bearing guide, with an adjustable-throw line-space pawl and a spring-raised locking plate under the key levers tripped by the carriage.

**How it works:** Each typebar carries two types and the platen sits above, writing on its underside. A secondary carriage holding the platen slides transversely inside a primary carriage that travels along the line under a spring drum and a toothed escapement bar with pawls; a push-rod and forked rock-arm move the platen fore and aft so the other type of each bar lines up (a platen shift). The primary carriage rides on a roller and a ball-bearing guide-case on a grooved rail, with an adjustable flange for wear take-up. A ratchet lever with a pivoted pawl turns the platen; an adjusting lever changes the pawl's idle travel so one or two teeth are fed per stroke. Near line end a tappet on the escapement bar trips a catch and a spring flips a locking plate up under the key levers; a restoring key pushes it down again.

**Features:** [ELEM:typebar](../features/elem-typebar.md), [SHIFT:platen](../features/shift-platen.md), [SEL:keyboard-full](../features/sel-keyboard-full.md), [IMP:element-to-platen](../features/imp-element-to-platen.md), [ESC:pawl-rack](../features/esc-pawl-rack.md), [ESC:spring-drum](../features/esc-spring-drum.md), [ESC:margin-stop](../features/esc-margin-stop.md), [PAP:platen-roller](../features/pap-platen-roller.md), [PAP:line-space](../features/pap-line-space.md), [FRM:adjustment-wear](../features/frm-adjustment-wear.md), [FRM:few-parts](../features/frm-few-parts.md)

**Parts:** typebar; key lever; platen; primary carriage; secondary carriage; rock-shaft; shifting rod; ball-bearing guide rail; guide case; spring drum; escapement bar; ratchet lever; line-space pawl; adjusting lever; locking plate; retaining catch; trip lever; tappet; restoring key

**Numbers:** two types per typebar; line feed of one or two ratchet teeth; 7 sheets of drawings

**Improves on:**

- earlier Jarvis typebar machines with shifting platen: fewer, lighter parts for supporting and shifting the platen
