## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Jarvis typebar machine with double-type bars and a shifting platen needed a simpler, lighter platen support and shift, a better carriage guide, and a lock to stop the keys near line end.

**Solution:** A two-part platen carriage (primary longitudinal, secondary transverse) on a ball-bearing guide, with an adjustable-throw line-space pawl and a spring-raised locking plate under the key levers tripped by the carriage.

**How it works:** Each typebar carries two types and the platen sits above, writing on its underside. A secondary carriage holding the platen slides transversely inside a primary carriage that travels along the line under a spring drum and a toothed escapement bar with pawls; a push-rod and forked rock-arm move the platen fore and aft so the other type of each bar lines up (a platen shift). The primary carriage rides on a roller and a ball-bearing guide-case on a grooved rail, with an adjustable flange for wear take-up. A ratchet lever with a pivoted pawl turns the platen; an adjusting lever changes the pawl's idle travel so one or two teeth are fed per stroke. Near line end a tappet on the escapement bar trips a catch and a spring flips a locking plate up under the key levers; a restoring key pushes it down again.
