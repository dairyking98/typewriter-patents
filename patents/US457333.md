## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** The inventor wants a complete keyboard type-wheel machine in which the wheel is positioned, locked and struck by one key stroke with few parts, perfect alignment, and automatic ribbon reversal, without the typebars and heavy mechanism of other writing machines.

**Solution:** Pressing a key first turns a hard-rubber type-wheel shaft through a rack and bail, locks it with a single graduated bail on a double eccentric ratchet, and then tilts the locked wheel frame to strike the paper as a hammer, with a fixed finger in a concentric ratchet correcting any residual misalignment.

**How it works:** A hollow hard-rubber type-wheel on a vertical shaft in a tilting frame carries several rows of characters and a flat-faced row of whole word types. Key levers press on three bails with stepped inclined flanges; one of two side bails pushes a pin into a segmental rack that turns the shaft clockwise or counterclockwise, and the central locking bail drops onto the matching tooth of an eccentric stopping ratchet to stop the rotation. Continuing the key stroke pushes the locking bail forward, tilting the shaft frame so the wheel strikes the paper, and as it arrives a fixed finger enters a flared recess of a concentric ratchet to steady the wheel and correct small errors. A catch holds the frame still until the wheel has turned to its place, a graduated bail then releases it. Shift keys slide the wheel up and down the shaft to bring another row to the print point; one key can be latched for continuous capitals. The carriage is stepped by pawls with letter and word spacing, the ribbon is fed on every stroke and reverses itself by a sliding plate that throws pawls between two spools, and the paper rolls hinge apart for inserting manifold sets.
