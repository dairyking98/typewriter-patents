## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** Typewriter carriages put the platen ends close against the end bars, so margins were equal and narrow, and a wide left margin for legal papers cost several letters per line.

**Solution:** Extend the carriage side bars and platen axle on the left so a sheet with a wide left margin can pass beside the roller while the full line is printed.

**How it works:** The left side bars of the traveling carriage are lengthened, as is the feed-roll axle; the roll is kept from end slipping by a pinned collar or sleeve. A cross bar joins the extended bars and carries a bearing for the axle, leaving an open space on both sides of the roll at the left. The sheet's blank margin lies in that space while the body of the sheet receives the full travel of the carriage. This replaces cutting letters off with the starting stop, which also slipped and gave a ragged margin.
