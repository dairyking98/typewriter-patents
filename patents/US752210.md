## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** On a front-strike Remington-type typebar machine a narrow ribbon fed on edge and vibrated to cover the printing point gets uneven edge tension and creases, and the ribbon must also follow the platen when the case shifts.

**Solution:** A ribbon vibrator with conoidal bearing cones and oblique guide fingers that equalise the slack from edge to edge, mounted on a support linked to the platen shift so the ribbon rises with the case shift.

**How it works:** Typebars strike rearward against a platen on a hinged frame, with a spring-drum carriage and escapement. A narrow ribbon winds on two side spools driven through bevel gears by the spring drum. Between spool and vibrator the ribbon twists from flat to on-edge through shaped plates and curved fingers. The vibrator arm has a type opening, an index, and forward-projecting cones that make the lower ribbon edge follow a longer path, taking up its slack. The vibrator is pivoted below and left of the printing point and a link from the dog rocker swings it diagonally up so the opening covers the printing point at each stroke. A spring returns it. The platen shift key also rocks a shaft that raises the vibrator's supporting arms, so the vibrator and index move with the platen shift. A spring-catch clamp with a finger piece attaches the ribbon to each spool.
