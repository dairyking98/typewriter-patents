## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Simplify the hand-twirled type-wheel typewriter, reduce its parts, give upper and lower case with a quick change, enlarge the dial, and put impression and carriage feed in a direct line.

**Solution:** A tilted type-disk and locking wheel on a vertical spindle are turned and pressed by one cross lever with a double-pointed indicator for case, and the relief and depressions on the disk's inner flange press a rod that sets how far the carriage feeds.

**How it works:** The bracket holds a fixed dial; a spindle through it carries a tilted printing disk and a toothed locking wheel at its lower end, and an impression lever pivoted across its top. Turning the lever turns the spindle, a double-pointed indicator (one end for upper, one for lower case) and the disk together to the character on the dial; the handle used, at either end of the lever, determines which case the indicator points to. Depressing a handle lowers the spindle, and a hook at the other end of the lever bears on the underside of the dial as the fulcrum. A V-shaped flange on an adjustable locking bracket meshes with the locking-wheel teeth when depressed to align the character, while a detent spring is always engaged. An arm with an ink pad swings across the characters via an inclined lug and spring when the spindle rises and falls. Space-varying elevations and depressions on the disk's flange press a single vertical rod onto a rock-arm that swings a pawl one to six rack teeth along the carriage, so spacing follows letter width, and a separate key presses the rod to space without printing.
