## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Releasing the fast wheel-starting latch from a key needs a sudden, repeatable knock regardless of how the operator presses the key.

**Solution:** A key-driven overthrow hammer knocks the latch's hook off its notch, with a detent and divided key so the hammer swing is instantaneous.

**How it works:** A spring-driven latch in a revolving turret has a spring hook engaging a fixed notch on the frame. A pivoted hammer helve is thrown up by a key; the hammer head flies up by momentum and knocks the hook out of the notch, releasing the latch. A spring on the helve stops against a pin so the hammer is brought to rest low enough for the hook to re-engage when the latch returns. In the improved form the key is split in two with a spring between, and a beveled detent spring holds the helve until the key spring is compressed enough, after which the helve snaps down. A pull spring resets the helve onto the detent. The latch is a wheel-starting device for typewriters like Dodson US299754, whose type wheels have independent orbital and axial rotation.
