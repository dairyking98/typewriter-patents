## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** On key-driven type-cylinder machines two actuating arms turn the cylinder in opposite directions, and if both are moved at once, or a key is struck before the last has returned, the arms interfere.

**Solution:** A disk on the cylinder axis with a stop and a flanged ring locks the cylinder at zero and locks the idle arm while the other arm turns the cylinder.

**How it works:** Two key-driven actuating arms each end in a toothed segment meshing with a pinion on the type-cylinder axis, one turning it each way from the zero position. A disk on the same axis has a stop and a ring with an opening on each side of the stop. At rest the arms' pins sit on either side of the stop and hold the cylinder still. When one arm moves, its pin passes through the ring opening and its segment turns the pinion and cylinder. The pin of the other arm then bears on the ring's rim and cannot move until the cylinder returns to zero.

**Features:** [ELEM:cylinder-multirow](../features/elem-cylinder-multirow.md), [SEL:differential-drive](../features/sel-differential-drive.md), [SEL:selector-locking](../features/sel-selector-locking.md), [SEL:keyboard-few-keys](../features/sel-keyboard-few-keys.md)

**Parts:** type cylinder; actuating arm; toothed segment; pinion; locking disk; stop; ring flange; arm pin

**Improves on:**

- earlier key-driven type-cylinder machines: arms could interfere when two keys were struck together or a key was struck before the previous had returned
