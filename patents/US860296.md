## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** A key-positioned type plate must land accurately at the print point on every stroke and be struck with the same force whatever the operator's touch, without needing a return to home.

**Solution:** Key levers with differential lugs drive two positive links that move a type plate vertically and sideways, paired stops clamp those links to lock the plate, and a spring-driven hammer, released by the key and reset by the carriage escapement, prints with constant force.

**How it works:** Each key lever carries lugs of key-specific length that push on two transverse bars. One bar, through a link, rocks a frame carrying the type plate carrier to set its row. The other, through a bell crank, slides a bar with inclined surfaces that raises a lug onto a carrier arm and moves the plate sideways against a return spring to set its column. As the key goes down, a pivoted stop with matching differential lugs swings up and clamps the bars, so the plate is locked and aligned. The Cap key bodily shifts the plate so the capital half takes the place of the lower-case half. A key depression also withdraws a plunger from a ratchet wheel on the carriage spring-drum shaft, so a spring rocks the hammer shaft, a stop halts the arm early and the hammer's momentum flings it against the type with a constant blow; the carriage step then rocks the shaft back and re-locks the hammer. A fixed and yielding tooth escapement under all keys and the space bar steps the spring-driven carriage, with tabulating stops on a disc, paper feed and ribbon spools as in the same inventor's other applications.
