## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Front-strike typebar machines (Williams) needed an easier, more even key touch, with typebars that do not wobble sideways and key-bar bearings that stay put.

**Solution:** A key-bar drives each typebar through a slotted link and a bell-crank whose leverage changes during the stroke, so the typebar accelerates and the touch is even.

**How it works:** The key-bar rocks on a bearing lug over a fixed plate, held back by a spring and kept straight by comb-teeth guides. A two-leaf link on the key-bar rests by its nose on the long arm of a bell-crank, so the first motion has good leverage and light touch. Once the pin in the link slot reaches the end of the slot, the push goes through the pin nearer the fulcrum, so the typebar speeds up. Paired links join the bell-crank to the typebar heel, and a hooked arm on them picks up a universal bar near the end of the stroke to work the escapement and ribbon feed. A conoidal alining pin enters a hole in the typebar at the printing point, and a spring pushes the bar back out. Wide cheek-plate hangers limit lateral vibration, and an overlapping bell-crank layout lets typebars be separated without changing the keyboard.
