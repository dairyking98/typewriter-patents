## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Elliott-Fisher key-operated type-wheel machine needed simple, low-friction, accurately adjustable motion transmission from keys to the printing wheel, and a ribbon feed that does not burden the keys.

**Solution:** Key levers act on two guided slides with graduated contact rollers, which rotate the wheel through a vibrator and pinion until a stop rack meets the depressed key's stop, then carry the wheel to the platen.

**How it works:** Keys are grouped in threes; each group's outer lever pushes a slide (one each side) backward via contact rollers of graduated size. The slide swings a vibrator with a toothed segment that turns the type wheel pinion, and the pinion drives a stop rack that halts against the stop end of the depressed key lever, fixing the rotation. Continued key travel moves the wheel carrier back to the platen (cam faces start this during rotation), and a spring returns it. A separate pair of keys raises the wheel for row selection. The slides run on ball races in adjustable guides. The wheel spindle, on retraction, cams a rock shaft that drives ribbon pawls and ratchets on two spools, reversible by a handle.
