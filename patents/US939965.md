## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typewriters are costly and complicated, full of levers and small parts; the inventor wants a cheap, sturdy machine cheap enough to sell as a toy.

**Solution:** A freely swinging type block hung on a ball (universal) joint with groups of three characters on a convex face is tilted by straight sliding plunger keys acting on a cam surface, then centred by a plunger entering a hole, with a rubber hammer striking the paper from below.

**How it works:** Plunger keys in a bank slide vertically and press on a convex cam surface on top of the type block, which hangs on a ball-ended guide post like a universal joint. Each key tilts the block roughly to its position; continued travel of the key pushes the centring plunger up through a hole in the cam into one of a ring of holes in a concave plate at the back of the block, locating the block exactly. Each key position brings one group of three characters (lower case, upper case, figure) to the print point. A special turning key inserted in an eccentric guide hole changes the tilt angle so that a different character of the group is chosen. The plunger also works a bent lever and oscillating shaft that throws a rubber-faced hammer up under the paper against the ribbon and type, and the same motion drives a pawl that steps the carriage along a toothed bar, with the spacing bar tripping the pawl for blank spaces. Spring-barrel drive, adjustable carriage stops and a bell are also included.
