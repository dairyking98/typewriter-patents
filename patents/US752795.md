## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Earlier Moya type-wheel machines needed many parts to bring the paper into print; the inventor wants a simpler, firmer and cheaper machine.

**Solution:** A type-wheel cylinder is turned to select the lengthwise row, the paper roll moves sideways to select the character in the row, and the whole wheel is thrown forward like a hammer to print.

**How it works:** Eighty-four characters sit on a cylinder in six circumferential rows of fourteen. A key turns the wheel by an angle through a cam and clutch common to all keys; the clutch frees the wheel shaft from the cam once the right lengthwise row is in the printing plane. The paper roll moves sidewise to pick one of three characters in each of the second, fourth and sixth rows. For the first, third and fifth rows the wheel is also moved endwise on its splined shaft by fourteen extra keys. Twenty-eight character keys in all drive the machine, and the paper roll has the usual letter spacing and rotation.

**Features:** [ELEM:cylinder-multirow](../features/elem-cylinder-multirow.md), [SEL:keyboard-few-keys](../features/sel-keyboard-few-keys.md), [SHIFT:axial](../features/shift-axial.md), [IMP:element-to-platen](../features/imp-element-to-platen.md), [PAP:platen-roller](../features/pap-platen-roller.md), [ESC:space-key](../features/esc-space-key.md), [FRM:few-parts](../features/frm-few-parts.md)

**Parts:** type-wheel cylinder; shaft; spline; cam; clutch; character key; paper roll; paper carriage; supporting frame

**Numbers:** 84 characters; 6 circumferential rows of 14; 14 lengthwise rows; 28 character keys

**Improves on:**

- Moya application Serial No. 125,037: type-wheel thrown forward as a hammer, reducing parts
