## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Small typewriters that slide over the paper should be simpler to operate and extremely compact.

**Solution:** A hand-held pen-shaped stylus moves a type selector in three dimensions through a zigzag-edged opening in a pivoted setting plate, so one motion selects the type lever on a disc and then fires the hammer.

**How it works:** Type levers of two lengths sit on a rotatable type disc, in two concentric circles, and the machine slides over the paper. The stylus tip engages a notch in a selector fixed in a disc. Moving the selector to a zigzag corner of the setting plate pulls a cord wrapped on the type disc hub drum, turning the disc so the matching type lever reaches the print point. Moving the stylus on further pivots the setting plate, whose noses swing a lever and push a rack that turns a pinion carrying a hammer, which strikes the back of the type lever. Each zigzag corner serves every second lever; pressing the stylus down tilts a plate that shifts a guide roller to reach the in-between levers. Ribbon or coloured types ink the impression.
