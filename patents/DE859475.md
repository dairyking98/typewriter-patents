## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** Line-space/carriage ratchet mechanisms in small typewriters still make switching noise in both directions.

**Solution:** The step-determining tooth is hinged on the shift lever and lifted out of the ratchet by a friction lever on return, with noise-damping stops.

**How it works:** A ratchet wheel with pinion is carried on a base plate. A shift lever driven by the universal rail via a draw bar carries a fixed tooth and a hinged step tooth, held by a spring against a damping disc. A reset (downshift) pawl guided in a slot is lifted clear in rest. When the reset key is hit the pawl turns the ratchet back one step; a friction spring swings the step tooth out of the ratchet so it moves silently, and the spring reinserts it on key release as the carriage spring pulls back. Adjustable stops of damping material limit the lever.
