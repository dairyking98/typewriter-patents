## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** In carriage-shift typewriters, a recoil device that accelerates the carriage's return after a shift stresses and wears parts needlessly when the shift key is pressed slowly.

**Solution:** The recoil spring is charged only when the shift key is struck quickly, because it engages through the inertia of the heavy carriage.

**How it works:** The carriage rides on swing arms and shift levers, and vertical balance springs offset its weight so the shift key needs little force. The shift key rod acts on a lever mounted on the shift lever through a spring. On a slow press, the lever and shift lever move together and nothing else engages. On a fast press the carriage lags from inertia, the spring lever turns relative to the shift lever and carries a pawl into a notch of a second lever, which tensions a spring. On key release this stored spring force gives the carriage a fast initial return. A spring also cushions the key at the bottom of its stroke. Claws and a lock button hold the carriage in the lower or upper position.
