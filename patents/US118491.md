## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Earlier Sholes typewriters needed a separate paper carriage and platen, and a way to space letters and lines exactly and to move the ribbon steadily.

**Solution:** A single revolving, sliding cylinder serves as platen and paper carriage, driven by a cord and weight and metered letter by letter by a pair of vibrating ratchets, with a wide-notch wheel and a screw cam giving the line space once per revolution.

**How it works:** Paper is wrapped round a rubber-covered cylinder on a main axle and held by a bail in a lengthwise groove. A cord and weight constantly urge the axle to turn, held back by two ratchet pawls on a bar lifted by every key stroke. Pressing a key thrusts the single pawl into the first ratchet wheel to hold the paper while the type strikes, and withdraws the two-way pawl from the second wheel. On key release the single pawl lets go and the two-way pawl, which a weighted arm has rolled sideways one tooth, catches the next notch, so the weight advances the cylinder one letter space. A wide notch in the second wheel lets the cylinder turn at once through the whole notch, and a screw cam on the cylinder then passes between pins under the axle to slide the cylinder lengthwise one line space. Two pulleys on the ribbon spools with a cord from the axle move the ribbon a little at each turn, in either direction depending on how the cord is routed.
