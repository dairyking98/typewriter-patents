## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Typebar hangers were cut or sawn from bar stock with pivot lugs bored and countersunk by hand, which was costly.

**Solution:** Make the hanger bracket and pivot from die-pressed sheet metal with lugs drilled in one operation and adjustable, oilable bearing screws.

**How it works:** A sheet-metal bracket blank is die-pressed to form circular lugs at both ends and a curved central shank; the lugs are bored together with equal-diameter aligned holes and threaded. Apertured bearing screws with countersunk inner ends thread into the lugs and carry the pivot ends. The pivot is a stamped T-shaped blank pressed to a round pivot bar with a lateral connecting plate offset to one side, so the typebar sits at the centre of the pivot. The typebar is riveted or headed to the plate, with angular lugs preventing relative movement. The bearing screws are adjusted against the pivot ends without jamming; their holes allow oiling and let grit escape.
