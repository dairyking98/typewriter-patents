## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Rubber and other deformable type materials are not rigid enough to make a whole type wheel from, while rigid materials are heavy, wear and are costly to produce.

**Solution:** A rigid metal disc with bent-over teeth on its rim is moulded into the type material, so that each bent tooth backs one character and the moulding holds on dovetail anchors.

**How it works:** A steel (or aluminium, bronze, brass) disc is stamped with a hub, two locating holes and many identical undercut teeth around its circumference. Each tooth is bent at right angles to the disc plane about midway between base and tip, leaving dovetail-shaped recesses between and behind the bent parts. The disc is placed in an embossing mould and surrounded with type material such as polyamide, cellulose acetate butyrate, natural or synthetic rubber, or phenol-formaldehyde resin. The material fills the recesses, which lock it against shifting, and forms the raised or sunk characters on a type band over the bent teeth. During printing each bent tooth supports its character against the radial force of the impression stroke.
