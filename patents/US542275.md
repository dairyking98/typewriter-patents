## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Improving an Oliver-style typebar machine that prints on the upper side of the platen in view of the operator: ribbon handling, three-case shifting, automatic line feed with adjustable spacing, margin stops and a line-end key lock.

**Solution:** A typebar machine whose carriage rides on a shifting frame moved forward or rearward from a middle rest position by two shift levers to present one of three type faces per type-head, with an automatic ribbon carrier, an end-of-line key lock and an adjustable automatic line feed.

**How it works:** Looped typebars with three faces per head are driven by key levers through links. The carriage slides on a guide-bar of a shifting frame; two shift levers with oppositely curved cam slots rock a shaft that pushes the frame forward (figures) or back (capitals) from a middle lower-case rest, with a lock to hold either case. A rack on the carriage is engaged by a dog on a rocking lever driven by the space bar, which every key depression actuates, so each key spaces the carriage under a carriage spring. The same space-bar motion tilts a ribbon holder into the path of the type and ratchets the ribbon spools. When the carriage is returned to the start, a cam slide hits the margin stop and swings a feed lever that turns the platen, its throw set by a cam-adjusted gage arm. A trip arm struck by a detent on the carriage locks the space bar at line end unless the figure shift is used for a hyphen.
