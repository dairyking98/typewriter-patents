## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** Typewriter carriages could not hold very wide paper without curling its edges, or very narrow strips well.

**Solution:** Curve the carriage ends and platen-journal brackets upward and keep all line-space parts inside the platen's planes, so wide paper rolled crosswise passes beneath, with spring fingers on the scale bar holding narrow paper.

**How it works:** The carriage frame is bent down opposite the platen end so the roller extended would pass over it. Forward-curving brackets rise from the rear of the frame and carry the platen journal in their lower ends. A pawl on the bracket holds the platen ratchet teeth, and a lever pivoted to the carriage with a spring pawl turns it for line spacing, all lying between vertical planes through the front and rear of the platen. Paper wider than the machine goes beneath the brackets, its edges overhanging the carriage ends. For narrow paper, spring paper-holding fingers with buttons slide in a slot in the scale bar.
