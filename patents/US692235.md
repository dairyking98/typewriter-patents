## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A ribbon frame driven by the universal bar can be stopped against the platen before the type has finished its stroke, which would block the universal bar and the spacing and printing that depend on it.

**Solution:** A spring-loaded lost-motion lever between the universal bar and the ribbon frame lets the ribbon frame stop at the platen while the universal bar and type bar carry on; one ratchet on the same bar also winds the ribbon through crossed-cord linked spool shafts.

**How it works:** Any key depresses a universal bar frame whose arm works the carriage escapement dogs. The arm also moves a vibrating ribbon frame through an intermediate lever held by a stiff spring against a stop, so the frame lifts the ribbon to the platen as the type approaches and drops it as the type leaves. If the ribbon frame is stopped at the platen early, the lever yields against its spring and the universal bar completes its travel, letting the type finish and make the impression. A pin on the same arm swings a pawl that steps a ratchet on one of two vertical spool shafts, which are tied together by pulleys and a crossed cord so they turn in opposite directions; a hand-nut clamps whichever spool is to wind, and swapping the nuts reverses the ribbon. Fixed guide plates with notched, flared openings guide the stems of the three-type blocks into exact alignment at the print point, and a platen carriage rod raised by cam levers on the shift key picks one of three type heights; an adjustable block on the rack bar rings a bell.
