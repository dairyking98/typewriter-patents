## Summary

**Kind:** a part or mechanism (any kind of machine) · **Subject (patent classes):** Typewriters or selective printing mechanisms characterised by the mounting, arrangement or disposition of the types or dies

**Class note:** J-PlatPat files it as a Western-type typewriter (B41J1/00@P); it is a Japanese-script (kanji or kana) machine built on Western lines.

**In the English translation of the patent’s own words:** This invention relates to a typewriter having a mechanism that gives vertical movements of different pitches, and a mechanism that, in the horizontal movement of the paper cylinder, makes the cylinder move automatically and horizontally by the pitch of a type of a given pitch, by the simple pressing of one key on the keyboard belonging to that type.

**How it works (reading):** Each key advances the paper cylinder automatically by the half or full pitch belonging to its type when the key is released after printing.

## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** In the earlier machine (JP43652) that builds kanji from component types of different widths, the operator had to move the paper cylinder by hand for each different pitch before printing each type.

**Solution:** Each key advances the paper cylinder automatically by the half or full pitch belonging to its type when the key is released after printing.

**How it works:** Keys for half-pitch components sit on one bent frame and full-pitch keys on another, each frame driving its own sliding bar. Pressing a key pulls its sliding bar forward by a set amount, which turns a feed unit (case, loose ring, pawls and ratchet wheel) back against a spring. On key release the spring turns the case forward, the pawls turn the ratchet wheel one tooth (half pitch) or two (full pitch), and a square shaft and pinion drive the rack to move the paper cylinder horizontally. A stop screw and cross lever then lift the pawls out of the ratchet to end the advance. Advance occurs only after printing, an advance key adds a half-pitch space, and a hold-down key lever (185) suppresses the advance.
