## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A reverse-feed escapement (carriage released on the key downstroke) suits experts but blurs letters when a student types slowly, because the carriage moves while the type is still striking. Carriage stops also make a jarring noise.

**Solution:** A locking dog on the escapement pawl catches a stop when the key is pushed fully down, so the carriage only advances after the impression, whether the typist is fast or slow.

**How it works:** A dog block rocks on a shaft linked to the key levers by a common bar. A rigid dog, pivoted on a vertical axis and sloped on its back, normally sits in the rack bar on the carriage. A second pivoted dog carries a locking dog. On the downstroke the rigid dog leaves the rack and the pivoted dog engages it. If the key goes to the end of its stroke, the locking dog hits an adjustable stop, or in a variant a rack tooth, and holds the carriage until the key returns. On return the lock frees and the carriage steps one space. A leather and metal washer at the pivot of the rack-bar rod deadens the noise of release.
