## Summary

**Kind:** type-element machine · **Subject (patent classes):** Typewriters or selective printing mechanisms characterised by the mounting, arrangement or disposition of the types or dies with types or dies arranged in stationary or sliding cases or frames or upon flexible strips, plates, bars or rods; Japanese-language typewriter

**In the English translation of the patent’s own words:** This invention relates to a Japanese-language typewriter characterised as follows: type cases, each holding many types and having a cover that can pivot forward and bears index characters, are set at a slant on the support plates of a machine frame in large numbers to form a type store;

**How it works (reading):** Store loose types in slanted cases with index-character covers, raise the selected case with a selector lever, pick one type onto a slanted type-holding rod, and swing the rod to the cylinder with a printing lever that also feeds the carriage.

## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A small Japanese typewriter for portable or household use needs easy type selection, sure catching of the type, clear printing, and a visible print area, with a simple construction.

**Solution:** Store loose types in slanted cases with index-character covers, raise the selected case with a selector lever, pick one type onto a slanted type-holding rod, and swing the rod to the cylinder with a printing lever that also feeds the carriage.

**How it works:** Many type cases stand slanted in support plates; the printing frame is slid sideways by its handle until a pointer under the type-holding rod indicates the wanted character, and a ratchet stop bar then fixes it. Pressing the case-selector lever lifts that case above the store through a lifting lever and swings its cover forward to expose the types. The same lever moves the type-holding rod forward so its prong enters the hole in the wanted type. Pressing the printing lever swings the rod up toward the paper cylinder while a cam-driven cover closes over the type to hold it, and the type strikes through a ribbon. A letter-feed bar built into the printing lever drives a ratchet and rack to space the cylinder carriage, and can also be worked alone by its knob.
