## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Typebar pivots need to be cheap, durable and low-friction, and need to hold the type in accurate alignment with wear that can be taken up. They must also stay narrow so many bars fit around a type ring.

**Solution:** Pivot each typebar on a single row of a few balls running between V-grooved conical bosses of a two-part hanger and a groove in the typebar's eye, with a screw to draw the cones together and take up wear.

**How it works:** The typebar has a hub with a transverse eye whose inside carries a ring-shaped V groove. The hanger is two identical side arms joined by a cross screw, each with a conical boss that enters the eye. Three balls sit between the cones and the groove, held apart by a thin notched disc. A tie screw through the bosses and the disc is turned in to draw the cones closer and remove play as the bearing wears. The wide cone faces give stable side alignment while the bearing stays thin.

**Parts:** typebar; hub with eye; hanger side arm; conical boss; ball; notched separator disc; adjusting tie screw; cross screw; connecting link

**Numbers:** three balls in the example

**Improves on:**

- Wide hanger or long trunnion typebar pivot: Gives the same stability in a narrower joint so more bars fit on a type ring, with adjustable ball bearings
