## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** On a two-character typebar machine with a shifting platen, the carriage spring pull varies along the travel, making keystroke force irregular, and the platen shift, ribbon spool movement and carriage guidance need to be accurate and light.

**Solution:** A snail-cam (scroll) spring drum evens out the carriage pull, and a secondary platen carriage on rollers is shifted at right angles by a long shifter bar with hooks that lock it at either position.

**How it works:** Finger keys link to double-character typebars in a circle around the opening of a top plate. The carriage rides on a bevelled rail with V-grooved rollers that seat without side play. The carriage spring drum is a scroll-shaped shell: as the spring unwinds, the strap winds toward the axis, so leverage is least at highest tension and greatest at lowest, and the pull on the carriage stays uniform. A worm and wheel adjust the spring tension. A secondary frame carrying the platen slides on rollers within the main carriage at right angles to the line. Shift keys rock elbow levers that move a rod, which through a yoke tips two blocks and lifts one set of hooks off studs, slides the platen frame, and drops the other hooks onto the second set of studs to lock it. The same spring drum also drives, via a ratchet and bevel pinions, two grooved spool shafts that move both ribbon spools together so the ribbon travels in a straight line.
