## Reading (full text, OCR poor)

**Problem:** In typewriters with two or three types per typebar where the platen shifts, the platen must be held in a central position and moved up or down quietly; the shifting mechanism should be simple and stop without impact or noise.

**Solution:** Two shift keys act on a double-armed floating lever connected to the platen carriage so that depressing either one moves the platen up or down from a central rest position, with toggle links that straighten to stop the motion without impact.

**How it works:** The platen carriage is held in a central position and can be shifted either way from it so that one of three types on each typebar meets the printing point. Each of two shift keys has a shift lever; a spring blocking member keeps the other shift lever locked while one is depressed. A duplex floating lever is pivoted to both shift levers at different points, and the locked lever's pivot acts as a fixed fulcrum so the lever swings the platen rod up or down according to which key is pressed. Pairs of toggle links with adjustable eccentrics straighten at the end of travel, arresting the swinging lever without concussion and noiselessly. A full elliptic spring balances the carriage in the middle and a return spring restores the shift levers and platen.
