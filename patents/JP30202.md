## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Sugimoto's earlier tray typewriter (JP27877) needed a simpler, stronger type magazine and lacked line spacing, character spacing without printing, an adjustable blow and a paper clamp that fits any sheet size.

**Solution:** A frame carrying a large paper cylinder and a lever that lifts one loose type out of a cell magazine, inks it and strikes it on the paper, with automatic character spacing from the same lever and added line-space, clamp and spring-adjust devices.

**How it works:** The operator slides the moving frame over a magazine of square cells (formed by crimped transverse plates crossing slotted longitudinal plates) until the square-hole end of the printing lever is over the wanted type. Pressing the pressing lever raises a type-lifting lever whose point pushes the type up into the hole of the printing lever. A presser then enters a nick in the type to hold it, and a link turns the printing lever upward so the type strikes the paper on the cylinder. An ink roller on a sprung lever rolls over the type face as it rises, then is cammed out of the way. On release the type drops back into its cell, and a bent lever driven by a roller on the pressing lever rocks a pawl frame that turns the paper cylinder one ratchet tooth, which is the character space. A key on that frame spaces without printing, and a lever with a stepped stop sleeve slides the cylinder frame one chosen line space.
