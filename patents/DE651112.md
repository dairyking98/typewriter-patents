## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** On type-roller machines the roller is placed roughly by a pointer and touch pad, then a spring-loaded key stroke drives it to the platen; the alignment knife may still be only partly seated in the comb, so the impression is uneven and the spring force is not fully used.

**Solution:** Pull the alignment knife fully into the selected comb notch by the key lever itself, and only then release a latched, pre-tensioned impression spring, whose tension is adjustable by an eccentric.

**How it works:** The type roller slides along its axis, set roughly by a pointer. Depressing the key lever pulls a second pivoting knife arm, joined to the lever by a link with a horizontal rail so axial travel is free, into the preselected notch of a fixed comb, locking the roller position. Meanwhile the pull rod carrying the type carrier is held back by a locking pawl against its tension spring. Near the end of the key stroke the lever trips the pawl, which releases the rod so the fully tensioned spring throws the type carrier to the platen. A spring returns the lever and rod, and the pawl relocks. A double lever on the pull rod with a rotatable eccentric sets the spring tension, with extra springs in slotted eyelets that join in at higher settings, and a scale reads the setting.
