## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A key-operated machine needs to trip a spring latch at a distance from the key, without rods and levers running between them.

**Solution:** Pressing a key drives a primary piston that forces oil through a pipe to a secondary piston, which trips a spring-projected latch.

**How it works:** A key lever pushes a primary piston in a cylinder fed by gravity from an oil reservoir. As the piston rises its valve port closes the supply, and the confined oil in the service pipe is forced forward to push a secondary piston whose rod rides up a cam face on a spring-loaded latch and releases it. The latch then springs forward and turns a starting arm of a turret mechanism, and the arm resets the latch afterwards. A relief port in the secondary piston lets surplus oil run back to the reservoir late in the stroke, so the stroke length sets the tripping force and travel. Springs return both pistons.
