## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Typewriters need fewer and simpler parts, a compact build, visible writing, easy substitution of type styles, variable spacing suited to the characters, whole-word printing from one key, and carriages of different lengths.

**Solution:** A square or polygonal type-sleeve slides along a rotatable rod so that the depressed key lever acts as a stop to choose the character, then the same key stroke releases a spring that lifts a platen-frame against the sleeve and the platen's fall advances the carriage by an amount set by spacing projections on the sleeve.

**How it works:** Key levers pivoted on both sides cross the path of a type-sleeve. Pressing a key moves the sleeve rearwards through a bail, levers and links until a stop on the sleeve meets the depressed key lever, so the character at that distance reaches the printing point; a yielding spring plate with adjusting screws absorbs the rest of the key travel to avoid rebound. A hand nut and notched disk with detent rotate the sleeve to present one of its faces (different type styles or whole words), carried on dovetailed removable type-strips. Further key travel withdraws a stop, releasing a spring stored by the rock-arm and the platen-frame, carriage and paper roll are thrown up against the type, then drop back by weight. The platen-frame carries a feed pawl on a rack along the carriage; a lever bearing on spacing projections on the sleeve controls how long the pawl is held back so the carriage steps by an amount fitted to the character, and a separate space key gives free spacing. An ink roller bears on the sleeve at the printing point.
