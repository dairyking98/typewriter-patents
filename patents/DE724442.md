## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** A typewriter writing a continuous helical line on a vertical cylindrical platen needs a way to move the platen to any position to start text sections, and to remove it.

**Solution:** A hollow vertical platen climbs a fixed screw spindle; two-armed levers inside it carry thread jaws that can be squeezed out of engagement by hand to reposition or remove the platen.

**How it works:** The cylindrical platen, with the paper wrapped round it with an overlap, turns on a fixed screw spindle fixed in the base plate. Spring-loaded thread jaws on levers hinged inside the hollow platen engage the spindle thread; gripping the lever handles releases them. A gear at the platen's lower end meshes with a long pinion driven by a clockwork or electric motor, so the platen turns and rises together and writing runs as an unbroken helix. The line spacing equals the thread pitch. Ink ribbon guide rollers stand either side of the print point.
