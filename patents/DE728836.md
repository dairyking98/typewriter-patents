## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** A key-driven type-cylinder machine needs the keys to set the type head without a lever system, and the impact strength has to be independent of how fast the keys are pressed.

**Solution:** Each key has a wedge-shaped control member that shifts a parallel-guided grid to a unique position, which turns and raises the small type head; pressing the key also cocks a spring through a rolling cam, and the spring throws the head at the platen once setting is finished.

**How it works:** Keys carry replaceable wedge control members above a grid that is parallel-guided in a plane and sprung to a middle position. A key's wedge pushes the grid sideways, and a pin on the grid drives a toothed segment, a toothed drum and an axle to turn the type head by about 33 degrees either side of the centre. A forward or back grid motion moves a pin against an angle lever and rod to raise or lower the head by one or two lines, according to key row. The head has eight type discs (four upper rows normal, four lower rows after the carriage shift). A light frame pushed down by the keys runs a roller over a cam to tension a leg spring on the head's pivot axis. A pawl releases the spring once setting is complete, throwing the head against the platen, with a ratchet wheel for fine locking. Stop collars with felt reduce noise.
