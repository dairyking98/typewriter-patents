## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typewriters were costly and complex; the inventors want one of few, cheap parts.

**Solution:** Eleven rods tipped with straight metal strips form every character as combinations of line segments, raised by notched key levers like key bits.

**How it works:** Push-button key levers pivot on standards; their inner ends carry notches and teeth like a key bit. Depressing a lever raises a particular subset of eleven bars, each with a rod ending in a metal strip. The strips are arranged as a square with diagonals and a transverse bar, so groups of raised strips print letters made of straight lines only through an inked ribbon. A roller platen moves on a base bar along a rack in a groove with ratchet and rocking pawl frame for spacing.

**Features:** [SEL:keyboard-full](../features/sel-keyboard-full.md), [ELEM:individual-slugs](../features/elem-individual-slugs.md), [ELEM:linear-bar](../features/elem-linear-bar.md), [INK:ribbon](../features/ink-ribbon.md), [ESC:pawl-rack](../features/esc-pawl-rack.md), [PAP:platen-roller](../features/pap-platen-roller.md), [FRM:few-parts](../features/frm-few-parts.md), [IMP:press-through](../features/imp-press-through.md)

**Parts:** base plate; key lever; push-button; notch; bar; rod; metal strip; top plate; ribbon; roller; rack; ratchet wheel; hook pawl; rocking frame

**Numbers:** eleven rods
