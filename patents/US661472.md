## Summary

**Kind:** type-element machine · **Subject (patent classes):** Typewriters or selective printing mechanisms characterised by the mounting, arrangement or disposition of the types or dies with types or dies mounted on carriers rotatable for selection the plane of the type or die face being parallel to the axis of rotation, e.g. with type on the periphery of cylindrical carriers

**In the patent’s own words:** The object of my invention is to prevent wear by changing the point of contact on the stop-arm with each key-lever.

## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** In the Elliott & Hatch book typewriter the type-wheel stop-arm always meets the key-lever stops at the same point on its square end, so it wears and the type-wheel drifts out of position.

**Solution:** Give the stop-arm a two-lobed rounded end so each key-lever stop touches it at a different point, spreading the wear.

**How it works:** A carrier sleeved on a vertical type-wheel shaft has a front stop-arm, a rear positioning arm that engages a spoke of the type-wheel, and two side wings with slotted centering faces. Depressing a key drives one of two actuators rearward against a wing slot, which rotates the carrier and wheel. The stop-arm sweeps under slotted stops on the key-levers until it meets the stop on the depressed lever, which halts the wheel at that key's position. Further key travel works the hammer. The patent replaces the square stop-arm end with two circular lobes, one serving the key-levers on each side of the keyboard, so the contact point varies with key position.
