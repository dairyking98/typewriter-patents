## Summary

**Kind:** a part or mechanism (any kind of machine) · **Subject (patent classes):** Line-spacing mechanisms

**In the patent’s own words:** This invention relates to improvements on that class of type-writers wherein the feedroll acting on the paper is mounted in a carriage which is adapted to be slid backwardly upon a pivoted or tipping frame when uppercase or capitals are to be printed.

**How it works (reading):** Mount the feed-roll on a rotating shaft that braces the carriage, and add a pawl controller that keeps the line-feed pawl clear of the roll teeth until the spacing lever is operated, with adjustable stroke.

## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** On Remington-type machines with a sliding feed-roll carriage for capitals, moving the roll quickly by hand required lifting the feed pawl by hand, and the carriage side pieces needed bracing.

**Solution:** Mount the feed-roll on a rotating shaft that braces the carriage, and add a pawl controller that keeps the line-feed pawl clear of the roll teeth until the spacing lever is operated, with adjustable stroke.

**How it works:** The feed-roll shaft turns in bearings on the side pieces of a carriage sliding on a tipping frame, holding them parallel. Finger-wheels on the shaft allow twirling the roll to insert or remove paper. The spacing lever carries a pawl that engages a ratchet plate on the roll. A controller normally holds the pawl end out of the teeth when the lever is at rest, and releases it as the lever moves; the controller position can be changed to lengthen or shorten the feed stroke. A rubber band on sheaves keeps the paper pressed to the roll.
