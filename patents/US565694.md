## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** The carriage of a matrix-making machine (Sears and Miller) must advance by distances proportionate to the width of each character.

**Solution:** A feed screw is turned through a ratchet by pawls whose stroke is set by pins of different lengths on each character's die case.

**How it works:** Each character has a key lever, die and case, and a horizontal pin on the rear of the case whose length is proportional to the character width. The carriage slides on a fixed guide and is engaged by a bracket to a feed screw, so turning the screw moves the carriage. A pawl-carrying frame on a sleeve rocks back when a link is pulled by a slide plate, and the pawls slide back over a ratchet a distance set by the plate's backward travel. A set-screw stops the frame's forward swing, and the sleeve is clutched to the screw shaft to feed.

**Features:**

- **Spacing and escapement:** [Proportional spacing](../features/esc-variable-spacing.md), [Pawl-and-rack escapement](../features/esc-pawl-rack.md)

**Parts:** feed screw; ratchet; pawl-carrying frame; pawl; link; slide plate; die; die case; key lever; carriage; set-screw
