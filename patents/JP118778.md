## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Japanese tray-type machines move the printing point in two directions and are slow if the two movements run one after the other or share the drive poorly.

**Solution:** Two linked differentials let one motor drive the lengthwise and crosswise positioning together, so that when one axis stops the other gets the full drive, and then trigger the print.

**How it works:** The intermediate wheels of differentials (a) and (b) are geared together. The motor drives one shaft of (a); the other shaft of (a) goes through a telescopic universal-jointed shaft to a cam or crank that works the printing lever. The two shafts of (b) drive the front-back sliding frame (by racks) and the side-to-side paper-cylinder carriage (by a square shaft and rack). Pressing one front-back key and one side key lets a rack pin stop each axis at the chosen place. Both axes move at once, the free one speeding up when the other stops, and once both are stopped the resistance makes the first differential's second shaft turn and the push-up rod and printing lever print the type. Releasing the keys reverses the friction drive and returns everything to the stops.

## Description (English translation of the Japanese text, opening)

> Specification. Power control device for a Japanese printing machine Summary of the nature and object of the invention. This invention relates to a power control device for a Japanese printing machine in which the intermediate wheels of two differential mechanisms (a) and (b) are connected to each other by gears or chains; one shaft of differential (a) is the driving shaft and is connected to a power source through a suitable coupling, its other shaft is connected to a suitable mechanism that works the printing mechanism of the Japanese printing machine; one shaft of differential (b) is engaged with the mechanism that moves the moving part of the printing machine lengthwise (front to back), and its other shaft with the mechanism that moves the moving part crosswise. Its object is to make the lengthwise and crosswise movements of the moving parts of the Japanese printing machine as nearly simultaneous as possible, and so to allow quicker printing work.
