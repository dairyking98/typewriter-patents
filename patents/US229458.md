## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typewriters of the time needed a simple, accurate way to guide typebars and space the carriage.

**Solution:** A table-top typebar machine in which each typebar has a conical stud that enters a guide hole to register the type, with a spring-driven carriage and an escapement operated by every key.

**How it works:** Typebars pivot in guides around a circular opening in the table, each with a conical stud on its short arm that seats in a guide hole, giving accurate register and a stop. Key levers of equal length carry keys at different distances from the front and connect to the typebars by rods. The carriage slides on a rod and is pulled by a strap to a spring drum. A rocking shaft driven by any key moves a sliding escapement bar with a fixed pin and a pivoted dog into an escapement wheel, so the carriage advances one tooth per key. A spacing frame moves the carriage without printing, and a ratchet and lever turn the roller for line feed.
