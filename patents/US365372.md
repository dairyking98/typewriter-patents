## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typewriters did not show the work and needed a compact arrangement with several characters per type lever.

**Solution:** A tilting head holds 26 radial type hammers, each triangular in section and carrying capital, small letter and figure on its three faces, rotated together by a ring gear to pick the case while keys depress the hammers.

**How it works:** The head's base ring is pivoted on goose-neck stands so it can tilt up to show the work and change paper. In the ring, 26 radial short shafts with bevel pinions mesh with a ring gear so all rotate at once. Each shaft is ball-connected to a type hammer whose inner end is triangular, with a capital, small letter and figure or punctuation on three inclined faces. Turning the ring gear brings the chosen face to the print point, and a key rod and link depress the hammer. A central disk depressed by the keys works the space or word-feed lever, and the paper roll rides on a carriage that is fed endwise, helped by a vacuum cylinder.
