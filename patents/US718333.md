## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A visible-writing typebar machine needs typebars that are positively aligned at the print point and a case shift that does not complicate the key linkage.

**Solution:** The key-levers and a segmental typebar support ride on a keyboard carriage that is slid at right angles to the paper carriage to shift case, with grooved typebar seats locking each bar in alignment.

**How it works:** Key-levers pivoted between their ends pull a ligament that lifts the inner end of a typebar and drives the type down onto the platen from above and in front, so the writing stays visible. The typebars pivot on a wire through lugs on a segmental support and drop into radial grooves that hold them in line. The whole keyboard carriage, with typebars and ribbon mechanism, is moved to the rear by a shift key against a spring on ball bearings to bring the capital characters to the print point. Depressing any key pushes a universal bail down onto the space lever, which trips the pivoted dog escapement, and also onto a ribbon feed lever that advances the ribbon ratchet after the type leaves. A separate space bar works the escapement alone, and sideways motion of it throws the dogs out of the rack for carriage release.
