## Reading (opening only, OCR poor)

**Problem:** A complete typewriter cannot be shown working in a shop window with the operator hidden from view.

**Solution:** Drive each typebar by a solenoid wired to a contact under each key of a remote keyboard, with an electric motor and cables moving the carriage.

**How it works:** Key levers on a distant keyboard close a contact beneath each lever and energise a solenoid above the exhibition machine. The solenoid core is pulled down hard, throwing the typebar up to print; a screw plug in the tube nearly touches the core at the moment of impact. A single wire joins all solenoids to a battery and the metal frame. A motor with a uniformly wound armature drives a spool and two cables, one pulling the carriage in letter feed and the other returning it, hooked to the usual spacing lever and line-space pawl.

**Features:**

- **Type element:** [Typebars](../features/elem-typebar.md)

- **Uses:** [Printing telegraph](../features/use-telegraph-printer.md)

**Parts:** solenoid; core; typebar; key lever; contact; battery; motor; spool; cable; paper carriage; spacing lever; platen
