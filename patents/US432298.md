## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** Underscoring, cancelling and filling blank space on a typewriter normally needs the key-levers or typing mechanism; the inventor wants a simple, quick device independent of them.

**Solution:** A pivoted casing holding an inking pad and a marking wheel is swung against the platen to rule a line under, through or across printed matter.

**How it works:** Two casings (one for underscoring, one for cancelling/filling) are pivoted on standards beside the platen. Each holds a marking wheel on a shaft above a horizontal rotating inking pad, so ink does not seep to one end. The operator swings a casing up so the wheel touches the platen, and the toothed wheel turns the pad as it rolls. The underscoring wheel is V-shaped for a continuous line and sits so the platen need not be turned; a hinged partition closes the ink-filling openings when idle. Shown on a Blickensderfer type-wheel machine.

**Parts:** casing; marking wheel; inking pad; pivot standard; partition; platen
