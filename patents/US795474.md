## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Typebar hangers screwed to a flat segment can rotate about their screw, because uneven contact surfaces press hardest near the screw, and the washers turn with the screw and crowd their neighbours.

**Solution:** Groove the segment faces around the screws so hangers touch along four lines far from the screw, and fix each washer's position with a notched band or a D-shaped edge against a step.

**How it works:** The typebar segment has two concentric steps, each with a shallow arc groove, and hanger screws enter tapped holes at the groove bottoms. Each hanger therefore bridges the groove and touches the segment along four lines distant from the screw, giving more frictional leverage against turning. Front-row washers have a top lug with spring-hook holes and a lower tongue that fits a notch in a metal strip screwed under the segment. Rear-row washers are D-shaped and their edges rest against the top of the front step. Neither kind can turn when the screw is tightened, and the washers spread the clamping pressure along the hanger top.

**Numbers:** two concentric steps; four lines of contact per hanger; two types per typeblock

**Improves on:**

- flat-faced typebar segments with plain washers: grooves move the contact away from the screw and the washers are keyed against turning
