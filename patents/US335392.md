## Summary

**Kind:** index machine · **Subject (patent classes):** Toy typewriters

**In the patent’s own words:** My invention relates to that class of typewriters which employ a rotary type-wheel mounted on a depressible lever a-rranged over a sliding paper-carriage, and provided with a central axial handle adapted to be twirled in the lingers to bring the desired letter into the printing position, after which the handle and wheel are depressed to print the letter.

**How it works (reading):** A depressible, hand-twirled type-wheel whose rim has recesses of varying depth between the types; a feed pawl's tail drops into the recess of the printed letter, so the carriage advances a number of rack teeth proportional to the letter's width.

## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Improves the inventor's earlier twirl-handle type-wheel machine: letter spacing should follow letter width, the wheel should be inked without blocking the print point, and the operator needs an indicator of the wheel position.

**Solution:** A depressible, hand-twirled type-wheel whose rim has recesses of varying depth between the types; a feed pawl's tail drops into the recess of the printed letter, so the carriage advances a number of rack teeth proportional to the letter's width.

**How it works:** A type-wheel with metal types set in its rim sits on a spring-raised lever above a sliding paper carriage, with a central twirling handle. The wheel is fixed to a toothed locking-wheel, and a fixed tooth on the frame enters between its teeth when the wheel is pressed down, locking the selected type during the impression. A toothed index wheel gears with the locking wheel and turns a pointer over a dial of characters; a click spring holds each step. An ink disk on a pendulous arm rides on the rim, inking every type as the wheel is twirled, and swings aside automatically when the wheel is depressed. Pressing the wheel lever swings an elbow lever and pawl back over the carriage rack until the pawl tail meets the bottom of the recess of the printed letter, lifting the pawl out of the rack; on release the pawl feeds the carriage forward by the number of teeth it slipped over, one to four. A separate knob feeds a full four-tooth space, and also frees the rack for sliding the carriage.
