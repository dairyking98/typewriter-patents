## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** On an Underwood platen-shift machine, writing a line of capitals needs a shift-lock, but a lock gives no easy way to drop to lower case for a few characters such as punctuation.

**Solution:** A separate finger lever latches the shifting frame in capital position through a normally idle spring, and a second key temporarily pulls the platen back to lower case against that spring for as long as it is held.

**How it works:** Types are on front-strike typebars, lower-case and capital, and the platen rides on a shifting frame hinged on a rod that moves up and down. The ordinary shift key presses a lever against the frame to raise the platen; releasing it lets gravity return the frame, and a locking lever notched on a pin of the frame holds it in lower case until the key releases it by a cam. Depressing a finger-piece tensions a normally slack spring that pulls up the locking lever, whose cam edge throws the frame to capital position, and a notch latch holds the finger lever there, leaving both hands free. A key at the rear of the keyboard, linked to the frame through a slotted connection, pulls the frame back to lower case against the spring while held; on release the spring restores capitals. Pressing the ordinary shift key again releases the latch.
