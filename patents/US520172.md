## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** In keyboard type-wheel machines the wheel is usually returned by a spring, so it may overshoot and does not go positively to the desired character from wherever the previous one left it.

**Solution:** Each key acts through two opposing levers, one pushing the wheel lever one way by a differential arm and one pulling it back by a pin on a broad lever, so the wheel lever is held rigidly at the exact point, and a V-tooth then aligns it on the wheel gear.

**How it works:** Key levers press arms at different distances along a pivoted lever 18, which through a toggle, segment and pinion rotates the type wheel. Pins of different lengths on the same key levers press a broad lever, linked to lever 18, so it is driven the opposite way; the two opposing forces hold lever 18 at the key's position, whichever side the wheel was on, so it cannot overshoot. The lever is in two sprung parts to yield slightly, and a V-tooth on the oscillating paper-cylinder shaft enters a gear on the wheel with one tooth per letter space to align the wheel exactly. A key-lever bail oscillates the shell carrying the paper cylinder against the wheel to print. Pawls on the carriage ratchet step it one tooth per oscillation and a spring strap and box return it, with pins that disengage the holding pawl at line end and a guide plate that keeps the ratchet clear for the return. A weighted arm and pawl turn the cylinder for a new line. Capitals come from the wheel's two rows: each finger key is pivoted on its lever, so striking it in front of its centre swings an arm under a bar to lift the wheel.
