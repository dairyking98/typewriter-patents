## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Typebar machines need duplicate keys and bars for capital and small letters.

**Solution:** Each typebar carries a capital and a small letter side by side, and the platen is vibrated transversely to the line of printing so one key prints either case.

**How it works:** Typebars pivoted around a frame opening all strike a common center, each bar bearing a capital and its small letter side by side. The cylindrical platen sits in a secondary frame that slides forward and back inside the carriage frame. A spiral spring normally holds the platen back, so the small letters print; pulling a handle rod draws the platen forward so the same keys print capitals. Foot or lever operation, or moving the typebars instead of the platen, are mentioned as equivalents.

**Features:** [ELEM:typebar](../features/elem-typebar.md), [SHIFT:platen](../features/shift-platen.md), [SEL:keyboard-full](../features/sel-keyboard-full.md), [IMP:element-to-platen](../features/imp-element-to-platen.md), [PAP:platen-roller](../features/pap-platen-roller.md)

**Parts:** typebar with two types; cylindrical platen; sliding platen frame; carriage frame; handle rod; return spring; key

**Improves on:**

- earlier vibrating-platen machines: earlier bars carried only upper-case letters printing side by side at different points, needing complicated platen travel along the line; here both cases print at a common center with one transverse platen shift
