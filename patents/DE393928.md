## Summary

**Kind:** type-element machine · **Subject (patent classes):** Typewriters or selective printing mechanisms characterised by the mounting, arrangement or disposition of the types or dies with types or dies mounted on carriers rotatable for selection the plane of the type or die face being parallel to the axis of rotation, e.g. with type on the periphery of cylindrical carriers

**In Google’s translation of the patent’s own words:** The invention relates to such drifting machines, in which a set of types with the aid of a button is arranged on a hearing body can be adjusted according to an insert plate, and in which the impact of the individual types or groups of types is carried out by a single beating link.

**How it works (reading):** A pivoting feeler arm sets a rotary body of radially sliding type carriers coarsely over a character plate, a solenoid pin fine-aligns and locks it just before one striker hits, and raising or lowering the paper roller gives three characters per carrier, six with a shifted gear segment.

## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Earlier button-and-plate typewriters with a single striker had slow writing because each button position gave at most two characters, so the setting plate was large, and the button had to be pressed into a toothing to lock the rotary body.

**Solution:** A pivoting feeler arm sets a rotary body of radially sliding type carriers coarsely over a character plate, a solenoid pin fine-aligns and locks it just before one striker hits, and raising or lowering the paper roller gives three characters per carrier, six with a shifted gear segment.

**How it works:** A feeler arm pivots over a circular setting plate and turns the rotary body through a gear segment and toothed wheel, so the desired type carrier lies in front of the single striking member. Closing the handle contact first energises a solenoid whose nose enters a flaring guide groove, correcting and locking the rotary body. A second electromagnet then snaps the striking member forward, it couples by a bayonet nose to the type carrier, releases its lock and throws it radially against the paper roller. Pushing the handle radially in or out closes contacts that raise or lower the paper roller by electromagnets, so the upper, middle or lower type of the carrier prints. A lever 49 slides the gear segment on its carrier against a spring to bring the neighbouring type carrier into position, giving six characters per plate field. The carrier hitting a lever steps the paper roller and ribbon; an eddy-current brake damps the rotary body, and the machine can be worked with one hand.
