## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Typebar alignment guides (V-notches) only control the bar at the very end of its stroke, so alignment suffers when thick paper stacks stop the bar short.

**Solution:** A rounded or acorn-shaped fixed pin enters a curved, parallel-walled hole in the typebar and guides it continuously over the last stretch of its swing.

**How it works:** The typebar has a curved slot whose parallel walls are arcs centred on the bar pivot. A spherical or acorn-shaped pin on an arm fixed to the machine bridge enters this slot as the bar nears the platen. Its largest diameter fits the slot walls, and its pointed tip eases entry at speed. The pin keeps guiding the bar over an eighth of an inch or more, so the type is aligned wherever the bar stops, whether one or ten sheets lie on the platen. The pin passes through a rebound leaf spring that also kicks the bar back from dead centre. The pin and hole may be swapped between bar and frame.
