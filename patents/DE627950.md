## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** In electromagnet-driven type-cylinder typewriters the magnets sat off the sector axis and drove it through an intermediate gear, and the sector moved with the cylinder's printing stroke, complicating the drive.

**Solution:** Mount the toothed sector on a fixed square shaft that the two opposed electromagnets turn directly, with the cylinder's swivel axis passing through the pitch-circle contact point so the sector stays in mesh while the cylinder swings to print.

**How it works:** Pressing a key on one half of the keyboard energises one of two electromagnets; their armatures sit on a fixed square shaft and turn it left or right. The shaft carries a toothed sector meshing with a gear on a sleeve around the type cylinder shaft, so the cylinder turns at most 180 degrees either way. A stop arm on the same shaft runs against a stop angle brought into its path by the pressed key, which fixes the rotation angle. Just before the end of rotation a bell crank on the stop arm lifts a pawl that had locked the cylinder's printing swing, which an electromagnet then performs, pivoting the cylinder carrier on pointed screws against the paper roller. Intermediate levers, pawls and contact plates connect each key to its stop and to the correct magnet; felt or leather strips quiet the stops.
