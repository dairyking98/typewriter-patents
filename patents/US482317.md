## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Existing writing machines have too many parts and a hard-working print action, and printing and alignment are imperfect.

**Solution:** A type-wheel machine where one hand turns a key around a lettered, notched arc to rotate the wheel via a band-and-pulley drive, then presses it down to release a hammer and step the carriage.

**How it works:** The type-wheel sits on the top of a vertically movable, non-rotating shaft; a pulley below is joined to the wheel by a stem. A hinged segment drives the pulley through two steel strips wound in opposite directions, and an arm carries the key-bar over a curved lettered plate with pointers. The operator swings the key to the character, then presses it down so it drops into a vertical notch on a toothed arc, which locates the wheel. The same downward stroke pushes a curved bar that throws the hammer against the type and releases the carriage trigger. On release the trigger pawl, pulled by a spring, advances the carriage one tooth. Two levers under the shaft raise the wheel one or two rows for capitals and figures.
