## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** In automatic ribbon reversing, the change-over can leave loose or sagging ribbon between guide fork and spool, upsetting the switching and print, and the switching can jam if the guide forks are pushed inward deliberately or by accident; an earlier fix with an extra winding movement was complex.

**Solution:** Transmit the guide fork's movement to the intermediate member through an inclined surface sloped so the fork is pushed back to its start position gradually, at about the speed the ribbon winds on the spool, so no slack arises.

**How it works:** Two ribbon spools on vertical shafts are driven alternately through bevel gears from a common horizontal drive shaft, which turns stepwise by a ratchet and pawl worked by the universal rod with each key stroke. The shaft also slides axially to engage the bevel gear of the spool that is to wind. The ribbon passes through guide forks that the ribbon displaces when it is fully unwound, moving an intermediate member that slides the drive shaft and so switches to the empty spool. A reversing pawl carries a resilient intermediate lever whose arm rides on the inclined surface of the fork part, and a pivot-eccentric stop on the frame sets the push. A ring cam beside each helical gear, with concentric and inclined surfaces, keeps the pawl from being forced past the teeth if the forks are pushed in.
