## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** Ribbon-spool drive on Remington typewriters: the ribbon must be wound in either direction from the carriage/mainspring motion, with both spools staying taut without separate tensioning parts.

**Solution:** A ratchet on the mainspring wheel turns a spool shaft one way only; a hand-shifted pair of bevel gears reverses the other spool, and both spools ride their shafts on spring-loaded felt-washer friction clutches.

**How it works:** The oscillating mainspring wheel carries a pawl that turns a ratchet and bevel gear on shaft 3 always in one direction. A cross shaft with two bevel gears is slid by hand to mesh one or the other with that gear, and a worm and worm wheel then turn the opposite spool shaft in either direction. Each wooden spool sits loosely on a sleeve held to its shaft by a coiled spring pressing between collars and felt washers, so it turns by friction. The spring on the driven side is stronger than on the other, so the driven spool winds ribbon against the light drag of the other spool, which is slowly carried along or backed against its shaft.
