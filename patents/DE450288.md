## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Earlier power-driven type-rod machines used a camshaft that was triggered only when a type advanced, giving jerky alternation of rest and motion, heavy wear and complicated control gear.

**Solution:** A continuously rotating, motor-driven bead (cam-ridge) shaft is engaged by the depressed key lever and presses the type rod on through a simple push rod, and the same shaft also returns the key lever, so no return springs are needed.

**How it works:** Each two-armed key lever is hinged to a vertical type rod carrying two types, guided in a tangential plane toward the platen. At rest the lever is held by a fork clamping spring and the longitudinal bead of the rotating shaft blocks it. Pressing a key preloads a spring in its key sleeve; once the shaft's bead has turned clear, that spring overcomes the clamping spring and the lever follows the shaft to its working position. There a cam bead on the shaft drives a roller and adjustable push rod that presses the type rod about 1 mm against the platen, with the length of the push rod setting impression strength. A platen shift lever chooses which of the two types strikes, and the bead then forces the key lever back to rest where the clamp catches it. The type rods sit in three staggered tangential planes for a compact build, and the ribbon is lifted by the rising rod.
