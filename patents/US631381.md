## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** A Hammond-type single-carrier machine needs a way to bring any of many types to the print point with only one key-lever motion, while keeping touch light and alignment exact.

**Solution:** A celluloid fan-shaped type plate of radial fingers is rocked about its centre by key-lever-driven driver and stop arms to either side, lifted one arc at a time, while the paper carriage with a cylindrical platen is shifted vertically, and a long-faced hammer strikes from behind.

**How it works:** Key levers (long and short alternating, in graded banks, with graded fulcra for equal rear travel) raise suspended frames that rock two cross-armed shafts, one end acting as driver and the opposite as stop, swinging the sector plate left or right about its pivot; the plate stays where the last stroke left it. Long levers also raise a lifter that moves the plate up one arc (row) in the same motion. The plate has 13 radial fingers slit from one sector with three couplets of two arched rows (capitals, lower case, numerals and punctuation), types molded on the rear face. The paper carriage rail is raised or lowered by a pair of shift levers with equalizing levers so the cylindrical platen meets a different row, while the feed rack stays still. A hammer with a face long enough for a whole finger strikes the plate and ribbon against the platen; its yielding spring links blow, ribbon-guide draw-back, ribbon feed through lever clutches, and letter spacing by two dogs on the rack. Line feed is through a friction-held pawl and ratchet on the platen, with a spring-drum carriage and a bell.
