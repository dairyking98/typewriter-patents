## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** A type-disc typewriter for any script or font (Fraktur, Latin, Greek, Cyrillic, photo-typesetting) that stops a continuously spinning disc with a friction lever suffers wear and uncertainty.

**Solution:** Spin an idle type disc by an electric motor on keypress and stop it at the chosen character with an electromagnetic brake, with no frictional contact, then strike it against the platen under spring force.

**How it works:** Characters are fixed on the circumference of an interchangeable disc of non-magnetic pressed material; keys with two contacts close one circuit that starts the motor winding and another that energises a brake magnet, which stops the disc when the pressed character reaches the writing position. The brake flux at that moment switches off a holding magnet that kept the disc in its normal position, letting a spring-driven device throw the disc against the platen or the platen against the disc. Interrupting the flux restores the holding magnet and the rest position. Multiple discs can sit on one shaft and be shifted sideways by a changeover switch for more fonts or characters; the platen may be vertical or replaced by a character-height ring, and is stepped magnetically per character and line.
