## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** A visible, foldable typebar machine needs a platen shift to three heights (lower, upper case, figures) from shift keys at one side, with positive stops for each position and locks.

**Solution:** Two shift levers on one side raise the platen support, and a pivoted stop arm with two adjustable screws of different lengths limits the lift, the arm being swung by the figures lever to change which screw is in the path.

**How it works:** The platen support slides vertically in corner posts and is lifted by a cross rod linked to the upper-case lever. The figures lever is attached to it by a slot-and-screw so the first lever can move a distance without it. When the figures lever is pressed, its cam end pushes a pivoted arm inward so a shorter adjustable screw enters the path of a bracket hanging from the platen support. Otherwise the longer screw sets the upper-case height. A single bifurcated lock lever, pivoted on the frame, enters a recess under either shift lever to hold the platen shifted; a lip can also lock the upper-case lever out.
