## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** In machines where the exchangeable type cylinder is held by spring pawls in a bushing on the shaft, the bushing adds diameter and moment of inertia, which gives a shock when the cylinder is stopped in the set position.

**Solution:** Cut recesses in the top of the shaft itself, hold the pawls there, and mount the cylinder sleeve directly on the shaft, with no extra bushing.

**How it works:** The shaft top is milled out on two opposite sides, leaving pointed side walls that guide and protect the pawls. Two spring pawls, riveted in the recesses, lie fully inside them. A sleeve with a clamping screw sits directly on the shaft and carries the type cylinder, which a spring inside the sleeve pushes upward. To fit, the cylinder is slid over the sleeve, pressing the pawls in until it passes and they spring out over its top. To remove it, the pawls are squeezed with two fingers into the recesses and the spring pushes the cylinder off.
