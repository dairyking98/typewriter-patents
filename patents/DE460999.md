The centrifugal crane that occurs drives the paint into a cushion made of absorbent fabric, on which the type that hits it is colored. It was believed that the liquid had to be applied to the cushion over the largest possible surface, or that the spout-like tapering of the container would allow the centrifugal effect of the container to come into its own as fully as possible. Experience has, however, shown that even a very little soak is enough to bring about sufficient coloring of the types, and on the contrary, an abundant supply of fluids soon smears the types. However, the lower the demand for a liquid, the smaller and thus lighter the storage container can be and the weaker the resistance that the device brings to the type movement.
