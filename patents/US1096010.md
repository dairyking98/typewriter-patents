## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Conventional carriage escapements with a fixed and a loose dog can skip or miss a letter space when keys are struck rapidly.

**Solution:** An escapement wheel carries a ring of individually movable pins or fingers; a key-driven actuator lifts one tooth past a fixed stop face so the wheel advances exactly one tooth, and each advancing tooth cams the actuator aside if it arrives early, preventing skipping.

**How it works:** The carriage feed rack meshes with a pinion on the escapement-wheel shaft driven by the carriage spring. In the first form the wheel is horizontal, with headed pins that slide up and down through it and rest on a supporting shelf; a fixed stop face blocks the next pin head. Pressing any printing key lowers the universal bar, rocking a lever whose actuator lifts the blocking pin clear of the stop, so the spring pulls the carriage one space until the next pin meets the stop; on release the pin falls by gravity or is cammed down. A clutch (or lifting/lowering of the shaft or abutment) frees the pinion for free carriage return, the pins riding up an inclined cam. The second form uses a vertical wheel with twelve radial keystone-shaped fingers loosely pivoted in a box, one lifted by a key-controlled rocker and pusher past a fixed plate stop, with a cam keeping the teeth in the stop's plane.
