## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Typewriters have many parts; the inventor wants one of few pieces where the same hand motion that presses the type to the paper also spaces the paper roller.

**Solution:** A single straight type-bar carrying all the characters slides lengthwise on a spring-hinged platform, its pointer set in a notch for a character, and pressing the bar down prints and, on return, steps the carriage one space by a dog.

**How it works:** A T-shaped frame carries a guide-bar on which a paper-roller carriage slides. At the rear, a platform pivoted in the frame and held level by a flat spring carries a notched rack with an index of letters, and a straight type-bar with all the characters on its underside. The operator slides the bar by its button until its pointer pin sits in the notch for the wanted character, which puts that type over the roller. Pressing the button bends the spring and pushes the bar down so the type, seen through an opening in a friction-held shield, prints on the paper. As the platform rises, a V-shaped dog strikes a fixed stop and presents an incline to a pin on the carriage, pushing it one letter space to the left; on the downstroke the dog swings out of the way. An absorbent inking roll on the platform inks the bar as it slides over it.
