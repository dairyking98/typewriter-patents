## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** A simple, accurate, cheap type-wheel typewriter is wanted, usable as a children's educational device and for business.

**Solution:** A hand-turned type wheel on a frame that slides along a rack bar prints by a downward movement, and the same up-down stroke of the frame advances it one tooth.

**How it works:** The base itself is the platen, with printed guide lines for aligning the paper, which is held by a pivoted bar. A frame slides on a rack bar and can rock down about it; it carries a type wheel or disk head that is turned to the wanted character and brought down onto the paper. A finger on a slotted lever holds the frame in the rack teeth at rest. When the frame is depressed, a pawl on the lever lifts and advances one tooth, and when it rises the pawl pulls the frame forward one space. For a word space the carrier is turned to a part with no type and depressed, so only the spacing occurs. A bell is also fitted.
