## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typewriters of the day were complicated; the inventor wants a compact, simple instrument with the full character range, worked by a single key that both selects and prints.

**Solution:** A single lever dipped into a stepped plate rotates a type disk (via rack, pinion and one-way ratchet) to the chosen character, and the same stroke trips a spring striker behind the paper and spaces the carriage.

**How it works:** A type disk on a spindle carries three groups of characters (capitals, lower case, figures) around its periphery, each group one third of the circumference. The operating lever carries a rack that turns a pinion; the pinion drives the disk through a ratchet and pawl, and a return spring brings the disk back when the lever rises. The lever tip is entered in one of a staggered series of steps or recesses in a stepped plate (each step labelled with two characters, chosen by sideways hinging of the lever tip), which fixes how far the lever and disk travel. Pressing further moves the plate down, which rocks levers, cocks and then releases a spring striker that flies up and strikes the paper and ribbon against the type at the print point, and also lowers the spacing lever so a dog escapes one rack tooth of the spring-driven paper carriage. Pressing the lever below the last step into a bottom notch carries the disk so far that a stepped cam on the spindle passes a spring detent, which holds the disk so that its angular relation to the driving ratchet advances one tooth and the next character group (case) comes into play; lifting the lever releases the detent and returns the disk to the first group. Ribbon spools with a pawl feed one tooth per stroke, and double spools hold two ribbons.
