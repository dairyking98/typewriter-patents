## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A typewriter with three types per typebar (126 characters, e.g. two or three languages) needs a platen shift to three positions, with locking, in a Remington No. 10 of unchanged outside size.

**Solution:** A single shift-key lever with a V-shaped cam slot and a switch sends a follower on a rock-shaft arm either up or down from a spring-held middle position, so two shift keys give three platen heights, with a separate lock key and release key.

**How it works:** The platen frame rides on a shift rail carried by a rock shaft, and stop screws are spaced further apart so the platen normally rests in a middle position where the middle type of each bar prints. A shift key lever has an inverted V slot at its rear end; a flanged roller follower on a rock-shaft arm normally sits at the apex. A pivoted switch on the lever directs the follower into the forward or rear branch depending on which of two shift keys (on a three-armed lever with a link to the switch) is pressed, lowering or raising the platen to its other two positions. A spring returns it to the middle, and springs balance the weight. A separate lock key lever pushes a nosed lug against a roller on an arm of the rock shaft, passing on either side depending on shift direction, and a hook on a two-armed lever catches under a bracket by friction to hold it until a release key is struck.

**Parts:** platen frame; shift rail; rock shaft; stop screw; typebar; shift key lever; V-shaped cam slot; flanged roller follower; switch lever; three-armed lever; link; return spring; balancing spring; lock key lever; lug; hook; release key

**Numbers:** three types per bar; 42 typebars; 126 types (versus 84 on the ordinary machine); three platen positions

**Improves on:**

- Remington No. 10 two-position case shift: adds a third platen position and a middle rest so three types fit on each bar with no change of external dimensions
