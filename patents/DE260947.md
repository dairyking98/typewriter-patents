## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** On a Yost typebar machine, the shift lever holds the platen raised only while the shift key is pressed, or is locked by a separate lock key; the writer wants a single capital letter followed automatically by lower case.

**Solution:** A latch on the shift lever can be switched on or off by a hand adjustment lever; when on, it locks the shift lever down on the first shift and is automatically released by the next letter key or space key via the universal rail.

**How it works:** The paper roller frame hangs on parallel levers and is raised and lowered by a shift rail, rods and the shift lever, with springs carrying the weight of the roller. A U-shaped bolt on the shift lever has a hook that can engage under a fixed angle-iron rail. A spring from a resilient adjustment lever pulls an angle lever on the bolt, so when the adjustment lever is in the working position the hook engages at the bottom of the shift stroke and a nose slips under the universal rail. The next letter key or the space key presses the universal rail down, which rotates the angle lever, frees the nose and unhooks the bolt so the roller falls back to lower case. With the adjustment lever in the other position the spring is slack and the shift acts only while pressed; a separate lock lever with its own key still gives a permanent shift lock.
