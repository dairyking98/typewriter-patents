## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** When the Japanese typewriter's feed pawl must move only a very small amount, it already acts when the printing bar or character-spacing bar is barely touched, so accurate printing can be lost.

**Solution:** Make the curved lever that lifts the feed pawl in two pivoted pieces whose angle is adjustable by a screw, so the pawl acts only at the end of the bar stroke.

**How it works:** The curved lever has an upper piece (1) with a fork holding the paper-cylinder frame and a lower piece (2), both pivoted on shaft (3); spring (6) between pins (4, 5) pulls them together against an adjusting screw (8) bearing on tongue (7). Turning the screw changes the angle between the pieces. Depressing the character-spacing bar lifts the lever through a roller on the bar's end, swinging the paper-cylinder frame up so the push feed pawl rides over a gear tooth; on release a spring returns it and the pawl turns the paper cylinder. Narrowing the angle increases the gap between lower piece and roller, so the bar travels some way before moving the lever; because the printing bar always ends at the same point, a small feed is produced only by the last part of the stroke, after printing.

## Description (English translation of the Japanese text, opening)

> Nature and object of the invention. This invention concerns a curved lever for a Japanese typewriter, made of an upper piece and a lower piece that act on the push feed pawl, pivoted together so that the angle the two pieces form can be set freely. Its object is to print accurately even when the movement of the feed pawl is very small. Brief description of the drawings. Fig. 1 is a front view of the lever of this invention; Fig. 2 a plan of the upper piece of the lever; Fig. 3 a plan of its lower piece. Detailed description. In known Japanese typewriters, to change the character spacing, a gear is fitted on one end of the paper cylinder and its feed pawl is made to feed it by any chosen number of teeth, or the gear is exchanged for one of another pitch. In such cases the character-spacing bar that moves the feed pawl always makes a movement of one fixed size together with the printing bar. Various devices have been designed to make the pawl's movement larger or smaller as needed, but none is yet perfect.
