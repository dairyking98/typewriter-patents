**In the patent’s own words:** My invention relates to that class of typewriters in which a rotary type-wheel is rotated by mechanical devices in which electricity is employed as the motive power.

## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Typewriters need to be fast, light to the touch, uniform in impression and quiet; the inventor wants a type-wheel typewriter driven electrically.

**Solution:** One electrical impulse from a key spins the type-wheel through gearing together with a helically striped stop cylinder, the key's pin stops the cylinder at the chosen character, and a second magnet then swings the held wheel against the paper.

**How it works:** Pressing a key closes the circuit of a solenoid that pulls a rack, which turns the type-wheel one full turn through gears and also turns a stop cylinder geared to it. The cylinder carries a helical contact strip dividing it into as many parts as there are characters in a row. The long pin on the pressed key rides on the cylinder until the strip reaches it, which stops the rotation and makes the circuit to the print magnet. That magnet swings the wheel frame against the paper roll, the wheel being inked by a roller, and its leg also releases the spacing dog so the carriage steps one tooth. Two shift magnets push the wheel hub endwise by one or two rows (three rows on the periphery). On release the spring returns the wheel to its home position.
