## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typewriters are costly and complex; a cheap simple machine is wanted.

**Solution:** A swinging type segment is rotated by key-driven levers with inclined cross-pieces and arrested by a key's stop piece, then struck from behind the paper by a hammer.

**How it works:** Keys on either side of the machine press on inclined cross-pieces of two lever sets, which swing a shoulder piece and so rotate the shaft carrying the type segment in one direction or the other. Each key lever carries a stop piece that, when depressed, rises into the path of a stop arm on the shaft and arrests the segment at that key's position. The key also depresses a common cross-bar that swings a hammer lever to drive the paper against the type, and works the carriage feed dog on the rack. A notch in the bar lets the space key move the carriage without striking. Case keys bring different type rows into action.
