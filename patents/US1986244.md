## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A cheap practice or toy typewriter is wanted that teaches the standard keyboard layout, whose keyboard and type carrier can be swapped for other languages, codes, decoding or raised characters for the blind.

**Solution:** A swinging arm with a button is dropped onto a depression in a keyboard-shaped plate, which turns an arcuate type segment to that character, and pressing down tilts the keyboard and platen frame so the paper meets the type.

**How it works:** A type segment with an upper and a lower case row sits on a sleeve that turns on a vertical post. A selector bar slides through a slot in the sleeve, so the operator moves its button over a plate shaped like the four-row universal keyboard, which turns the segment and sets the radial distance. Pressing the button into a depression tilts the keyboard plate and the platen, which are one rocking frame, so the paper is pushed against the chosen type. A tapered nose enters a V slot in an apron on the segment to locate it exactly. On the return stroke a bell-crank pawl advances the platen one ratchet tooth and a spring finger advances the ribbon spool ratchet. Shift keys lift or lower the sleeve to change between the two rows. A blank recess and spacing-bar extension spaces without printing. A second embodiment turns a type drum through a gear and sector, with a telescoped selector arm.
