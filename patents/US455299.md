## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A cheap, easily operated typewriter is wanted that prints both capitals and small letters.

**Solution:** A flat two-row type plate is slid by hand under a pointer over an index plate, and a print lever with a chisel point locks the plate into a tooth space and presses the type down while a cam also steps the frame along one letter.

**How it works:** The index plate and type plate sit in a pivoted frame; the index plate is stepped along guide rods one letter at a time while the type plate on its carrier is moved by the operator's finger-piece and pointer over the index, both along the line and toward or away from the operator on a rock-shaft. The type plate carries two parallel rows of characters, so moving it toward or away selects small or capital letters. Depressing a front lever turns an oscillating bar that pushes a print lever down; its chisel point enters a tooth on the carrier's rear edge to place the character exactly, and the pin presses the type onto the paper over a rod. The same bar's cam moves a toothed rack by one tooth so a detent steps the frame forward, and a second lever advances without printing to make a space. The plate rubs over two rotating ink pads before printing. A bent arm and ratchet step the feed roll for a new line, and a pivoted detent arm releases the rack to return the index plate.
