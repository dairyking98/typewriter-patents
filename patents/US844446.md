## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** In front-strike visible-writing typebar machines with many typebars on one small arc, the types at the sides of the segment overlap and risk interfering with each other.

**Solution:** Typebars are pivoted in radial slots whose spacing widens toward both ends of the segment, so overlapping end types have clearance while the key sublevers stay parallel and equally spaced and pull the bars with straight links.

**How it works:** Key-levers carry upright sublevers that pivot against a fixed fulcrum rod and are linked by straight draw-links to typebars of uniform length. The typebars pivot on a single pivot wire in slots in an open segment, the slots radiating from the printing center and spreading farther apart toward the ends. Each bar carries two types, angled increasingly toward the ends so they overlap in fore-and-aft planes. Pressing a key pulls the typebar up and back to the platen through the link, and springs return key and bar. The segment can be shifted on ball guides for upper and lower case.
