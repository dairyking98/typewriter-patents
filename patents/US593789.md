## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Type-wheel machines of the time used an independent lock for the wheel that wore and let the wheel shift, and were complex, hard to align and heavy.

**Solution:** A compact keyboard machine in which two opposing rack levers constantly meshed with one pinion set the type wheel through gearing and a flier, so that the gearing wedges itself and holds the wheel without a separate lock, followed by a spring-cocked hammer blow, carriage step and ribbon feed all from the same key stroke.

**How it works:** Key levers on arcs of three lengths end in cam faces and are split into two groups, one turning the wheel each way. A depressed key's cam pushes one of two operating levers on oscillating shafts by an amount that depends on the key; its rack bar turns a central pinion, and the other rack bar moves back in mesh. A toothed segment on the pinion drives the elongated pinion on the type-wheel shank, while a loose flier segment turns oppositely until its arm strikes the pressed key lever and stops. Both rack bars still in mesh then wedge the gearing so the wheel cannot move until the key is released. A key hook lifts one of two vertically moving frames, unlocking the pin-and-cam lock on its shaft; the frame's weight returns everything on release. The same frame cocks springs on a force hammer through a yoke, then trips it to strike an impression hammer with a removable rubber or hard head, pressing paper and ribbon against the wheel. It also steps the carriage dog on a ratchet bar and winds the ribbon reel. Capitals and figures are got by two keys lifting the wheel to different heights.
