## Summary

**Kind:** a part or mechanism (any kind of machine) · **Subject (patent classes):** Control for hammer-impression mechanisms for deciding or adjusting hammer-drive energy

**In the patent’s own words:** This invention relates to improvements in that class of typewriters in which type wheels or segments and a hammer coacting therewith are employed for making the impression as distinguished from those machines employing type bars.

**How it works (reading):** A type wheel or segment that is moved in one direction only by deflectors on key levers, with a hammer spring whose energy also drives other parts and a return spring that resists only late in the wheel's travel.

## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Type wheel or segment machines with key-driven position plates had wheels moving both ways for the impression; a one-direction wheel was wanted, and the hammer spring's force after the blow was wasted while extra springs were needed elsewhere.

**Solution:** A type wheel or segment that is moved in one direction only by deflectors on key levers, with a hammer spring whose energy also drives other parts and a return spring that resists only late in the wheel's travel.

**How it works:** Each type key has a lever with a deflector arm. Position plates, set at different angles on a transverse shaft, are struck by the deflectors, which turn the shaft by a different amount for each key. The shaft carries a type wheel or segment bearing characters in vertical rows, and it is pinned so it can slide sideways to bring a different row to the print point. A hammer behind the paper strikes the selected type. A spring cocked by key depression drives the hammer and its leftover force operates other parts, and a return spring positioned to give little resistance early lets the wheel gain momentum so the key feels light.
