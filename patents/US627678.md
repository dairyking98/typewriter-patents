## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** The platen of a Densmore typewriter could not be turned freely by any arc for writing on ruled or printed forms, because the line-space ratchet detent held it at fixed steps; with the detent lifted the platen would spin too freely.

**Solution:** A single lever lifts the platen ratchet detent and at the same time presses a friction shoe on the platen so it stays where it is set.

**How it works:** The detent is a small roller on a spring-loaded pivoted arm that rests in the platen ratchet teeth. A lever pivoted on the detent pivot has a cam-like lower end that rides on the smooth head plate of the platen. Pulling the finger piece forward wedges the cam against the plate and lifts the detent roller clear of the teeth against its leaf spring. The same motion brings a friction brake or shoe to bear on the platen so it holds at any position. Releasing the lever lets the spring drop the detent back into the ratchet.

- **Paper and carriage:** [Roller platen](../features/pap-platen-roller.md), [Line spacing](../features/pap-line-space.md)
- **Frame and body:** [Adjustment and wear take-up](../features/frm-adjustment-wear.md)

**Parts:** platen; ratchet wheel; detent roller; detent arm; leaf spring; release lever; cam end; friction shoe; line-spacing pawl

**Improves on:**

- Usual fixed platen detent: platen can be turned any arc with the detent out and held by friction
