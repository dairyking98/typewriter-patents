## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Typebar machines hid the writing from the operator, and carriage spacing and ribbon feed were irregular and needed separate mechanisms.

**Solution:** Typebars on concentric arcs angled to the platen so the print stays visible, with one swinging frame under the key levers driving a pawl-and-ratchet carriage escapement (with a stop) and the ribbon feed together.

**How it works:** Each key lever is linked to a typebar standing on one of two concentric arcs, angled so the struck line is visible. All key levers rest on a common bar hung from a swinging frame; pressing any key swings the frame, which rocks a bell-crank against a spring. On the spring return, a pawl on the bell-crank advances the carriage rack one tooth, and a pin or set-screw stop stops the rack from moving more than one tooth. The same frame motion drives a pawl and ratchet that winds the ribbon spool a short step. A spacing key works the same way without printing; a deeper-travel key lifts the pawl clear of the rack to let the carriage run freely. A bell is struck by a lug on the carriage near line end.
