## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Typewriters were complex and could not quickly change between capitals, small letters, figures and whole type styles or alphabets without interrupting printing.

**Solution:** One hand lever swings sideways over a notched sector plate to rotate a multi-row type wheel and presses down to print, while a slide shifts the wheel along its shaft to change rows and the wheel can be quickly swapped for another style.

**How it works:** A lever pivoted on a vertical pivot and a horizontal pin swings sideways across a sector plate of notches marked with letters, and a toothed sector on its bearing turns a conical gear and so the type-wheel shaft to the matching angle. Pressing the lever down seats a wedge in the notch, which locks the position, and the plate is pushed down so an abutment on the lever drives the printing hammer up against the paper and type. A slide with a spring stop lever shifts the sleeve and type wheel lengthwise into one of three notched positions to select the row of types, lifting an ink wheel off the type while shifting. Paper runs between two rollers on a frame pulled by a clock spring through a ratchet and escape lever, advancing half a tooth before and half after each print, and a hand-moved cross feed sets the next line. Removing one bearing lets the type wheel be changed for another style, and letters are placed by frequency around a blank central notch used as the space.
