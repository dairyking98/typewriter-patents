## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** In a portable Japanese-script typewriter with many type wheels on one shaft, a wheel whose rotation is stopped suddenly drifts off-centre under its own momentum, and a wheel pushed off-centre for printing returns slowly.

**Solution:** Two or more cross rods carried on spring-loaded levers press outward on the inner circumference of all type wheels to hold them centred on the shaft, and are pulled inward by the existing cords when a wheel is pushed out to print.

**How it works:** Many type wheels with types round the rim are strung side by side on a common shaft between support discs, held by cords and springs from the earlier invention (application No. 11781 of 1934). Three levers (1') are pivoted symmetrically on each outer disc; a cross rod (6') passes through arc grooves (3') in the discs and links the ends of facing levers, and lever springs (8') press the rods outward on the wheels' inner circumference, centring every wheel. The wanted wheel is pushed off-centre by an arc-shaped push-out arm (28), whose two new projections (9') enter the gaps between the types next to the middle one to stop the wheel turning. The outward movement of the connecting cords (7) presses balls (7') on the lever ends, overcomes the springs and draws the cross rods inward so they do not hinder the pushed wheel, while the other wheels are held by cord tension. When the arm drops, the springs snap the levers back and the arc-groove ends stop the rods on a circle of half the wheel inner diameter, re-centring the wheels.

## Description (English translation of the Japanese text, opening)

> Specification. Wheel-aligning device for a Japanese-script printing machine using type wheels. Nature and object of the invention. This invention improves the type-wheel aligning device of the portable Japanese-script typewriter of the original invention, in which many type wheels with types fitted round their circumference are mounted, at their inner circumference, on the circumference of a common [printed 其通, read 共通] shaft by way of spiral springs and discs. Without using the spiral springs [using both is not excluded], two or more common cross rods are made to touch the inner circumference of the many type wheels from inside. Springs press them outward against each other, holding every wheel on the centre of the shaft; when needed the rods are moved inward together, so that they do not hinder some type wheels from going off-centre for printing. The object is to stop the wheels from going off-centre under their own momentum when their rotation is stopped suddenly, and to make a wheel that was put off-centre for printing return quickly.
