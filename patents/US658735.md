## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Margin stops must limit carriage travel at both ends, lock the key levers at the line end, and allow either margin to be written upon when needed.

**Solution:** A stop arm adjustable by notches on the spring-barrel disk locks the key levers at the right limit, with a left margin stop and a release.

**How it works:** On an Oliver-type machine, the carriage-spring barrel has a notched top disk, with notches matching the carriage scale divisions. A pivoted stop arm with a tooth engages any notch under spring pressure, so its position sets the margin. At the chosen point in barrel rotation the arm trips a bell-crank locking lever acting on a locking bar under the key levers, so they lock. A release lets the right margin be written upon, and a left margin stop is also described. The remainder is not in the text.

**Features:** [ESC:margin-stop](../features/esc-margin-stop.md), [ESC:spring-drum](../features/esc-spring-drum.md), [ESC:carriage-release](../features/esc-carriage-release.md), [PAP:line-indicator](../features/pap-line-indicator.md), [ELEM:typebar](../features/elem-typebar.md), [SEL:keyboard-full](../features/sel-keyboard-full.md)

**Parts:** spring barrel; notched disk; stop arm; tooth; locking bar; bell-crank locking lever; key lever; carriage scale
