## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** The operator of a Caligraph has no signal that the bottom of the page or folio has been reached.

**Solution:** A pinion on the paper-cylinder ratchet turns a geared shaft with a dial hand that, on the last line, lets a bell hammer fall and sound an alarm.

**How it works:** A pinion engages the platen ratchet wheel and drives, through bevel gears, a shaft that carries a pivoted bar which raises the bell hammer lever as it turns. After about thirty-two lines the bar passes the hammer arm and the hammer falls on the bell. A dial plate and hand show lines written; a lever disconnects the gearing and a finger-piece resets the hand for fewer than the standard lines.

**Features:**

- **Spacing and escapement:** [Line-end bell](../features/esc-bell.md)
- **Paper and carriage:** [Line and point indicator](../features/pap-line-indicator.md), [Line spacing](../features/pap-line-space.md)
- **Uses:** [Attachment or accessory](../features/use-accessory.md)

**Parts:** pinion; bevel gears; shaft; bell hammer; bell; dial plate; indicating hand; disconnect lever

**Numbers:** 32 lines standard page; alarm at about 20 lines alternative

**Improves on:**

- US293000 style line-end alarms (Tinsley 1884, Belden 1884): the alarm is driven by paper feed to mark end of page
