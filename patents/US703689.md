## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Cheap simple index machines make the operator look for the letter, check the position, and then print with the other hand, so they are slow, cannot be used in the dark or by the blind or one-handed, and rely only on sight.

**Solution:** A finger guide attached to a sliding type bar is moved over a lettered indicator plate by feel, and when the finger is over the character, pressing the plate itself impresses that type and spaces the paper.

**How it works:** Type are on elastic arms of one or two thin sheet-metal type bars that slide on fixed rods under the keyboard plates. The operator puts a finger in a finger guide fixed to the bar and slides it over a hinged plate marked with the characters in sunk depressions, so the bar carries the matching type over a slot in the ink strip. Pressing the plate pushes an actuating arm that bends the chosen type arm through the slot, through the ink strip and onto the paper on the roller, while the other arms stay in contact with the ink strip and are re-inked. The plate also depresses a spring-supported space bar between the plates; its return rocks a lever whose pawl advances the ratchet on the paper carriage one space. The paper roller sits at the front below the wrists, with a knee-operated lever and pawl for line feed, and the stamped-metal frame clamps to a table by springs or is held in one hand.
