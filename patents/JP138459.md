**How it works (reading):** Hang the key links on springs from a cross-bar that a handle-moved slide raises or lowers through slanted slots, changing spring tension.

## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Typists differ in the key touch they want, yet most Western typewriters have no touch adjustment.

**Solution:** Hang the key links on springs from a cross-bar that a handle-moved slide raises or lowers through slanted slots, changing spring tension.

**How it works:** Each typebar is joined to its key lever by a link, and each link is hung by a spring from the lower edge of a cross-bar. The cross-bar's arms pivot on the frame and are pulled by springs so the bar tends to rise. A slide with studs at both ends engages slanted slots in the cross-bar, and a handle sticking out through a slot in the cover moves it. Moving the handle raises the cross-bar step by step, stretching the key springs for a heavier touch; lowering it gives a light touch.

## Description (English translation of the Japanese text, opening)

> Nature and object of the invention. In a Western-script typewriter, the links that connect the key levers to the typebars are connected by springs to a cross-bar; slanted slots in the cross-bar are engaged by the studs of a slide; moving the slide by a handle raises or lowers the cross-bar and so increases or reduces the strength of the springs. The object is a mechanism by which the touch of the keys can be set at will by a simple operation. Brief description of the drawings. Fig. 1: part-sectioned front view of the main parts. Fig. 2: the same front view with the touch made heavier. Figs. 3 and 4: side views of Figs. 1 and 2. Detailed description. The invention is a mechanism for adjusting the so-called touch of a Western typewriter, that is, the resistance felt by the fingertip in striking.
