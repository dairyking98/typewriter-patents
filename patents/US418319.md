## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** The existing shift devices for platen-shifting typebar machines (as in US202923) are inconvenient, not positive enough and slow to operate.

**Solution:** Add a lever on the shift rock-shaft with an L-shaped key at each side so the operator can shift the platen forward or back directly, with a finger-piece for machines normally set to upper case.

**How it works:** An elbow lever is screwed to the existing elbow lever on the rock-shaft that carries the platen-frame rod, with its bearing point on the front or rear of the lever for the right or left side. Its lower end pivots (through a slot, to avoid binding) to an L-shaped key placed level with the bottom key row. Pressing the key rocks the shaft and swings the rod back, sliding the platen frame to the upper-case position; a spring returns it on release. The spring and bracket can instead be set to hold the platen normally at upper case, in which case pressing the finger-piece on the lever shifts to lower case. Keys can be on one or both sides of the machine.
