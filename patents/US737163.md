## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** At high speed, a type key struck before the case shift completes gives misaligned, blurred or mixed-case impressions, and type carriers can clash.

**Solution:** Blocking fingers prevent the type carriers from operating while the case-shift is moving, combined with a line-lock that locks them at line end.

**How it works:** Shown on a No. 6 Remington. The platen frame rides on a shift rod moved transversely by shift arms on a rock-shaft to change case, limited by stop pins. A dog-rocker frame and blocking fingers on arms hold the type carriers out of operation during shifting. The same blocking system, working with the universal bar and key levers, acts as line-lock at end of line.

**Features:** [SHIFT:platen](../features/shift-platen.md), [ESC:margin-stop](../features/esc-margin-stop.md), [ELEM:typebar](../features/elem-typebar.md)

**Parts:** platen frame; shift rod; shift arm; rock-shaft; blocking finger; dog-rocker frame; universal bar; key lever; stop pin

**Improves on:**

- Remington No. 6 case shifting: keys are locked out during shift so impressions cannot misalign
