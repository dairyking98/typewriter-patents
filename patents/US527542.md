## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Hinged key levers in cash registers, musical instruments and typewriters are costly to make and their motion becomes inaccurate as the pivots wear.

**Solution:** Hinge each key by a spherical end seated in a socket in a cross bar, held there by a spring that pulls the key endwise so the ball keeps contact as it wears.

**How it works:** Each key lever ends in a spherical (or, in a variant, cylindrically rounded) tip that sits in a socket in a stationary cross bar. A contracting coil spring attached to the key pulls it rearward and upward, holding the ball in the socket and the key against an overhead stop. The socket touches the ball on all radial lines, so the key can only rotate about the ball's centre. When ball or socket wears, the spring draws the key further into the socket and contact is kept. Sockets may pass through the bar if smaller than the ball.
