## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Existing keyboard typewriters were costly and bulky and often hid the writing. The inventor wants a simple, light, cheap machine, keyed like the big ones, that writes on horizontal paper in plain sight.

**Solution:** A Crandall-style type cylinder held above the paper is turned and slid to the right character by cam-slotted key levers, then pulled down onto the ribbon and paper.

**How it works:** Each key lever has a cam-shaped slot that bears on a pin on an arm of a long rock-shaft; the slot shape sets the shaft angle absolutely, whatever the previous key left. Segmental gears turn the shaft angle into rotation of a pinion keyed (with a feather, so it can slide) on the type-cylinder shaft. Two key bars (for the lower and upper key rows) also work cam-slotted bell-cranks that slide the cylinder lengthwise, choosing one of its rows, and two shift keys slide it further for capitals and for figures and punctuation. Lifting a key bar then depresses a spring lever that pulls the cylinder down onto the ribbon and platen, and as it goes down a toothed bar engages the cylinder pinion to lock the cylinder while it strikes. The carriage runs on a rail and is spaced by a rack, pinion and ratchet with a spring-lever dog that the space key lifts and releases one tooth.
