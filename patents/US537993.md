## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typewriters lost time returning the carriage after every line; Perkins wanted continuous forward paper movement, whole-word printing from one key, and easy correction.

**Solution:** A key-oscillated type wheel prints on a spring-driven paper cylinder that turns and slides on its own through a double-toothed escapement, so lines and line spacing advance automatically with no carriage return.

**How it works:** Keys on the right or left half depress one of two rods that rock a driving shaft by an amount set by each key's lever lug; a large segment gear turns a pinion on the type wheel shaft, so a small rock gives a large wheel rotation, and a trigger arrangement lets the shaft rock either way. The key then presses a bar that tips the shaft frame so the type wheel edge strikes the ribbon against the paper, and the wheel carries capitals and small letters side by side, shifted by sliding the wheel frame forward with a button. The paper roll is turned by a spring through gears and is held by a double-toothed escapement wheel that a key pawl releases one tooth per stroke; a push bar changes the pawl throw to vary spacing. A gap in the roll's gear carries the paper from line end to next line start, and a second escapement and rack slide the roll sideways for line spacing. Springs with loops centre the type wheel shaft to damp its momentum. Word-printing keys drive a tappet shaft, like a music box, which presses the key levers in sequence, and can print the whole word or part of it. Sheet-clamping rods lie in a groove of the roll.
