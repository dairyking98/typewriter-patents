## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Keyless pointer-set typewriters with a type star needed a simpler mechanism and smoother action for driving both the type lever and the carriage stepping.

**Solution:** A single hand-swung frame drives, through an adjustable angle lever and a tilting beam, the strike of the selected type lever and the carriage advance together.

**How it works:** A star of 27 flat type levers lies on edge in radial slots of a disc on a sleeve, each hung from its own coil spring and resting on a damped disc; a marked disc shows the stop position. A pointer moved over a scale and guided by a notch bar turns the whole star through a linkage, a fork with a round rack and a gear on the star axis until the wanted lever is at the apex. Swinging a handled frame pulls tie rods that tilt a profile beam, which pulls down a finger; the finger presses the nose of the apex lever so it pivots and prints against the roller. The same tilt moves a pawl that advances a roller carriage on a rack. Each lever and disc is removable so the whole star or single levers can be changed for another font, and a slotted guide ring around the levers stops rattling.
