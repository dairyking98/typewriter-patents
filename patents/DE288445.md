## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Shift mechanisms for typebars carrying three types need exact adjustment and special adjustable stops.

**Solution:** Two toggle levers joined by a link hold the shiftable frame locked in the normal position when both are straight, and either one buckling gives one of the two shifted positions.

**How it works:** Each typebar carries three types and the typebar segment is part of a frame swung on arms on the machine frame. A two-armed lever carries two toggle joints, one extending up and forward, one down and back. A spring around each toggle pin and a nose on one arm stop the joint buckling the wrong way. A shift key on a pivoted switching lever buckles one toggle to reach one shift position, the other toggle buckles for the other, and the unbuckled toggle acts as the support for the lever. In normal position both straight toggles lock the frame without separate stops.
