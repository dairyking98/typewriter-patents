## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** The inventor wants an inexpensive, simple and durable typewriter, with the type carrier positioned by keys without a complicated linkage per character.

**Solution:** A key-per-character bank pushes sliding travelers whose differently angled arms strike a flat position plate on the type-wheel shaft, turning the wheel to a different angle for each key, and a hammer inside the rim then strikes the chosen type out against a movable platen.

**How it works:** Each key is a vertical bar with a cam that, when pressed, pulls a spring-returned traveler forward. A deflecting arm on each traveler has its own inclination and lands on a flat position plate fixed on the horizontal type-wheel shaft, turning the shaft and wheel through a small, different angle per key so the wanted type faces the platen. Further key travel depresses a common bail, rocking a hammer shaft whose toe drives a hammer slide, and the hammer passes through a slot in the wheel rim and pushes the rubber band type (or spring-held individual type) out against the paper. The wheel carries three rows of type on a segment of 90 to 150 degrees; two shift keys slide the wheel and shaft endwise to put another row at the hammer. The same hammer stroke swings a pawl lever that slips the platen pawl back one tooth, and the pawl carrier spring advances the platen carrier on key release; a pawl lifter yoke frees it for hand return, and a spacing key strikes the hammer at a blank in the type band.
