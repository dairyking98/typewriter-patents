## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** Carriage switching on a typebar machine happens at different points of the type lever stroke and the key stroke is hard at its end.

**Solution:** A resilient tongue in the universal-rail linkage triggers the carriage step about a centimetre before the type hits the platen and then yields, letting the type lever finish.

**How it works:** The universal rail, driven by an intermediate lever or by the type lever, carries a spring-held tongue lever pressing on a crosspiece that withdraws the escapement pawls from the ratchet wheel. Once the pawls reach a rigid stop, the tongue swings and tensions its spring so the rail and type lever continue, giving an elastic end to the keystroke.

**Features:** [Pawl-and-rack escapement](../features/esc-pawl-rack.md), [Prints and spaces in one stroke](../features/esc-print-and-space.md), [Typebars](../features/elem-typebar.md)

**Parts:** universal rail; tongue lever; spring; escapement pawl; ratchet wheel; type lever; intermediate lever; eccentric stop

**Numbers:** about 1 cm before the platen
