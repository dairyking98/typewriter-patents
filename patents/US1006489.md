## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** The Underwood escapement has many working parts and must serve both fast and slow typists.

**Solution:** Two fixed dogs on an independently mounted dog rocker are knocked by a hammer blow from the universal-bar frame to release the carriage rack just before the type reaches the platen.

**How it works:** A typebar heel strikes a universal bar on a reciprocating frame whose enlargement acts as a hammer on the upper end of the dog rocker. The rocker carries two fixed dogs separated by an angular channel for a rack tooth, beveled at about 45 degrees. The blow whips the holding dog out of the rack, releasing the carriage so printing occurs before it moves perceptibly. On the return stroke the back of the engaging dog can slide down a tooth if the carriage is slow; an optional spring returns the rocker.

**Features:**

- **Spacing and escapement:** [Pawl-and-rack escapement](../features/esc-pawl-rack.md), [Prints and spaces in one stroke](../features/esc-print-and-space.md)
- **Frame and body:** [Few parts, low cost](../features/frm-few-parts.md)

**Parts:** typebar; universal bar; reciprocating frame; hammer; dog rocker; fixed dog; rack tooth; return spring; rock shaft

**Numbers:** bevel angle about 45 degrees

**Improves on:**
