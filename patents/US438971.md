## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** The inventor wants a simple, cheap, one-hand typewriter that is quiet, lets the operator see the printed characters at once, and offers upper and lower case plus special characters.

**Solution:** A horizontal rubber-rimmed type wheel on a fixed post is swung by a lever with a pointer over a half-circle index plate, then the same lever lifts it against a locating pin and drives a plunger that presses the type through to the platen, and the same stroke spaces the carriage.

**How it works:** The type wheel sits loose on a vertical post, resting low on a spring-loaded collar. The operator swings the type-wheel lever sideways, which turns an arm and wheel together so a pointer moves over a fixed index plate to the chosen character. Depressing the lever lifts the wheel until a pin in the post head enters the countersunk hole behind that character, locking it in line with a plunger. Further travel compresses the spring and, through a yoke, link and toggle bars, pushes the plunger forward so the slit rubber type is pressed flat against the paper on the platen roller. On release the plunger retracts first, then the yoke lowers a pawl one tooth along the carriage rack and a latch holds the carriage during impression, and the wheel drops by gravity so the last letter is visible. A second finger lever disengages from one of two opposite notches to let the wheel be turned half a turn, switching between the lower-case half and the capital half, and inking rollers on a guard plate ink the type as the wheel turns.
