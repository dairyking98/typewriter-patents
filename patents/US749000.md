## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Inventor wants a continuously turning type wheel to give a clean, unsmeared impression, which requires a chosen type to be momentarily at rest at the print point; applicable to typewriters and printing telegraphs.

**Solution:** A type wheel kept continuously revolving by a geared drive is bodily moved sideways at the moment of impression by an amount that cancels the surface motion of the chosen type, so that type is momentarily stationary for the hammer to strike.

**How it works:** The type-wheel shaft sits in a rocking frame and is turned continuously by a belt-driven pulley, pinion and gear. A drum on the shaft has pins in a spiral, one per type in a row, and a key lever for each type sits under its pin. Depressing a key puts its arm in the path of its pin; when the pin hits the arm, the shaft is pushed up, which rocks the frame and lets the pin slide off. This translation of the axis opposite to the rim motion holds the type at the print point momentarily at rest, and the same rocking frame, through a rod and lever, trips a hammer that strikes that type. The wheel has two rows of type and slides along its shaft (keyed to it) by a separate key lever and spring to bring either row to the hammer.
