## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A Japanese typewriter that can type both vertically and horizontally needs a line space of exact, adjustable size in either direction, but the line-space motion differs (cylinder sideways for vertical lines, cylinder turn for horizontal lines).

**Solution:** A handle-set switching device routes the print-key and spacing-lever motion to either the cylinder rotation (vertical-feed) mechanism or the carriage sideways (horizontal-feed) mechanism, while one line-space key does the opposite motion in each mode.

**How it works:** A frame carrying the paper cylinder is pulled sideways by a cord and take-up, its travel metered by a rack and a horizontal-feed mechanism built from a sector gear, a brake plate and an auxiliary brake plate, which release a spring-driven sector gear a set amount each stroke (stepped adjusting rod sets the character pitch). A similar vertical-feed mechanism turns the cylinder through gears by a sector gear whose swing is limited by pins of different length. A three-position handle (vertical / off / horizontal) moves a push rod so that print-key motion either swings the movable frame of the vertical-feed mechanism or tips the horizontal-feed brake plate via a rocker. The line-space key carries a free plate with two lugs; depending on the handle, pressing the key either lifts a toothed L-plate into the rack to slide the frame sideways (vertical typing, with an overrunning ratchet so the horizontal escapement is unaffected) or pushes up the movable frame to turn the cylinder (horizontal typing). The distance is set by steps on the character-spacing adjusting rod.
