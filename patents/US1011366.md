## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Machines needed a cheap ribbon shift, fitted without altering the machine, to print in either colour or use the whole width of a ribbon; paper ends and envelope flaps also smudged on the ribbon.

**Solution:** A stamped sheet-metal frame carrying a hand-slid ribbon guide and a spring-loaded pointer is screwed to the platen-shift frame.

**How it works:** A sheet-metal frame screws to the frame that shifts the platen for upper case, so it moves with the platen. Its sides join at one end to form a pointer that reads a scale on the carriage. A sliding ribbon guide with lips and a handle rides on two arms of the frame, and the operator moves it crosswise to bring the black or red half of the ribbon, or an unused part, under the printing point. Lugs in notches limit its travel. A second pivoted pointer on a coil spring follows the platen when it is lifted to inspect the writing and marks the next letter position. It also keeps the start and tail of a sheet or an envelope flap off the ribbon.
