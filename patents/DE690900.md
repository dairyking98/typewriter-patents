## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Power-driven machines needed three buttons (single shift, held shift, release) to keep the platen raised for capitals, unlike hand machines where one key held down keeps the shift; another power shift lacked automatic release after each stroke.

**Solution:** A motion link between the shift key and the lock of the raised position holds the release linkage out of reach of the release drive as long as the key is depressed, while a brief tap lets the platen drop automatically after the next character.

**How it works:** The shift key lever turns the platen frame to a raised position against a tension spring. A power shaft carries single-turn clutch discs, whose outer ones have a spiral lifting cam, triggered only by the shift key; the inner ones drive the type lever linkage and a rocker that normally releases the lock after each character. The key lever carries an extension and a pawl; a rigid arm on the key, hinged to the free end of the release linkage, swings the linkage out of the rocker's range when the key is pressed down and back into the ready-to-trigger position when released. So holding the key keeps the platen up unaffected by release, while a quick tap is released automatically after the next stroke.
