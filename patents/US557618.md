## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** In a typebar machine whose platen carriage is shifted for upper case, jars or heavy key pressure while the types touch the platen can move the carriage and misalign the writing.

**Solution:** A spring-returned platen-carriage frame is held at its normal position by a hinged locking pawl that the shift key lifts automatically just before it draws the frame forward.

**How it works:** The platen carriage rides on rails D that sit on cross-rails E, so the whole frame can slide front-to-back at right angles to the platen axis, with a spring pulling it to its rear limit. A shift key on a rock-shaft carries an arm that presses against a cam face on a hinged locking pawl, lifting the pawl's shoulder out of the way of a stirrup on the frame. Continued key travel brings another arm against a drop arm on the frame and draws it forward until the stirrup meets a second shoulder that limits travel. Releasing the key lets the springs return both frame and key, and the pawl drops back to lock the frame. The unlock is coupled to the shift key so no extra operation is needed.

**Features:** [SHIFT:platen](../features/shift-platen.md), [SHIFT:lock](../features/shift-lock.md), [ELEM:typebar](../features/elem-typebar.md), [ESC:carriage-release](../features/esc-carriage-release.md), [FRM:adjustment-wear](../features/frm-adjustment-wear.md)

**Parts:** platen carriage; carriage-supporting frame; rail; cross-rail; stirrup; locking pawl; shift key; rock-shaft; return spring
