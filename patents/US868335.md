## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** On a Hammond-type shuttle machine the line being typed is hidden at the print point; earlier visible-writing devices took their power from the keys or from the hammer blow.

**Solution:** A plate in front of the paper presses the sheet back away from the shuttle so the last-printed text can be seen, driven by the hammer's own return to rest so no key or blow energy is used.

**How it works:** When a key is depressed the hammer is released and strikes the paper against the type presented by the shuttle. A pivoted lever with a roller sits in the path of an arm on the hammer; when the hammer returns to rest it rocks the lever, which slides a hinged plate forward to press the paper back, exposing the line just written. Releasing the hammer lets a spiral spring on the lever withdraw the plate to the ribbon shield so the type can strike. The two prongs of the plate rest just below the writing line, helping to re-position the sheet for corrections.
