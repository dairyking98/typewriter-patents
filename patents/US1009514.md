## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Back-space devices must set the rack back exactly one tooth per key depression without the rack jumping out of the escape pinion.

**Solution:** A key-driven lever with a tooth guided by a cam slot enters the rack and pushes it back one tooth, with a leather-faced hold-down arm preventing rack lift.

**How it works:** The back space key lever acts through an adjustable link and bell crank on a horizontal lever whose forked end has an upward-pointing tooth. A pin on the lever rides a cam way in a plate so the tooth enters a gap in the rack and then thrusts the rack back one tooth. A spring-drawn arm faced with leather bears on the rack top during this stress to prevent lifting, and is clear of it in normal operation. Springs restore everything on release.

**Features:**

- **Spacing and escapement:** [Backspace](../features/esc-backspace.md), [Pawl-and-rack escapement](../features/esc-pawl-rack.md)
- **Frame and body:** [Adjustment and wear take-up](../features/frm-adjustment-wear.md)

**Parts:** back space key lever; link; turnbuckle; bell crank; lever with tooth; cam plate; pin; hold-down arm; feed rack; coiled spring

**Numbers:** one tooth per depression
