## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** The earlier continuously rotating Japanese typewriter (JP79071) was bulky and tall, needed one printing lever per type row, and moving the whole wheel or belt for an auxiliary key was awkward.

**Solution:** A horizontal, constantly motor-turned disc holds radially sliding type cases; a key energises magnets that raise guide plates which push only the wanted case inward, and that case picks up a normally idle printing starter which lifts one type and strikes it against the paper cylinder.

**How it works:** A motor and worm turn a horizontal disc at constant speed; its radial slots hold type cases, held outward by a ring and centrifugal force. Each case has two projections whose position differs per case; a key press closes a column circuit and a row circuit, energising two magnets that each raise one guide plate, so only the case whose projections match both is pushed inward. The pushed case presses a receiver that frees a normally stationary rotating arm, carrying the printing lever and a type-lifting lever with two prongs that enter grooves of the type in the case. An auxiliary key swings a guide which leads a third projection into one of several concentric circular guides, sliding the case so a chosen one of its five types is under the prongs. A cam raises the lifting lever and a spring plunger drops to strike it, printing the type on the paper cylinder, after which the type is returned to its case and an ink roller inks the faces.

## Description (English translation of the Japanese text, opening)

> Specification. Japanese-script typewriter. Nature and object of the invention. This invention improves the invention of the Japanese-script typewriter of Patent No. 79071. Radial slots are cut from the centre in a disc that is turned horizontally, always in the same direction and at a constant speed; type cases holding types are fitted in them so that they can move in and out radially along the slots. Pressing one of the many printing keys on the front of the machine closes an electric circuit and energises an electromagnet, which raises one each of two sets of guide plates mounted under the horizontally turning disc; these act on projections under the corresponding type case and push the wanted case out of its row. The case then takes along with it, in its rotation, a printing starter which is normally at rest in one place, and as it turns, one type corresponding to the key is pulled up out of the case and printed against the paper cylinder.
