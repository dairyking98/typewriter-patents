## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** On a Japanese machine the paper cylinder could only be moved back a set step at a time by a back-space handle, or by freeing it entirely, so it was awkward to return to an arbitrary position and then carry on with regular spacing.

**Solution:** An intermediate gear train with a one-way fine ratchet and pawl follows the step-feed drive in one direction only, so the cylinder can be turned back freely by any amount at once, and a knob can disengage it for free turning both ways.

**How it works:** The cylinder's handle shaft meshes through a long gear to the line-feed device, whose racks step the main feed gear at each impression. A loose gear body sits on the sleeve of the feed gears, carrying a gear, flanges, a fine feed ratchet and a cam ring. A pawl on the secondary feed gear lets the ratchet turn freely in the back direction but locks it in the forward direction, so forward movement comes only from the feed stepping. Pulling out a knob shifts the long gear and the gear body, and the cam ring lifts the pawl clear, so the cylinder turns freely both ways. Pushing the knob back re-engages the fine ratchet almost exactly where the cylinder stands, and stepping resumes. The same idea is said to apply to the horizontal feed.
