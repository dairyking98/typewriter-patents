## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A key-operated type-wheel machine where all keys move the same distance but must still turn the wheel to different angles, and where the impact should not depend on the finger force.

**Solution:** Equal-stroke keys press stepped finger plates at different distances so racks turn the wheel pinion by differing amounts, while the same stroke cocks and then trips a spring striker that knocks the swaying wheel shaft onto the platen.

**How it works:** A horizontal type wheel sits on a vertical rod standing on a needle pivot, free to sway toward the platen. Each key, over a full equal stroke, pushes a terraced finger on a rock plate; the finger's distance from the plate's fulcrum, and its set gap from the key, make each key rock the plate a different amount. Two plates (left and right halves of the keyboard) drive two racks that turn a pinion on the rod in opposite directions from a centre position, and a detent wheel with notches aligns the type. At the end of the stroke the key rocks a lever that bows a flat spring and then releases a latch (pallet), so a slidable striker is thrown against the rod and sends the wheel to the platen. Springs return the plates and wheel.
