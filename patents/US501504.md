## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Hammond's earlier type shuttles had to be hollowed out to be light, which weakened them against the impression hammer and allowed a slight rebound that could cause split letters.

**Solution:** A light curved hard-rubber type shuttle slides on a rigid steel anvil segment that backs it against the hammer, moved by a separate shuttle arm and stopped by a key-raised stop pin, with the anvil raisable to bring other rows of type to the print line.

**How it works:** A hard-rubber strip carrying the characters is clipped to the circumference of a fixed circular anvil segment on a vertical shaft, touching it only at its ends to cut friction. A combined shuttle and stop arm, pivoted on the same shaft, has a finger engaging bosses on the shuttle. Pressing a key lever raises one stop pin and swings a driving arm and lever that rotate the shuttle arm left or right from its normal point until its stop arm strikes the raised pin, which places the chosen character opposite the hammer. The hammer then strikes the paper against the type backed by the anvil; release springs return everything. Levers can lift the anvil shaft, which has a key-and-groove preventing rotation, to bring a second or third row of characters into line, and a split two-part anvil or a metal-ribbed shuttle allow the shuttle to be changed quickly.
