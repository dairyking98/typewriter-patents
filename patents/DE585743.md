## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** In an escapement with two offset toothed rings and a locking pin, the pin rubs along the parallel back flanks of the holding teeth as it returns, braking the pin and slowing the return of the switching bridge.

**Solution:** Give the back flanks of the holding teeth a much smaller angle to the pin's plane of movement than the front flanks of the shift teeth, so the pin snaps back quickly.

**How it works:** An escape wheel pulled by the carriage mainspring carries two offset tooth rings with a groove between them guiding a locking pin. In rest the pin sits against the front flank of a holding tooth. A struck type lever moves the switching bridge, which moves the pin's slide to push it into the gap of the opposite ring. The wheel then turns under the carriage spring, the shift tooth carries the pin over the tip of the holding tooth and it jumps smoothly to the next holding tooth, returning the bridge and type lever suddenly. The shallow back flank, or a pin-shaped tooth, removes the grinding friction.
