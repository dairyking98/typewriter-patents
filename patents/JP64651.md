## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A typebar machine with a three-position (lower, upper, figures) shifting type frame needs a shift that is simple, durable, quiet and cheap, and that locks reliably at the normal position.

**Solution:** A floating shift lever on a cross shaft first turns slightly to cam a lock plate free, then moves the whole type frame up or down from its locked central position, depending on which of two shift keys is pressed.

**How it works:** The type frame (segment) is carried on ball-race guides and normally held in a central position by a pin engaged in a recess of a spring-loaded lock plate. Two shift keys press on rollers at the front and rear arms of floating levers on a cross shaft. The first movement turns the shaft against a spring and a cam on it pushes the lock plate so the pin leaves the recess. Further key travel swings the shaft on its support arms and moves the type frame up or down until the pin meets the upper or lower stop of the lock plate. Releasing the key lets springs and gravity return the frame, where the lock plate snaps back into the recess. A slotted stud and eccentric plate adjust the pin against the recess and stops. Key hold-down latches keep a shift held.

## Description (English translation of the Japanese text, opening)

> Specification. Frame-shifting mechanism in a typewriter. Nature and object of the invention. This invention relates to a frame-shifting mechanism in a typewriter, that is, a so-called double frame-shifting mechanism, in which the type frame and the platen frame make a relative movement in two different directions. Normally the shift mechanism is held in a locked central position. When a shift key is worked, the lock is easily released, and then a movement shifts the frame from that central position in one of two opposite directions. This is done with a floating actuating lever: the shift key first gives the lever a turning movement, which releases the lock, and then the movement that does the shifting. The object is a shift mechanism for a typewriter that is simple, durable and quiet in action, and also easy and cheap to make. Brief description of the drawings.
