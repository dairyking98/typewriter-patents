## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** A Hammond-type machine with oscillating type segments needs an inking ribbon, and its spring-driven hammer blows vary in force when the carriage escapement is differential.

**Solution:** Replace the ribbon with a swinging roller arm that is thrown up across the character at the impression point by the hammer's own motion, and give the hammer a separate spring-barrel and escapement so its blows are uniform.

**How it works:** Finger keys swing the oscillating type segments about a vertical axis until the chosen character is at the impression point. A light pivoted arm carries an inking roller (felt saturated with aniline ink) that normally rests below the segments in front of the paper. When the spring-driven hammer is released, a rod on its rock-shaft drives a bell-crank that swings the arm up, so the roller rolls over the character just before the hammer completes its stroke. Hammer resetting and releasing uses a toothed spring-barrel, sliding rack bar and escapement, made independent of the carriage feed (unlike the Hammond). Each key trips both the carriage escapement and this uniform escapement.
