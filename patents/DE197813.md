## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Setting a type cylinder by a slide plate whose stroke is limited by key pins needed long adjustment paths, crowded keys and many parts, and pressing several keys at once could cause jams.

**Solution:** Two perforated slides, one moving left and one moving right under spring force, sit under the slotted slide plate: the depressed key pin locks one slide, and the other drags the slide plate until the pin hits the end of its slot, so slot length sets the cylinder rotation.

**How it works:** Key buttons on springs carry pins that pass down through a keyboard plate and through slots of differing length in a slide plate. Below it two slides, p and q, are in a central position with holes aligned under the pins, so a pressed pin enters a hole and locks that slide. A spring between the two slides is released when a key lever is pressed; only the unlocked slide is free and moves outward, taking the slide plate with it through a spring pawl coupling. The plate stops when the pin reaches the end of its slot, and a rack on the plate turns a pinion on the cylinder axis by the corresponding amount. Pressing the key lever also tips the whole keyboard frame with the cylinder so that the set type is struck against the paper roller. In a variant the keyboard plate's own downward swing releases the spring instead of a separate key lever.
