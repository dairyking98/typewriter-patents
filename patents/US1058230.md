## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** A tabulating carriage released by a strong spring slams into its stop after a long run, causing shock, noise and wear; fixed cushions or ordinary brakes either fail on short or long runs or make the carriage sluggish.

**Solution:** Mount the frame-side decimal stops on a friction-coupled movable frame that is driven opposite to the carriage by gearing, so the stop meets the carriage early and yields against friction in proportion to the run.

**How it works:** Pressing a tabulator key pushes a decimal stop lever into the path of a column stop on the carriage and trips a toggle lever that engages a spur gear with the carriage rack and disengages the feed dogs. The carriage runs left under its spring drum, driving through a worm and worm wheel and a friction disc clutch the frame carrying the stop levers slowly to the right, a small fraction of an inch per inch of travel. When the carriage reaches the stop it pushes the stop frame back against the disc friction, decelerating gradually with force proportional to the run length. Stop levers sit loosely in links, and light springs hold them in place.

**Features:**

- **Spacing and escapement:** [Tabulator](../features/esc-tabulator.md), [Mainspring-drawn carriage](../features/esc-spring-drum.md), [Pawl-and-rack escapement](../features/esc-pawl-rack.md), [Carriage release](../features/esc-carriage-release.md)

**Parts:** carriage; spring drum; carriage rack; tabulator key; decimal stop lever; column stop; stop frame; guide bar; friction disc; worm wheel; worm; spur gear; toggle lever; tabulator universal bar; feed dog rocker

**Numbers:** ten decimal stops; frame moves 1/16 in. per inch of carriage travel (example); 8 in. example carriage run

**Improves on:**

- spring cushions and brakes for tabulator carriages: retarding force scales automatically with the run and acts only at the end, leaving the carriage free at full speed
