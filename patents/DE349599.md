## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** In a machine that copies by reproduction a typewriter-like script from an embossed sheet-metal cylinder jacket through an inked ribbon, the ribbon also presses against smooth areas and colors the paper there, especially with short embossed line parts such as addresses.

**Solution:** Wrap a metal-foil stencil with cutouts matching the embossing around the ribbon and cylinder so only embossed areas can print.

**How it works:** A roller carries a sheet-metal jacket embossed in typewriter script, held by a wedge bar, pins and latches. The ribbon runs around the jacket and a guide roller, and on its outer side lies an endless inextensible metal foil stencil, whose cutouts coincide exactly with the embossed lines. Ribbon and stencil are joined along their longitudinal edges by sewing, stapling or riveting, and the stencil is wider than the narrower ribbon so it can have a reinforced edge. Pins on the roller or jacket engage edge perforations of the stencil so it always stays in register with the embossing, and toothing could replace the pins. A paper web pressed against the stencil is printed only through the cutouts, so unprinted areas stay clean.
