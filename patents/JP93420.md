## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** In a rotating cylindrical type magazine for a Japanese typewriter the loose types can fly out by centrifugal force or gravity when the magazine is turned quickly, and earlier retaining devices rub and make noise.

**Solution:** Lock each row of notched types with a sliding bar inside the cylinder whose bumps sit in the type notches and whose hollows, slid into line by the print handle's first travel, free only the type to be printed.

**How it works:** A cylinder (7) turning on a sideways-sliding carriage has type holes (9) in rows along its axis; each type (8) has a notch (13) in its shank. Inside the cylinder, one locking bar (10) per row lies parallel to the axis through the notches, with bumps (11) and hollows (12) at the type spacing; at rest a spring (23) keeps the bumps in the notches so no type can leave its hole. Pressing the print handle (4) turns a link (15), a lever (16), a crank rod (17) and a loose lever (18) on the magazine shaft, which pushes a wedge (20) on the carriage, in the upper half of the cylinder, against the end of the bar of the selected row. The bar slides until its hollows line up with the notches, freeing the types of that row so the print lever (5) can take the chosen type out and print it. On release the wedge and bar spring back and the bumps lock the types again.

## Description (English translation of the Japanese text, opening)

> Specification. Round type magazine for a Japanese typewriter. Nature and object of the invention. This invention relates to a round type magazine in which: a cylinder, mounted so as to turn on a carriage that slides freely left and right on the base of a Japanese typewriter, has type holes drilled in it side by side; types with a notch in one side of the shank are set in them; type-locking bars, with bumps and hollows at the same spacing as the types along the cylinder axis, are laid loosely inside the cylinder parallel to the axis, through the notches in the shanks; normally the bumps engage the notches and lock the types; and, linked with the pressing of the printing handle, a locking bar is slid so that its hollows line up with the notches, leaving the printing lever free to take the type out. Its object is to hold the types firmly in the magazine so that they do not fly out when the magazine is turned quickly, to make the magazine turn smoothly and quietly, and to make it simple to build.
