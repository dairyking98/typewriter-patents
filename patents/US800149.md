## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** Embossing Braille on a Hammond needs a uniform impression made with a light stroke so the paper is not punched through.

**Solution:** A small Hammond shuttle bearing only a few single points or point groups, struck against a hammer with only three pits, builds each Braille cell from two successive key strokes.

**How it works:** The shuttle carries seven characters made of single points or groups; the hammer face is pitted with three vertical pits at the printing point. One key brings the left half of the cell to the pits and the hammer strikes, the ordinary letter-spacing moves the paper, and a second key brings the right half. Spaces between words take two space-key strokes. A variant puts the points on the hammer and the pits on the shuttle. The shuttle and hammer face are the interchangeable Hammond parts.

- **Type element:** [Type sector or segment](../features/elem-sector-segment.md), [Interchangeable element](../features/elem-interchangeable.md)

- **Spacing and escapement:** [Space key or bar](../features/esc-space-key.md), [Prints and spaces in one stroke](../features/esc-print-and-space.md)
- **Uses:** [For the blind](../features/use-blind.md)

**Parts:** shuttle; hammer; pit; point; keyboard; space key

**Numbers:** 3 pits on hammer; 7 characters on shuttle; 6-dot Braille cell (2 rows of 3)
