**In the patent’s own words:** My invention relates to typewriters in which a frame carrying the carriage with the platen is raised through a certain distance on depressing a shift-key and through double this distance on depressing another shift-key.

## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** In Wasmuth's earlier shift mechanism, raising the carriage frame the single distance made its nose strike a locking lever, kicking the operator's finger and letting the frame drop early so type printed below the line.

**Solution:** Hinged vertical locking levers on adjustable supports let the frame pass freely for the double lift and block it for the single lift, taking the load in the machine frame.

**How it works:** Two shift-key levers on a common shaft raise the frame carrying the carriage and platen via levers and rollers. The first key raises the frame the double distance, since two hinged locking levers normally stand vertical and pass through holes in the frame bottoms. The second key also lifts the first, and through rods swings the locking levers so their noses lie over the frame bottoms and stop them at the single lift. The supports are adjustable by nuts so alignment of the writing is set.
