## Summary

**Kind:** type-element machine · **Subject (patent classes):** Typewriters or selective printing mechanisms characterised by the mounting, arrangement or disposition of the types or dies with types or dies mounted on carriers rotatable for selection the plane of the type or die face being parallel to the axis of rotation, e.g. with type on the periphery of cylindrical carriers

**In the patent’s own words:** One object of the invention being to minimize the number of constituent parts of a type-Writing machine and reduce the cost of manufacture Without detracting from the efficiency of the machine, but, on the contrary, enhance its capacity, ease of manipulation, and its effectual execution of the functions required of a modern type-writing machine of the index-Wheel type.

**How it works (reading):** The operator pulls a finger-loop cord to bring the type wheel roughly to the letter, then pressing a character key drives a dog into a notched ring on the wheel shaft, which finishes the positioning, locks the wheel, and tilts the whole carrier frame to print.

## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Index-wheel typewriters had too many parts and were costly, and the operator had to position the type wheel exactly by hand; the inventor wants a cheap, visible-writing index-wheel machine with positive locking of the wheel.

**Solution:** The operator pulls a finger-loop cord to bring the type wheel roughly to the letter, then pressing a character key drives a dog into a notched ring on the wheel shaft, which finishes the positioning, locks the wheel, and tilts the whole carrier frame to print.

**How it works:** A three-row type wheel sits on a vertical shaft in a counterbalanced pivoted carrier frame. A grooved wheel at the foot of the shaft is turned by a cord running over pulleys to a leather loop on the operator's index finger; moving the finger to a key rotates the type wheel to about the right place against a convolute return spring. The 30 character keys sit on three key bars of ten. A key bar works a lever and a dog that rises into the V-shaped notches of a ten-toothed ring on the shaft. The three dogs engage the notch at its left slope, centre or right slope, so the ring turns a small extra distance in one direction, none, or the other, to settle on the exact character in its group of three. Continued key pressure tilts the carrier frame and throws the wheel against the paper. The platen frame is lowered by Cap and Fig keys for the second and third type rows. Inking rollers on the frame ink a ribbon that travels with the carriage; a spring drum and ratchet bar space the carriage.
