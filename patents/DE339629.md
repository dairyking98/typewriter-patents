## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typewriters with a cylindrical type carrier needed a way to print where hammer, type and ribbon work together and the written line stays visible.

**Solution:** A hammer strikes the back of resilient lamellar types on a rotating cylindrical carrier, pressing them against the paper and a ribbon, with the key choosing the carrier rotation by a stop pin.

**How it works:** Resilient types on a cylinder-shaped carrier are struck from behind by a hammer, which presses type, letter paper and a ribbon against an anvil so the character prints on the front of the sheet through the ribbon's back. Every key lever raises a common rod that lifts the hammer on an axle; a spring pulls it back. Keys are split left and right: right keys, via a rod, spring and lever, push on a lug of the carrier to turn it one way, left keys the other way. A limiting pin above each key is lifted when its key is pressed and the carrier's stop comes to rest against it, so the carrier stops exactly at the type of that key. The carrier may be a frame with type plate or individual types screwed on for easy exchange, guided by a ring and a slot for the hammer. Anvil and ribbon may be adjusted so the line stays visible; drive could be pneumatic or electric.
