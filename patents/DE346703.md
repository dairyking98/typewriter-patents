## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typebar machines need a full keyboard and finger force; the aim is a machine with few keys, e.g. six, that writes the whole alphabet.

**Solution:** Selector disc valves between a few keys and the typebars route suction from foot-driven bellows to pneumatic bellows that move the typebars.

**How it works:** Type levers are arranged in four sectors A to D, each with its own keyboard and printing point. Two foot-driven bellows rarefy air in a suction chamber. Pressing a key rotates stacked selector discs with bores and outlets in a disc housing so a channel forms to one of many nozzles and hoses. Opening the channel connects a bellows to the suction, which collapses it and pulls the type lever via a rod to the platen. The platen is on the back of the machine and moves up and down. The housing front has an opening for cleaning and viewing the writing.
