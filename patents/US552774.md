## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typewriters write one character at a time; common short words and syllables take many strokes, and machines are complex.

**Solution:** A typebar machine with a second set of bars carrying duplex or multiple characters (an, as, is) and an escapement that advances the carriage by varying distances.

**How it works:** Single-character and multi-character typebars print against a curved ink pad on the machine back, with a pivoted pad frame that swings out for inking. A special escapement permits different carriage feed distances matching the width of the character or syllable on the operated bar. Shift keys with a locking and releasing mechanism move the platen between two positions relative to the type. A ratchet turns the platen, a bell sounds near line end, and the carriage is supported on a lever-actuated frame.
