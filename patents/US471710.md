## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Shifting-platen typebar machines of the Judy type needed a sturdier, simpler way to carry and shift the platen and to let the operator inspect the writing.

**Solution:** The carriage only slides on two parallel rails, and a separate platen carrier hung on rocker arms from a rock-shaft swings back to shift case and can be hinged up to view the work.

**How it works:** The carriage runs on a main rod and a shorter rear rod so it cannot tilt. A rock-shaft in the carriage has upward-forward arms, and the platen carrier is hinged to their tops. Depressing a shift key moves bell cranks that push the carrier rearward about the rock-shaft, so the platen takes the upper-case position at the same height as lower case, then a spring returns it. The carrier can also be flipped up on its hinges to see the writing. A spring-loaded pawl and ratchet turns the platen one or two notches for line spacing, and a spring drum with a two-dog escapement feeds the carriage.
