## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** The line-space mechanism of a front-strike machine needed an adjustable line spacing, a platen that is easy to remove, and a line-feed pawl that stays out of the way.

**Solution:** The carriage return handle also turns the platen: it rocks a shaft that swings a line-space pawl onto the platen ratchet, and a slide-type space regulator sets how far up the ratchet the pawl first engages.

**How it works:** Pulling the carriage-return handle rocks a short shaft whose arm presses a plunger, which rocks a bell-crank lever and swings the pawl back onto the platen ratchet. The pawl turns the ratchet until a stop arm on the pawl meets the ratchet, and a stop pin in a slot limits the handle's travel. A spring holds the pawl normally clear of the ratchet, and a second spring presses it down. The pawl's arm rests on an inclined cam flange on a slide, the space regulator. With the slide forward the pawl drops early and engages a low tooth for wide spacing, and with the slide back it engages a higher tooth for single spacing. A spring detent pin in holes locks the slide in several positions. The platen sits in flanged sleeve bearings held by spring locking plates that slide back so the platen lifts out.
