## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Keyboard typewriters with a type wheel that swings against the roller needed a more regular, faster and safer way to rotate the wheel to the selected character and then swing it to print, without centrifugal overrun of the wheel.

**Solution:** Each key moves a sliding rail by a roller in an inclined groove, the rail turns the type wheel via steel bands and a gear sector, and a stepped stop on the wheel axis halts the rotation and then converts further key travel into the swing of the wheel against the roller.

**How it works:** Pressing a key first lowers a rod that withdraws a latch on its side, releasing a sliding rail that has one guide groove per character, arranged in two symmetrical groups so keys on the right half push it right and keys on the left half push it left. The key's roller in its groove moves the rail sideways; steel bands of adjustable tension connect the rail ends to a disc sector, whose gear sector turns a spherical-zone gear on the type wheel axle, so the engagement persists while the wheel swings. A stepped hollow cylindrical member on the axle, its steps facing the swing axis, meets a stop arm at the step matching the key, ending the rotation. Further key travel, now blocked from rotating the wheel, swings the wheel with its axle against the paper roller through levers on two swing axes and prints. The axle is carried by a curved support on a vertical rod set by two toggle keys to lift the wheel to the capital, figure and sign positions, and a spring returns everything.
