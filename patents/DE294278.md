## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typebar machines print by the typist's keystroke force, giving uneven impressions and loud blows.

**Solution:** Each keystroke sets a swing weight in motion via a double toggle linkage, so the typebar is driven by stored kinetic energy that is converted to pressure near the platen.

**How it works:** The key lever drives, through a roller, a first toggle lever carrying a weight which swings against gravity to store energy only after the key travel begins. A second toggle lever joined to the type bar is nearly extended at the moment of impression, so the speed falls to nearly zero and the weight's inertia becomes pressure. Printing force is adjusted by moving the frame pivot of the second toggle. The universal rail is driven by the weighted toggle so spacing follows the weight action, not the typist. Radial typebars slide between a base plate and head plate, each with three types, with snap-on spherical joints to the links.
