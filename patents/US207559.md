## Reading (full text, OCR poor)

**Problem:** Early Sholes typewriters needed improvements in a lifting paper carriage, platen line spacing, line-end bell, spring-driven carriage return and ribbon feed.

**Solution:** A hinged, sliding paper carriage carrying a ratcheted cylindrical platen is pulled by a spring wheel past a double letter-space ratchet, with an adjustable trip-stop that rings a bell and line-spaces the platen at line end, plus a carriage-driven ribbon feed.

**How it works:** Typebars pivoted in the top plate strike up against the underside of the platen at one point, each worked by a key lever and a wire. The paper carriage hinges on a rail so the platen can be flipped up to view the writing or reach the types and ribbon. A pair of vibrating letter-space ratchets worked by a bar across the key levers holds the carriage still during the impression and releases it a type-space after, pulled by a coiled spring wheel through a cord (or a weight). An adjustable trip-stop on the ratchet bar rings a bell near line end and presses the platen-driving ratchet to turn the platen one line space, the stroke length set by an adjustable guide-rack. A hand lever or treadle with a cone pulley winds the carriage back, and the spring-wheel motion drives the ribbon spools through ratchets, with a rocking frame shifting the ribbon sideways when worn.
