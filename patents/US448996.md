## Summary

**Kind:** type-element machine · **Subject (patent classes):** Typewriters or selective printing mechanisms characterised by the mounting, arrangement or disposition of the types or dies with types or dies mounted on carriers rotatable for selection the plane of the type or die face being parallel to the axis of rotation, e.g. with type on the periphery of cylindrical carriers

**In the patent’s own words:** My invention consists in mechanism whereby stored power may be used to give an oscillatory motion to a type-wheel to bring the desired letter' opposite the platen or roller for serial No.

**How it works (reading):** A mainspring drives an oscillating type wheel that is released from its spindle and braked at the right letter chosen by the key, then the paper roller is rocked forward to take a uniform impression, with variable letter spacing set by the key.

## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Key-driven typewriters give uneven impressions that depend on how hard the keys are struck, and they do not make the spacing or type changes easy. The inventor wants the type, ink and paper movements run by stored power, with the keys only calling it into action.

**Solution:** A mainspring drives an oscillating type wheel that is released from its spindle and braked at the right letter chosen by the key, then the paper roller is rocked forward to take a uniform impression, with variable letter spacing set by the key.

**How it works:** A key lever moves a tapered bar and a rack bar by an amount set by the key's position. This sets a cam pin so that, as the spindle swings round, a latch lets go of the type wheel when the chosen letter is opposite the platen, and a wedge expands a slit ring spring inside the wheel rim so a turret brakes the wheel at once. The key also releases a trip so a spring-loaded drum makes one revolution, swinging an oscillating lever that turns the spindle through a little more than a full turn each way and, by a slotted lever, rocks the paper-roller carriage forward to print. A second bar and finger disengage the carriage feed pawl after a distance that depends on the key, giving narrower or wider spacing per letter. A separate capitals key lifts the wheel, with its rim supported inside by the turret, to bring the capital row into line, and a ribbon on interchangeable bobbins (or ink rollers) inks the wheel.
