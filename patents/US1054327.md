## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** Sudden stopping of the shifted platen frame makes its rear vibrate and the next character prints out of alignment.

**Solution:** Rear rests on the carriage engage the rear corners of the platen frame in upper case at the same time as the front stops.

**How it works:** The platen frame sits on the carriage on front and rear stops in lower case. The shift mechanism lifts it by a shift rail and roll until front shoulders hit fixed stops. Adjustable rests on hinged standards overhang the rear corners of the frame so they are held too, preventing vibration. Adjustable take-up rests seat the rear corners on return, and the standards swing away to remove the frame.

This invention relates 'to the shiftabl platen frames of typewriting machines. In certain front strike writing machines, the platen'is mounted in a platen frame normally seated on the carriage. A shifting mechanism is connected to the platen. A vigorous operation of the shifting mechanism is found sometimes to cause irregularity in the writing, especially during rapid operation of the machine. I have found that this is'due to the fact that the sudden arrest of the platen frame at its front portion by means of the usual stops causes a slight momentary vibration of the rear portion thereof. If a type key is struck during such vibratory movement, the type will not print in perfect alinement.…
