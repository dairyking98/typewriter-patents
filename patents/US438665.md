## Summary

**Kind:** index machine · **Subject (patent classes):** Typewriters or selective printing mechanisms characterised by the mounting, arrangement or disposition of the types or dies with types or dies mounted on carriers rotatable for selection the plane of the type or die face being parallel to the axis of rotation, e.g. with type on the periphery of cylindrical carriers

**In the patent’s own words:** My invention relates to that class of typewriiing machines wherein the type are borne by awheel and this wheel is rotated to the proper point by'the movement of a pointer over an index-plate on which are marked the letters,numerals, and characters used in writing or printing.

**How it works (reading):** Couple the pointer and the type-wheel shaft by a flexible cord wound oppositely on two equal pulleys, so either can drive the other and the pointer self-corrects when the register wheel lines up the type.

## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** In pointer-and-index machines where the pointer rides a screw on the wheel shaft, the pointer cannot be driven back by the shaft, so when the register wheel snaps the type wheel to exact position at impression a part must yield, causing strain and wear if the pointer was set slightly off.

**Solution:** Couple the pointer and the type-wheel shaft by a flexible cord wound oppositely on two equal pulleys, so either can drive the other and the pointer self-corrects when the register wheel lines up the type.

**How it works:** The type wheel and a toothed register wheel (one tooth per type) sit on a shaft in a hinged frame; pressing the frame down engages a notch of the register wheel with a fixed stud, which fixes the wheel exactly and also feeds the paper by a pawl. A pointer slides on the plain shaft, and on a rod below, along a slotted index plate of characters. The pointer is clamped to a catgut or annealed-wire connector whose ends go over guide sheaves to two equal pulleys on the wheel shaft, wound in opposite directions so the cord winds on one as it unwinds from the other. Moving the pointer by hand therefore turns the wheel, and turning a milled knob on the shaft end moves the pointer. If the pointer was set slightly off, the register wheel turns the type wheel to exact register and the pointer follows without strain. One pulley is adjustable by a set screw to take up cord slack, and the pointer can be unclamped to calibrate it against the printed character.
