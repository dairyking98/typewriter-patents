## Summary

**Kind:** a part or mechanism (any kind of machine) · **Subject (patent classes):** Typewriters or selective printing or marking mechanisms characterised by the purpose for which they are constructed for embossing, e.g. for making matrices for stereotypes

**In the patent’s own words:** The object of my invention is to furnish a machine which will manifold as well as any of the type bar machines now in use, and which will have an alignment equal to that of the best wheel machines.

**How it works (reading):** A typebar machine whose bars, each carrying three types, are mounted in long fixed bearings and rotate on their own axes (crank-driven by fingers on the keys) to throw the type at the platen, with the keyboard shifted to select the type.

## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** Typebar machines manifold well but soon fall out of alignment, while wheel machines keep alignment but manifold poorly; a machine is wanted with both, plus simplicity, compactness, low cost and light weight.

**Solution:** A typebar machine whose bars, each carrying three types, are mounted in long fixed bearings and rotate on their own axes (crank-driven by fingers on the keys) to throw the type at the platen, with the keyboard shifted to select the type.

**How it works:** Twenty-six type-bars, 13 on each side, are made each from a single piece of metal carrying three types (small letters, capitals, figures). Their rear portions are parallel to the machine axis and turn in end-plate bearings; each has a crank engaged by a finger on a key, so pressing the key rotates the bar and throws its type to the platen. Upper bars are bent down and outward, lower ones down and inward, with bearings 4 or 6 inches apart. Cams throw the movable part of the base and keyboard backward or forward to shift between the three types. A rock shaft carries pawls that regulate carriage spacing, and the carriage drives the ribbon spools.
