**In Google’s translation of the patent’s own words:** The invention relates to a typewriter in which the individual characters in of different heights on a longitudinally guided and transversely displaceable paper strip are printed, and consists in the fact that the guide roller with the associated guide and feed means for the paper strip arranged on a vertical axis and its height can be adjusted by means of a button lever linkage.

## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** Shorthand with vowels omitted needed a way to mark which vowel belongs to each consonant.

**Solution:** The keyboard prints consonants on a narrow tape in one of five offset lines, with four thumb levers shifting the platen and tape vertically by half a type height to mark the vowel.

**How it works:** A 55-key typebar keyboard prints characters of identical height on a narrow paper strip fed lengthways. Four toggle levers by the thumbs raise or lower the vertically sliding platen, guide and feed rollers so the character falls on one of four offset lines for the vowels a, i, o, u (the base line means e). Two space keys sit at the sides and ink comes from a pad.
