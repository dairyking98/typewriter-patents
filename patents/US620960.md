## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Same aims as Pratt's companion patent: speed, accuracy, uniform touch, manifolding, simplicity and cheapness in a one-carrier machine.

**Solution:** A flexible flat type-plate with horizontal rows of slit type triplets slides to and fro on a guide rod, driven by two driver levers and stop arms, and is struck by a hammer.

**How it works:** Rows of type sit on a rectangular plate of thin metal, celluloid or parchment, in vertical triplets of lower case, capitals and figures, slit apart for clean impressions in manifolding. The plate's curved ends form short cylinders sliding laterally on a guide rod that sits in flexible strips, so the rod and plate can be lifted off instantly for cleaning. A yoke on the plate prolongation engages a pin on an arm fixed to the hub of the shaft with stop and driver arms. Driver levers across the key levers rock the shaft right or left, the amount depending on the key's nearness to the fulcrum, and over half their travel is waste so each can restart from the previous extreme. The plate sits about a quarter inch from the platen and is driven near-contact before the impression, and a reversing mechanism is used.
