## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** A small-keyboard typebar machine needs three cases (lower, upper, figures) from one key per bar, so the platen shift must reach two different heights, hold each, and still fold away.

**Solution:** Two shift levers raise the platen support by different amounts to a single pivoted stop arm with two adjustable screws, which the figure-shift lever swings aside to allow the higher lift, and a hooked lock lever holds either position.

**How it works:** The cap-shift lever and the figure-shift lever both lift a cross rod that raises slides carrying the carriage bed, platen and attached mechanism. A spring-loaded arm at one side has a head with two screws. The cap-shift lift stops against the first screw. Pressing the figure-shift lever cams the arm inward so the second screw is in the path, giving a longer lift. A hooked lock lever with two recesses grips the cross rod to hold either raised position. A spring around the shift rod with an adjusting screw balances the weight of the carriage.
