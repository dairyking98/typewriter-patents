## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** In motor-driven typebar machines a shoe dropped into the camshaft path can be caught by the next cam and print twice, limiting shaft speed.

**Solution:** A swinging shoe on a rocker arm is released by a key-lever pawl, taken by a cam for one stroke and then re-latched even while the key is still held.

**How it works:** Pressing the key lever turns a pawl that frees the swing shoe, which a spring throws into the path of the rotating camshaft cams. The cam carries the shoe and rocker arm, pulling the type lever to the platen by a pull rod. On impact the shoe is re-caught by its pawl and leaves the cam, so a held key cannot repeat. A lever disengages the pawls and raises the key stop, allowing manual typing if the motor fails.
