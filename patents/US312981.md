## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Writing without tapping keys, and a typewriter usable on blank-book pages, which ordinary typewriters cannot handle.

**Solution:** A hand-held type-wheel carriage rides on a frame over the book; a knobbed handle turns a pointer over a lettered dial to choose a type, pressing the handle drives a plunger onto that spring-held type, and a thumb lever advances the carriage by a chain.

**How it works:** The type-wheel carriage runs on slotted bed bars. A vertical shaft with a pointer and a knobbed lever turns the type-wheel; the pointer is read against a matching lettered dial. Types are individual slugs in holes around the wheel margin, each held up by a spiral spring on a stud, with a rectangular head that keeps it square. Pressing the handle forces a spring plunger down on the type under it, through a slot in the carriage floor to the paper. A thumb lever on the handle works a pawl and ratchet that drives an endless chain; a tooth on a sliding block in the carriage engages the chain and so spaces the carriage. A second lever disengages the chain so the carriage can be pushed back to any point. Removable ink pads on the carriage are turned by passing types.
