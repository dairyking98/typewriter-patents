## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typebar machines need the carriage or platen moved to shift case, the ink pad wastes space and wears in one spot, and the paper guide, paper table and platen ratchet are rigid or awkward to use on ruled paper or thick stacks.

**Solution:** A set of independent improvements to a two-character typebar machine: case shift by letting the typebar swing further (limited by a movable abutment) instead of moving the platen, an endless ink pad indexed by the space bar, a resilient paper guide, a self-locking tilting paper table, and a platen ratchet that can be freed.

**How it works:** Each typebar carries two characters and is linked through an arm with a notch. For lower case, an abutment plate limits the arm so the lower character strikes the fixed platen. A rod turned by hand hooks the abutment to the universal bail, so the next keystroke pulls the abutment down, lets the arm travel further and brings the upper character to the platen, with a tooth entering the notch to stop sliding that would blur. An endless ink pad band on rollers sits on the spacer and is stepped one ratchet notch each time the space bar is pressed. A slit spring-steel band guides the paper around about two-thirds of the platen. The paper table is sprung on polygonal journals with adjustable tension and is held up by a gravity pawl dropping into a notched collar. A thumb lever drives the platen ratchet one or two teeth, and a detent with a sliding pin frees the platen to turn either way for ruled paper.
