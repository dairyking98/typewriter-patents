## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** In stylus typewriters with a spherical type carrier the hand both guides the stylus over the index plate and presses it down to print, and the printing force tires the wrist and prevents fast writing.

**Solution:** The stylus sits on the end of a one-armed vertical-plane lever that carries the spherical type carrier at an intermediate point, so the hand force at the free end is only a fraction of the force needed at the type.

**How it works:** A base plate has a spherically hollowed letter field and curved side arms carrying a shaft with a forward bolt. A sleeve-shaped impression lever rotates on the bolt and has a middle extension from which the spherical type carrier hangs by lateral arms. The stylus button is hinged to the free end of the lever, and a forked rod links the button to the type carrier, forming a jointed parallelogram. Moving the stylus over the index plate swings the parallelogram forward and back with the lever and sideways by rotating the parallelogram about the lever's long axis, so the carrier turns to bring the chosen type over the platen. Pressing the stylus against a weak spring then prints. Escapement, carriage and inking are not part of the claim.

**Features:** [Pointer over a chart](../features/sel-pointer-chart.md), [Press the pointer to print](../features/sel-pointer-press-prints.md), [Type sphere](../features/elem-sphere.md), [Element struck to the platen](../features/imp-element-to-platen.md), [Hand presses the type through](../features/imp-press-through.md)

**Improves on:**
