## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Escapements with two coaxial ratchet wheels and a locking pawl need hard-to-make tooth shapes and cannot balance light and heavy keystrokes or be set quickly and exactly.

**Solution:** The locking pawl engages only one ratchet wheel, while a swing-out lever tied to the switching bridge rides the tooth backs of the second wheel and returns the bridge and pawl.

**How it works:** A second ratchet wheel sits on the axis of the shift gear wheel, which the carriage spring turns. A key lever strikes the switching bridge, whose lever moves a switching body so the locking pawl leaves the shift wheel. The wheels turn under the carriage tension. A lever resting on a tooth back of the second wheel is swung by it, and its connecting rod pulls the bridge back to rest. The switch body returns and the pawl catches the next tooth. Adjustable nuts on the connecting rod, loosely coupled to the bridge, tune the action, and the switching body can also carry an ordinary shift pawl.
