## Summary

**Kind:** type-element machine · **Subject (patent classes):** Typewriters or selective printing mechanisms characterised by the mounting, arrangement or disposition of the types or dies with types or dies mounted on carriers rotatable for selection the plane of the type or die face being parallel to the axis of rotation, e.g. with type on the periphery of cylindrical carriers

**In the patent’s own words:** This invention relates to type writing machines, and its objects are to simplify their construction and to insure the exact positioning of the written characters relatively to each other.

**How it works (reading):** Keys turn a rocking shaft through wings set at graded angles, which turns the type cylinder through a quadrant and pinion, and a spring detent is released by a trigger to snap into the pinion teeth just before the impression.

## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Simplify the Gardner type-cylinder machine and make sure the type is positioned exactly each time so characters line up.

**Solution:** Keys turn a rocking shaft through wings set at graded angles, which turns the type cylinder through a quadrant and pinion, and a spring detent is released by a trigger to snap into the pinion teeth just before the impression.

**How it works:** Fourteen keys, seven each side of centre, press wings fixed at graded positions on a rocking shaft, so each key turns the shaft a different amount (one and a half pinion teeth for the first letter, seven and a half for the seventh). A quadrant on the shaft turns a 16-tooth pinion on the type cylinder, one tooth per letter space plus two teeth for the gap at the line ends. Cylinder fields hold small letters, capitals and figures in two lines each; a slide lifts the cylinder to pick the field and the space key pressed with a key moves the slide to pick the lower line, giving six characters per key. At the end of key travel a rocking lever trips a trigger and a spring-loaded T-shaped detent drops into a pinion gap, locking the cylinder, while the same lever drives the impression hammer and carriage feed. The levers also return the quadrant on the upstroke.
