## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** The two-ratchet escapement of DE617940 can be simplified and its return force from the carriage spring increased.

**Solution:** The return lever is fixed to the pawl carrier and reaches the second ratchet wheel farther from the common pivot than the pawl reaches the first, so the pawl moves little while the lever moves a lot.

**How it works:** A ratchet wheel meshing with the carriage rack and a second ratchet wheel sit on one axis. A pawl carrier pivots on the frame and bears the holding pawl and a shift lever on the same pivot. A key lever swings the switching bridge, whose arm pushes the pawl carrier so the pawl frees the held tooth. The shift lever is thus placed in front of a tooth of the second wheel. The carriage spring turns the wheels and drives the lever back to rest, bringing the pawl in front of the next tooth. Because the lever end is farther from the pivot, the pawl moves a short distance with less friction while the lever pushes the bridge with more force.

**Features:** [ESC:escape-wheel](../features/esc-escape-wheel.md), [ESC:pawl-rack](../features/esc-pawl-rack.md), [ESC:spring-drum](../features/esc-spring-drum.md), [FRM:few-parts](../features/frm-few-parts.md), [ELEM:metal-type](../features/elem-metal-type.md)

**Parts:** first ratchet wheel; second ratchet wheel; pawl carrier; pawl; shift lever; switching bridge; key lever; carriage rack; carriage spring

**Improves on:**

- DE617940: lever on the pawl carrier with unequal arm lengths, and punched sheet-steel wheels with pin teeth instead of milled ones
