## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typebar typewriters needed many screws, nuts and adjusting rings to mount shift, carriage-bracket and space-bar levers, which was costly to assemble and prone to distortion when tightened.

**Solution:** Mount the shift levers, universal bar, carriage bracket and space bar on two plain bright steel wire axles pushed through bearing eyes in an injection-moulded base plate and held in place by the sheet-metal casing.

**How it works:** An injection-moulded base plate carries pressed-in bearings and mounting lugs. Two plain steel wire axles run across the machine through the bearings. The space bar with its stamped bracket, the shift keys and the universal rail (angle iron with welded strip) sit on the axles between bearings or in milled slots, so they cannot slide sideways. The axle ends butt against the sheet-metal jacket, which stops the axles from moving. A spring holds the universal bracket against the intermediate levers, and rubber feet in the base plate steady the machine.
