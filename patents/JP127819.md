## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Earlier Japanese machines lose time choosing characters from a flat board or reversed types, then must still move the mechanism, tiring eyes and posture.

**Solution:** One handle turns a disc type magazine, a character-table band and pointers together, so the group shown in the window is already under the printing levers and a numbered key prints it.

**How it works:** Turning an outside handle drives, through bevel and internal gears, sprockets that move an endless band carrying the character table past a display window, and gear trains that turn pointers and the disc type magazine. The pointers appear in slots between rows of index characters on a front plate. When a pointer indicates a character, the row of characters under it shows in the window and the same types rest under type-holding tubes of several printing levers. The operator hits the numbered press key matching the wanted character, which grips the type and prints it through a ribbon on the paper cylinder. The cylinder moves sideways or lengthwise per impression or alone.

## Description (English translation of the Japanese text, opening)

> Japanese-script printing machine. Nature and object of the invention. This invention relates to a Japanese-script printing machine with the following features. On top is a paper cylinder that can be fed sideways and lengthwise, at each impression or on its own. On the front wall are several press keys that make up the printing mechanism, and the printing levers worked by them are set side by side in a row with their lower ends reaching into the inside. On one side of the body [printed 構體] are a display window and a character index plate. Inside the body are a type magazine that can rotate freely; a character table, in the form of an endless band that can travel, showing the types held in the magazine, with one part facing the display window; and pointers to point to the characters on the index plate. The type magazine, the character table and the pointers are made to turn in a fixed relation to one another, by way of a linkage, when a handle on the outside of the body is turned. So the group of characters within the range of the index character the pointer shows is shown in the display window, and at the same time the types for those characters can come to rest under the printing…
