## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** On a dial-operated type-wheel machine, the operator needs a clear, widely spaced index that shows which character is selected, with a way to set the wheel by hand.

**Solution:** A flat dial plate with a spiral groove, the characters marked along its edge, is geared to the type-wheel so that a pointer running in the groove shows the character that is at the print position.

**How it works:** A thin dial plate with a spiral groove sits above a bracket on the wheel frame, with letters, figures and stops marked along the groove edge. Under it a slotted arm on a vertical spindle carries a sliding block with a pin that projects up into the groove as a pointer. A bevel gear on the spindle meshes with a second bevel wheel fixed to the type-wheel, so turning the wheel by its rack (or the milled boss on the spindle) swings the arm and the pointer runs in or out along the spiral. The characters are laid out so that when the pointer is at one, the same character on the wheel is at the print position. The long spiral allows many characters to be spaced far apart to avoid confusion.
