## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** On Hammond-type shuttle machines a rubber impact strip sits behind the paper; it is useful for ordinary work but undesirable for manifolding or stencil cutting, and fixed mounting makes it hard to remove, renew or clean.

**Solution:** Mount the rubber impact strip on an auxiliary pivoting frame so it can be swung into or out of the printing position at will.

**How it works:** The Hammond machine turns a type shuttle to bring a character opposite the hammer, and the hammer then strikes the paper against the type with a ribbon between. Behind the paper lies a rubber impact strip. Here the strip is carried on studs on two arms fixed to a rod pivoted on the paper carriage, with a knurled knob on the rod end. Turning the knob flips the strip clear of the shield and ribbon; a light spring holds the frame in either position. Guide pieces on the line gauges deflect the strip so it clears the shield and ribbon as it swings.
