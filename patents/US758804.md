## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Platen shift on double-case machines was cramped and frictional, and the shift key had to be held for a series of capitals.

**Solution:** A platen shift with two keys, one momentary and one latching, and a linkage keeping the shifting rail at nearly the same angle to the guide rail in both positions.

**How it works:** The platen shaft is carried in side plates pivoted to the carriage side frames, so raising those plates lifts the platen to upper case, with the raise distance definite. One key moves the platen up and it returns when released. The other moves it up and leaves it there until the first key is operated, releasing it to spring back. The linkage keeps the lifting rail at about the same relative angle to the guide rail upper and lower, reducing friction and allowing the carriage to be replaced without trouble.
