## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Existing music typewriters use intricate rods to position notes vertically, step the carriage by a fixed amount, and give stiff, inaccurate scores that cannot be corrected or reinserted.

**Solution:** A conventional typewriter modified with music type levers, a carriage brake and a print-point indicator showing both coordinates, so music is positioned freely by eye on the staves without mechanical adjustment.

**How it works:** A normal typewriter has keys and type levers carrying musical characters. A novel indicator at the print point shows the horizontal and vertical coordinates of the next impression, so the operator moves the paper and carriage freely to place any sign on any staff, with ordinary line space and carriage release. A carriage brake, released by the space key or a lever with a caps-lock-like latch, holds the carriage; the usual carriage step circuit can be disengaged by a lever on the carriage. The staves can be of any size or spacing, and paper can be removed and reinserted with the same positions.
