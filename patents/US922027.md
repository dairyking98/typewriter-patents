## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** When typing bills or invoices on a long record strip with carbon copies, the operator must turn the platen back by a repeatable number of line spaces before inserting each new sheet so the copies sit at uniform spacing, and has no way to measure that.

**Solution:** A gear segment geared to the platen shaft through a clutched pinion measures how far the platen is turned back, with a scale and an adjustable stop pin, and drops out of action when the platen turns forward.

**How it works:** A pinion on the platen shaft sleeve meshes with a pivoted gear wheel (a segment on an arm) on the carriage end plate. A spring and a radial pin lock the pinion to the shaft by default. Turning the platen forward feeds paper freely until a cam projection on the segment pushes an arm on the pinion sideways, unlocking the pinion from the shaft pin so the segment stays parked. Turning the platen backward lets friction turn the pinion with the shaft, which frees the arm from the cam and relocks the pinion, so the segment now swings back with the platen until its arm hits a stop pin placed in one of a row of line-space holes or reaches a mark on a line-space scale. A sliding bolt on the segment can hold the pinion out of action entirely. The base plate carrying the device detaches from the carriage end plate.
