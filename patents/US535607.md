## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Writing in books needs a typewriter that is faster and more compact, switches between book and sheet writing, moves from page to page without moving the book, and spaces according to character width.

**Solution:** A book typewriter in which a hand lever turns a type cylinder to an index finger position and a plunger presses the chosen type, riding on a carriage over the book frame.

**How it works:** A hand lever with finger keys turns the type cylinder shaft, and a bell crank operates a pointer-bearing index finger against a notched index gauge. A plunger barrel locks and frees the cylinder, tied to an inked-pad ink well arm; type with springs sit in the cylinder, whose type springs can be reached through a collar opening. A hammer hub and fall deliver the blow. A letter-spacing cylinder driven by an actuating spring and ratchet, with a clutch, advances the carriage by a step that can be adjusted to the width of the character. A grooved shaft and pin give line spacing, and the book rests on adjustable beds in a book frame. The OCR is largely a figure list, so detail is limited.
