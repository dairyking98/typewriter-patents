## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** In earlier pneumatic typewriters (US676590) the type wheel and paper carriage moved together while the type struck, so the print was not clean; the wheel should be stopped before the strike and the carriage moved only after it.

**Solution:** Compressed-air logic: a perforated key sends a pressure signal to a cylinder that, through a lever with a flap valve, stops the type wheel, fires the hammer, and only then releases the paper carriage.

**How it works:** A rotating arm sweeps an air passage past a ring of holes, one per key, each hole piped to a perforated key. When the operator closes the key with a finger the pressure in its pipe rises as the arm passes, driving a piston that raises a double lever. This lever releases the paper carriage rod and arrests the type-wheel rod. The wheel stops and a flap valve closes a nozzle, raising pressure in a cylinder pair that open a stop-cock to let compressed air push the hammer slide against the type. A second piston then opens an outlet so the lever drops under a spring, releasing the carriage to space and re-starting the wheel.
