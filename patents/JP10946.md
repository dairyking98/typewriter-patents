## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Pictures and trade marks on bicycle frames and other metal or wooden articles are painted by hand at great cost, and uniform fine patterns are nearly impossible.

**Solution:** A transfer paper: gum-arabic-coated backing paper lithographically printed (optionally with gold or silver leaf), over-coated with white ink, enamelled and stuck on, so that only the picture stays when the backing is wetted and peeled off.

**How it works:** Paper is coated several times with boiled, strained gum arabic and dried. The picture is printed on it in colourless varnish ink and dusted with gold or silver leaf or colour powder, or printed directly in colour inks. The whole face is coated thinly with white lithographic ink so the object's colour does not show through. Enamel is applied, the paper is laid on the metal or wood while wet, water is applied to the back, and the backing paper is peeled away leaving the picture.

**Parts:** backing paper; gum arabic coating; lithographic ink; gold or silver leaf; white ink layer; enamel

**Numbers:** about 240 monme (900 g) gum arabic; about 7.5 go (1.35 litres) water; about 40 minutes boiling

**Improves on:**

- hand painting of trade marks: uniform, fine, cheap, lithographed picture

## Description (English translation of the Japanese text, opening)

> Specification. Transfer paper. This invention is a transfer paper made by coating a backing paper with gum arabic, printing on it as required, and then smearing white ink over the whole surface. Enamel is applied to it and it is at once stuck onto metal, wood or the like; water is applied from the back, and only the backing paper is peeled off, leaving the printed part stuck to the metal or wood. Its object is to make it possible to transfer any detailed picture clearly onto metal or another suitable object, most simply and with no risk of its peeling off. First, about 240 monme (about 900 g) of gum arabic is boiled with about 7.5 gō (about 1.35 litres) of clean water; after about forty minutes' boiling the solution is strained through silk cloth to remove dust. A suitable Western paper is then coated with this gum arabic solution and dried, and the coating is repeated several times. After drying, the paper is run off a roller, and on the face coated with gum arabic the wanted picture is printed with varnish or another colourless lithographic ink.
