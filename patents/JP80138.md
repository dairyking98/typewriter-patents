## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** In the earlier chain-band Japanese typewriter (JP73560) the bar types were pivoted on the chain and turned face-up to print, giving a poor view of the print point and making types hard to change.

**Solution:** An endless chain of pivoted links carries rods that clamp loose bar types on the straight run and spread open on the curve around a sprocket, so the selected type can be taken out of the chain, raised in front of the paper and struck from behind by a hammer.

**How it works:** Two endless chains of pivoted links, hung on sprockets in a slanted frame, carry projecting rods whose gaps form rectangular holding holes for lugs at both ends of each bar type, faces to one side. The chain is turned until the wanted type reaches the foot of a guide groove on the rails above the sprocket. There the rods spread radially round the curve and open the hole, and a swinging clamp piece takes the type's lug out of the chain. The type rises up the guide groove to stand in front of the paper on the cylinder, its face passing an ink roller on the way. A hammer behind the paper then presses the paper onto the type face.

## Description (English translation of the Japanese text, opening)

> Specification. Japanese-script typewriter. Nature and object of the invention. This invention is an improvement of the printing device of the Japanese typewriter of patent No. 73560. An endless chain band is made of many pivoted links carrying projecting rods. Its feature is that, where the chain runs straight, the rods clamp bar-shaped types, and where it runs round a curve, the rods spread out [開被〔披〕] radially so that the type can come out of the chain. In printing, the type is taken out of the chain and raised in front of the paper, and a printing hammer presses the paper from behind onto the type face. The object is a simple device that is easy to work, gives a wide view of the printing point, and lets the types be changed freely. Brief description of the drawings.
