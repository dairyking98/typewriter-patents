## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Key-bars of a chord-struck shorthand typewriter were weakened by pivot rods passing through them, and printing and shift keys crowded each other.

**Solution:** Mount each key-bar on a long tubular bearing fastened beneath it, and put the shift keys on a lower plane nearer the operator so they cross under the printing keys.

**How it works:** Revolubly mounted type-frames with rows of type sit above printing keys placed under the matching rows. Each key-bar has a thin metal strip with a soldered tube through which a pivot rod passes, so the bar is not drilled and the bearing is longer. Shift keys on a separate pivot rod move the type-frames to bring different rows over the printing keys; only two shift keys have spiral springs. A feed frame rests on the printing key-bar ends.
