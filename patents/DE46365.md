## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A keyboard typewriter with few parts is wanted in which the type wheel stops quickly at the chosen character without the operator supplying the printing force.

**Solution:** A hollow shaft carrying two type wheels turns continuously under power, and each key's hooked lever drags on the shaft until it drops into that key's own bore, arresting the wheel while a hammer strikes the type.

**How it works:** A hollow shaft driven by a spring motor through gears carries two type wheels, one for capitals and one for small letters, and has 40 bores arranged along a single helix so no two lie in line. Each of 40 keys is linked to a sprung hooked lever; pressing a key lets its hook drag on the turning shaft until its bore comes round, then the hook falls in and stops the shaft. The same key motion presses a sprung frame that swings a hammer behind the selected wheel, striking the type through the paper, and trips an anchor that releases the spring-pushed paper carriage by one tooth. A sleeve with a switching lever disengages one type wheel so the hammer reaches only the other. Space keys release the anchor without the hammer, and variants use notched rails of different depth to give longer carriage release for wider characters. An alternative hand-wound drive puts a key-pressed lever through a tension band and opposed spiral springs to turn the shaft only as far as needed.
