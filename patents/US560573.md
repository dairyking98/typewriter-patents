## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Electrical typewriters need a simple arrangement, and shift carriages are awkward, clumsy and expensive.

**Solution:** One large magnet raises a reciprocating plate that feeds the carriage and drives the typebars, with an electrical shifting keyboard that avoids any shifting carriage.

**How it works:** An energised magnet pulls a bell-crank armature that raises a reciprocating annular plate about half an inch. A rod from the plate works a feed pawl in a rack on the carriage, advancing it against a spring drum that returns it when the pawl and a holding pawl are drawn off by cords. A hinged plate of keys with flat springs sits above a second non-conducting plate; depressing the shift key slides the lower plate about one-eighth inch so each key spring lands over the other of a pair of contact pins, selecting lower or upper case circuits. A further magnet and armature lever turns the platen roller.
