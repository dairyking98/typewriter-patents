## Summary

**Kind:** type-element machine · **Subject (patent classes):** Carriers stationary for impression, e.g. with the types or dies not moving relative to the carriers with the types or dies moving relative to the carriers or mounted on flexible carriers; Manual press type

**In the English translation of the patent’s own words:** The object: to make the construction as simple and as small as possible, and, by doing away with an individual holder for each type, to hold more types, and to be strong and durable.

**How it works (reading):** Pack the types loosely round the whole ring with a single follower, and use a plunger with ratchet and cap to print and step in one stroke or step alone.

## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** The earlier JP120239 machine (outer cylinder of types around a fixed inner cylinder) was complex and bulky, with an individual holder per type limiting how many types fit.

**Solution:** Pack the types loosely round the whole ring with a single follower, and use a plunger with ratchet and cap to print and step in one stroke or step alone.

**How it works:** Types are packed side by side in the gap between a fixed inner cylinder and a turning outer cylinder, with one follower pushing them round on a ridge. The operator turns the outer cylinder by handles until the wanted character on its dial is at an index mark; a spring ball in a tooth ring gives one-type detents. Pressing the plunger lowers a slider whose lug carries the type at the print position down onto paper over a table and roller, while its pawl turns a ratchet and gear that rolls along a rack to move the whole assembly one step on a shaft. Turning a cap so a plunger stud strikes it shortens the stroke: no type prints but the ratchet still steps, giving a space. A spring-loaded bottom plate with a pad keeps type faces in place.
