## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A typewriter carriage needs a sideways feed that can step by a small, adjustable amount (down to 1 mm for typing inside ruled lines) and serve both horizontal and vertical writing.

**Solution:** A rocking pawl lever alternately holds a pinion and lets a spring-loaded toothed sector turn it through an adjustable arc, so each rock steps the carriage a set distance.

**How it works:** An upright flat lever carries a pawl and, at its foot, a toothed sector meshing with a crosswise gear. When the lever is pushed back, the pawl enters the gear to stop it while the sector, under an upper spring, swings back against an adjustable stop lever. When the lever returns under its spring, the pawl withdraws and the sector meshes and turns the gear forward to a lower stop, so the gear turns by the sector's swing. A cord wound on a drum against a spring drives the carriage, which has two facing racks; a handle swings a bracket so the drum gear meshes with one rack (horizontal writing) or the other (vertical writing) and, through a cranked rod and the crosswise arm, reverses which spring biases the sector.

## Description (English translation of the Japanese text, opening)

> Specification. Carriage feed mechanism for a typewriter. Nature and object of the invention. This invention relates to a carriage feed mechanism for a typewriter, made as follows. On the frame-and-base of the typewriter are mounted a crosswise "arm" and an upright flat "lever" with a pawl. On the upright pawl lever, at its base, a crosswise toothed sector is pivoted, which meshes with a crosswise gear. The upper and lower parts of the sector and the upper and lower parts of the crosswise arm are connected by springs. An upright swinging "bracket" carrying a moving gear upright is connected to a handle, and the handle and the lower end of the crosswise arm are engaged by a cranked connecting rod. Its object is that, by the horizontal rocking of the upright pawl lever, the pawl of that lever and the toothed sector are made to mesh alternately with the crosswise gear, the pawl taking charge of stopping the gear's rotation and the sector of limiting the range of its turn, so as to feed the typewriter's "carriage" sideways.
