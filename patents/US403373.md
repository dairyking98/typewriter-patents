## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Dodson's two-catch latch escapement was noisy when the latch hit its stop, needed lateral restraint of the latch, and had no easy way to remove the escapement for repair.

**Solution:** Refine the two-catch latch escapement with a bent spring lever that also locks the escapement in place by a tongue, a conical thimble, a slotted lateral restraint, and a graduated buffer spring that deadens the forward impact.

**How it works:** A spring-driven round latch with two catches, one ahead of the other, sits in a bore beside a pivoted escapement bar having a holding catch and a detaining catch. A bent lever, pushed by the latch spring through a conical thimble, presses the latch against the escapement; its tongue sits in a notch on the escapement and locks it in place until the lever is pushed aside. Pressing the key lifts the escapement arm, which frees the first catch and the latch springs forward. If the key is released, the detaining catch drops to re-catch the latch on return; if still held, the forward catch is caught and the spring lever re-seats the holding catch as the key rises. A short buffer spring forward of a collar on the latch, graduated to close coil to coil under the latch force, reduces impact noise without disturbing the final position. The reduced latch diameter in a slot in the lever stops sideways movement from the starting arm.
