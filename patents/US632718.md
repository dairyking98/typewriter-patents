## Summary

**Kind:** typebar machine · **Subject (patent classes):** Typewriters or selective printing mechanisms characterised by the mounting, arrangement or disposition of the types or dies with types or dies carried on sliding bars or rods on side surfaces thereof, e.g. fixed thereto the types or dies being movable relative to the bars or rods

**In the patent’s own words:** This invention relates to an automatic restoring device for shift-key type-writers,its object being to automatically restore the mechanism to position for printing normal or smallletter type on or directly after completion of the act of striking or printing a cap or other special type when the platen has been shifted to bring such special type into operation.

**How it works (reading):** A lock holds the shifted platen frame down, and the first carriage movement after the impression trips the lock so a spring automatically restores the platen to normal.

## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** On shift-key typewriters the operator must release the shift after each capital or special character to return to small letters.

**Solution:** A lock holds the shifted platen frame down, and the first carriage movement after the impression trips the lock so a spring automatically restores the platen to normal.

**How it works:** Depressing the shift key lowers the platen frame on the carriage through a shaft and lever, to one of two stopped depths. A dog catches the tail of the shift lever to lock it in the shifted position, and at the same time a trip lever on a slide bar is allowed by the shift shaft to drop into engagement with the carriage rack bar. When any type key is pressed, the universal mechanism releases the rack bar, and the carriage spring advances it only after the impression. As the rack moves it carries the trip lever and slide bar, pulling the dog off the shift lever, so the platen spring snaps it back to the normal position. A cam plate then lifts the trip lever off the rack and a spring returns the slide.
