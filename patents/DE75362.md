## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** In the Gardner type-cylinder machine the key motion needs to turn the cylinder to a letter and hold it there, return it to zero after each stroke, and do so with a single drive rod turning the cylinder in both directions and with keys in straight rows instead of a circle.

**Solution:** Two toothed quadrants on a drive rod and a parallel locking (escapement) rod mesh with a toothed drum under the type cylinder, so a pressed key turns the cylinder through an angle set by its lug on the drive rod and then locks it by a matching lug on the locking rod.

**How it works:** A toothed cylinder fixed under the type cylinder meshes with two quadrants, one on the drive rod and one on the locking rod; the two rods move together and carry the cylinder. Each key stem has a driving projection, a locking projection and a nose; the rods carry inclined rows of lugs, one pair per key. As a key goes down, its nose first pushes a rod that frees sliding rollers from a locking plate that holds the cylinder at zero, then the driving projection presses its lug on the drive rod and turns the cylinder through an angle proportional to lug length, and the corresponding locking-rod lug meets the key's locking projection and stops the motion, holding the cylinder in print position. The same key movement works the lever that steps the paper carriage and releases the pressure hammer, as in the inventor's earlier DE55409. When the key is released, springs return the cylinder to zero where the rollers hold it again.
