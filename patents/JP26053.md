## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** The original Sakai typewriter (JP22193) had the paper cylinder upright against the type cylinder, which was awkward to handle.

**Solution:** Lay the paper cylinder across above the type cylinder and redesign its swinging frame so that pressing a lever swings the paper cylinder down onto the chosen character, with a feed wheel and pawl advancing the sliding frame on release.

**How it works:** A large type cylinder with very many characters turns and slides on a horizontal shaft to bring the wanted character to a fixed spot. The paper cylinder, at right angles above it, is carried on arms pivoted on a sliding frame; a pressing lever pulls a semicircular bar which, through connecting levers, arc levers and a rod, swings the arms down so the paper meets the type, and a coil spring returns it. The sliding frame rides on a support bar with anti-friction balls and is held at height by triangular ridges and grooves. A rack on the frame meshes a pinion with a many-toothed feed wheel; a pawl linked to the swing mechanism releases it as the paper cylinder returns, so the frame advances one tooth, one character space. Ink comes from a felt on an arm instead of a ribbon.
