## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typebar key linkages needed a very light touch and levers that could be fitted or replaced quickly.

**Solution:** A cam groove in an upright lever, driven by a pin on the key lever, accelerates the typebar, and notched bearing rods let levers be slid on and off their pivots.

**How it works:** Pressing the key lever makes its pin slide down the cam groove in the lower end of a centrally pivoted upright lever. That pushes the upright lever's heel back, and a link from its top swings the typebar up and forward with accelerating motion. A coil spring returns key lever, upright lever and typebar, helped by the typebar's own weight. Each lever has a round bearing opening into a narrower slot, and the bearing rod has notched portions. Shifting the rod lengthwise lines the notches with the slots so a lever can be removed or added, and shifting back locks them in place.
