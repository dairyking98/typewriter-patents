## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Earlier Japanese typewriters built the type tray separately and mounted it on a machine base, making the body complicated, heavy and bulky.

**Solution:** Use the type tray's own side frames as the machine body and guide rails for the carriage, with folding rear rails and a folding-link index board so the whole machine collapses small.

**How it works:** The tray frames, on legs on a base plate, are extended rearward with grooves in their outer faces as rails; a carriage with paper cylinder and printing mechanism slides forward and back on them and left and right on its own rail parts. Rear rails are pivoted to the frame ends and held in line by spring stud and recess detents; folded round, their end studs lock into recesses of the printing mechanism frame, which also stops it sliding. The celluloid index board in a frame is carried by two-part links pivoted to the tray front and the board underside; stretched, spring studs hold it in front of the tray, folded at their hinges it slides back over the tray. Folded, the machine is small for storage and carrying.
