## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Wheel typewriters needed a simple way to turn the type wheel to a key's character, ink it, hold it exactly true while printing, and space letters variably.

**Solution:** Each key lever carries a cam that swings a bar and gear segment to rotate the type wheel to its position, then continues down to ink the type, lock the wheel with a prong in a V-toothed wheel, and throw the platen against the type.

**How it works:** A type wheel with types on its periphery slides on a shaft for row shift and is turned by a pinion driven by a gear segment on a rock shaft carrying a swinging bar. Each key lever has a cam which, when the key is pressed, pushes the bar to a position characteristic of that key, turning the wheel to the printing point; a flat dwell beyond the cam surface lets the key keep descending without moving the bar. Further travel presses the key on a universal bar which swings an inking roller to the type and then another bar that tilts the platen carriage forward against the type, while a prong enters a V-toothed wheel on the shaft to correct and lock the wheel position. On release the platen retreats and a variable-spacing bar with a notched surface lets pawls on a frame turn a ratchet wheel and pinion that advance the platen carriage rack by a variable amount.
