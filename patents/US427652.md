## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Mainspring-driven paper carriages feed unevenly because spring tension changes with extension, and oblique driving force causes friction and binding on the carriage.

**Solution:** A pivoted driving arm pulled by a spring attached below its pivot bears with a roller on a vertical track on the carriage, so the force always acts along the carriage path and the changing spring angle keeps the feed force nearly constant.

**How it works:** A driving arm is pivoted under the bed plate. A stud below the pivot holds one end of a spiral spring whose other end is anchored on a nut run by a screw pin through the bed plate, so turning the screw sets the tension. The arm's upper end carries a grooved roller that rides on a vertical track formed on a bracket fixed to the carriage yoke. As the carriage is let along one letter space at a time by the feed dog, the spring swings the arm and the roller travels up and then down the track. As the spring stretches, the stud's pull angle changes, and the leverage at the lower end varies, so the push on the carriage stays about constant across the line.

**Features:** [ESC:spring-drum](../features/esc-spring-drum.md), [ESC:pawl-rack](../features/esc-pawl-rack.md), [FRM:adjustment-wear](../features/frm-adjustment-wear.md), [FRM:few-parts](../features/frm-few-parts.md)

**Parts:** driving arm; spiral spring; stud; nut; adjusting screw pin; roller; vertical track; carriage yoke; guide rail; feed dog; rack; bed plate

**Improves on:**

- earlier spring-and-strap carriage drives: force applied in the line of carriage motion and nearly constant across the travel
