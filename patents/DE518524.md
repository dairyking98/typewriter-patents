## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** On typebar machines with 42-46 keys and single shift, the outer typebars strike the platen at too sharp an angle, giving a heavy keystroke and poor impression; machines with two bar groups need two different key-lever drives.

**Solution:** Two rows of typebars share the same comb guides and drive, and differently curved bars bring both rows to the common print point.

**How it works:** Upper-row bars 1 and lower-row bars 2 run in combs 3 and 4 and are driven by intermediate lever 5 and key lever 6 against platen 7. The type bodies sit in two rows one above the other, the bars of one row interleaved between the types of the other. Upper bars move in a straight line to the platen while the lower bars are curved so that they arrive at the same print point. Curved bars give a lighter, more even keystroke.

**Features:** [ELEM:typebar](../features/elem-typebar.md), [SEL:keyboard-full](../features/sel-keyboard-full.md), [IMP:element-to-platen](../features/imp-element-to-platen.md)

**Parts:** typebar; comb guide; intermediate lever; key lever; platen; support rail

**Numbers:** outer type angle about 50 degrees on 30-key double-shift machines; under 90 degrees for lateral types

**Improves on:**

- two-group typebar machines with separate lever drives: same guides and drive, only bar shape differs
