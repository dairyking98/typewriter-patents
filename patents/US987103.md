## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Wants a typewheel keyboard typewriter with visible writing that is simple and cheap yet solidly built.

**Solution:** Each key lever slides a toothed slide sideways by a key-specific amount, which turns the type wheel through a rack and pinion, and the common bar then swings the wheel to the platen and trips the escapement.

**How it works:** About 20 key levers pivot on a common shaft at the rear. Pressing one makes it glide down a slanted tooth flank of a toothed slide, shifting the slide left or right by a distance set by the slant (about 10 positions each side). A rack on the slide turns a pinion on the type wheel shaft, rotating the wheel to the letter. At the bottom of the tooth gap the lever enters a slit and a bar common to all levers presses down on a projection of the wheel bearing, swinging the bearing and wheel over against the paper cylinder to print. The same bar's shaft carries the carriage escapement of two swinging detents, so each press spaces the carriage. Two spring-loaded knee levers pushed by a pin on the slide return it to centre and arrest it almost instantly. The wheel has four rings of types and is shifted axially to four heights, the top ring holding the common small letters.
