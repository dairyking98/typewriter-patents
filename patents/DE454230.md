## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Automatic ribbon reversal that relies on long-lived springs on the main drive parts is unreliable.

**Solution:** Bumpers driven from the universal rail serve directly as switching elements; a feeler arm on the emptying spool shifts a rocker so one bumper disengages and the other engages the opposite spool gear.

**How it works:** Each ribbon spool shaft carries a gear stepped by a pointed push rod (bumper) driven back and forth by the universal rail through a linkage. A pawl lever stops reverse rotation. A feeler arm with torsion spring rests on the ribbon winding of each spool; as a spool empties its feeler swings in and its thumb enters the path of a pendulum lever that is coupled to the bumper. This blocks that bumper, and via a rocker linked by a coupling rod the other bumper is brought into engagement, reversing the winding direction. A detent lever with notches holds the rocker state.

**Features:** [INK:ribbon](../features/ink-ribbon.md), [INK:ribbon-feed](../features/ink-ribbon-feed.md), [INK:ribbon-reverse](../features/ink-ribbon-reverse.md)

**Parts:** ribbon spool; spool gear; bumper push rod; universal rail; feeler arm; thumb; pendulum lever; rocker lever; coupling rod; pawl lever; detent lever

**Improves on:**

- ribbon reversers using springs on main drive parts: bumpers themselves are switched, independent of such springs
