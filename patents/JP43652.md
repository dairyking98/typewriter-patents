## Summary

**Kind:** a part or mechanism (any kind of machine) · **Subject (patent classes):** Typewriters or selective printing mechanisms characterised by the mounting, arrangement or disposition of the types or dies

**Class note:** J-PlatPat files it as a Western-type typewriter (B41J1/00@P); it is a Japanese-script (kanji or kana) machine built on Western lines.

**In the English translation of the patent’s own words:** The invention relates to a typewriter that makes horizontal and vertical movements of differing "pitch", and reciprocating movements of these.

**How it works (reading):** A typebar typewriter with about 600 radical types whose paper cylinder is stepped by full, half and quarter pitches sideways, lowered by ten row pitches, and rotated in quarter-height steps so that component types can be stacked and placed side by side into one kanji.

## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Printing about ten thousand kanji would need thousands of types; the inventor wants to build kanji from a few hundred component types (radicals) of differing size, which needs unequal horizontal and vertical positioning.

**Solution:** A typebar typewriter with about 600 radical types whose paper cylinder is stepped by full, half and quarter pitches sideways, lowered by ten row pitches, and rotated in quarter-height steps so that component types can be stacked and placed side by side into one kanji.

**How it works:** Sixty keys per row, ten rows, each key swinging a type bar to the cylinder; each bar carries ten types one above another. Row keys E1-E10 lower the whole paper cylinder frame by 1 to 10 type pitches (adjustable screw stops set the stroke) so the wanted type on the bar meets the cylinder's fixed centre line, and a pawl latches it down until the type key is released. Keys A, B and D step the cylinder carriage sideways by pawls driving a gear and rack (full pitch = 4 teeth, half = 2, quarter back = 1 tooth anticlockwise). Rotation keys F0-F6 and F1'-F2' turn the cylinder through a belt, pulleys, sliding gear and square shaft by multiples of a quarter type height, moving the print line on the paper up or down; a notched pulley and roller return it to normal on release. A kanji is composed by a sequence of row key, rotation key, type key and pitch keys for each component.
