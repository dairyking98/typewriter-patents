## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** Cutting a rectangular type-guiding opening in the centre guide arm so that it lands exactly at the printing point is slow and skilled work, and the guide can snag thick paper.

**Solution:** Make the centre guide (directrix) a separate round piece with a rectangular opening, rotatable and screw-threaded in its support so it can be aligned and raised or lowered.

**How it works:** In a Yost-type machine all the typebar types are aligned by a central guide opening. Here the guide is a round disc with a central rectangular hole, seated in a circular hole at the end of the support arm. The disc is turned by finger or tool with a set screw loosened to square the hole's walls to the lines of writing. With an external thread engaging an internal thread on the support, the disc can be screwed up or down to clear the platen paper, and a transverse screw locks it. A variant allows turning only.
