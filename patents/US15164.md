## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** The blind need a way to produce raised letters they can read by touch as fast as they are written.

**Solution:** Pivoted pairs of male and female type-keys meet at a common center to emboss a raised letter on a paper strip, with a clockwork escapement advancing the paper after each stroke.

**How it works:** Each type-key is a pair of levers pivoted on standards; their inner ends carry raised and intaglio types that meet at one common center and emboss the paper between them. A spiral spring restores each pair to open. The paper strip passes between a roller on a clockwork shaft and a spring-loaded pressing roller. All keys are tied by a single cord to a bell-crank escapement that releases a crank on the clockwork pinion shaft, so every keystroke advances the paper a short step. For black printing the types strike an endless inked (lamp-black) belt over the paper against a fixed anvil, and the belt creeps so a type never hits the same spot twice.
