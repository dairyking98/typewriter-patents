## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Typebar pivots wear, develop lost motion and lateral play (spoiling alignment), need frequent adjustment, collect dust, and take up too much width on the type ring.

**Solution:** A pivotless typebar bearing: a core disk on the hanger sits between two side disks on the typebar, with two annular rings of balls in large-diameter races, one side disk screwing in for wear adjustment.

**How it works:** The hanger carries a central core disk with ball races in both faces. The typebar carries a band, slotted underneath for the disk's neck, with two side disks threaded into it whose inner faces have matching races. Two rings of balls run between the faces and also locate the bar radially, so there is no pivot pin. Screwing the side disks inward takes up wear, and an adjusting tool with a centring nib engages notches in the disks. The races are wider in diameter than the gap between them, which gives lateral rigidity in an axially narrow bearing, and the slot faces downward to keep dust out.
