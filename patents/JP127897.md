## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A power-struck Japanese typewriter strikes with a fixed force, but Japanese characters differ greatly in stroke density, giving dark and light print.

**Solution:** A stud on the sliding type magazine acts on the striking-force regulator automatically when a type from a chosen row (dense or light) is at the printing point.

**How it works:** The type magazine slides sideways on a frame that slides fore and aft on the bed. A stud on the magazine's back wall presses a spring-returned bell crank on the frame when the magazine reaches a particular position. The bell crank, through a drop rod, a crank frame, an arm and a link, tilts a regulating plate that has a long and a short slot in line. Normally the lever pin sits in the short slot and the full motor-driven stroke is passed on; with the plate tilted, the pin lies in the long slot, the cross lever's stroke is shorter and the blow on the type-bar link is much weaker. The types needing the change are gathered in particular rows so that a single stud suffices.
