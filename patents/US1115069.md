## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Key return springs pull harder the further a key is depressed, so fast, sharp keystrokes meet extra resistance and the touch is fatiguing.

**Solution:** Each sheet-metal key lever is folded back on itself so the key-carrying body can flex away from the pivoted arm, leaving the return spring tension nearly constant on a quick blow.

**How it works:** Each key lever is stamped from sheet metal with a body and an arm alongside each other and joined at one end. The arm pivots on the key-lever shaft and the body extends beneath the shaft. A tension spring attaches to the lever behind the shaft. When the key is struck quickly the body yields downward relative to the arm, so the spring attachment hardly moves and spring tension stays about constant. Short levers have longer rear portions than long ones so the resilience is the same on all keys, and springs attach further back on longer levers.
