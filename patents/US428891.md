## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** The case-shift mechanism of typebar machines is complicated.

**Solution:** Pairs of upper-case and lower-case typebars pivot on one type-base that slides through a small arc, so the key-levers engage one set of bars or the other.

**How it works:** Typebars are pivoted in pairs on a curved type-base, each bar with a pin pointing at the pin of its neighbour. Each key-lever ends in a socket between the two pins and engages one of them. A shifter-lever and bent lever swing the whole type-base about the common printing point by a limited slot-guided amount, which pulls one set of pins out of the key-levers and brings the other in. A spring restores the base on release. The frame edge has gaps, so only the engaged set of bars can swing out; the other set is locked.
