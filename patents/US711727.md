**In the patent’s own words:** The present invention relates to that class of type-writers in which a rotary type-wheel is employed.

## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** In type-wheel machines the wheel strikes the paper hard, wearing the type; a more elastic approach and a different actuation of the wheel are wanted.

**Solution:** Keys swing a toothed segment that turns the wheel through a long pinion, then a key-tilted frame taps the wheel carrier so the wheel tilts to the paper against a spring for a softer impression.

**How it works:** The type wheel sits on a shaft balanced on points in a carrier plate so it can tilt to and from the paper roll. A long pinion on the shaft meshes with a segment held central by two springs; each key lever pushes one of two lever arms under the keys, pulling the segment a distance to the right or left that sets how far the wheel turns. Immediately after, a frame tipped by the key lever swings a striker that hits an arm of the carrier and tilts the wheel to the paper, the impression being elastic through a spring-pressed shaft. For the capital and figure rows, extra keys work a lever that pushes the shaft axially away from the writer; a two-toothed latch engages a pin on the shaft cross-bar and holds that row until the wheel returns, when the latch strikes a stationary lug and releases.

**Parts:** type wheel; wheel shaft; carrier plate; long pinion; toothed segment; centering spring; key lever; lever arm; tilting frame; striker; shift lever; latch; pin; return spring; paper roll

**Numbers:** three rows on the wheel (small letters, capitals, figures and signs)

**Improves on:**

- earlier type-wheel machines: softer, more elastic wheel-to-paper motion that lessens type wear, and a segment-and-pinion wheel drive
