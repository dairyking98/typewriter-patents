## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** On Remington No. 2 machines the platen is shifted for capitals by separate upper-case and lower-case keys, which clutter the keyboard and slow the operator.

**Solution:** Replace the two shift keys with a single horizontal shift-bar across the front of the keyboard, pressed inward by a thumb, linked to the platen-shifting rod.

**How it works:** A thin metal shift-bar runs the full width of the keyboard parallel to the space-bar. Rods fixed to its ends run back beneath the space levers to arms hanging from a rock-shaft under the type basket. A vertical rod joins that rock-shaft to the existing rock-shaft that moves the platen shifting rod. Pressing the bar inward shifts the platen for capitals; a spiral spring returns the platen to lower case when pressure is released.

**Parts:** shift-bar; push rod; rock-shaft; vertical connecting rod; platen shifting rod; return spring

**Numbers:** bar projects one-fourth inch from the front
