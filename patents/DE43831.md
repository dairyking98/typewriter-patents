## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Existing keyboard typewriters are complicated and costly; the inventor wants a simpler, strong, cheaper machine.

**Solution:** A type wheel is spun continuously by a spring motor and a key press releases a brake and presents a stop that halts the wheel at the chosen character, after which the paper roller is lifted against the wheel.

**How it works:** A type wheel with 75 characters on its rim sits on a shaft driven by a spring motor, held still by a brake block on a toothed brake disc. Keys sit in parallel rows on a plate; each key stem has a different depth shoulder so that a cross-row lever carries a stop plate down by an amount unique to the key. Pressing any key lifts the brake, the wheel starts turning, and the stop plate meets one pin on one of several stop discs on the shaft and arrests the wheel at that character. The same stroke, through a slotted link, first steps the paper-roller carrier sideways one letter by a pawl on a rack and then lifts the roller against the wheel to print. Sliding the key plate sideways brings the stop plate over a different stop disc to choose capitals, small letters or figures. Ink rollers rest on the wheel rim by their own weight, a separate brake allows repeated letters without a full turn, and a bell warns at line end.
