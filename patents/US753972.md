## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Key-to-typebar connections in a front-strike typewriter are complicated, and dirt and paper rubbings fall into the typebar pivots; key touch cannot be varied.

**Solution:** Connect each key lever to its typebar through a straight upright sublever with a cam-slot roller drive and a link, give the typebars dirt-shielding inverted-U pivots, and make key touch adjustable by moving a fulcrum bar.

**How it works:** Each typebar has an inverted-U end embracing a parallel-faced blade on a curved segment below the platen, pivoting on a wire in notches in the blades. A link joins a lug on the typebar to the top of a straight sublever pivoted on a rod mid-length. The sublever's lower end carries a flanged roller running in a curved cam-slot in the key lever, so depressing the key swings the sublever and throws the typebar up to the platen. A spring returns the sublever and typebar. A fulcrum bar insertable into different holes and notches in the key levers changes their leverage and hence the touch.
