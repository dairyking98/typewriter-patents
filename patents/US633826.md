## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Basket-type typebar machines need hand force for every impression.

**Solution:** An electromagnet-driven rod common to all typebars strikes the impression, while mechanical means ahead of it determine which typebar it operates.

**How it works:** Bell-crank typebars hang in hangers around a central opening in the top plate, with a buffer ring below. A vertically reciprocating rod in a frame is joined to a bell-crank carrying an iron armature, drawn by one or two magnets. Each key sits on its own rock shaft with a spring; pressing a key makes its stem touch an insulated plate and close the circuit, energizing the magnets and pulling the rod down. Mechanical means set in advance by the key move the chosen typebar into the path of the rod, and a toothed disk under the cross bar takes part in this selection. The text is cut off here.
