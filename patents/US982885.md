## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Changing the line length or paragraph indent on a typewriter required moving margin stops by hand on the carriage.

**Solution:** A single stop on the machine frame, shifted between two positions by two keys beside the keyboard, engages either the terminal margin stops or the intermediate (paragraph/inset) stops on the carriage.

**How it works:** The carriage rail is a toothed bar carrying adjustable terminal margin stops and intermediate stops with bills of different length. A stop on an arm pivoted to the fixed frame sits in the carriage path. A rock shaft with two keys marked Out and In swings a lever and cam slot that moves the arm between a low position (meets only terminal stops) and a raised position (meets the intermediate stops). A spring jaw gives a soft touch and lets the stop yield when a stop passes it. The stop bills have inclined rear sides so they ride over the fixed stop on carriage return, and a spring-loaded toothed plate locks each stop on the bar.
