## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Platen shifters of underneath-strike typebar machines were driven by a shift rail and let the platen frame wander or disconnect when the frame was lifted to view the writing; carriage and platen-frame bearings were also sliding and stiff.

**Solution:** Hinge the platen frame on a key-operated eccentric shifter that rides on a fixed guide rail, with ball-bearings and a rolling front track, so the platen shifts case at any carriage position and can still swing up to show the writing.

**How it works:** A fixed, laterally motionless guide rail behind the platen carries a tubular shifter with two eccentric end disks and a toothed middle section. The platen frame is hinged on the eccentrics, so turning the shifter moves the platen frame rearwards for capitals. The shift key, a lever pivoted on the machine base, works a thrust rod, crank arm and rocking shaft whose pinion meshes with the long teeth on the shifter, so shifting works at every carriage position. A spring on the fixed shaft returns it, with adjustable stops on the frame. The spring-drum-driven spacing rack rides on the same rail with the shifter between its arms. The front of the platen frame rolls on a long cylinder track kept parallel by end gear teeth on stationary racks. Balls in a cage run between the rail and the shifter tube, and cup-and-cone ball hinges join frame and eccentrics.
