## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Direct-thrust typebars with a limited throw (the noiseless machine) need the platen set to the thickness of the work, and earlier manual adjusters needed operator attention.

**Solution:** The first typebar struck against the paper pushes the platen back to the right printing position, and a lock then holds it there until the operator releases it.

**How it works:** The platen and its carriage are backed by a center-tie that a coil spring pulls toward the front against a threaded rod (first form) or a stud screw (second form), which sets the zero position. When a typebar is thrown against the paper sheets, the type face pushes platen, carriage and center-tie rearward, compressing the spring, until the throw ends, which gives the right pressure for that thickness. In the first form the threaded rod, urged by a coil spring, unscrews to follow the center-tie back and then blocks forward return, and a pointer on a dial shows the setting. In the second form a universal bar tips a toggle lever at the end of the key stroke, and a spring-loaded rod head clamps the split center-tie on the stud screw by friction. A handle releases the lock so the spring returns the platen to zero.
