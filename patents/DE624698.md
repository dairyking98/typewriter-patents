## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** Jerky parts such as the loose escapement tooth hit their end position noisily; a toggle stop was noisy and slowed return.

**Solution:** A slightly curved spring, unstressed at the start of the motion and stretched straight at the end, gives a rapidly rising counterforce that limits the movement softly.

**How it works:** The loose escapement tooth rides on a rocker. When a typebar moves the switch bracket, the rocker swings out the loose tooth while the fixed tooth enters the gap, releasing the ratchet wheel. The spring is pulled from its bent rest shape towards a straight line as the tooth swings and so increasingly opposes the carriage spring. It stops the motion where forces balance. Tooth and rocker swing in parallel planes tangential to the ratchet wheel so axial play does not matter.

**Features:**

- **Spacing and escapement:** [Escape-wheel escapement](../features/esc-escape-wheel.md), [Pawl-and-rack escapement](../features/esc-pawl-rack.md)
- **Frame and body:** [Adjustment and wear take-up](../features/frm-adjustment-wear.md)

**Parts:** ratchet wheel; loose tooth; fixed tooth; switch rocker; bent spring; roller

**Improves on:**

- Toggle-lever limit for the loose escapement tooth: spring replaces knee lever; less noise and quicker return
