## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Build an efficient, cheap, simple typewriter with perfect alignment, in which each character is brought to the print point by the least movement of the type carrier, to keep it fast and keep its momentum low.

**Solution:** A small type sleeve of nine faces of ten characters both slides along and rotates on a shaft, each of 30 keys setting a stop position (shared by three keys) and a rotation step (by key row), then striking a hammer.

**How it works:** The type sleeve (about 1 in. long, 1/2 in. diameter) slides on a square shaft and the shaft turns, so the chosen type moves in a spiral to the hammer. The 30 keys sit in three rows; each set of three keys (one per row) pushes one of ten vertical stop pins through a bent lever, and the pin's shoulder acts at a different distance on a bent lever that slides the sleeve left or right by a set amount. The second and third rows also turn the shaft one-ninth or two-ninths of a revolution through a three-pin lantern gear, and cap and figure set-keys turn it one-third or two-thirds through a loose gear and rack, with a notch to hold them down. At the end of the key travel the rear of the key lever strikes the hammer, which prints through an inking ribbon onto paper backed by a rubber band, and a spring returns the sleeve to its middle position. The same spring lever releases a rack carriage spacer (two spring-coupled gears that advance one tooth under a spring drum) and steps the ribbon spools, with a plug choosing which spool is driven to reverse the ribbon by hand.
