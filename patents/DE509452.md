## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Key locks for a counter that steps with the carriage (before the first digit, at the decimal point, after the last digit, zero key) acted on the number-key levers, which was unreliable.

**Solution:** A star-shaped stop and slide piece, pushed down in stages by a slotted track on the counter, shifts a locking slide that moves the key locks via the main drive of the calculating unit.

**How it works:** A star-shaped piece on an angle lever rides on the rail of the counter as it steps with the carriage. A bevel at the left pushes it down once (lock keys before the first digit), another edge at the decimal point and a notch let it rise and fall, and the right boundary returns it. The motion is carried by a push rod to a locking slide, which swings a double lever. That moves a locking comb that blocks the key rods and moves a shift drum into or out of the working position. A lug on the slide locks the zero key rod as well.
