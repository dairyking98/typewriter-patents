## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Type-wheel machines of the time needed a more efficient, simpler, more durable design; in particular a key needs enough throw at the wheel-positioning linkage without a long key stroke.

**Solution:** A three-row keyboard lifts levers against oppositely inclined rocking frames that turn the type wheel through racks, then a further lever stroke trips a spring hammer that strikes the wheel against the paper and ribbon.

**How it works:** Each key lifts a lever whose front end meets two oppositely inclined side bars of a rocking frame; where the lever stops fixes the frame angle, and a rack on the frame turns a pinion on the type-wheel shaft to the right character. Two such frames sit end to end on separate shafts, which gives each lever more throw while shortening the key stroke. Two upper-case keys and a character key raise the wheel shaft by different amounts so the wheel's other rows come into line. A lock pawl engages the wheel pinion, then the lever's further travel frees a spring-loaded trip hammer that strikes the reciprocating hammer, pressing the paper through the ribbon onto the type. The carriage steps through a pair of toothed wheels, a pawl rocked by the levers and a mainspring with adjustable tension, while the trip-hammer spring has a ratchet adjustment for manifolding. The paper rides a spring-tensioned feed roller via a cloth belt, with a line-space ratchet, marginal stops, a bell and a carriage-release lever.
