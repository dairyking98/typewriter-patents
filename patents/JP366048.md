## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A simple typewriter needed a type holder that stores many types compactly and keeps them from shaking loose.

**Solution:** A turnable disc with radial wedge spacers forming type-wide recesses holds several tiers of types, clamped by a notched-rib holding plate with index characters on top.

**How it works:** A disc turns freely on a vertical shaft which swings forward and back to strike a horizontal platen. Wedge spacers on the disc form parallel-walled recesses one type wide, each holding types in several tiers (for example three) with partition plates between. A holding plate on top has a downward ring rib fitting notches in the spacers, tightened by a nut and knob. Its upper face shows index rings (capitals, lower case, numerals) so the wanted type is easy to find. The shaft is raised or lowered to bring the chosen tier to the platen centre line, and each swing prints and moves the platen one space.

## Description (English translation of the Japanese text, opening)

> Type holder for a simple typewriter. Brief description of the drawings. Fig. 1 is a part-sectioned side view of the type holder in this simple typewriter. Fig. 2 is a plan cut on line A, B of Fig. 1, with part of the index plate cut away. Nature, action and effect of the utility model. This device concerns the type holder of a simple typewriter of the kind that prints on the paper on a horizontal platen (2) by swinging a vertical shaft (1) carrying the types forward and back. That is: a disc (4) is fitted, free to turn, on the vertical shaft (1), which is pivoted at its lower part (3). Round the circumference of the disc, wedge-shaped spacers (5) are fixed radially in a row, forming recesses (6) with parallel walls the width of a type. In each recess, types (7), (8), (9) are housed in several tiers one above another (for example three tiers), with partition plates (10) between the upper and lower types. On the spacers (5) notches (11) (printed 切嵌部) are made on one circle, and the upper holding plate (12) has a circular, downward-facing rib (13) that fits these notches and is tightened by a nut (14); a knob (15) is fitted at the centre of the top.
