## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typebar machines need a very light key touch yet a strong printing blow, and the type should not linger against the platen after the blow.

**Solution:** A link between key-driven actuating lever and typebar connects first at a point far from the typebar pivot (easy start) and then through a lost-motion pin-and-slot near the pivot (strong finish).

**How it works:** Depressing the key lever swings a pivoted actuating lever, whose rear arm pulls a link to the rear. A pin on the typebar heel, far from its pivot, rests on a shoulder of the link and gives the initial light-touch impulse. As the link moves, the end of a slot in it meets a second pin much nearer the typebar pivot, and this short-radius connection carries the rest of the stroke with great force. The first pin leaves its shoulder and runs along a recess until it strikes a rear shoulder just before the type reaches the platen, so the bar's own resilience frees the type from the paper and returns it quickly.
