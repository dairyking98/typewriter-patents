## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Printing machines have over 300 parts and are expensive, and machines whose types rest on an ink pad tend to soil the paper.

**Solution:** A cheap pad-inked key machine in which each key swings a V-shaped lever that throws a type arm up and onto the paper along a curve that lifts straight off again, with the platen shoved back instead of a shift mechanism and a bowed leaf spring on a rack as the escapement.

**How it works:** Keys in three staggered rows press on V-shaped levers pivoted near one end in slots of an arc plate; each lever's lower arm is jointed to a nearly horizontal type arm carrying several types, resting on a curved ink pad and guided through slots in an arc-shaped guide bar. A quick key press snaps the lever so the type arm flies up and forward over the platen and down onto the paper, leaving it vertically so as not to smear. The lever falls back by its own weight. For capitals the platen is pushed by hand back along oval slots so that the rearmost type on the arm reaches the paper, replacing a shift key. A ratchet and pawl key line-spaces the platen, and a space key presses an arc bar and tilting levers that flatten a bowed leaf spring engaging a straight rack, moving the carriage one tooth. A Z-shaped slide key lifts the spring out of the rack to release the carriage.

## Description (English translation of the Japanese text, opening)

> Specification. Printing machine. Nature and object of the invention. In this invention an arc-shaped plate, with as many slots as there are keys, is set inside the frame. In each slot is a V-shaped lever pivoted at a point near one end. The end of the lever's upper arm is linked to the stem of a key, and the end of its lower arm is pivoted to an arm carrying types. The type arm is guided, at a point near its tip, by a guide member with slots in its underside. When a key is struck, the other end of the V-shaped lever makes a sudden arc-shaped movement, so that the type is first carried up above the paper and then falls onto the paper. Instead of the ordinary shift-key mechanism (轉移控紐機構, glossed "shifting key") for capital letters and the like, a spring arrangement is used that moves the platen at right angles to its own axis. A ratchet wheel is used for line spacing, and for letter spacing a rack worked by a leaf spring. The invention relates to the kind of printing machine in which the types always lie on an ink pad and move [printed "are placed"] onto the paper when a key is worked, and its object is a printing machine of very simple construction that nevertheless prints clearly.…
