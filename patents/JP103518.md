## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A typewriter for Japanese needs both vertical and horizontal writing, but a single platen normally feeds only one way.

**Solution:** Split the platen's printing face into a left and a right area, and let a bar moving with the platen carriage rock a lever so that only the vertical feed or only the horizontal feed engages, depending on which area is at the print point.

**How it works:** Each print stroke swings links and a rocking lever that carries two engaging pieces, one for horizontal feed and one for vertical feed. For horizontal feed the lever piece tips a rocking body so a pinion leaves the platen's rack and a sector gear engages it and swings through a set arc; on return the pinion re-engages, held by a ratchet and pawl, completing one character step. Vertical feed works the same way but drives a gear on the platen shaft end. A horizontal bar fixed to the platen frame, wide over half its length and narrow over the rest, acts on a stud of the lever: the wide part presses the lever down so only the vertical piece is in reach, the narrow part frees it so only the horizontal piece is in reach. Moving the platen sideways across the boundary therefore changes the feed automatically. A stop bar with a catch stops the platen at the boundary at line end so it cannot stray into the other area, and a handle releases the catch.
