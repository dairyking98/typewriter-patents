## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Typewriters were complex, bulky and costly, hid the writing, and handled only a fixed set of characters. The inventor wants a simple, compact, cheap index machine whose last line stays in view and whose symbol section can be changed for professional use.

**Solution:** A hand-held knob turns, slides and presses a hinged type roller over a character board, with pointers showing the character, a bar locking the roller at the print position, and a spring-driven carrier that steps along a grooved rod each time the roller is raised.

**How it works:** The type roller is mounted on a sliding plate in a carrier block that hinges and travels on a grooved rod. One knob turns it, slides it endwise, and presses the whole printing head down onto the paper on a feed roll. The character board has three sections (capitals, lower case, figures and symbols), each 35 characters in 5 rows of 7, and the roller carries the same three sections in reverse; pulling the knob frees a pin and plate so the roller can be indexed one-third turn to select the section. Pointers on a rack bar that follows the roller rotation show the character on the board; when the knob is pressed, notches on the rack bar descend on a bar to lock rotation and the bar's teeth lock the endwise slide, so the character is held exactly. During the downstroke an ink roller is carried around the type roller by a segment and pinion, and a pair of cam levers (a locking dog and a jump lever) pick successive grooves on the rod so the clock-spring carrier advances one letter space per stroke.

**Parts:** type roller; operating knob; character board; pointer; rack bar; segment gear; locking bar; sliding plate; carrier block; grooved hinge rod; fulcrum rod; barrel spring; locking lever; jump lever; ink roller; feed roller; paper guard; position index

**Numbers:** 35 characters per section, 5 rows of 7; 3 sections on board and roller; 20 of 35 characters per section reached by stops without the pointers; about 1/2 in. of feed roll exposed to the types; roller limited to one-third turn per section

**Improves on:**

- contemporary keyboard typewriters: simpler, more compact, cheaper, with the last line and each word visible to the writer
