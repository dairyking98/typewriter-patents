## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Typebar machines of the time hid the writing and spaced all letters equally regardless of width. Brooks wants the typing always in sight and equal visual spacing for wide and narrow letters.

**Solution:** Typelevers carrying two or three types each slide forward on carriages above the platen to one of three stops, then tip down to print on top of the paper, while key-lever notches vary the carriage feed per letter.

**How it works:** Each key-lever drives a bell-crank and connecting rod to a type-lever mounted on a small carriage set in an arc above the platen. Each type-lever carries three types (lower case, capital, figure) on its underside. The carriage slides forward until a shoulder meets one of three stops, two of which are movable via shift levers, so the chosen type sits over the platen centre. Once the lever clears the guide plate the connecting rod tips its front end down against a spring and the type strikes the top of the paper. On release springs return the lever, dragging the type across an ink roller fed from a trough. A pinion, dog and ratchet advance the platen carriage by an amount set by where the key lever bears on a notched cross-bar, so wide and narrow letters get different feed.
