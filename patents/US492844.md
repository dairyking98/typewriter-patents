## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** On a machine where a rocking platen is pressed to a vertical type-wheel, the inking ribbon needs a simple, cheap feed with a safe tension and must not smear the paper.

**Solution:** Drive the ribbon spools by elastic pawls on a sleeve that is vibrated by the rocker frame itself, shift the sleeve to reverse, and hold the ribbon off the paper with a slotted guide.

**How it works:** The type-wheel is on a vertical spindle and an elastic roller platen on a sliding carriage is mounted on a rocking frame; a finger key tips the frame to press the paper to the type. Two ribbon spools sit on studs either side of the wheel, each with a ratchet wheel, and the two ratchets face opposite ways. Two thin elastic pawls on a sleeve on the rocker shaft are pushed by the underside of the rocker frame at each key press and advance one ratchet one tooth. Sliding the sleeve along the shaft by hand engages the other pawl and winds the ribbon back the other way. A nut clamping a tension disk to a fixed shoulder sets spool tension to a fixed amount. The ribbon runs through slots in a guide between the type and paper, with a central aperture where the type strikes.
