## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typewriters offered for coin-operated public use need a way to be locked after a limited number of keystrokes.

**Solution:** A add-on box with a coin catcher, a keystroke counter driven from the ribbon transport, and a spring-loaded locking rod that is released to lock the machine after the paid number of strokes.

**How it works:** At rest a locking rod sits between the key switch and a stop screw and blocks the machine. A correct coin in the slot is held by a coin catcher; pulling its lever turns an angle lever that shifts the switch lever and locking rod aside, tensioning a spring, and a pawl latches them open. The coin then drops into a collection box. Each keystroke advances a four-digit counter via the ribbon transport lever. At 999 strokes a cam disc on the fourth counter wheel lifts the pawl and the spring returns the locking rod to the blocking position. The operator can also lock early and reset the counter, and the count is visible through a window.
