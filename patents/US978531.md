## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** A typebar needs to start gently but reach the platen fast enough to print firmly, and to return quickly afterward.

**Solution:** The key link engages the bell crank first by a hook near its tip, then by a pin-and-slot connection nearer the pivot, so leverage changes mid-stroke to accelerate the bar.

**How it works:** A key lever drives an actuating link joined to one arm of a bell crank whose other arm moves the typebar. At the start of the stroke the link's hook bears on the extremity of the bell-crank arm, a long lever with slow, easy motion. Partway through, a pin-and-slot lost-motion connection nearer the pivot takes up the load, shortening the lever so the bar is accelerated for the late part of the stroke. The change is nearly imperceptible to the typist. At the end of the stroke a shoulder on the link meets an extension of the bell crank and starts the quick return of the bar.
