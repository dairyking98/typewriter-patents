## Summary

**Kind:** type-element machine · **Subject (patent classes):** Typewriters or selective printing mechanisms characterised by the mounting, arrangement or disposition of the types or dies with types or dies mounted on carriers rotatable for selection the plane of the type or die face being parallel to the axis of rotation, e.g. with type on the periphery of cylindrical carriers

## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** The text states no specific shortcoming; it presents an improved typewriter in which a full keyboard selects a type segment without typebars.

**Solution:** Each key throws up a location pin into a slotted arm on a sliding type-segment carriage; the carriage then moves back to the platen, and the pin acts as a pivot that rotates the segment to the chosen character as it travels.

**How it works:** A sleeve carrying a two-row type segment, a notched locking plate and two curved slotted location arms slides on a guide bar and is held forward by a spring. Pressing a key first lifts its location pin through the slot of a location arm. Further key travel pushes a cross bar and bell-crank that draw the carriage rearward. The pin now acts as a fixed pivot, so the arm and segment rotate by an amount set by the pin position and the desired character comes in line with the impression point. At the end of travel a V-shaped stud enters a notch in the locking plate to align the character exactly, and the segment strikes the paper held vertically against a star-shaped rubber-pointed impression plate. A bar on the standard trips an escapement lever as the carriage moves back, feeding the paper carriage one tooth. The upper case key raises the paper carriage and impression plate to the upper row of type.
