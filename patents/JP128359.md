## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** In a typewriter where the printing mechanism moves over a flat type magazine, the jolt of the paper cylinder's sideways feed during the print stroke interferes with the type's return to its place.

**Solution:** Arm the sideways-feed pawl at the end of the key's forward stroke but only release the actual feed near the end of the key's return or after it, with most of the key's travel free of the feed device.

**How it works:** A movable feed pawl, spring-loaded on a base pawl lever, engages the sideways-feed rack on the cylinder part. A bell crank, whose end lies between two contact pieces on a vertical rod moved by the printing key, bears on a slope of the base pawl lever. On the forward stroke, after a free gap, the rod lifts the bell crank, which lifts the base pawl so its pawl enters the rack and the movable pawl leaves it and shifts one space ready to feed. On the return stroke a second free gap lets the bell crank stay up until the key is almost home, and then the pawls drop and the feed takes place. A variant fits a dashpot to the rod so that the feed comes slightly after the key has returned.
