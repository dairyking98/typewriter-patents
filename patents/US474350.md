## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** The inventor's earlier Munson sliding-sleeve machines (US411066, US446394) wrapped the paper around a storage cylinder to clear the hammer, which was costly and awkward, and the machine was complicated and bulky.

**Solution:** Mount the impression hammer on a telescoping goose-neck frame so paper passes around it and the paper cylinder can be dropped, and simplify the key-to-sleeve rotate-and-slide linkage.

**How it works:** A type sleeve with nine faces of ten characters each (three cases of three faces) slides and rotates on a splined shaft. Thirty keys in three rows pick a position: each key set of three shares one rock-shaft that slides the sleeve by a lever amount and sets stop-pins in registering notches; the middle and rear rows also turn the sleeve one-ninth turn either way via cross-bars, a cam and a swinging-arm gear train. Cap and figure keys turn it one-third or two-thirds turn through a swinging segment gear, with a latch to hold them down. The key rock-shaft also moves a hammer lever that strikes through a slotted arm so the hammer flies free and rebounds. It also feeds the ribbon ratchet and trips the escapement. The hammer sits on a sliding goose-neck frame that telescopes into the main frame for carrying, coupling to the main-frame lever by a conical pin when slid out.
