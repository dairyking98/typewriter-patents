## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typebars need to be light and stiff, and their bearings thin enough for many bars in a small space while guiding the type accurately.

**Solution:** Fold a punched sheet-metal blank with a U-section rib into a rigid typebar, and mount it on an adjustable ball-bearing hub in a thin hanger set into a radial slotted segment.

**How it works:** A strip blank forms two typebars joined at the middle; folding it brings the shanks together around a socket for the type stem, riveted at the socket and ends. The widened hub plates are forked and clamp a perforated disk with a tubular eyelet rivet, its edge grooved as a ball race. The hanger carries a ring with a beveled eye that forms the matching race, and ball bearings run between them. Screws draw the ring inward to take up shake. A link from the key lever attaches near the hub; hangers drop into radial notches in a segment covered by a plate and are held by set screws, adjustable radially.

**Features:** [ELEM:typebar](../features/elem-typebar.md), [ELEM:element-construction](../features/elem-element-construction.md), [FRM:adjustment-wear](../features/frm-adjustment-wear.md)

**Parts:** typebar blank; rib; socket; rivet; hub plate; disk; eyelet; ball race; ball bearing; hanger; ring; link; segment; cover plate; set screw

**Improves on:**

- heavier typebars with wide bearings: thin folded bar and thin ball bearing mean more bars in less space
