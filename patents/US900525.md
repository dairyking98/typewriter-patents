## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Pressing typewriter keys takes effort, and earlier electric key devices need a contact pin on the key or separate contact buttons that reduce typing speed.

**Solution:** Each key carries two contact plates that the typist bridges with a metal thimble worn on the finger, energizing an electromagnet whose core pulls the key lever down.

**How it works:** A key lever has two contact pieces on its top. A thimble on the finger bridges them when the key is touched, closing a normal-open circuit from battery through the key lever, the contact, thimble, a second contact and wire to an electromagnet. The magnet attracts a core or plunger attached to the key lever, which fully depresses it so the type prints. The slightest touch is enough.

**Features:** [IMP:electric](../features/imp-electric.md), [SEL:keyboard-full](../features/sel-keyboard-full.md)

**Parts:** key lever; contact plate; thimble; electromagnet; core; battery; wire

**Improves on:**

- electric key devices using a contact pin or contact buttons: touch of a thimble on the key itself is enough
