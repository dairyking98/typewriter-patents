## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Shift locks on typebar machines needed a separate key or a distant key, causing confusion; the inventor wants a simple, cheap lock that works with either of two shift keys and does not release accidentally.

**Solution:** A spring-tempered lock lever sits just behind the shift key so the same finger can depress shift alone or shift plus lock, setting a latch that holds the shifted platen frame until either shift key is pressed again.

**How it works:** Pressing a shift lever rocks a shaft that turns an eccentric and lifts the pivoted shift frame carrying the platen to its second printing position. An L-shaped latch pivoted beside the left shift lever has a notch that catches a pin on the rock-shaft arm. A lock lever, pivoted on the frame cross-bar and spring-held, lies just behind the shift key; if the fingertip also presses it, its elastic rear end lifts the pin past the latch, which springs under the pin and holds the frame shifted. On release the lock lever keeps the latch engaged until the shifted weight rests on it. Pressing either shift lever lifts the pin off the latch so it swings clear and the frame returns. The latch and lock lever can be mounted on the right side instead, and an optional stud on the shift lever positively blocks the latch for tabulating shocks.

**Parts:** shift frame; platen; oscillating eccentric; rock shaft; shift lever; L-shaped latch; lock lever; pin on rock arm; locking stud; comb plate; spring
