## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A Shimada-type machine needs to print either in vertical columns or horizontal lines, so the paper cylinder must be turned for character spacing or slid along its axis for it, at will.

**Solution:** A pair of rocking levers joined by a detachable hook, plus a bell-crank piece driving a push bar and rack, lets the same print and space buttons either rotate the cylinder or traverse it.

**How it works:** An upper rocking lever on the cylinder frame carries a pawl for a saw-tooth wheel on the cylinder axis and a hook hanging down; a lower rocking lever on the base is linked to the print button and the space button. With the hook engaged, pressing the button rocks the upper lever and the pawl advances the saw wheel one tooth, turning the cylinder one character width. Holding the upper lever down by a stop disengages the hook and the pawl and releases the feed pawl onto a rack; the lower lever then moves a push bar through a bell-crank piece, so the cylinder traverses one rack tooth per stroke for horizontal lines. A spring-loaded round-headed detent on the rack gives accurate clicks when moving by hand, and moving the lever up and down sets line spacing.

## Description (English translation of the Japanese text, opening)

> Specification. Shimada-type printing machine. Nature and object of the invention. This invention relates to the Shimada-type printing machine, in which a rocking lever mounted on the paper-cylinder frame and a rocking lever mounted on the frame of the base are set parallel, one above the other; a hook hanging from the upper rocking lever can be engaged with and released from the lower rocking lever; and a pawl is provided which, engaging a pin on one end of the push bar that pushes the cylinder along and a pin on the lower rocking lever, acts as a bell crank. Its object is to give the paper cylinder, at will, either a turning motion or a back-and-forth motion along its axis, so that it can print in vertical columns or in horizontal lines.
