## Summary

**Kind:** type-element machine · **Subject (patent classes):** Typewriters or selective printing mechanisms characterised by the mounting, arrangement or disposition of the types or dies with types or dies on spherical, truncated-spherical, or like surfaces

**In the patent’s own words:** Object of the invention is to provide improved means for raising and lowering the arc to present different type-faces to the printing-surface, as well as to provide means for moving the platen-roller away from the type, as when manifolding or when writing upon thick paper.

**How it works (reading):** Mount short type-levers on a resilient arc that is rotated about a vertical shaft by cam slots on the key-levers and raised for case shift, and strike the selected lever with a spring hammer tripped during the key stroke.

## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Inventor wants a simple full-keyboard typewriter whose type are carried on one pivoted arc turned by key cams, struck by a tripped hammer, with case shift and the ability to write on thick or manifold paper.

**Solution:** Mount short type-levers on a resilient arc that is rotated about a vertical shaft by cam slots on the key-levers and raised for case shift, and strike the selected lever with a spring hammer tripped during the key stroke.

**How it works:** Each key-lever has a plate with a differently shaped cam slot that engages one of several diverging rods on a tube around a vertical shaft; the key's descent turns the tube and the squared shaft, rotating the arc so the chosen type-lever faces the paper. Type-levers are rigidly fixed at their lower ends to a spring-steel segment and carry lower-case, capital and figure characters in vertical rows. A shift key lifts the shaft by a step lever so the capital or figure row is opposite the paper, with a catch for shift lock. The first part of the key stroke releases catches holding the rod; a cross-bar under the keys then moves a weighted dog that cocks and releases a spring hammer, which strikes the type-lever, and the segment's torsion springs it back. The same cross-bar drives two weighted pawls on a feed rack for half-space steps each on descent and ascent, so printing also spaces the carriage. A space key and line key work a third pawl; an ink roller inks the types; the whole arc and shaft can be lifted out and replaced by another for a different type style, and the platen can be lowered to the rear for thick paper.
