## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Ordinary typewriter escapements release the carriage late, so they are slow for the ordinary operator, and the carriage spring pushes back on the type bars and keys too little.

**Solution:** A dog rocker carrying a spacing dog and a holding dog releases the carriage at the start of the type's return stroke and can be converted to a reverse-acting escapement.

**How it works:** An escapement wheel with teeth turns in a vertical plane under the carriage's main spring. A dog rocker pivoted on posts carries a spacing dog on a cross pivot and a holding dog on a rock shaft. A spring throws the spacing dog toward the wheel and shoulders and stops limit its travel; a return spring brings the rocker to normal. A wire from the key mechanism pulls the rocker so the holding dog catches a tooth before the spacing dog leaves it, letting the wheel advance one tooth. The wire can be moved from one arm of the rock shaft to the other to change the timing so the carriage is released before the type reach the platen.

**Parts:** escapement wheel; dog rocker; spacing dog; holding dog; rock shaft; arm; wire; bell crank; spring; stop; post
