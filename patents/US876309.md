## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Underwood's ribbon mechanism needs a ribbon guide whose throw suits ribbon width and number of copies, and a ribbon reverse that is triggered reliably when a spool runs out.

**Solution:** Drive both ribbon spools from the escapement through a sliding shaft with bevel gearing, reverse the direction with spring-loaded push fingers released by the ribbon itself, and move the ribbon guide by an adjustable-fulcrum lever.

**How it works:** Each key stroke moves the universal bar and the escapement. The escapement shaft drives, through pawl-connected pinions, a toothed wheel and bevel gears, a transverse shaft that carries collars and bevel pinions. Only one pinion of the pair meshes at a time with a gear that turns one ribbon spool through bevel gearing. When the ribbon is nearly unwound, a loop on its end pulls a guide aside and frees a latch. A spring-loaded push finger then shifts the transverse shaft endwise to engage the other spool gear, which reverses the ribbon. Depressing the carriage shift key lifts bars with hooks that reset the push fingers. A lever linked to the universal bar swings the ribbon guide at each stroke, and the lever's fulcrum can be raised or lowered to vary the throw for wide ribbon or many copies. A detent ball in two grooves holds the reverse shaft in position, and a handle turns either spool by hand.
