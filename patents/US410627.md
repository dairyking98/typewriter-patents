## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** The Becker pivoted-segment index machine (US350717) could print only one case (upper); the inventor wants upper and lower case from the same type segment.

**Solution:** The segment carries two concentric rows of characters (upper and lower case) and is shifted toward or away from the printing lever by a key-operated shifting bar that slides a sub-section of the carriage at right angles to its travel.

**How it works:** A flexible rubber type sheet on a pivoted segment carries concentric rows of characters; the operator swings the segment by hand over an index plate so the wanted character is under a plunger on the printing lever, centred by a pin entering one of a series of holes in the segment. Pressing the rocking lever depresses the printing lever against a spring, which presses the type with the plunger onto the paper and, through a dog on a ratchet bar, moves the carriage one tooth. The carriage is in two sections, one sliding on the ratchet bar and carrying the printing lever, the other carrying the segment and index plate, sliding on dovetail guides toward or from the bar. A sliding bar engaging forked lugs on that section is moved by rods, a spring-returned rock-shaft and a key lever, so the segment is held in the lower-case position by default and pushed back to bring the upper-case row under the plunger; two pins serve the centring holes in each position. One hand works the shift, space lever and rocking lever while the other turns the segment.
