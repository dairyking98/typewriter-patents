## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** A simple, cheap child's or light-duty typewriter, accurate and fairly quick, that can also be turned into a book typewriter.

**Solution:** An endless type belt on two hand-turned spools rides on a traveling plate, and a single hammer lever presses the aligned type through a guideway opening onto the paper while the same stroke advances the plate one tooth.

**How it works:** An endless belt carries the type on its outer face and indications on its inner face, running over two spools, one with a hand wheel. The operator turns the belt until the wanted character is under a pointer and opposite an opening in a flanged type guideway. Ink pads on the traveling plate touch the types as the belt moves. Pressing the lever drives a stud on its underside against the belt's inner face, pushing that type through the opening onto the paper. The lever carries a feed pawl that steps the traveling plate one tooth along a rack on the carrier plate, which spaces the letters. A front handle on the pawl disengages it for free movement. A spring retracts the pawl and raises the lever. The bed plate with printing devices lifts off the platen standards and can be used as a book typewriter.
