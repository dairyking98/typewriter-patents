## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** The ten-finger system forces hands to bend outward at the wrist on a standard keyboard, causing fatigue, and earlier angled or split keyboards were clumsy or still unnatural.

**Solution:** Split the keyboard into two halves set so the same-height rows form an open angle toward the platen, leaving a trapezoidal gap, with keys stepped outward, shift and caps-lock keys in the gap, and a space bar for both thumbs.

**How it works:** The two key halves sit side by side with an isosceles trapezoid free field between them, the top edge hardly wider than the key spacing. Keys in each row are stepped outward from the centre so fingertips lie in a staircase. Shift and caps-lock keys are placed between the halves, and the shift key closes the gap at the bottom. Each half has one key fewer in a row, and the gap is put at the outer end of the bottom row to give the little finger a tactile reference. The space bar is used by both thumbs.

**Features:** [SEL:keyboard-full](../features/sel-keyboard-full.md), [ESC:space-key](../features/esc-space-key.md), [SHIFT:lock](../features/shift-lock.md)

**Parts:** keyboard half; key row; shift key; caps lock key; space bar; key lever

**Numbers:** 48 keys besides the space bar; four key rows; about 70 degree row slope

**Improves on:**

- roof-shaped angled keyboard: keeps keys horizontal and vertically pressed
- widely split keyboard: narrower gap and open-angle halves
