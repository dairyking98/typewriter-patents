## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Improving the inventor's earlier typebar machine (US382036): carriage spacing needs a way to step back one space to correct mistakes and to return the whole carriage, and line spacing should be adjustable.

**Solution:** A rocking frame under the key levers raises a feather-cam actuating pawl for forward spacing, while two cam-lever-controlled holding pawls let the spring-driven carriage step back letter by letter or run fully home, and a toothed escapement wheel with three rows gives three line spacings.

**How it works:** Pressing any key lever or the space bar rocks a frame whose sliding plate carries a spring-pressed feather-cam; on its return stroke it pushes the carriage ratchet one tooth left against a barrel spring while a holding pawl catches the next tooth. Holding the rocking frame down lifts the feather-cam clear, so a backspace key works the two holding pawls alternately and the spring walks the carriage right one space per press, and a second key lifts one pawl for a full return. Typebars slide in converging square holes, pushed by bell-crank levers through flexible links. The ribbon holder rises just ahead of the type and a ratchet advances the ribbon spool. A paper roller turned by a spring is stopped by a three-row escapement wheel selected by a thumb nut for the line space, and a bell and adjustable carriage stop work at the line end.
