## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Typebar machines wore one spot on the ink pad and needed a way to print two characters per bar (upper and lower case) without shifting the carriage.

**Solution:** A key-driven ink pad that steps along an arc at every keystroke and reverses itself at the ends, plus two-character typebars whose throw is changed by moving a fulcrum plate with a case key.

**How it works:** Each key lever drives a typebar through a link; the bar carries two types and notches that lock into a toothed lock plate at the print point. A link on the bar rides on one of two fulcrum wires; a special case key swings a movable arc plate that removes the stops and changes the fulcrum, giving the bar extra throw so the second character reaches the platen. Separately, the universal bail pulls down spring-mounted posts carrying an arc-shaped ink pad carrier, and a pawl on a rack bar advances the pad one tooth per keystroke. At each end of travel a lug trips a double-armed lever that swaps the pawls and reverses the direction.

**Features:** [ELEM:typebar](../features/elem-typebar.md), [SEL:keyboard-full](../features/sel-keyboard-full.md), [SHIFT:rotary](../features/shift-rotary.md), [ELEM:alignment-lock](../features/elem-alignment-lock.md), [INK:pad](../features/ink-pad.md), [IMP:element-to-platen](../features/imp-element-to-platen.md)

**Parts:** typebar; link; fulcrum wire; movable arc plate; lock plate; case key; ink pad; ink pad carrier; rack bar; feed pawl; double-armed reversing lever; universal bail

**Numbers:** two types per bar; fourteen teeth on lock plate, each receiving three typebars
