## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Ordinary typewriters cannot write conveniently in bound books; the machine should travel over the book and avoid a separate shift mechanism.

**Solution:** A book-writing typewriter whose printing carriage runs on a frame resting on the book, with typearms in two opposed rows (capitals and lowercase) joined to common keys by evener levers and alternately locked by a sliding frame.

**How it works:** A frame rests on the book on a table and is height-adjusted by rack posts; a traveling frame crosses it, and the printing carriage runs on that frame, drawn by a spring-drum tape. Type-arms are strung on wires in radial slots of an annular base, capitals on one side and lowercase on the other, each key joined to a corresponding pair by an evener lever. A sliding frame with segmental flanges hooks the heels of one series so only the other can strike; the case key shifts it. A pinion on the carriage is checked by a dog escapement that a universal bar releases at each keystroke, advancing one letter. Line feed moves the traveling frame down by a ratcheted rack pinion when the carriage returns. The ribbon is on two drums and is shifted sideways by helical-grooved spindles driven by the rack, and wound longitudinally by a ratchet when the carriage returns.
