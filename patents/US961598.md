## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Shift keys on a platen-shift typewriter need a way to be held down (locked) and released without separate lock keys.

**Solution:** Each shift key tablet is pivoted so a touch on one side just depresses it, while a touch on the other side rocks it and hooks a finger into a notched plate that holds it down until touched on the opposite side.

**How it works:** Two shift-key levers couple through links to a double bell lever on a spindle, whose arms are joined by adjustable rods to a frame pivoted on the base that carries the rods the platen carriage runs on; the frame rocks in either direction for capitals and figures, with travel limited by adjustable stops on yoke rods. Each key is a tablet pivoted on its lever and can rock sideways. A hooked finger on the tablet engages a notched striking plate on the base when the key is pressed on one side, holding the lever down. Pressing the other side rocks it back and releases it, and a spring keeps the finger out of engagement normally.

**Parts:** pivoted frame; platen carriage; traversing rod; shift-key lever; double bell lever; link; adjustable rod; yoke rod; adjustable stop; key tablet; hooked finger; notched striking plate; spring
