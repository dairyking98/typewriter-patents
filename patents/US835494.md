## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** On a typebar machine with a tilting platen, the operator cannot see where the next character will print; earlier pointers swung up automatically with the platen carrier, which is a disadvantage.

**Solution:** A light spring pointer on the shifting frame is swung up by a hand button, independent of platen tilt, to show the print point, then springs back flat out of the way.

**How it works:** The pointer pivots on a support rod at the front of the shifting frame, and a stop pin and a rest stop limit its swing. A connecting wire joins it to a push rod with a finger button at the side of the machine; a coil spring between the button and the frame end pulls the pointer back down. The right hand pushes the button to raise the pointer to vertical close to the platen, while the left hand releases the escapement and moves the carriage until the pointer sits on the wanted print point. Releasing the button lets the spring drop the pointer flush with the frame, after which the platen is lowered to the writing position. The pointer is of light spring material so it is not damaged if the platen is lowered before it is released.

**Features:** [PAP:line-indicator](../features/pap-line-indicator.md), [USE:accessory](../features/use-accessory.md), [FRM:writing-visible](../features/frm-writing-visible.md), [FRM:few-parts](../features/frm-few-parts.md)

**Parts:** pointer; pivot support; stop pin; rest stop; connecting wire; push rod; finger button; coil spring; shifting frame; tilting platen carrier

**Improves on:**

- pointers that swing up automatically with the tilting platen carrier: swing is operator-controlled and independent of platen motion, so it also suits non-tilting platens
