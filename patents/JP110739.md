## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Quiet typewriters of the day needed complicated switching links or many types per bar; the blow of long-travelling typebars is noisy.

**Solution:** Two-type typebars hung on two links rest on a 90-degree arc near platen height and travel almost straight to the print point, freed from the key just before impact so they print by momentum.

**How it works:** Each typebar carries two types and is carried on a front and rear link forming a four-bar linkage. A key lever drives a starting lever, which turns a driving lever and a weighted intermediate link. The starting lever is stopped by a pin just before printing, and the driving links carry on by the stored momentum of a swinging weight to print. Stops on the levers prevent overrun and rebound on return, and a buffer checks the typebar. In the third form the bar swings in arcs at the middle and rear while the type moves nearly straight, guided by a comb, with the link centre-lines crossing on the platen tangent plane.

## Description (English translation of the Japanese text, opening)

> Specification. Silent-printing typewriter. Nature and object of the invention. This invention relates to a typewriter whose types print quietly. It is characterised in that typebars each carrying two types are each mounted on two links so that, at rest, the types lie side by side on a 90° arc in a plane nearly level with the centre of the platen, the radius of the arc being determined by half the number of types, the width of the type blocks and the least gap between them, and so that the types move from rest to the printing point in a nearly straight line; and in that each typebar is worked from one key through intermediate links in such a way that, just before the printing point, the type is freed from the action of the key and prints by momentum. The object is a typewriter that greatly reduces noise, prints fast, and is simple in construction and durable. Brief description of the drawings. The drawings show three embodiments.
