## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Hand-struck Japanese-script printing machines tire the typist, who must supply the whole printing force over long hours.

**Solution:** Pressing the handle only engages a clutch between a constantly running motor shaft and an intermittent shaft whose striker flicks the tail of the printing mechanism's starting lever, so the motor supplies the blow.

**How it works:** The handle, through a link, lever and bell crank, pushes the motor-driven main shaft so its cone friction clutch engages the intermittent shaft. A striker on that shaft turns, hits the tail of the starting lever of the printing mechanism, knocks it aside and runs on. The starting lever sets off the printing lever, which strikes forward to the paper cylinder. Engaging the clutch faster or gentler varies the striking speed, so characters with many or few strokes print evenly; the mechanism returns freely, independent of the handle, allowing fast repeated printing. Type selection and picking are left to existing machines.

## Description (English translation of the Japanese text, opening)

> Specification. Japanese-script electric printing machine. Summary of the nature and purpose of the invention. This invention relates to an electric printing machine in which, linked to the pressing down of a handle, a "clutch" is engaged between a main shaft kept turning all the time by an electric motor and an intermittent shaft; a striker projects from the intermittent shaft, and the drive part of the type-printing mechanism is struck and started by this turning striker. Its purpose is that pressing down the printing handle needs only the slight force of engaging a clutch, while the power of the printing itself comes from electric force that strikes the tail of a lever in an instant, so saving the typist's labour and making fast printing over long hours easy.
