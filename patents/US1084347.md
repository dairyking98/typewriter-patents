## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Shifting a typewriter carriage between several case positions produces impact and noise, which defeats a silent typewriter.

**Solution:** Shift the platen carriage through a roller riding a cam path with dwells, driven by a spring-centred rock shaft from two shift levers, so motion is smooth and the shift positions are fixed by the dwells.

**How it works:** Two shift levers on a cross rod each have an arm that engages one end of a rocker member on a rock shaft, so depressing either lever rocks the shaft in opposite directions. The rock shaft moves a swinging lever whose roller travels a fixed cam groove with two cams at different distances from the carriage and slight dwells. Moving the roller one way shifts the carriage to a second position, the other way to a third, and the dwells fix each position and tolerate slight roller motion. A spring-loaded tilt bar with a rubber cushion presses on the rocker member and returns the shaft to centre when the levers are released. Variants use a rocking cam on a double lever or a reciprocating cam, with cushioned stops.

**Features:** [SHIFT:platen](../features/shift-platen.md), [SHIFT:triple](../features/shift-triple.md), [FRM:adjustment-wear](../features/frm-adjustment-wear.md)

**Parts:** platen carriage; shift lever; rock shaft; rocker member; fixed cam; cam groove; swinging lever; roller; tilt bar; leaf spring; cushion; cushioned stop

**Numbers:** three carriage positions (initial, second, third)

**Improves on:**

- earlier carriage-shifting mechanisms: no impact or concussion during the shift, so silent
