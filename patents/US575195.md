## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Anderson's earlier patent (11 Feb 1896) returned the carriage by the same propelling spring that advances it, but the machine was complicated and costly.

**Solution:** A simplified version of the self-returning spring carriage, shown on a Remington No. 2, where a reservoir spring restores energy to the propelling spring at each return.

**How it works:** A drum on an arbor takes the place of the usual spring drum, with a strap to the carriage, and the Remington escapement (rock-shaft, dogs, universal bar) is kept. At the end of a line the carriage is returned automatically by the propelling spring through a pinion and power-wheel on the rack-bar, while energy is transferred from a second, occasionally wound spring. A further device spaces the lines automatically on the return.

**Features:** [ESC:spring-drum](../features/esc-spring-drum.md), [ESC:carriage-return](../features/esc-carriage-return.md), [ESC:pawl-rack](../features/esc-pawl-rack.md), [PAP:line-space](../features/pap-line-space.md), [ELEM:typebar](../features/elem-typebar.md)

**Parts:** frame; rack-bar; escapement dog; spring drum; strap; propelling spring; reservoir spring; pinion; power-wheel; platen carriage

**Numbers:** 6 sheets of drawings

**Improves on:**

- US(Anderson, 11 Feb 1896): fewer parts and lower cost while keeping the self-returning carriage
