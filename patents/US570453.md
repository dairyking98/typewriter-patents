## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typewriter carriages need shifting mechanism and manual paper feeding, and the writing is not visible.

**Solution:** A hollow rotating cylinder takes paper from an internal bobbin through a slot, with a spiral-grooved shaft for spacing and a dog for line feed.

**How it works:** A shaft with a spiral groove on one half and a slanted stepped groove on the other carries a loose hollow cylinder. A sprung pawl follows the groove as the collar turns. The cylinder has a quarter-inch slot between two steel rollers and a rubber roller, and a bobbin of continuous (perforated) paper inside feeds the sheet out round the cylinder. A pinion on the rubber roller meshes with a large pinion that slides on the collar by a rod, engaging or releasing a ratchet and dog to feed the paper.

**Features:**

- **Paper and carriage:** [Roller platen](../features/pap-platen-roller.md), [Feed rollers and paper release](../features/pap-feed-rollers.md), [Line spacing](../features/pap-line-space.md)
- **Frame and body:** [Visible writing](../features/frm-writing-visible.md)

**Parts:** shaft; spiral groove; hollow cylinder; collar; handle bar; large pinion; ratchet rib; dog; pawl; steel roller; rubber roller; paper bobbin; spring

**Numbers:** slot about 1/4 in. wide; steel rollers about 3/32 in. diameter; bearings project 3/8 in. and 3/16 in.
