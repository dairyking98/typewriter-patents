## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Type-bar brackets sit so close together that the adjustable cone bearings are hard to reach and the bracket walls can spread when adjusted.

**Solution:** Slotted cone bearings adjusted with a tapered edgewise tool, a threaded tie-bar locked by a punch mark, and bar pivots angled so three bars reach one print point.

**How it works:** Each type-bar has a transverse shaft with coned ends, running in the bracket wall at one end and in a screw-threaded slotted cone bearing in the other wall. A thin tapered lever entered edgewise in the slot turns the bearing. A threaded tie-bar between the walls is cut flush and locked against turning by a depression punched across rod and wall. The shafts of the three bars in a bracket are out of parallel so all three types come to one printing point and enter the bar lock correctly. Lugs on the bracket base are formed by pressing down depressions.

**Parts:** type-bar bracket; type-bar; cone bearing; tie-bar; bearing shaft; adjusting tool; bridge; bar lock; bracket base lug

**Numbers:** three bars per bracket
