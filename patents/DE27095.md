## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** The writer wants to see the finished script at once while typing, which front-ribbon or hidden-strike machines do not allow.

**Solution:** A narrow ink ribbon runs under a glass plate across the index plate at right angles to the writing line, so the letter just printed is visible, and a ratchet driven by the type rod advances it at each stroke.

**How it works:** The ribbon is a long narrow rolled strip of ink-impregnated paper or silk in a capsule under the index plate and is drawn out through a slot in its rim, over a glass plate, to a feed roller. A ratchet wheel on the roller axis is turned by a pawl on a bracket that is pressed down every time the type rod is depressed, so fresh ribbon lies under each type. The sheet being written lies on a printing block and is held flat by a parallelogram of angle levers and rollers. In the extended position of the lever the arm lifts and the roller does not touch the feed roller, and in the depressed position the rollers close and the sheet is pushed on one line height. The text is a fragment and gives no more on the type carrier or index.
