## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Pins held by springs in a line-spacing clutch can go lame so some fail to engage.

**Solution:** Clutch pins slide freely in a clutch disc and are reset to equal length by butting against a fixed knob when the disc is withdrawn.

**How it works:** The line-spacing wheel turns freely on the platen axle and has holes on a circle. A clutch disc on the axle carries a different number of axially sliding pins on the same circle. Pushing the disc against the wheel makes some pins enter holes while others are pushed back. Withdrawing the disc lets the platen knob turn freely, and the pins hit the fixed knob and are reset to equal length. Because pin and hole counts differ, engagement shifts each time, so platen wear spreads around the circumference.
