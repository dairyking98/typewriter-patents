## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typewriters could not write on the pages of a bound book, and the printed line was often hidden from the operator.

**Solution:** A small type-wheel machine that travels on guide bars over a flat table, with a book page held under the table, leaving the printed line in view.

**How it works:** A flat metal table on short legs carries a rectangular frame on a rock-shaft so the whole typewriter can be raised to place a book or sheet; half the open book is strapped beneath the table with the leaf passing over the top. The typewriter frame slides on parallel guide-bars toward and away from the operator. A light type-wheel about 1.5 in. diameter, of hard rubber with two grooved flanges, carries metal type with vulcanized blocks and is shifted and given a stop mechanism for its throw, a feed chain moves the machine across the page.

**Features:**

- **Shift and cases:** [Shift by sliding the element](../features/shift-axial.md)
- **Impression:** [Element struck to the platen](../features/imp-element-to-platen.md)
- **Spacing and escapement:** [Moving print head](../features/esc-moving-print-head.md)
- **Paper and carriage:** [Flat bed or book typewriter](../features/pap-flat-bed.md)
- **Frame and body:** [Visible writing](../features/frm-writing-visible.md)
- **Uses:** [Book typewriter](../features/use-book-typewriter.md)

**Parts:** table; rock-shaft; guide bar; typewriter frame; type-wheel; grooved flange; type block; feed chain; stop mechanism; book straps

**Numbers:** type-wheel about 1.5 in. diameter; legs about 3 in. long
