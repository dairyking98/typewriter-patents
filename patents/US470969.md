## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A small, simple, cheap index typewriter was wanted, with capitals and figures and good manifolding, improving on the Wagner patents.

**Solution:** A pointer slid along a two-row chart turns a drum of loose notched type through a rack, and a printing bar then locks the drum, pushes the top type out through a guide to meet a swinging platen.

**How it works:** The operator slides a pointer along a keyboard chart; the pointer is fixed to a comb bar and rack that turns a gear on the type drum, so the chosen type comes uppermost. Types sit in groups of three (lower case, capital, figure) around the drum, and two shift levers move the chart sideways by one or two type widths to select the case. Pressing the printing bar drops a tooth between comb teeth and an ejector tooth between gear teeth, locking the drum. The bar also drives the ejector, which engages a notch in the uppermost type and pushes it through a guide plate, inking it with a rising felt roller, while the platen carrier swings toward it. A hook plate drops behind the ejector to take the blow, and on release the carriage feeds one space by a dog on a rack.
