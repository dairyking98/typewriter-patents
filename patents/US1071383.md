## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Conventional escapements can skip under rapid typing, and their let-off timing varies from tooth to tooth because of a warped wheel or a loose mounting.

**Solution:** An escapement wheel whose teeth are separate, loosely pivoted fingers that run against one fixed abutment and are released one at a time by a single key-driven actuator.

**How it works:** The spring drum turns a shaft through a lost-motion gear pair, and an escapement wheel on the shaft carries twelve radial fingers in a slotted box, each free to swing fore and aft. A fixed stop plate catches the forward-positioned tooth, and a fixed cam flares each tooth back into the plane of the stop as it comes round. A key stroke, through a universal bar and rocker, makes a pusher (the actuator) shove the engaged tooth rearward out of the stop's plane, and the spring drum turns the wheel one letter space until the next tooth strikes the stop. If the actuator fails to retract, a cam face on it lets the advancing tooth push it aside, and it springs back up for the next key.
