## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typewriters of the time were complicated and expensive; the inventor wants a simple, cheap, easy-to-use writer.

**Solution:** A hand arm swung over a flat dial chart turns a rubber-typed wheel through a bevel gear and sector, and a separate lever fires a hammer arm through a slot at the selected resilient type finger while also stepping the carriage.

**How it works:** The operator swings arm G over a horizontal dial carrying the alphabets; a segment gear and bevel gear turn the type wheel so that the matching type finger lies opposite the slot in the stand. Pressing lever A1 pushes a sliding pawl on the carriage rack, moving the carriage one tooth; near the end of the stroke the carriage trips lever M, which swings hammer arm L through the aligned slot in the wheel's guide plate to strike the rubber type finger against the paper on a bar. If the wheel is slightly out of line, bevelled slot edges cam it into register. At the end of the stroke a wedge stop locks the carriage while printing. A separate lever B1 spaces without printing. Ink comes from felt pads painted with ink that the type rubs past; the paper roller has detents for even line spacing, and a bell rings at the carriage end.
