## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** The single oscillating escapement dog of a Remington wears quickly from striking the carriage rack.

**Solution:** Replace the oscillating dog with a toothed steel disc that only turns one way, stepped by a ratchet and pawl worked from the key universal bar.

**How it works:** A thin steel disc dog with peripheral teeth sits on a shaft below the carriage rack, which has two staggered rows of teeth. Each key or space-lever stroke retracts a pawl via the universal bar; on release a spring drives the pawl to advance a ratchet wheel on the dog shaft, turning the dog one tooth and letting the rack advance half a tooth pitch. The pawl's spring also brakes the ratchet, and a retaining pawl stops back motion. A plowshare plate on the rack guides the dog teeth into place after the carriage is returned.

**Parts:** circular dog disc; carriage rack; dog shaft; ratchet wheel; driving pawl; retaining pawl; universal bar; plowshare plate; carriage spring fusee

**Numbers:** wear less than one twentieth of a single oscillating dog; two staggered rows of rack teeth

**Improves on:**

- Remington single oscillating dog escapement: rotating many-toothed disc spreads wear
