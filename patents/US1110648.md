## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typebar machines need heavy finger force; an electric type action is wanted that is quick, reliable and simple while letting a very light key touch fire the bars.

**Solution:** Each key lifts a floating plunger that unlocks its typebar and closes a magnet circuit; the magnet steps a ratcheted reel of equally spaced rods, one of which throws the lifted plunger forward to drive the typebar.

**How it works:** Pressing a key lifts a vertical plunger between the key lever and the typebar's link; the plunger releases the link's locking latch and moves in front of a rod of a rotating reel (the universal driver). A cam on the plunger tilts a universal bar to close the contacts of an electromagnet circuit. The magnet pulls a lever whose pawl turns a ratchet wheel on the reel axle by one rod pitch, and a stop pin limits it to prevent overthrow. The rod strikes the plunger forward, which pushes the link and swings the typebar to the platen, driving positively for part of the stroke and then letting momentum finish it. A rock arm moved by the lever opens the switch at the end of the stroke; a spring returns the lever, and a spring-pressed arm between two rods holds the reel position. Only small manual force is used to lift the plunger, release the latch and close the circuit.
