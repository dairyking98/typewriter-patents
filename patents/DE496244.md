## Summary

**Kind:** typebar machine · **Subject (patent classes):** Construction of type-levers

**How it works (reading):** The rods are punched from unhardened wire with a tongue that bends over the lever bearing pin to retain the lever, and the rod is not hardened afterwards.

## Reading (short summary from the opening, OCR good)

**Problem:** Spring-hard retaining tongues on push or pull rods are hard to make and break, and hardened rods are unevenly hard.

**Solution:** The rods are punched from unhardened wire with a tongue that bends over the lever bearing pin to retain the lever, and the rod is not hardened afterwards.

These are usually made of wire, with the pivot pin to be hinged to it Lever or the like is bent or upset onto the initially unhardened wire. To secure this lever, a spring steel sheet is punched over the pull rod and bent spring pushed with a tongue against the bearing pin of the wire rod presses, so that a fork is formed between which the lever moves. Should the Pull or push rod are unhooked from the lever, so the resilient tongue pushed to the side. However, the production of the spring is difficult because that Spring-hard material has to be bent in different directions and often breaks, so that a lot of waste is created. Manufactured from unhardened material and subsequent hardening results in too uneven hardness, and the springs often break in use, or they are too soft. The same evils arise also on if the pull or push rods with the resilient tongue made of spring-hard Material can be punched or bent from a piece.
