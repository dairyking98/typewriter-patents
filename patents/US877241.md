## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** With plain rectangular escapement teeth the carriage does not finish its letter-space move before the next impression in fast work, so letters space unevenly or overlap.

**Solution:** Bevel the rear corner of each escapement tooth to an oblique working face and add a detent dog behind the feeding dog so the carriage advances more promptly.

**How it works:** On the Remington No. 6 a spring barrel and strap pull the carriage, whose rack meshes a pinion on the escapement wheel, with dogs on a rocker beneath the key levers. Each tooth has an oblique working face, with a small square portion kept for the feeding dog so it is not cut. A normally disengaged detent dog on the rocker, with its face parallel to the bevel and about thirty degrees to the feeding dog, catches the tooth after release. The rocker's return movement then lets the tooth advance faster under the carriage spring.

- Remington No. 6 escapement with plain rectangular teeth: oblique tooth face and detent dog give a quicker carriage advance
