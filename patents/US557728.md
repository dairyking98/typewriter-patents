## Summary

**Kind:** type-element machine · **Subject (patent classes):** Typewriters or selective printing mechanisms characterised by the mounting, arrangement or disposition of the types or dies with types or dies mounted on carriers rotatable for selection the plane of the type or die face being parallel to the axis of rotation, e.g. with type on the periphery of cylindrical carriers

**In the patent’s own words:** My invention relates to that class of typewriting machines known as wheel typewriters, and more particularly to such machines in which finger-keys are employed to turn the type-wheel in either direction from a normal position of rest, and also to operate the hammer to make the impression and to control the movement of the traversing carriage for the paper.

## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A keyboard wheel typewriter needs a light touch, accurate and fast type-wheel positioning, and a way to see the last printed letters hidden behind the type-wheel.

**Solution:** Each key-lever carries a differently shaped cam that rocks one of two rockers to turn the type-wheel a set angle either way from a rest position, then locks the wheel while a spring hammer prints and the carriage steps.

**How it works:** Each key-lever ends in an arc-shaped cam that enters between two bars of the left or right rocker, swinging it by an amount set by the cam shape. The rocker's segmental gear arm meshes with a pinion on the tubular shaft that carries the type-wheel, turning it in one direction or the other from rest. As the key finishes its travel, the cam's parallel faces sit between the rocker bars and hold the wheel locked, and a notched positioning wheel on the shaft is also engaged by a vibrating dog driven from the universal bar. The universal bar also trips a vibrating lever that releases a spring-driven hammer to strike the paper against the type and lets the carriage advance one tooth by its spring drum. The shift key lifts the wheel on its shaft by a lifting pin to change rows, and a second shift lever latches it up. The whole type-wheel, key and ribbon frame can be drawn back from the carriage to expose the last print.
