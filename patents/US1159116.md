## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** On a machine whose carriage travels between two typebar sets, the platen must be shifted to upper or lower case at any carriage position.

**Solution:** A long shift rail carrying the platen, lifted by pairs of shift keys (one pair per type set) coupled by rock shafts, with a cam-and-lever lock for upper case.

**How it works:** Two Underwood typebar machines stand side by side and one carriage runs across both, the platen riding on a shift rail long enough for every carriage position. One pair of shift keys serves each type set; the left keys are linked together by a rock shaft and the right keys likewise. The left key lifts the rail and lets it return when released; the right key lifts it and a cam on a rock shaft moves a locking lever to hold the rail in upper case. Depressing a left key releases the lock through the cam. The linkage means the double-length shift frame is always supported by two key levers, preventing sagging.
