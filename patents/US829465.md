## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Paper-feed rollers on a typewriter platen either grip a single sheet too weakly or, when a wad of thick paper is fed in, lose their grip as one roller is pushed off the platen.

**Solution:** Two spring-pressed feed rollers (front and rear) on pivoted frames are coupled with slight lost motion, so a thick wad lifting the rear roller also lifts the front roller and transfers the front springs' pressure to the rear one.

**How it works:** A rear (primary) and a front (secondary) feed roller sit beneath the platen, each on its own pivoted frame pressed up by independent springs. The frames are linked either by intermeshing teeth or by overlapping arm ends, with slight lost motion. A thin sheet moves only the rear roller; a thick wad moves it further, which carries the front frame away too and passes the front spring tension to the rear roller, so total pressure on the wad stays the same. A finger-piece on a rock-shaft lifts both rollers away from the platen to release or adjust the paper. A paper deflector hangs from the front roller shaft and swings away with the rollers. In a variant, straight rod springs have adjustable clips on a bracket to set tension.
