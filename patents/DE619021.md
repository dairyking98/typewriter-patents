## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typewriters with a motor-driven type drum made fast typing hard because lifting the drum to the right row needed extra shift keys, or the drum became large and heavy if all characters sat in two rows.

**Solution:** A small-diameter drum with ten column groups and five rows spins continuously; each key of an ordinary four-row keyboard drops a stop pin into the path of a selector arm to halt the rotation and sets a stepped lift stop for the row, so any character is chosen by one key press.

**How it works:** A motor continuously turns a hollow spindle through a belt; the type drum shaft slides in the spindle (keyed) and turns with it. Pressing a key moves a slide rod, whose cam raises one of ten stop pins into the path of a selector arm on the spindle, stopping the drum at that column; the belt then slips on the pulley. The same slide rod has a cam of one of three heights (or none) that pushes a crossbar and slides a stepped stop plate, limiting the lift of the drum so the second, third or fourth row meets the printing plate; the top row needs a shift key. A clutch gives one full turn of a cam shaft per key, which locks the drum with a toothed locking wheel and rod, then pushes the plate with serrated face against the drum, printing through the paper. The carriage is pulled by a spring and escaped by a rack, gear and ratchet with three pawls through the space key; a felt inking roller touches the drum.
