## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** The inventor wants a compact typewriter with a minimum number of parts and only a few keys that can print on an endless paper ribbon or a sheet, singly or several characters at once.

**Solution:** A few-key machine in a small case in which the keys shift a type cylinder (turning and sliding on a shaft via an internal spiral groove) to the wanted character and then operate a printing device, with the paper ribbon or sheet fed along after each letter.

**How it works:** The key levers are pivoted at the back to a bar; the type cylinder sits on a shaft and carries characters on its face with alternate perforations sized to receive a pin on a frame operated by the keys. The cylinder is internally spirally grooved and pins on the shaft run in the groove, so axial movement turns the cylinder; a spiral spring and a return spring bring it back. A shifting lever with quadrangle lever arms and chains turns a hub and rocker arm, and the keys cause the impression immediately after the cylinder has been shifted. Inking rollers in a slotted frame touch the cylinder. Separate mechanism carries and feeds the paper ribbon or sheet progressively with each letter printed, with an escapement; a diagram of the character layout explains the key combinations. Only the opening of the description is available.
