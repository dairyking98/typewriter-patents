## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Smith's earlier printing machine (application Serial 266,523) was too complicated and costly; he wants a simpler, cheaper machine giving better results, one that can also print on the leaves of a bound book.

**Solution:** A small carriage travels over a thin flat platform; a knob turns a central shaft carrying both a pointer over a letter dial and a rubber type segment, and a key drops a latch into a slot to register the type and hammer it onto the paper while also stepping the carriage by a dog on a rack.

**How it works:** The paper or book leaf lies on a thin platform that can slide between book pages; a bar with grooves sets line spacing through a spring-held sliding bracket whose guide rod carries a fixed rack. An inverted-cup carriage slides on the rod. The operator turns a knob on a central vertical shaft to point at a character on the dial; the same shaft carries a segment bearing rubber type in the same order, so the matching type sits under the print point. Pressing the key forces a hinged latch on the shaft down into one of the slots above the segment (one per character), registering it exactly, and the hammer on the latch strikes the type through an opening in the carriage bottom onto the paper. The same key, via a shaft and crank-arm with a dog on the rack, advances the carriage one step on release. Ink pads on each side of the opening ink the types as they sweep past. A variant puts the knob on a bevel-geared side shaft so the hand does not hide the dial.
