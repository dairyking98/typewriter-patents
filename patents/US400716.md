## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typebar writing machines had too many parts and complicated linkages, and lacked simple variable letter spacing, compact typebar grouping, direct inking of the type, and a combined carriage/platen line feed.

**Solution:** A simplified typebar machine with letter-width-dependent variable spacing driven by notched key levers, direct type inking, and a line-feed lever that also positions the carriage for the next line.

**How it works:** Key levers pivot on a rod and are limited by a stop rod; each lever is notched according to the width of its character. A double bail under the levers varies how far a double-ended spacing pawl travels back over the carriage rack, so the next feed step matches the letter width. A pawl lever at the carriage end turns a platen ratchet by an adjustable screw-set amount for line feed, leaving the carriage ready to be drawn to the start of the next line. A spring-pressed roller holds paper against the platen. Inking is done directly on the type.

**Features:** [SEL:keyboard-full](../features/sel-keyboard-full.md), [ELEM:typebar](../features/elem-typebar.md), [ESC:variable-spacing](../features/esc-variable-spacing.md), [ESC:pawl-rack](../features/esc-pawl-rack.md), [INK:roller](../features/ink-roller.md), [PAP:platen-roller](../features/pap-platen-roller.md), [PAP:line-space](../features/pap-line-space.md), [PAP:feed-rollers](../features/pap-feed-rollers.md), [ESC:carriage-return](../features/esc-carriage-return.md)

**Parts:** base; key lever; key-lever rod; carriage; platen; ratchet; pawl lever; spacing pawl; carriage rack; double bail; pressure roller; paper guide; typebar hanger
