## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Pointer-type cylinder typewriters with a swivelling, shiftable cylinder need many parts to move the cylinder to its writing positions and to the impression position.

**Solution:** One cross-machine axis carries both the swivel plate and the shift lever, with a single key lever and stop buttons on the pushers limiting the shift to three rows.

**How it works:** A rotary arm carries a pointer that slides over a type scale and turns the type cylinder via a fork, a pin and a gear sector meshing with a gear on the cylinder axis. An impression lever on the rotary arm lifts the swivel plate, which is mounted on the cross axis, and swings the cylinder to the paper roller. A shift lever driven by one key moves the cylinder axially; buttons on two pushers act as stops so the cylinder strikes with the top, second or third row. The cylinder sits on a plate with a driver that engages in its opening. A pawl, released as the cylinder returns, advances the carriage rack one tooth under tension spring action.
