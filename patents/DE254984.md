## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Accent keys on a visible-writing typewriter must not shift the carriage yet must still advance the ribbon, and any number should be placeable on either side of the keyboard.

**Solution:** Connect accent keys to a swingable cross-frame that moves the ribbon fork through a thumb, bypassing the universal rail so the shift mechanism does not act.

**How it works:** Normal keys pull a type lever and the universal rail, which actuates both the ribbon fork and the shift lever. An oscillating shaft behind the universal rail carries a lifting thumb that strikes the ribbon-fork link directly, and arms with cross rods on both sides. Each accent key's connecting rod hooks onto a cross rod; pressing it brings the type lever to the platen and swings the shaft so the thumb lifts the ribbon fork, but the universal rail does not move, so no shift occurs. Rod positions are adjustable by screws.
