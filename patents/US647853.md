## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** The inventor's earlier pocket typewriter needed improving and a way to do shorthand as well as long-hand work.

**Solution:** Finger levers print dots and dashes around a vertical dash, and a thumb lever with its own type prints in three positions, making the pocket machine usable for shorthand.

**How it works:** A rectangular casing holds two paper reels; a strip winds from one to the other over a rubber cushion. Four L-shaped finger levers, two at each end, carry beveled type on their free ends and are returned by coil springs against a cross-piece. Light pressure prints a dot and heavier pressure a dash. Any finger lever also moves a pivoted member carrying the vertical-dash type, whose pawl turns a ratchet on the take-up reel one tooth to advance the strip. An ink ribbon passes over guides between two reels with a recessed one for its feed.
