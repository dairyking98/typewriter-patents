## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Typewriters were too expensive because of complicated construction, and gave a limited range of type; the inventor wants a cheaper machine with upper and lower case, figures and punctuation from one set of keys.

**Solution:** A single interchangeable type sleeve carrying 9 rows of 9 types is slid and twisted on an oscillating printing lever by key-driven cams so any type reaches one printing point, with a lateral field-shifter for case.

**How it works:** Each key lever has a pin that engages a differently eccentric cam on a rock shaft; the rocking is carried by a pitman to a groove on the type sleeve, sliding it lengthwise by an amount set per key. Keys for side rows also press a row-shifting bail whose cam enters a recessed flange on the printing lever and twirls the sleeve a set angle, locking it before the strike. A hand-operated sliding casing moves a rack and pinion that turns the sleeve to bring the lower-case, capital or figure field to the printing point. The key's motion also oscillates the printing lever so the chosen type strikes a polygonal platen; a toothed segment and pointed arm lock the sleeve at the exact position. A ribbon, wound alternately between two spools by pawls on the printing lever, inks the type, and the platen carriage steps by pawls on a rack with a step stop giving wider spacing for m and w and capitals.
