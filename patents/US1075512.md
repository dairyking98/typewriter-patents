## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Earlier compressed-air typewriters set and printed the types by complicated means; the inventor wants to simplify them.

**Solution:** Two small pneumatic motors with self-controlling valves: one runs the type wheel until a key-raised abutment stops it, the other then presses the set type to the platen.

**How it works:** Motor 2 drives the type wheel (type barrel) continuously through a flexible shaft. Around it a circle of abutment levers is each coupled to a key; pressing a key raises its abutment into the path of an arm turning with the motor shaft, stopping the shaft and the type wheel at that position. The arm's stopping makes the shaft's valve stem hit a tappet, opening compressed air to a diaphragm that lifts a pawl and releases motor 5 for one revolution. A crank and rod on motor 5 press the type barrel to the platen to print and return it. The motor 5 shaft in turn routes air to a second diaphragm that drops the abutment, freeing motor 2 to turn again.
