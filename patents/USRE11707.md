## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Earlier Fisher machines hid the line of writing and had awkward ribbon-spool and carriage-feed arrangements; the inventor wants a permanently visible line with an ordinary keyboard.

**Solution:** A crescent-shaped type-bar ring hung under an elevated carriage leaves the front open so the line of writing is visible when the ribbon is pushed aside, with removable ribbon spools and a two-rack carriage feed with release.

**How it works:** The whole machine rides on track rails and the keyboard carriage moves along elevated front and rear guides. Keys connect through pivoted levers and draw wires to typebars pivoted in open seats on a semicircular ring hung below the guides, open to the front, held in by a notched flexible strip. Ribbon spools sit on cantilevered spindles that can be pushed rearward to expose the writing, where catches hold them; the spindles also shift sideways to feed the ribbon across. Carriage feed uses a rigid double-edged pawl between a fixed and a spring-returned movable ratchet bar, and a release key lifts the movable bar free of the pawl so the carriage moves freely.
