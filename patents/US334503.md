## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** The inventor wants a typewriter simple in parts, practical in operation and cheap to make.

**Solution:** A frame holding a battery of hand-held type levers slides along ways; the operator slides it until the chosen lever is over a notch in a guide bar and presses that lever down to print, which also steps the paper bed one or more notches according to the lever's cut-away end.

**How it works:** Type levers, each with a type in a recessed projection and a letter knob on top, are pivoted on a common centre in a sliding frame that runs on a rod and on grooved wheels along a guide bar. Springs under the lever ends keep them lifted so no type can touch the ribbon until a lever is over the centring notch of the guide bar. The operator slides the frame to the wanted lever, then presses it into the notch, where its wedge-shaped end centres it and the type hits the paper through an ink ribbon between two spools. The pressed lever end pushes down a sliding pawl in the guide bar, which pushes a rack bar on the paper frame forward; levers for wide letters are uncut and push the pawl farthest, medium ones are cut away part way and narrow ones barely move it, giving varied spacing. Levers with no type serve as spacers. At line end pawls are lifted and springs return the frame, then the paper is moved over for line spacing by a set screw.
