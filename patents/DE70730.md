## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Typewriters of the time are complicated in their type-moving and paper-feed devices.

**Solution:** A simple typebar machine whose types sit on a rotatable shoe pressed against an ink pad, with a simplified carriage feed by spring and teeth.

**How it works:** Typebars sit in small bearing blocks on a sickle-shaped cover plate, with radial key levers connected by rods. A curved frame above carries a felt ink pad covered in muslin, and each bar's upper end carries a rotatable shoe with a type head bearing, for example, three types, which lie against the pad. A link from the shoe to an angle lever with a leaf spring lets the bar yield at the end of travel to avoid impure pressure. A frame with locking lever can be set rearmost, middle or foremost so the top, middle or lower type of every shoe prints. The carriage advances one type width by a spring-driven switching rod with teeth, actuated by each key or the space lever, and a bell is struck near the line end.
