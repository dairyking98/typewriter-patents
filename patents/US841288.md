## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Improve the carriage feed, carriage release, line lock and platen mounting of the Yost typebar machine, where earlier arrangements had drawbacks.

**Solution:** A vertical-shaft escapement with a spring-loaded two-part clutch lets the carriage be freed by one key or run back without driving the escape wheel, with a shaft-operated line lock and a detachable, plunger-locked platen frame.

**How it works:** Typebars sit in a circle on a type ring with drivers, guide-links and key-linked bell-cranks, their types resting on an ink pad; each typebar's guide-link lifts a circular universal bar, which lifts a central rod and rocks a dog-rocker with one fixed and one loose dog on a horizontal feed wheel, advancing the carriage one space. The space key lifts the same rod without moving the universal bar. The carriage rack drives a pinion on a hollow vertical shaft that is clutched to the feed wheel through a spring-pressed toothed clutch and a gravity-pawl ratchet clutch, so the carriage can be moved right without turning the feed wheel but turns it going left. A finger-key on the carriage raises the pinion shaft to disengage the clutch for free carriage movement. A carriage abutment turns a line-lock shaft running through the hollow shaft, swinging a lever that blocks the stem of the universal bar and dog-rocker at line end; a separate key lifts the abutment to release it. A pinion also drives a rack on the ball separator of the carriage bearings. The platen frame hinges on spring plungers with locking heads and can be removed, and a feed roller and paper blade are lifted by one finger-key.
