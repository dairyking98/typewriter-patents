## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** On machines that feed the paper during the key stroke, a key held down after the blow causes double impressions, blurring, sluggishness and a jarring abrupt stop.

**Solution:** A 'repulser' (spring tip, or pneumatic or gravity form) acts on the universal bar to kick the key back once the type has been thrown hard enough to print, so the staccato stroke becomes natural.

**How it works:** A stiff spring fixed to each side of the base projects under the universal bar and carries a rubber tip, adjusted by a set-screw on a lug. When the key is pressed far enough that the type has struck with force, the bar meets the spring and the key is pushed back, making the operator lift the finger by instinct. The escapement is reversed: the detaining dog holds the rack at rest and the spacing dog engages on key depression, feeding the paper during the stroke. Shown on Remington and Smith Premier with pneumatic and gravity variants.
