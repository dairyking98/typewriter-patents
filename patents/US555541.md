## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** In typebar machines the word space is a separate keystroke, which costs time and limits typing speed.

**Solution:** Pressing a space key together with the last letter of a word lets the escapement pawl, which has a slotted pivot and a spring, move the rack two teeth in one operation so the word space and the first letter's space happen together.

**How it works:** The escapement is the usual oscillating detent and a pivoted pawl working in a carriage rack. The pawl has a slot at its pivot instead of a round hole, and a coil spring in a bore pushes it one way. A releasing lever, connected only to the space keys, normally bears on the pawl heel and prevents it from sliding in the slot, so the carriage advances one tooth. When a space key is pressed with a letter key, the lever lets the pawl slide with the rack by the slot length, equal to one tooth pitch, so the carriage moves two teeth on release. A spring-loaded pivoted detent holds the lever raised until the pawl has slid, then the next key press returns everything to normal.
