## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Hand-turned type-wheel machines space every letter equally; the inventor wants the carriage feed proportioned to each letter's width and a simple way to turn and press the wheel directly.

**Solution:** A rotary type-wheel with an axial knurled handle sits on a depressible spring-raised lever above the carriage, and pins of different lengths on the wheel, one per letter, limit the feed-pawl so each print advances the carriage by the letter's width.

**How it works:** The type-wheel is fitted over the hub of a lettered, notched index wheel and both turn freely on the end of a pivoted lever, twirled by a long slender sleeve used as a handle. An index point shows the chosen letter on the wheel face or the index-wheel rim, and a spring click snaps into the slots to give an audible position. When the handle is pressed down, a beveled tooth on the lever guide enters a slot and notch of the index wheel's internal ratchet rim, locking the wheel and correcting small misalignment while the slot length permits downward play. The type prints on a paper roller below, with an adjustable stop screw setting the impression pressure, and ink rollers or a ribbon ink the type. A spring raises the lever after each stroke. The lever's up-and-down motion works a feed pawl on a carriage rack through an elbow lever and spring; a sliding stop link meets the pin of the letter being printed, and pin length (short for wide letters) limits the pawl's backward travel from 1 to 4 teeth. A knob lifts the pawl out or strokes it by hand for word spaces, and the carriage is a dovetailed slide that can be withdrawn.
