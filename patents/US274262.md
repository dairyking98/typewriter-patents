## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Wheel-type typewriters need the keys only to trigger the action and want equal letter spacing between wide and narrow characters, with several rows of type available.

**Solution:** Power-driven continuously rotating type wheels on a rocking beam are dropped onto the platen by keyed cams, with a toothed wheel of unequal teeth setting a pawl that controls platen return per letter and an axially shifting type-wheel shaft selecting the row.

**How it works:** A pulley drives, through gearing, a type-wheel shaft and a cam shaft that both turn continuously and hang on a pair of vibrating beams pivoted on studs. Pressing a key engages a hook lever so the corresponding cam rides an inclined face on its cam lever and rocks the beam, dropping the type wheel onto the platen. A ratchet wheel on the type shaft locks to a projection on the platen at impact and carries the platen forward with the type against a spring. A serrated pawl holds the paper, and a toothed wheel with teeth of unequal length moves it so the platen falls back by an amount tied to type width. Two side levers shift a clutch that slides the type-wheel shaft left or right to bring row 1, 2 or 3 to the print point, and a spring cam in a grooved wheel centres it again.

**Parts:** type wheel; type-wheel shaft; cam shaft; vibrating beam; key-lever; cam; cam lever; hook lever; ratchet wheel; platen; serrated paper pawl; unequal-tooth wheel; shifting clutch; grooved cam wheel; spring cam; drive pulley

**Numbers:** 3 rows of type per wheel; paper cut in narrow strips

**Improves on:**

- key-driven wheel machines: a continuous power drive does the printing and the keys only trigger it
