## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Simplify and improve index typewriters whose type-wheel rotates and moves vertically, with easier operator motion and visible writing.

**Solution:** An index slide in notched index plate is joined by a cord to a type-wheel carried on a yoke-lever key; the slide rotates the wheel (cord one way, spring the other), and depressing the key prints and, on its return stroke, spaces the carriage.

**How it works:** The operator slides and oscillates an index slide along a guide beside the machine and drops its point into the notch opposite the wanted letter. A cord over pulleys turns the type-wheel (an electrotype strip on a rim) against a return spring, so the chosen letter lies on the underside. The wheel is carried on pointed bearing screws in a yoke-lever key pivoted on a standard; pressing the key swings the wheel down onto an ink ribbon and the paper on a platen. A pawl on the key turns a toothed wheel meshing with a rack on the carriage, stepping it one letter as the spring-raised key returns, with a detent preventing overrun. A word-space notch lets a pin on the wheel hit a stop so the key descends without printing. The platen has a line scale and is pushed forward one line by a toothed bar and spring lever when the carriage returns; a spring click sounds near the line end. The ribbon spools are fed by a ratchet worked from the key.
