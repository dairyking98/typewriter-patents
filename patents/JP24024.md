## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A cheap, simple machine is wanted for embossing Braille on paper clearly and quickly.

**Solution:** Six finger-keys lift six pins against a die through rocking levers while a spring-driven slide steps the paper one cell left per stroke.

**How it works:** Six key levers pivoted on a shaft at the left have buttons at the right, one per finger, for the six Braille dots. Each lever is linked to a rocking lever that pushes a vertical pin up against a pitted die, embossing the paper held between pin and die. A bent lever lying under all key levers is pressed by any key and drives a feed lever up and down; the feed lever lets a rack on the underside of the paper slide advance one tooth, pulled by a clockwork spring through a cord. A separate space button moves the slide without embossing. A large roller turned by a knob feeds the paper forward for the next line, and the slide is pushed back by hand.

## Description (English translation of the Japanese text, opening)

> Specification. Kishitaka-type Braille writer. Nature and object of the invention. This invention relates to a Braille writer in which six key levers are mounted in a metal frame; pressing down one end of a key lever, through a link, raises the pin at the other end of a rocking lever; the paper on a slide is led by rollers between the pin and the die; and Braille is produced at will.
