## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Small cheap pocket typewriters use rubber types on an elastic band, which print unsharply and break, while metal-type pocket machines are too bulky to carry.

**Solution:** A slide-rule-sized typewriter in which spring-loaded metal types sit in holes of a double rail that is stepped along a frame by a spring drum each time the single print button is pressed.

**How it works:** Two parallel rails with equally spaced rectangular holes form a type carrier sliding between two side bars; each hole holds a metal type on a pin and coil spring, its head standing slightly above the top rail. The operator slides the carrier by a knob until the wanted character (labelled on the cover rail) is under the print button. Pressing the two-armed button presses the type down through an ink ribbon onto the paper, while a pin on the button drops into a slot parallel to the type holes to lock the carrier and centre the type exactly. The same press releases an upper pin and engages a lower pin in offset rows of holes on a J-rail, so the button bracket is advanced one letter space by a spring drum through a cord. The paper sits in a folding frame and is moved line by line using lines on angle rails visible through the paper.
