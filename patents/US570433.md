## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Improve operation, cheapness and durability of the inventor's 1893 plunger-typebar machine with a cylindrical platen.

**Solution:** A cheaply built, mostly stamped and cast keyboard machine whose converging reciprocating typebars print on a cylinder, with a tilting cradle for case shift and dual-pawl escapement.

**How it works:** Key levers pull stamped links that rock bell-crank levers, which push straight sliding typebars forward on paths converging to one printing point on the paper cylinder. The bars run in a tiltable cradle: tipping it brings the lower row of type (capitals) into line, with a lock for the shift. Fixed guide plates and a swinging central guide aim the type squarely at the paper. A sheet-metal ribbon frame is lifted by a bar under the keys so the ribbon rises before the blow. The carriage is driven by a spring and held by two alternating escapement pawls on a rack, worked by a cam plate under a universal bar, with a long-stroke key that frees both pawls. Line spacing is done by a pawl tripped by a sliding rod striking a stop on carriage return.
