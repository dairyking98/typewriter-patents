## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Line-space levers on earlier carriages hung down toward the keyboard, were awkward, easily turned the platen by accident when the carriage was pulled back, and gave no easy half-line spacing.

**Solution:** A horizontal line-space lever with a fixed thumb-piece above it and an adjustable stop pin that sets half-line or full-line platen rotation.

**How it works:** A bent lever pivoted on the carriage front rod carries a pawl that engages a ratchet wheel (circular rack) on the platen. Lifting the horizontal outer arm swings the pawl and turns the platen until the arm hits a stop pin. The stop pin sits on a spindle with a knob, held by a spring against friction, and can be turned between two shoulders so the lever travel is one tooth (half line) or two teeth (full line). A spring-loaded holding dog re-engages the ratchet after each step, and can be lifted to reverse the platen. A fixed thumb-piece above the arm lets the operator squeeze the hand to space and balances the force so the carriage does not lift, and also serves for pulling the carriage back.

**Features:**

- **Spacing and escapement:** [Carriage return](../features/esc-carriage-return.md)
- **Paper and carriage:** [Line spacing](../features/pap-line-space.md), [Roller platen](../features/pap-platen-roller.md)
- **Frame and body:** [Adjustment and wear take-up](../features/frm-adjustment-wear.md)

**Parts:** line-space lever; pawl; ratchet wheel; holding dog; stop pin; spindle; knob; spring; thumb-piece; platen; carriage

**Numbers:** half-line = 1 ratchet tooth; full line = 2 ratchet teeth

**Improves on:**

- earlier line-space levers extending down between keyboard and type circle: lever is horizontal and ends at the carriage side, less accidental platen turning and more hand room
