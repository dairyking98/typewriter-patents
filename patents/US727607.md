## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** Key stroke shocks from typebar inertia and platen impact, plus typebar rebound after retraction, in downward-strike machines like the Fisher.

**Solution:** A bell-crank in the draw-wire between key lever and typebar varies the motion ratio, moving the bar little at first and more late, and the bar is locked when retracted.

**How it works:** A fixed-fulcrum bell-crank is connected at triangularly arranged points to the draw-rod sections joining the key lever rear to the typebar. The geometry makes the typebar move about a quarter of its stroke in the first half of the key stroke and three quarters in the remainder, so the key starts easily and load rises gradually. A device automatically locks the typebar in its retracted position to stop rebound. It is applied to the Fisher book typewriter.

**Features:** [ELEM:typebar](../features/elem-typebar.md), [USE:book-typewriter](../features/use-book-typewriter.md), [SEL:keyboard-full](../features/sel-keyboard-full.md)

**Parts:** key lever; bell-crank lever; draw-wire; draw-rod; typebar; platen

**Numbers:** one-fourth of bar stroke in first half of key stroke; three-fourths in the remainder
