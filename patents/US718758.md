## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** On Hammond-type machines, moving the type-wheel takes about 75 percent of the key force, making touch uneven and hard, and forcing a light wheel that cannot manifold.

**Solution:** Key depression closes an electric circuit so an electromagnet moves the type-wheel and releases the hammer and carriage, leaving the operator only the small contact stroke.

**How it works:** In the Hammond-style machine, a key lifts a stop-pin that stops the type-wheel's stop-arm at the right position. Here the key closes a contact so an electromagnet or similar device does the work of swinging the type-wheel to the printing point, and also releases the hammer spring and the carriage spring so the carriage advances one notch. Modifications are shown in detail figures; the key stroke becomes short and even, and a metal wheel can replace the hard-rubber one for manifolding.
