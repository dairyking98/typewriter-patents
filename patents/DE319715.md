## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Known Braille-capable typewriters either cannot write Braille and ordinary script at once, or put Braille on a different side or reading direction (right to left) from the print.

**Solution:** A type wheel carries both hole patterns (Braille) and block letters, with a spring-pin stamp on the opposite side of the paper pressing paper into the holes while the type prints, so both appear on the same side, reading left to right.

**How it works:** A type wheel has Braille hole groups in its upper zone and, below each, the matching block letter. The wheel is set by a hand lever arm that swings over three stepped arc scales and turns the wheel through a toothed sector and gear. A cutout in the arm shows the character selected. A ratchet disc on the wheel axis is engaged by a lever during printing to hold position exactly. Pressing the write button moves the wheel against the paper via a cam and linkage, and at the same time a stamp with six spring-loaded pins plus an abutment bolt is pressed from the other side, so the pins embossing the paper into the wheel holes. The paper is held only at its side margins by pegs and rubber bands so both faces stay free; a ratchet lever advances the paper holder.
