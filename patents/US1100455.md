## Reading (opening only, OCR fair)

**Problem:** In a front-strike machine whose carriage shifts up and down for case, the escapement connection from the universal bar to the carriage must work the same in lower and upper case.

**Solution:** The escapement train from the universal bar to the carriage dogs is joined at a pivot coinciding with the hinge axis about which the carriage frame shifts, so shifting does not disturb the escapement.

**How it works:** The carriage rides on balls in a grooved rail that is part of a movable frame hinged at the rear to the main framework; the frame swings up for case shift or tilts up for access. Feed and detent dogs on a rock shaft engage a rack on the carriage, and the rack is on hinged arms with a carriage-release key. The rock shaft's operating arm connects by a pendent link at a pivot nearly coincident with the frame hinge axis. The link goes to a rocker arm on a transverse rock shaft carrying the universal bar, which the type-bar levers push.
