## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** A simple, cheap, easy-to-use kana typewriter, also suitable for European text, with proper inking of a type wheel.

**Solution:** A single type key swung over an arc-shaped chart turns a double-row type wheel through a sector gear, and the button on it presses the platen against the wheel; ink rollers on a rocking arm are swung by the same key.

**How it works:** The type key's carrier arm pivots at the centre of the arc chart (37); a pointer at the front and a sector gear (32) at the rear. The gear turns pinion (24) and an index wheel (25), which drives the type wheel (28) on shaft (29) through a post (26) that lets the wheel slide vertically. Gear ratio makes the wheel face the platen with the pointed character. Pressing the button (2) on a lever whose rear end always sits on the pivot axis acts through a bell crank and link to swing the platen (11) against the type wheel; an indexing pin (10) first enters a Y-notch of the index wheel to correct the angle. A shift key (19) raises or lowers the wheel to bring the lower (31) or upper (30) row to the platen. A saw bar (49) on the platen carriage and spring pawl (47) space the platen one tooth per stroke; the space key swings the platen forward without touching the wheel and releases the pawl so spacing happens without printing and the carriage can be moved by hand. A rocking arm on the wheel shaft carrying two ink rollers is driven by a pin in a slot in the carrier arm, moving the rollers opposite the wheel's rotation to ink the whole face. A guide saw under the chart stops printing between positions.

## Description (English translation of the Japanese text, opening)

> Specification. Kana typewriter. Nature and object of the invention. This invention is a kind of type-wheel typewriter. It has one shift key and one space key, and only a single type key. At the centre of an arc-shaped character chart on the front of the machine is the pivot of the type key. From this pivot runs a carrier arm with a pointer on its front end and a sector gear on its rear end. On a shaft rod fixed in the middle of this arm a lever is pivoted at its centre; the lever has a button on its front end, and its rear end always lies on the axis of the type key's pivot, so that the button's up-and-down movement moves it up and down on that axis. Facing it on the same axis is a driven crank, joined by a link to the platen (印字筒, "printing cylinder"). The operator swings the type key left or right until the pointer shows the wanted character, and presses the button; this presses the platen against the type wheel, which prints with the wanted type. The type-wheel shaft is extended upward and carries a pivoted rocking arm holding ink rollers.
