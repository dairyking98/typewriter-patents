## Reading (full text, OCR fair)

**Problem:** Improves the author's earlier index typewriter (US350717): paper needs to be held on the feed roll, the inking pads need to move with the printing head, rubber types must print independently, and spacing and the bell need to be easy to reach.

**Solution:** An index machine with a hand-moved index plate carrying a pivoted rubber type segment, which slides on a ratchet-toothed bar with moving ink pads so that one rocking lever spaces or prints and spaces.

**How it works:** The index plate, with guide arms, slides by hand along a ratchet-toothed feed bar. A type-carrying segment pivots on the index plate and has a rubber segment on its underside with projecting types separated by deep grooves, so the type under the stud of the pivoted printing lever can be pressed on the paper without squashing its neighbours. A sleeve with inking pads rides on the feed bar with the index plate, cut away so the pads sit under the types and leave a gap for the selected type to reach the paper. A rocking lever running along the machine presses the printing lever; pressing its yielding finger-piece depresses the lever just enough for the feed dog to step the carriage one tooth (a space), while pressing the lever itself goes further and prints, with the dog stepping the carriage at the same time. A spring-finger presser bar holds the paper on the feed roll, and a bell on the segment is struck by a hammer on the rocking lever near the end of the line.
