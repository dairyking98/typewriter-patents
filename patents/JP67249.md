## Reading (full text, OCR good)

**Problem:** Earlier Japanese typewriters with a sliding type case (GB5860 of 1889) had fixed printing levers so only one row of types was usable, and printed on the underside of the cylinder where the writing could not be seen.

**Solution:** A flat type case slides in four directions under the paper cylinder, carrying a comb of printing levers (one per column) sideways with it, and the lever hooked to the chosen type pulls it up through an arc guide to print on the front of the cylinder.

**How it works:** The operator holds a handle on the type case and slides it forward/back and left/right until a pointer sits over the wanted character on a character plate. Each column of types has its own printing lever pivoted in a comb bearing on a frame that slides sideways with the case, so a lever tip with a projection always sits in the groove of one type per column. Pressing the press bar first turns a locking pawl and knife-edge pawls into racks under the case, locking it in both axes. A cam then swings the lower part of a two-part arc type guide so an ink roller wipes the type face. The printing lever then rises, pulls the type up through the arc guide and strikes it straight onto the front of the paper cylinder, then pushes it back into the case.

## Description (English translation of the Japanese text, opening)

> Specification. High-speed Japanese-character typewriter. Nature and object of the invention. This invention relates to a Japanese-character typewriter that improves on British patent No. 5860 of 1889. The types are stored in a flat type case, which is made to move forward, back, left and right. One printing lever is provided for each lengthwise column of types; the printing levers move sideways together with the type case. When the press bar is pressed, only the type at the tip of the printing lever that has come to the centre of the base is pulled up, and it prints directly on the paper cylinder through an arc-shaped type guide. The object is to print very quickly and surely with a simple mechanism and so to raise the efficiency of a Japanese typewriter. Brief description of the drawings.
