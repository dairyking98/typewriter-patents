## Reading (opening only, OCR good)

**Problem:** Typebar type are slow to align (hours) and can stick in the center guide, punch through the paper or print too heavily.

**Solution:** Give each type a polygonal body with beveled flanges at its root that seats in a matching beveled-mouth center guide, which aligns the type and limits its travel.

**How it works:** Typebars radiate to a center guide (directrix) with an oblong opening at the printing point. Each type has a rectangular body, bevel flanges at the root and a tapering shank fitted into a socket on the bar; the guide has straight upper walls and flared lower walls. Types are fitted loosely, then driven home by sharp blows on the keys so the guide itself fixes their alignment. The flanges act as stops so the type cannot go too far or wedge. A variant uses a hollow shank and a rubber type face in a recess.
