# The American typewriter, landmark by landmark

From the Sholes, Glidden & Soule patents of 1868 to the IBM Executive of 1944: the machines and patents that brought each familiar feature to market in the United States. Every entry is sourced; where sources disagree about a "first" (they often do), the notes give both sides. Machines and patents link to their pages in this library where it has them.

See also [Typewriter features through time](typewriter-features.md) (when each feature appears in the patents and became standard), the [master timeline](../guides/master-timeline.md) and the [timeline browser](../explore/timeline.md).

## 1860–1879: Beginnings: Milwaukee and Ilion

### 1868: Second Sholes, Glidden & Soule patent

A second patent of 14 July 1868 covers the earlier wire-linked version of the Milwaukee machine.

**Machine:** Sholes, Glidden & Soule type-writer (C. Latham Sholes, Carlos Glidden, Samuel W. Soule (Milwaukee)) · **Patent:** [US79868](../patents/US79868.md), 1868-07-14, Christopher Latham Sholes; Carlos Glidden; Samuel W. Soule · **Confidence:** high

Sources: [patents.google.com](https://patents.google.com/patent/US79868A/en), [asme.org](https://www.asme.org/wwwasmeorg/media/ResourceFiles/AboutASME/Who%20We%20Are/Engineering%20History/Landmarks/249-Sholes-Glidden-Type-Writer.pdf)

*Grant date checked on Google Patents.*

### 1868: Sholes, Glidden & Soule patent the type-writer

Sholes, Glidden and Soule of Milwaukee receive US patent 79,265 for "Improvement in type-writing machines", the patent from which the name "type-writer" spread.

**Machine:** Sholes, Glidden & Soule type-writer (patent model) (C. Latham Sholes, Carlos Glidden, Samuel W. Soule (Milwaukee)) · **Patent:** [US79265](../patents/US79265.md), 1868-06-23, Christopher Latham Sholes; Carlos Glidden; Samuel W. Soule · **Confidence:** high

Sources: [patents.google.com](https://patents.google.com/patent/US79265A/en), [asme.org](https://www.asme.org/wwwasmeorg/media/ResourceFiles/AboutASME/Who%20We%20Are/Engineering%20History/Landmarks/249-Sholes-Glidden-Type-Writer.pdf)

*Patent text names all three inventors. ASME says the June patent covered the later Sholes/Soule design and the July one the earlier design.*

### 1873: Remington contracts to build the Sholes & Glidden

On 1 March 1873 Densmore signs with E. Remington & Sons, whose mechanics Jenne and Clough rework the Milwaukee machine for mass production (at least 1,000 machines).

**Machine:** [Sholes-Glidden](../machines/sholes-glidden-2.md) · **Confidence:** high

Sources: [asme.org](https://www.asme.org/wwwasmeorg/media/ResourceFiles/AboutASME/Who%20We%20Are/Engineering%20History/Landmarks/249-Sholes-Glidden-Type-Writer.pdf), [en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sholes_and_Glidden_typewriter)

*Remington did not acquire the patent rights; Densmore paid for manufacture.*

### 1874: Carriage return by foot treadle, soon replaced by a hand lever

The Sholes & Glidden returns its carriage with a foot treadle on its sewing-machine stand; it was quickly displaced by a hand lever.

**Machine:** [Sholes & Glidden](../machines/sholes-glidden-3.md) · **Feature:** [Carriage return and line space](typewriter-features.md) · **Confidence:** medium

Sources: [gutenberg.org](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/60794), [archive.org](https://archive.org/details/TypewriterTopicsVol55No2Oct1923-medium)

*Mares (1909) says the early machine used a long lever or "hand-treadle" at the right and the No. 2 supplied a proper handle.*

### 1874: Sholes & Glidden goes on sale with the QWERTY keyboard

The Sholes & Glidden reaches the market on 1 July 1874 with four rows of keys in the QWERTY order, typing capitals only, as a blind (understrike) writer at about $125.

**Machine:** [Sholes & Glidden](../machines/sholes-glidden-3.md) · **Feature:** [QWERTY keyboard](typewriter-features.md) · **Confidence:** high

Sources: [en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sholes_and_Glidden_typewriter), [asme.org](https://www.asme.org/wwwasmeorg/media/ResourceFiles/AboutASME/Who%20We%20Are/Engineering%20History/Landmarks/249-Sholes-Glidden-Type-Writer.pdf)

*ASME: QWERTY was on the very first Sholes & Glidden machines, i.e. before the 1878 keyboard patent. About 400 sold by Dec 1874 (Wikipedia). Remington placed it on the market 1874; production began 1873.*

### 1874: Sholes & Glidden prints through a travelling inked ribbon

The first production typewriter prints through an inked ribbon that travels across the printing point with the carriage, the arrangement every later standard machine kept.

**Machine:** [Sholes & Glidden](../machines/sholes-glidden-3.md) · **Feature:** [Ink ribbon](typewriter-features.md) · **Confidence:** medium

Sources: [gutenberg.org](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/60794), [en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sholes_and_Glidden_typewriter)

*The ribbon was not new with Sholes (earlier inventors used inked ribbons/pads); this row marks its arrival on a production machine. Story of the Typewriter (1923) is a Remington publication.*

### 1878: Bell warns of the line end

On the early Remington shift-key machines a bell rings several spaces before the end of the writing line.

**Machine:** Remington blind models (No. 2 era) (E. Remington & Sons) · **Feature:** [Line-end bell](typewriter-features.md) · **Confidence:** low

Sources: [archive.org](https://archive.org/details/evolutionoftypew00oden), [psprint.com](https://www.psprint.com/resources/printing-and-typography-history-the-typewriter/)

*Oden (1917) describes the bell on the early Remington blind models without giving a first date. A secondary source credits Samuel W. Francis's 1857 writing-machine patent with a line-end bell. Not established when the bell first appeared on a production machine.*

### 1878: Remington No. 2 adds the shift key

Remington No. 2 adds a shift key, so one key types both a capital and a small letter; Brooks's 1878 patent shifts the platen between two types on each bar.

**Machine:** [Remington 2](../machines/remington-2.md) · **Patent:** [US202923](../patents/US202923.md), 1878-04-30, Byron A. Brooks (two-type typebars); Lucien S. Crandall (cylinder shift, per Remington) · **Feature:** [Shift key (case shift)](typewriter-features.md) · **Confidence:** high

Sources: [gutenberg.org](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/60794), [patents.google.com](https://patents.google.com/patent/US202923A/en)

*Patent filed 1875-12-30; its stated object is printing capitals and small letters "without duplicate keys". Remington's own 1923 history credits the cylinder-shifting device to Lucien S. Crandall and the two-type bar to Brooks, so the credit is split.*

### 1878: Sholes patents the QWERTY keyboard

Sholes's patent of 27 August 1878 is the first documented appearance of the QWERTY layout in a patent.

**Machine:** Sholes type-writer (keyboard patent) (C. Latham Sholes) · **Patent:** [US207559](../patents/US207559.md), 1878-08-27, Christopher Latham Sholes · **Feature:** [QWERTY keyboard](typewriter-features.md) · **Confidence:** high

Sources: [hagley.org](https://hagley.org/node/2906), [patents.google.com](https://patents.google.com/patent/US207559A/en)

*The layout was already on the 1873-74 Sholes & Glidden (ASME), so the patent records rather than introduces it.*


## 1880–1889: Rivals and the shift key

### 1880: Hammond patent: interchangeable type shuttle

James B. Hammond patents a type-wheel machine whose type shuttle can be swapped in seconds to change typeface or language; the Hammond No. 1 reached buyers in 1884.

**Machine:** Hammond No. 1 (Ideal keyboard) (Hammond Typewriter Co. (New York)) · **Patent:** [US224088](../patents/US224088.md), 1880-02-03, James B. Hammond · **Feature:** [Interchangeable type](typewriter-features.md) · **Confidence:** high

Sources: [gutenberg.org](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/60794), [princeton.edu](https://www.princeton.edu/~graphicarts/2012/07/a_typewriter.html)

*Patent applied 1875-11-24. Market date varies: 1880 (patent), 1881 (planned), 1884 (first sales) per collectors. Story of the Typewriter: Pratt yielded type-wheel priority to Hammond after an interference.*

### 1881: Caligraph: first double-keyboard machine

The Caligraph gives every character its own key (a double keyboard) instead of a shift, the first such commercial typebar machine.

**Machine:** Caligraph No. 1 (caps only) and Caligraph No. 2 (upper and lower case) (American Writing Machine Co. (New York)) · **Confidence:** medium

Sources: [antiquetypewriters.com](https://www.antiquetypewriters.com/typewriter/calligraph-2/), [gutenberg.org](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/60794)

*Dates disagree: Caligraph Patent Co. organised 1880 (Typewriter Topics 1923); introduced 1881 (Scientific American via antiquetypewriters); full double keyboard on No. 2 of 1882; "placed on the market in 1883" (Story of the Typewriter 1923, Typewriter Topics 1923). antiquetypewriters links US341089 (Yost, 1886,) but that is a later patent, not the founding one; no founding patent number established.*

### 1883: Crandall type-sleeve machine

Crandall's type sleeve, a removable cylinder carrying all the characters, goes into production with the Crandall No. 1 in 1883 after his 1881 patent.

**Machine:** Crandall No. 1 (patent: three-bank Crandall) (Crandall Machine Co. (Groton / Syracuse, NY)) · **Patent:** [US251338](../patents/US251338.md), 1881-12-20, Lucien S. Crandall · **Feature:** [Interchangeable type](typewriter-features.md) · **Confidence:** medium

Sources: [antiquetypewriters.com](https://www.antiquetypewriters.com/typewriter/crandall-1-typewriter/), [patents.google.com](https://patents.google.com/patent/US251338A/en)

*The 1881 patent machine (three-bank) is believed never produced; patent model at the Smithsonian. antiquetypewriters also claims the Crandall 1 had the first proportional spacing and first @ key (single source). Typewriter Topics 1923 dates Crandall's first invention to 1879.*

### 1887: Yost: ink pad instead of a ribbon

The Yost drops the ribbon: its typebars rest on a ring-shaped felt ink pad and print directly, through a type guide that keeps alignment.

**Machine:** [Yost 1](../machines/yost-1.md) · **Confidence:** medium

Sources: [gutenberg.org](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/60794), [antiquetypewriters.com](https://www.antiquetypewriters.com/typewriter/yost-1-typewriter/)

*Story of the Typewriter (1923) dates it 1888; Typewriter Topics (1923) says built at Merritt Bros., Springfield, Jan 1887; collector examples dated 1887. First model had a double keyboard. Oden (1917): first machine of importance to use an ink pad. antiquetypewriters links US522333 (Yost, 1894,), a later patent.*

### 1889: Columbia Bar-Lock: early downstrike visible writer

The Bar-Lock's typebars strike down onto the top of the platen, so the typist can see the line, and it was claimed as the first keyed visible writer on the market.

**Machine:** Columbia Bar-Lock (Columbia Typewriter Mfg. Co. (New York)) · **Patent:** [US400265](../patents/US400265.md), 1889-03-26, Charles Spiro · **Feature:** [Visible writing](typewriter-features.md) · **Confidence:** medium

Sources: [collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk](https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/objects/co38141/bar-lock-typewriter-1889), [archive.org](https://archive.org/details/TypewriterTopicsVol55No2Oct1923-medium)

*Dates: 1888 (Story of the Typewriter), 1889 (Science Museum). Typewriter Topics lists Bar-Lock patents of 1889-1891; US400265 (filed 1887) shows the locking pins; US447438 (1891,) is close to production. Story of the Typewriter calls the Canadian Horton (1883) the first typebar visible writer. Double keyboard.*


## 1890–1899: Visible writing and the office machine

### 1890: Gathright tabulator patent

Josiah B. Gathright patents a tabulating stop that jumps the carriage to set columns, later held by a US appeals court to be the first commercially successful tabulator.

**Machine:** Underwood (tabulator later controlled by Underwood) · **Patent:** [US436916](../patents/US436916.md), 1890-09-23, Josiah B. Gathright · **Feature:** [Tabulator](typewriter-features.md) · **Confidence:** medium

Sources: [archive.org](https://archive.org/details/evolutionoftypew00oden), [patents.google.com](https://patents.google.com/patent/US436916A/en)

*Second Gathright patent US452268 (1891-05-12,). Underwood owned and enforced these patents (Underwood v. Fox, 1909). Oden (1917) says the Underwood "controlled all patents... covering the tabulating idea".*

### 1890: Smith Premier No. 1

Lyman C. Smith markets Alexander T. Brown's Smith Premier, which became the leading double-keyboard machine and had a built-in type-cleaning brush.

**Machine:** [Smith Premier 1](../machines/smith-premier-1.md) · **Patent:** [US411421](../patents/US411421.md), 1889-09-24, Alexander T. Brown · **Confidence:** medium

Sources: [gutenberg.org](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/60794), [archive.org](https://archive.org/details/evolutionoftypew00oden)

*US411421 is Brown's patent for the type-cleaning brush, not the whole machine. Oden (1917) also credits the Smith Premier with the first practical automatic ribbon reverse, conflicting with Remington's 1896 claim. Production start: 1889 vs 1890 in different sources.*

### 1891: Daugherty patent: front-strike visible machine

James D. Daugherty patents a front-strike typebar machine; the Daugherty Visible was among the first to type in full view with a four-bank keyboard and single shift.

**Machine:** Daugherty Visible (Daugherty Typewriter Co. (Kittanning, PA)) · **Patent:** [US457258](../patents/US457258.md), 1891-08-04, James Denny Daugherty · **Feature:** [Visible writing](typewriter-features.md) · **Confidence:** medium

Sources: [typewriterdatabase.com](https://www.typewriterdatabase.com/1893-daugherty-visible.8332.typewriter), [patents.google.com](https://patents.google.com/patent/US457258A/en)

*Market date: 1890 (Typewriter Topics 1923) vs 1893 (most collector sources). Follow-up patent US478925 (1892-07-12,). Prouty & Hynes had patented a front-stroke visible design in 1888 (US389854, 1888-09-18, per Oden;).*

### 1891: Densmore: early practical back-spacer

The Densmore, launched in 1891 with ball-bearing typebars, is credited with introducing the back-spacer "in a practical manner".

**Machine:** [Densmore 1](../machines/densmore-1.md) · **Feature:** [Backspace](typewriter-features.md) · **Confidence:** low

Sources: [archive.org](https://archive.org/details/evolutionoftypew00oden), [archive.org](https://archive.org/details/TypewriterTopicsVol55No2Oct1923-medium)

*Disputed first: Oden (1917) credits the Densmore; Mares (1909) thinks the Hammond had it first, then the Densmore; hammondtypewriter.com puts Hammond first (1898) and cites a Travis patent (1897). It is not established that the 1891 Densmore already had the back-spacer.*

### 1891: Oliver patent; Oliver No. 1 follows

Thomas Oliver patents his U-shaped downstrike typebars; the Oliver went on sale around 1894-95 and became the most successful downstrike visible writer.

**Machine:** [Oliver 1](../machines/oliver-1.md) · **Patent:** [US450107](../patents/US450107.md), 1891-04-07, Thomas Oliver · **Feature:** [Visible writing](typewriter-features.md) · **Confidence:** medium

Sources: [patents.google.com](https://patents.google.com/patent/US450107A/en), [olivertypewriters.com](https://olivertypewriters.com/history)

*Patent filed 1890-08-26 (olivertypewriters.com gives that as the first patent date). Story of the Typewriter: Oliver 1894. Woodstock factory from Jan 1896. Later patent US562337 (1896,).*

### 1893: Blickensderfer: portable type-wheel machine

Blickensderfer's light type-wheel machine, patented in 1892 and shown at the 1893 Chicago World's Fair, is the first portable produced in quantity, with an interchangeable type wheel.

**Machine:** [Blickensderfer 5 (A)](../machines/blickensderfer-5-a.md) · **Patent:** [US472692](../patents/US472692.md), 1892-04-12, George C. Blickensderfer · **Feature:** [Portable machines](typewriter-features.md) · **Confidence:** medium

Sources: [connecticuthistory.org](https://connecticuthistory.org/the-portable-typewriting-machine-today-in-history/), [archive.org](https://archive.org/details/TypewriterTopicsVol55No2Oct1923-medium)

*Earlier patent US457333 (1891-08-04,). Wikipedia: Models 1 and 5 shown in 1893; Invention & Technology: Nos. 1 and 3 shown, No. 5 on sale 1893, volume production 1896.*

### 1893: Crandall offers a two-colour ribbon

A later Crandall model carries a ribbon mechanism for two colours, one of the earliest two-colour ribbons on an American machine.

**Machine:** [Crandall No. 3](../machines/crandall-no-3.md) · **Feature:** [Two-colour ribbon](typewriter-features.md) · **Confidence:** low

Sources: [archive.org](https://archive.org/details/TypewriterTopicsVol55No2Oct1923-medium), [en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucien_Stephen_Crandall)

*Typewriter Topics (1923) says the last Crandall model had a two-colour ribbon mechanism; a web source dates it to the Crandall No. 4 of 1893. No source found that names the first two-colour ribbon.*

### 1893: Union Typewriter Co. makes QWERTY the standard

Five large US makers merge as the Union Typewriter Company in 1893 and adopt QWERTY, after which it is the industry standard.

**Feature:** [QWERTY keyboard](typewriter-features.md) · **Confidence:** medium

Sources: [hagley.org](https://hagley.org/node/2906), [en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/QWERTY)

*Hagley statement; the list of the five makers is from general histories.*

### 1894: Wagner visible-writing patent (basis of the Underwood)

The front-strike visible-writing patent behind the Underwood is issued on 31 July 1894, filed by Herman L. Wagner and assigned to his father Franz X. Wagner.

**Machine:** Wagner / Underwood front-strike (Wagner Typewriter Co.) · **Patent:** [US523698](../patents/US523698.md), 1894-07-31, Herman L. Wagner (assignor to Franz X. Wagner) · **Feature:** [Visible writing](typewriter-features.md) · **Confidence:** medium

Sources: [oztypewriter.blogspot.com](https://oztypewriter.blogspot.com/2011/07/on-this-day-in-typewriter-history-lxxii.html), [patents.google.com](https://patents.google.com/patent/US523698A/en)

*Filed 1893-04-27. Usually credited to Franz X. Wagner; the patent names Herman. Related: US497560 (1893, alignment) and US559345 (1896, joint), both.*

### 1895: Bar-Lock No. 4 tabulator

The Bar-Lock No. 4 carries a tabulator under Spiro patents of 23 July 1895, claimed by the trade press as the first practical tabulator.

**Machine:** Columbia Bar-Lock No. 4 (Columbia Typewriter Mfg. Co.) · **Feature:** [Tabulator](typewriter-features.md) · **Confidence:** low

Sources: [archive.org](https://archive.org/details/TypewriterTopicsVol55No2Oct1923-medium)

*Single source (Typewriter Topics 1923); conflicts with Gathright (1890, court-recognised) and Underwood (first in-built). Patent number not found.*

### 1896: Remington automatic ribbon reverse

Remington fits the first automatic ribbon reverse, by George B. Webb, so typists no longer had to reverse the ribbon by hand.

**Machine:** [Remington 7](../machines/remington-7.md) · **Feature:** [Automatic ribbon reverse](typewriter-features.md) · **Confidence:** medium

Sources: [gutenberg.org](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/60794), [archive.org](https://archive.org/details/TypewriterTopicsVol55No2Oct1923-medium)

*Remington's own claim (Story of the Typewriter and Remington copy in Typewriter Topics 1923). Oden (1917) credits the Smith Premier with the first practical automatic ribbon reverse; Spiro had ribbon-reverse patents from 1890; US446886 (Joshua Pusey, 1891,) is an earlier ribbon-feed-reversing patent for Remington-type machines. Webb's patent number not found.*

### 1896: Underwood No. 1: front-strike, fully visible

The Underwood No. 1 strikes the front of the platen so the whole line stays in view, the layout that every standard typewriter later copied.

**Machine:** Underwood No. 1 (Wagner Typewriter Co. / Underwood Typewriter Co. (New York)) · **Patent:** [US559345](../patents/US559345.md), 1896-04-28, Herman L. Wagner; Franz X. Wagner · **Feature:** [Visible writing](typewriter-features.md) · **Confidence:** high

Sources: [antiquetypewriters.com](https://www.antiquetypewriters.com/typewriter/underwood-1-typewriter/), [collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk](https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/objects/co38171/underwood-1-typewriter-1897)

*Date: 1896 (antiquetypewriters, serial 2326) vs "placed on the market in 1897" (Story of the Typewriter); Science Museum dates its example 1897. Daugherty had front-strike visible writing earlier, so "first" is contested; Oden calls the Underwood the first practical front-stroke fully visible machine.*

### 1897: Underwood builds the tabulator into the machine

The Underwood is the first machine with a tabulator built in at no extra charge, for setting work in neat columns.

**Machine:** Underwood No. 1 (Underwood Typewriter Co.) · **Feature:** [Tabulator](typewriter-features.md) · **Confidence:** medium

Sources: [collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk](https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/objects/co38171/underwood-1-typewriter-1897), [archive.org](https://archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.201721)

*Science Museum: "first machine with a tabulator"; Mares (1909): first fitted with a tabulator as part of the machine; Oden (1917): first in-built tabulator. Exact year the tabulator first shipped on Underwoods not established.*

### 1898: Gorin decimal tabulator on the Remington

The Gorin Tabulator, the first decimal tabulator, appears as a Remington attachment and steps the carriage to units, tens or hundreds for columns of figures.

**Machine:** [Remington 8](../machines/remington-8.md) · **Patent:** [US559449](../patents/US559449.md), 1896-05-05, Fred P. Gorin (Chicago) · **Feature:** [Decimal tabulator](typewriter-features.md) · **Confidence:** medium

Sources: [gutenberg.org](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/60794), [patents.google.com](https://patents.google.com/patent/US559449A/en)

*Remington claim. US559449 is Gorin's spacing attachment "for tabulated work" (1896); Museums Victoria says patented 1895 and assigned to Remington. Mares (1909) says the Stearns Visible was the first with a decimal tabulator built in. Later Remington claims the first key-set decimal tabulator (1911).*

### 1898: Hammond back spacer

Hammond offers a back-spacing attachment, standard on new machines from 1 December 1898, one of the earliest backspace keys.

**Machine:** [Hammond 2 Ideal](../machines/hammond-2-ideal.md) · **Feature:** [Backspace](typewriter-features.md) · **Confidence:** medium

Sources: [hammondtypewriter.com](https://hammondtypewriter.com/2023/04/25/backspaces/), [etymonline.com](https://www.etymonline.com/word/backspace)

*Etymonline quotes an 1899 Phonetic Journal report of a Hammond "back-space key". Mares (1909): first on the Hammond, then the Densmore (he was unsure). Travis patent of April 1897 also cited as an early backspace; number not found.*


## 1900–1919: The standard typewriter

### 1900: Cahill electric typewriter

Thaddeus Cahill patents and builds an electric typewriter, called the first electric typewriter made in series.

**Machine:** Cahill electric (Thaddeus Cahill) · **Patent:** [US656576](../patents/US656576.md), 1900-08-21, Thaddeus Cahill · **Feature:** [Electric drive](typewriter-features.md) · **Confidence:** low

Sources: [en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typewriter), [patents.google.com](https://patents.google.com/patent/US656576A/en)

*Wikipedia only; not cross-checked. US656576 is a Cahill typewriter patent issued 1900 from an 1896 application; it was not confirmed that this is his electric machine.*

### 1900: Underwood No. 5: the standard form

The Underwood No. 5 (front-strike, four-bank, single shift, visible) begins production in 1900 and sets the standard form of the office typewriter for decades.

**Machine:** [Underwood 5](../machines/underwood-5.md) · **Confidence:** high

Sources: [antiquetypewriters.com](https://www.antiquetypewriters.com/typewriter/underwood-1-typewriter/), [nps.gov](https://www.nps.gov/long/blogs/underwood-no-5-typewriter.htm)

*Produced with Nos. 3 and 4 from late 1900 to about 1931-32; about five million sold. Backspace and bichrome ribbon added about serial 245,000 (c. 1908-09; Davis Typewriter Works).*

### 1902: Blickensderfer Electric

Blickensderfer builds a motor-driven type-wheel typewriter, an early electric that failed commercially because electric supply was not standardised.

**Machine:** Blickensderfer Electric (Blickensderfer Mfg. Co. (Stamford, CT)) · **Patent:** [US656085](../patents/US656085.md), 1900-08-14, George C. Blickensderfer · **Feature:** [Electric drive](typewriter-features.md) · **Confidence:** medium

Sources: [en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blickensderfer_typewriter), [davidhayes.ca](https://davidhayes.ca/?p=2804)

*Debut 1901 (Pan-American Exposition, Buffalo) vs 1902. Called "first electric" by some sources, second (after Cahill) by others. Three known survivors. Patent text mentions a motor case.*

### 1906: Standard Folding: first typebar portable

Frank Rose's folding typebar portable, whose platen folds over the keyboard, is the ancestor of the Corona.

**Machine:** [Standard Folding 1](../machines/standard-folding-1.md) · **Patent:** [US754242](../patents/US754242.md), 1904-03-08, Frank S. Rose; George F. Rose · **Feature:** [Portable machines](typewriter-features.md) · **Confidence:** medium

Sources: [archive.org](https://archive.org/details/TypewriterTopicsVol55No2Oct1923-medium), [si.edu](https://si.edu/es/object/corona-no-3-typewriter:nmah_849921)

*Dates: Corona advertising (Typewriter Topics 1923) says 1906; Smithsonian: Groton production around 1907; other accounts move to Groton late 1909. Patent filed 1903-02-11.*

### 1908: Remington No. 10: Remington goes visible

The Remington No. 10 of 1908 is the first visible typewriter sold under the Remington name, marking the end of the blind-writer era among the big makers.

**Machine:** [Remington 10](../machines/remington-10.md) · **Feature:** [Visible writing](typewriter-features.md) · **Confidence:** medium

Sources: [archive.org](https://archive.org/details/TypewriterTopicsVol55No2Oct1923-medium), [archive.org](https://archive.org/details/evolutionoftypew00oden)

*Union Typewriter Co. had earlier tried visible writing through the Monarch (Oden). Smith Premier No. 10 followed into visible form.*

### 1909: Bichrome ribbon becomes standard

By 1909 the two-colour (bichrome) ribbon with a colour selector is almost universal on standard machines; Underwood adds it to the No. 5 around 1908-09.

**Machine:** Standard office machines (e.g. Underwood No. 5) (various) · **Feature:** [Two-colour ribbon](typewriter-features.md) · **Confidence:** medium

Sources: [archive.org](https://archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.201721), [davistypewriters.blogspot.com](http://davistypewriters.blogspot.com/2013/09/underwood-standard-3-4-5-sub-model.html)

*Mares (1909, British) speaks of the market generally; the Underwood date comes from serial-number evidence. Oden (1917) says the Royal No. 5 added backspace, tabulator and bichrome ribbon because they were already on all the older machines.*

### 1909: Built-in tabulator and backspace become standard

By 1909 every "standard" machine has a built-in tabulator at no extra cost and the back-space key is found even on low-priced machines.

**Machine:** Standard office machines (various) · **Feature:** [Backspace](typewriter-features.md) · **Confidence:** medium

Sources: [archive.org](https://archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.201721), [hammondtypewriter.com](https://hammondtypewriter.com/2023/04/25/backspaces/)

*hammondtypewriter.com puts general adoption of the backspace (Blickensderfer, Densmore, Remington and others) around 1906. This row also covers the tabulator becoming standard.*

### 1910: Noiseless typewriter

Kidder's Noiseless replaces the hammer blow with a pressing typebar so the machine is quiet; a cam-driven first model was replaced by Anderson's 1912 type action.

**Machine:** Noiseless No. 1 (Noiseless Typewriter Co. (Middletown, CT)) · **Confidence:** medium

Sources: [archive.org](https://archive.org/details/TypewriterTopicsVol55No2Oct1923-medium), [typewriterdatabase.com](https://typewriterdatabase.com/1910-noiseless-no1.11099.typewriter)

*Dates: first production Noiseless No. 1 c. 1910 (typewriterdatabase, serial 1066); 1912 company catalogue (Hagley); Wikipedia says the company released its first model in 1917. Kidder's noiseless patents filed 1908-09; numbers not established.*

### 1911: Remington key-set decimal tabulator

Remington brings out the first key-set decimal tabulator, letting the operator set column stops from the keyboard.

**Machine:** [Remington 11](../machines/remington-11.md) · **Feature:** [Decimal tabulator](typewriter-features.md) · **Confidence:** low

Sources: [archive.org](https://archive.org/details/TypewriterTopicsVol55No2Oct1923-medium)

*Remington advertising claim printed in Typewriter Topics (Oct 1923); not cross-checked.*

### 1912: Corona No. 3 folding portable

The three-bank, folding Corona No. 3 becomes the first typebar portable to sell widely.

**Machine:** [Corona 3](../machines/corona-3.md) · **Feature:** [Portable machines](typewriter-features.md) · **Confidence:** high

Sources: [gutenberg.org](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/60794), [si.edu](https://si.edu/es/object/corona-no-3-typewriter:nmah_849921)

*Story of the Typewriter (1923): "the Corona, which dates from the year 1912". Sales of about 6,000 in 1912, rising rapidly.*

### 1912: Smathers power typewriter patent

James Fields Smathers files in 1912 for a power-driven typewriter that needs less effort to operate; his design later underlay the Remington Electric and the Electromatic.

**Machine:** Smathers power-operated typewriter · **Patent:** [US1077005](../patents/US1077005.md), 1913-10-28, James Fields Smathers (Kansas City) · **Feature:** [Electric drive](typewriter-features.md) · **Confidence:** medium

Sources: [patents.google.com](https://patents.google.com/patent/US1077005A/en), [en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Fields_Smathers)

*Filed 1912-04-18, issued 1913-10-28. Wikipedia dates his "first practical power-operated typewriter" to 1914. Later patent US1227846 (1917).*


## 1920–1950: Portables, electrics and proportional spacing

### 1920: Remington Portable: four-row keyboard

The Remington Portable is the first portable with the full four-row standard keyboard, shown at the New York Business Show in October 1920.

**Machine:** Remington Portable No. 1 (Remington Typewriter Co.) · **Feature:** [Portable machines](typewriter-features.md) · **Confidence:** high

Sources: [archive.org](https://archive.org/details/TypewriterTopicsVol55No2Oct1923-medium), [si.edu](https://www.si.edu/object/nmah_850041)

*Remington claim in Typewriter Topics corroborated by Smithsonian and Museums Victoria. Royal and Underwood portables followed in 1926.*

### 1925: Remington Electric

Remington sells an electric typewriter built on its No. 12 with Northeast Electric motors and Smathers's design; about 2,500 were made.

**Machine:** [Remington Electric](../machines/remington-electric.md) · **Feature:** [Electric drive](typewriter-features.md) · **Confidence:** medium

Sources: [en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM_Electric), [typewriterdatabase.com](https://www.typewriterdatabase.com/1925-remington-electric.11556.typewriter)

*A collector argues Woodstock's Electrite was on sale in 1925 while Remington advertised its electric only from 1927; "first commercial electric" is contested.*

### 1929: Electromatic

Northeast Electric produces its own Electromatic electric typewriter after the Remington deal lapses.

**Machine:** [Electromatic](../machines/electromatic.md) · **Feature:** [Electric drive](typewriter-features.md) · **Confidence:** medium

Sources: [en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM_Electric), [historyofinformation.com](https://historyofinformation.com/detail.php?id=612)

*Electromatic Typewriters Inc. was acquired by IBM in 1933.*

### 1935: IBM Electric Model 01

IBM, having bought Electromatic in 1933, issues the improved Electric Model 01, the first commercially successful electric typewriter in the US.

**Machine:** IBM Electric Typewriter Model 01 (IBM (Rochester, NY)) · **Feature:** [Electric drive](typewriter-features.md) · **Confidence:** high

Sources: [si.edu](https://www.si.edu/object/nmah_849996), [en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM_Electric)

*Museums Victoria gives 1933 for the Model 01, apparently confusing it with the purchase date.*

### 1944: IBM Executive: proportional spacing

The IBM Executive gives each letter a width in units of 1/32 inch (proportional spacing), so typed pages look typeset; announced 1941 but delayed by the war until 1944.

**Machine:** IBM Executive (IBM) · **Feature:** [Proportional spacing](typewriter-features.md) · **Confidence:** medium

Sources: [en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM_Electric), [typewriterdatabase.com](https://typewriterdatabase.com/1950-ibm-model-04-executive.15625.typewriter)

*"First proportional" is disputed: antiquetypewriters credits the Crandall No. 1 (1883) with 2-, 3- and 4-unit spacing, and Hammond advertised variable spacing in 1923 (Typewriter Topics). Typewriter Database says the first Executive went to President Roosevelt. Patent number not found.*

## About this page

Compiled from museum and collector references, Google Patents, and period sources (Remington's *The Story of the Typewriter 1873–1923*, *Typewriter Topics* 1923, Oden's *Evolution of the Typewriter* 1917, Mares's *History of the Typewriter* 1909), several of which are company histories and favour their makers. "Firsts" are contested in typewriter history; the confidence and notes say how solid each one is.
