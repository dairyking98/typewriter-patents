# Typewriter features through time

The features typewriters gained between the 1860s and 1950, in the order they first appear in this library's patents: when each shows up, how often patents dealt with it decade by decade, when it became standard on new machines, and what survives of it today.

## What each one did

### Portable machines

Small, light machines with a case, for travel and the home.

Earliest patent here that mentions it: [US39296](../patents/US39296.md), 1863, Livermoore, Benjamin. 151 patents in the library deal with it. **Today:** Laptops took the idea over.

### Ink ribbon

An inked fabric ribbon between the type and the paper, in place of ink pads and rollers.

Earliest patent here that mentions it: [US65807](../patents/US65807.md), 1867, Hall, Thomas. 2601 patents in the library deal with it. First machine in the Lexikon with it: [nicht fabrikmäßig hergestellt machine, 1867](../machines/nicht-fabrikma-ig-hergestellt-1867.md) (1867). Share of new machines (inking = ribbon): 1850s 0/7, 1860s 1/5, 1870s 5/8, 1880s 15/49, 1890s 89/136, 1900s 121/151, 1910s 102/120, 1920s 164/173, 1930s 157/162. **Today:** Typewriters to the end; impact and receipt printers still use ribbons.

### Space bar

One long bar under the keys that moves the carriage one space without printing.

Earliest patent here that mentions it: [US175051](../patents/US175051.md), 1876, Deming, Philander. 572 patents in the library deal with it. **Today:** The space bar.

### Line-end bell

A bell that rings a few characters before the right margin.

Earliest patent here that mentions it: [US224088](../patents/US224088.md), 1880, Hammond, James B.. 74 patents in the library deal with it. **Today:** The terminal bell (BEL in ASCII).

### Proportional spacing

Each letter takes a space to suit its width (m wide, i narrow) instead of one fixed pitch; IBM's Executive (1944) brought it to offices.

Earliest patent here that mentions it: [US251338](../patents/US251338.md), 1881, Crandall, Lucien S.. 68 patents in the library deal with it. **Today:** Proportional fonts: nearly all text today.

### Correction and erasing

Ways to remove or cover a mistake: erasing tables and shields, then (after 1950) correcting ribbons.

Earliest patent here that mentions it: [US246960](../patents/US246960.md), 1881, Lindgren, Justus F.. 219 patents in the library deal with it. **Today:** Delete and undo.

### Shift key (case shift)

Two characters on each type bar or row, and a key that shifts the carriage or the type basket so the second one prints: half the keys for the same character set.

Earliest patent here that mentions it: [US284231](../patents/US284231.md), 1883, Pritchard, Arthur W.. 840 patents in the library deal with it. First machine in the Lexikon with it: [Burt](../machines/burt.md) (1829). Share of new machines (shift = single shift): 1850s 1/9, 1860s 0/7, 1870s 2/8, 1880s 8/40, 1890s 39/132, 1900s 78/156, 1910s 61/121, 1920s 131/178, 1930s 155/170. **Today:** The Shift key.

### Automatic ribbon reverse

The ribbon spools change direction by themselves when one runs empty.

Earliest patent here that mentions it: [US309272](../patents/US309272.md), 1884, Whitcomb, James A.. 288 patents in the library deal with it.

### Carriage return and line space

One lever that turns the platen to the next line and pushes the carriage back to the start.

Earliest patent here that mentions it: [US315700](../patents/US315700.md), 1885, Belden, Edwin S.. 358 patents in the library deal with it. **Today:** The Return or Enter key; CR and LF in character codes (ASCII, 1963).

### Interchangeable type

A type wheel, shuttle or sleeve that lifts out, so one machine writes in several typefaces or languages.

Earliest patent here that mentions it: [US322989](../patents/US322989.md), 1885, Spiro, Charles. 26 patents in the library deal with it. **Today:** Changeable fonts: the Selectric's golf ball (1961), daisy wheels, then digital fonts.

### Margin stops and release

Adjustable stops for the left and right margins, and a key to type past them.

Earliest patent here that mentions it: [US349349](../patents/US349349.md), 1886, Jenne, William K.. 402 patents in the library deal with it. **Today:** Page margins in word processors.

### Electric drive

A motor or magnets do the work of the type stroke and carriage, so a light touch prints evenly.

Earliest patent here that mentions it: [US335669](../patents/US335669.md), 1886, Worrall, Thomas D.. 582 patents in the library deal with it. **Today:** Electric and electronic typewriters, then computer keyboards.

### Tabulator

Stops set along the carriage and a key that runs the carriage to the next one, for columns and indents.

Earliest patent here that mentions it: [US439544](../patents/US439544.md), 1890, McCormack, Ulysses Sherman. 825 patents in the library deal with it. **Today:** The Tab key and tab stops (HT in ASCII).

### Shift lock

Holds the shift down for a run of capitals.

Earliest patent here that mentions it: [US457673](../patents/US457673.md), 1891, Scudder, Wilbur S.. 89 patents in the library deal with it. **Today:** Caps Lock.

### QWERTY keyboard

The Sholes and Glidden layout, which Remington's success made the norm; rivals such as the Ideal (DHIATENSOR) layout lost out.

Earliest patent here that mentions it: [US557728](../patents/US557728.md), 1896, Travis, William H.. 24 patents in the library deal with it. First machine in the Lexikon with it: [E. Remington & Sons machine, 1879](../machines/e-remington-sons-1879.md) (1879). Share of new machines (keyboard = QWERTY-type keyboard): 1850s 0/10, 1860s 0/9, 1870s 4/10, 1880s 5/51, 1890s 55/138, 1900s 125/158, 1910s 101/121, 1920s 164/180, 1930s 156/167. **Today:** Still the standard layout of computer and phone keyboards.

### Visible writing

Type that strikes the front of the platen, so the typist sees the line as it is written; on early understrike machines the writing was hidden until the carriage was lifted.

Earliest patent here that mentions it: [US575916](../patents/US575916.md), 1897, Webb, George B.. 1003 patents in the library deal with it. First machine in the Lexikon with it: [Cooper](../machines/cooper.md) (1856). Share of new machines (strike = front strike): 1850s 1/10, 1860s 1/9, 1870s 0/10, 1880s 8/50, 1890s 42/142, 1900s 91/161, 1910s 100/125, 1920s 166/179, 1930s 164/169. **Today:** Seeing the text as you type, on every later typewriter and every screen.

### Backspace

A key that moves the carriage back one space, to correct or to overstrike.

Earliest patent here that mentions it: [US589387](../patents/US589387.md), 1897, Gilbert, Justin. 122 patents in the library deal with it. **Today:** The Backspace key (BS in ASCII).

### Two-colour ribbon

A ribbon with two bands (usually black and red) and a lever that lifts the ribbon to print in the second colour.

Earliest patent here that mentions it: [US787566](../patents/US787566.md), 1905, Dowlen, Connell H.. 74 patents in the library deal with it. **Today:** Coloured text in word processors.

### Decimal tabulator

A row of tab keys for units, tens, hundreds and so on, so figures line up on the decimal point.

Earliest patent here that mentions it: [US858713](../patents/US858713.md), 1907, Gabrielson, Carl. 107 patents in the library deal with it. **Today:** Decimal tab stops in word processors.

## How this was worked out

- A patent counts for a feature when its CPC class (assigned by the patent offices, often long afterwards) matches, or its text mentions the feature; the earliest patent is taken from the text where there is one. A mention is not always an invention: an early hit can be a patent that only refers to the feature.
- The library holds the US typewriter patents to 1951 and German ones to 1950, so "first here" is not necessarily the first anywhere.
- "Standard on new machines" is the first decade in which at least half the machines introduced (with the field known, and at least 5 of them) had the feature, from the machine data of Dingwerth's Lexikon. The Lexikon leans to German machines.
- Bars are relative to each feature's own busiest decade.
